# Writing the piece

Before you write, ask the user for what you are missing. The user's request is never everything the user knows. Send one message with at most six questions, the most useful first. Ask only what would change the piece and what the request does not already answer. Look for these:

- **What the reader gets and what it costs them.** The terms as they touch this reader: pay, price, dates, what they keep, what they lose, what they can still do if they change their mind.
- **What answers the reader's main objection.** Name the objection this reader will raise and ask what the user has that answers it: what has already been done or offered about it, what has changed since, what the proposal leaves in place.
- **What shows the writer can deliver, or knows the reader's world.** A record, a credential, a time they did this before, someone who has checked their work or will vouch for it.
- **What the writer can commit to.** A date, a number, a named person, what happens if it is missed.
- **The reader.** Who exactly reads this, what they already think of the writer, and anything they hold against the writer, when the request does not say.
- **The names and dates the piece cannot go out without.** Who it is addressed to, who signs it, a deadline.
- **What the reader will find out anyway.** Close with one open question: is there a cost, a limit, a weak number or a past failure that the request leaves out and this reader knows or will learn?

When the answers come back, write the piece as you normally would, and hand it over. When the user does not know something, write without it and do not guess.
