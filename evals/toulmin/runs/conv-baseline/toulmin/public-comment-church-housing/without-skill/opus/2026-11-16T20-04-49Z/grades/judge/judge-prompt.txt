Good evening, Commissioners. My name is [Name], and I've lived on Alder Road for nineteen years, two blocks from [Church Name]. I'm also a member of that church, and I'm here to ask you to approve the 64 apartments planned for its back parking lot.

I'll start with traffic, because that's my street too. The traffic study estimates 310 more car trips a day on Alder Road, and it says the intersection at Alder and 4th would drop from a C to a D at rush hour. I'm not going to pretend that's nothing. I drive that intersection every day, and I'll feel it.

But I want you to weigh that against what's on the other side of the scale.

Right now, 412 households are on the county housing authority's waiting list. The average wait is three years. Those households are working, raising kids, caring for parents, and waiting. Your own housing report says this county is short about 2,100 affordable homes.

This project would add 64 homes, and every one of them would rent to households earning under 60 percent of the area median income. That won't close a 2,100-home gap by itself. But 64 families off a waiting list is 64 families who stop waiting.

And look at where it would go. The lot is 1.8 acres of asphalt that sits empty six days a week. We wouldn't be clearing a field or tearing anything down. We'd be building homes on land that's already paved, on a lot that's already part of the neighborhood.

The plan includes 72 parking spaces for 64 units, so it covers its own parking and won't push residents' cars onto Alder or the side streets.

So here's the tradeoff as I see it. My neighbors and I will wait a little longer at one intersection during rush hour. In exchange, 64 households who have been waiting years for a stable, affordable place to live finally get one. I'll take that trade, and I live there.

If the traffic at Alder and 4th is the concern, let's solve it with signal timing, turn lanes, or whatever the engineers recommend. That's a problem we know how to fix. A three-year wait for a home is much harder to fix, and this project actually does something about it.

Please approve it. Thank you.

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.
