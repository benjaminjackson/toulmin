Here's a draft that runs about 2 minutes 45 seconds at a normal speaking pace, so you have a little room to slow down. It addresses traffic directly. Commissioners will hear about traffic from opponents, and it carries more weight when you, a neighbor on Alder Road, bring it up first.

<final_document>
Good evening, Commissioners. My name is [Your Name], and I've lived on Alder Road for nineteen years, two blocks from [Church Name]. I'm a member of the church, and I'm here to ask you to approve these 64 homes.

I'll start with what you'll hear most tonight, because it affects me too: traffic.

The traffic study says this project will add about 310 car trips a day on Alder Road. It says the intersection at Alder and 4th would go from a C to a D at rush hour. I drive through that intersection all the time. I'm not going to tell you that number is zero. It isn't.

But here is what a D means: a longer wait at a light during the busiest part of the day. It doesn't mean gridlock or failure. And I'd ask you to weigh a slightly longer wait for me against what's on the other side of the scale.

Our county's own housing report says we are short about 2,100 affordable homes. Right now, 412 households are on the housing authority's waiting list, and the average wait is three years. That's three years of a family paying more than they can afford, or doubling up with relatives, or moving away from their jobs, their schools, and their churches.

Every one of these 64 apartments would go to a household earning under 60 percent of the area median income. These are the people who work in our stores, care for our parents, and teach in our classrooms.

And look at where they'd be built. The church's back lot is 1.8 acres of pavement that sits empty six days a week. We wouldn't be tearing anything down or clearing open space, and no one would be displaced. We'd be putting a parking lot that mostly holds nothing to use as homes.

The plan also includes 72 parking spaces for 64 units, so residents won't be pushing their cars out onto our street.

Sixty-four homes won't solve a 2,100-home shortage. But they are 64 families who won't be waiting anymore, and this is a site where it can actually happen, with a willing owner and land that's already paved.

I've been a neighbor here for nineteen years. I'd be glad to have 64 more. I ask you to approve this project.

Thank you.
</final_document>

**Before you go:**
- **Fill in the brackets.** Add your name and the church's name.
- **Time it out loud.** If you run long, cut the sentence "These are the people who work in our stores…" first. It's the only line that isn't based on your facts.
- **Have a fact check ready.** Someone may say 72 spaces is too few. You can answer that it's more than one per unit and that the church lot already handles Sunday crowds. Only use the second point if it's true.
- **Bring the numbers.** If commissioners ask questions afterward, it helps to have the housing report and traffic study page numbers with you.
