Good evening, Commissioners. My name is [NAME]. I've lived on Alder Road for 19 years, two blocks from [CHURCH NAME]. I'm also a member of that church, so I'll say that up front. I'm here to ask you to approve the 64 apartments on its back lot.

I want to start with what neighbors are worried about, because I'm one of those neighbors.

The traffic study says this adds about 310 car trips a day on Alder, and that Alder and 4th drops from a C to a D at rush hour. I drive through that intersection. I'll be sitting in that slower line with everyone else. It means a longer wait at a light during rush hour. It's a D, not a failing grade. I'd support the commission asking the county to review the signal timing at Alder and 4th as part of this approval.

On parking: the plan has 72 spaces for 64 apartments. Every household gets a space on site, with eight to spare.

Now the other side of the ledger.

The county's own housing report says we are short about 2,100 affordable homes. Today, 412 households are on the housing authority's waiting list. The average wait is three years. Three years.

This project would house 64 of those households. That's about one in every six families on that list right now.

And it does that on land that isn't being used. That lot is 1.8 acres of pavement that sits empty six days a week. No homes come down, and no one is pushed out.

So here is the trade. On one side, a slower rush hour at one intersection, and I'll be sharing it. On the other, 64 families getting off a three-year waiting list.

If this is turned down, nothing changes. The lot stays empty, the county stays 2,100 homes short, and those 412 households keep waiting.

I've watched this neighborhood for 19 years. I'd like it to be a place that made room. Please approve this project.

Thank you.
