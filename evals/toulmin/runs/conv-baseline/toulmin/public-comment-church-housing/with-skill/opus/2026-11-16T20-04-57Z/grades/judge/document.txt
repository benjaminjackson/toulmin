Good evening, Commissioners. My name is [Name], and I've lived on Alder Road for 19 years, two blocks from [Church Name]. I'm also a member of that church, and I want to say so up front. I'm here as both a neighbor and a member, and I'm asking you to approve these 64 apartments.

Your own housing report says this county is about 2,100 affordable homes short. Right now, 412 households are on the housing authority's waiting list, and on average they wait three years. Every one of these 64 apartments would rent to a household earning under 60 percent of the area median income. That's enough homes for about one in six of the families on that list.

The land is already there. It's a 1.8-acre parking lot that sits empty six days a week. We don't have to clear anything or bring in new roads. We'd just be putting homes on blacktop that mostly stores nothing.

I know traffic is the main worry, and I take it seriously, because I'm the one who lives on Alder Road. The county's traffic study estimates 310 more car trips a day, and it says the Alder and 4th intersection would drop from a C to a D at rush hour. I drive through that intersection every day, and I've thought about what that means. It means a slower light at rush hour. It does not mean a failing intersection. If the commission wants to soften it further, I'd ask you to make a signal-timing review at Alder and 4th a condition of approval.

On parking, the plan has 72 spaces for 64 units, so every apartment gets a space and there are eight left over. These residents won't be parking up and down our street.

So here's the trade as I see it. On one side, my commute gets a little slower at rush hour. On the other side, 64 households get a home they can afford, after a wait that averages three years. I'll take that trade, and I'm the neighbor who has to live with it.

If you say no tonight, nothing really changes, and that's the problem. The lot stays empty six days a week, and the list stays at 412 families and three years.

Please approve this project. Thank you.
