**An Open Letter to Keystone Property Group**

To Diane Weller, Regional Manager:

Harbor View Apartments, at 220 Grant Street, has nine floors, 112 apartments and one elevator. This year that elevator has been out of service on 41 days. That is about one day in every eight. One outage in August lasted 12 days in a row.

Twenty-three of our neighbors are over 75, and six use wheelchairs or walkers. If you use a wheelchair and live above the ground floor, a broken elevator means you cannot leave the building. You can't get to the doctor, the pharmacy or the grocery store, and you have no safe way out in an emergency.

In June, Keystone told us that a new controller part was on order and would arrive in six to eight weeks. That meant mid-August at the latest. It is now November, and we are being told the same thing: six to eight weeks.

We know the elevator passed its city inspection in September. That certificate means the elevator is safe when it runs. It says nothing about how often it runs.

Seventy-four of our 112 households, two-thirds of the building, signed a petition asking for a second elevator. Keystone has told us it would cost about $400,000 and cannot be done in this building. We are willing to hear that. But a decision that leaves our oldest and least mobile neighbors dependent on one unreliable elevator needs more than a one-line answer.

We ask Keystone Property Group for four things:

1. **A firm repair date, in writing,** and written notice to every resident if that date changes.
2. **A stair-climbing chair, kept in the building, and staff trained to use it,** so that residents who cannot use the stairs can get in and out during an outage.
3. **A rent credit for every day the elevator is out of service.** Residents pay rent for a building with a working elevator. On 41 days this year, they did not have one.
4. **The assessment behind the conclusion that a second elevator cannot be built,** shared in writing, along with Keystone's plan to keep residents from being stranded if a second elevator is truly not possible.

If nothing changes, this year's record means residents should expect the elevator to be down about four days a month. Every one of those days, some of our neighbors will be stuck at home.

We would welcome a written response. We will post it in the lobby next to this letter so that every resident can read it.

Sincerely,

The Harbor View Tenants' Association
