<document>
**An Open Letter to [Management Company Name]**
**From the Harbor View Apartments Tenants' Association**

Harbor View has 9 stories, 112 apartments and one elevator. This year that elevator has been out of service on 41 days, about one day in every eight. In August it was out for 12 days in a row.

For most of us, an outage means carrying groceries, laundry and children up the stairs. For some of our neighbors it means much more. Twenty-three residents are over 75, and six use wheelchairs or walkers. When the elevator stops, residents who cannot manage the stairs cannot leave their homes. They can't get to a doctor's appointment, go out for food or get out in an emergency. If they are out when it stops, they cannot get back in.

In June, management told us a new controller part was on order and would arrive in 6 to 8 weeks. More than five months later, we are being told the same thing: 6 to 8 weeks.

We know the city inspected the elevator in September and issued a passing certificate, and we are glad it is safe. But a certificate says the elevator is safe when it runs. It does not say how often it runs, and that is the problem.

Seventy-four households signed our petition for a second elevator. Management says that would cost about $400,000 and cannot be done in this building. We accept that it may not be possible. But if this building will only ever have one elevator, keeping that one running matters all the more, and so does having a plan for when it stops.

We ask for three things:

1. **A written date for the repair.** We need a date in writing, not another estimate.
2. **A stair-climbing chair and trained staff**, so residents who cannot walk the stairs can get in and out of the building during outages.
3. **A rent credit for every day the elevator is out of service.** Our rent pays for a building with a working elevator. On 41 days this year, we did not have one.

We are ready to do our part. The association will keep an up-to-date list of residents who need help on the stairs and share it with management for use in emergencies. We will also meet with management on any weekday evening to work out the details.

Our neighbors have been waiting since June. We ask management to respond in writing and to set a date for that meeting.

Sincerely,

Rosa Delgado, Secretary
Harbor View Apartments Tenants' Association
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
