Last summer, local farms offered us about 9,000 pounds of fresh produce over three months. We had to turn all of it down. We had no way to pick it up and keep it cold, so food grown a few miles away never reached the families who rely on us.

We are a volunteer-run food bank serving a town of about 9,000 people. Each month, our 23 regular volunteers hand out about 38,000 pounds of food to 610 households. About 6,000 pounds of that is fresh produce, milk and eggs, which comes to roughly 10 pounds per household per month.

The produce we refused last summer would have raised our fresh food distribution by half during those three months. In total, it equals a month and a half of all the fresh food we currently give out. A refrigerated van would let us accept those offers.

In 2023, the foundation granted us $12,000 for shelving. Our final report on that grant was four months late, because our treasurer at the time was in the hospital for most of the reporting period. Our current treasurer, Dave Lin, will be responsible for the final report on this grant and will submit it within 30 days of the end of the grant year. We will also send the foundation our fresh food totals every quarter, so you can follow the results as they come in rather than waiting for the final report.

The van costs $68,000. We are asking the foundation for $50,000 and will cover the remaining $18,000 from our own reserves, nearly two-thirds of our savings. We are willing to commit that much of our own money because the food is already being offered to us. What we lack is a way to collect it.

Four of our volunteers have committed to driving the van on a weekly schedule, so farm pickups can begin once the van is in service.

Insurance and fuel for the van will cost about $4,500 a year. That cost is not yet in our operating budget. Our board will identify where it will come from before the van is purchased, and we will confirm the source in our first quarterly report.

Each quarter we will report the pounds of fresh food we distribute, compared with our current 6,000 pounds a month. That will show the foundation exactly how much more fresh food the van puts in the hands of local families.

Without a refrigerated van, next summer will look like last summer. Farms will offer us fresh food, and we will have to turn it away. With it, 610 households can receive the produce that local farms are already offering.
