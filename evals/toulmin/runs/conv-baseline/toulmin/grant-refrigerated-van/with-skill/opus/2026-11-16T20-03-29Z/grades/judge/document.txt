[Food Bank Name] is a volunteer-run food bank serving 610 households in a town of about 9,000 people. Each month we hand out about 38,000 pounds of food, but only 6,000 pounds of it is fresh produce, milk and eggs. That comes to about 10 pounds per household. A refrigerated van would let us accept fresh food we now have to refuse.

**The problem**

Last summer, local farms offered us about 9,000 pounds of produce over three months. We turned all of it down. The farms were willing to give and the food was there, but we had no way to pick it up cold or store it. That is more fresh food than we hand out in six weeks. If we had been able to take it, we could have handed out 50% more fresh food during those months.

**What the van changes**

Two of those farms have written letters (attached) saying they will give again if we can pick up cold. The van lets us do that. Harvests and offers vary, so we are not promising a set number of pounds. We are promising that cold donations we have been turning away will reach our families instead.

Four of our 23 regular volunteers have committed to driving the van on a weekly schedule, so pickups will not depend on any one person.

**Accountability**

The Foundation granted us $12,000 for shelving in 2023. Our final report on that grant was four months late. We have changed how we will report on this one:

- Dave Lin, our treasurer, is responsible for the final report and will submit it within 30 days of the end of the grant year.
- Every quarter, we will send the Foundation our fresh-food pounds, measured against our current 6,000 pounds a month, so you can see the van's effect as it happens rather than at the end of the year.

**Our request and our share**

The van costs $68,000. We are asking the Foundation for $50,000 and will cover the remaining $18,000 from our reserves. That is more than a quarter of the cost, and it leaves us $11,000 in reserve. Insurance and fuel will cost about $4,500 a year. [HOW YOU WILL PAY THE $4,500 — SEE NOTE.]

**Without the van**

Without cold transport, next summer will repeat the last. Farms will offer fresh food and we will have to say no, while our families make do with about 10 pounds of fresh food a month. A $50,000 grant turns produce that is already being offered into food on their tables.
