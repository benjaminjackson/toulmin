# Bookkeeping for hair and nail salons. Only salons.

I do the books, payroll and sales tax for 31 salons, and nothing else. I don't have restaurants or contractors on my list. When you send me your month, I already know what a salon's month looks like: tips, commission, retail product and the slow weeks after the holidays.

## What you get for $199 a month

- **Monthly books**, closed by the 10th of the following month.
- **Payroll** for up to 10 staff.
- **Sales tax filing**, done for you.

## What clean books are worth at tax time

Last tax season, 9 of my clients told me their accountant charged them less because the books came in clean and ready. One of them saved $800 on that one bill.

Your accountant charges for their time. If they spend it sorting a year of receipts and bank statements, you pay for that every year, whether you have a bookkeeper or not.

## About the price

Until January I charged $149. I raised it to $199 for everyone, including clients who had been with me for years. Two clients left last year, and both left because they sold their salons. None of them left over the price.

## What I promise, and how I've done

I close each month's books by the 10th. Last year I missed that date three times. I'm telling you because it's true, and because you should know how a bookkeeper does against their own deadlines before you hire one.

## Who you'll be working with

You'll be working with me. I'm one person, so the person who does your books is the person who answers your questions. You won't be passed to a new account manager every few months.

I don't answer the phone on Sundays or Mondays. On every other day you're talking to the person who knows your numbers.

The first call is free and takes 20 minutes. Tell me about your salon: how many staff you have, how you pay them and where your books stand now. By the end of the call you'll know whether I'm the right fit, and so will I.

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
