I'm sorry I didn't send updates for the last two quarters. You should have heard from me, and from now on you'll get one every quarter, good or bad.

**The numbers**
- Monthly recurring revenue: $41,000, the same as three months ago.
- Clinics: 9 added, 4 lost.
- Cash: $480,000. Net burn: about $44,000 a month, which gives us roughly 11 months of runway.

**What happened**
The flat revenue hides two separate changes. We lost our largest customer, a 12-clinic chain paying $5,500 a month, about 13% of our revenue. They moved to the scheduler built into their electronic records system.

The rest of the business grew. Without that chain, revenue went from $35,500 to $41,000, up 15% in one quarter.

The loss still matters. A records system with its own scheduler can take a customer from us without us doing anything wrong. That will happen again, and I'd rather plan for it than hope it doesn't.

**What's working**
Clinics that use our automatic text reminders have a no-show rate of 7%, against 13% before they started. That's almost half of their missed appointments gone. Every no-show is a therapist's hour the clinic pays for and can't bill, so clinics see this result directly in their revenue.

**What's next**
Next month we start selling text reminders on their own for $150 a month. This responds directly to the customer we lost. A clinic that keeps the scheduler in its records system can still buy reminders from us. The competitor that cost us our biggest customer becomes a way to sell the feature that gets our best results.

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
