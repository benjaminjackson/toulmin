I am applying for the Training Coordinator position at Calder Pharmaceuticals' Westfield plant. For nine years I have taught high school chemistry, where my job has been to train people to handle hazardous materials correctly, to document that training, and to prove it worked. I would like to do that work at Calder.

In 2019 I wrote my school's lab safety program. About 140 students a year work in our labs under it, and in the seven years since, we have had no injury that needed more than first aid. Every student passes a safety quiz before working in the lab, and I keep a signed record of each one. The state has audited those records twice and found nothing.

I also train every new science teacher on our lab procedures, about two a year. In 2020 I rebuilt that training and cut it from 20 hours to 12, and our safety record has held since. I didn't get there by dropping requirements. I cut repetition and taught the reasons behind each procedure, so new teachers learned the steps faster and remembered them. Each of those teachers has a signed training record too.

The same approach works in my classroom. Over the four years I have taught AP Chemistry, our pass rate rose from 41% to 58%.

I have not yet worked under GMP, and I know training records there are held to a different standard. To close that gap, I have enrolled in an online GMP basics course, which I will finish on May 2. I am used to records that have to survive an audit, and I would treat Calder's procedures as the fixed requirements they are.

If I join Calder, in my first 90 days I would measure how long new hires currently take to become qualified. Then I would agree on a target with you and the plant's managers for getting them there sooner, without cutting anything the procedures require.

Thank you for your time. I would welcome the chance to talk about how I could support training at the Westfield plant.
