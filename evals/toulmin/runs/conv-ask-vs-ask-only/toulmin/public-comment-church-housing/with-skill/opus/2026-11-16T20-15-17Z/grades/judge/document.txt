Good evening, Chair and commissioners.

I've lived on Alder Road for nineteen years, two blocks from St. Luke's Lutheran. I'm also a member of St. Luke's, so I'll be upfront: I want this project to happen. But I'm also the neighbor who will drive past it every morning. So I read the traffic study as someone who will have to live with it.

I'm asking you to approve the permit for these 64 apartments, with the traffic signal at Alder and 4th as a condition of approval.

First, the need. The county's own housing report says we're about 2,100 affordable homes short. Right now, 412 households are on the housing authority's waiting list, and the average wait is three years. Every one of these 64 apartments would go to a household earning under 60 percent of the area median income. That's enough for about one in six of the households on that list.

Second, the land. It's a 1.8-acre parking lot that sits empty six days a week. No homes are torn down, and no open space is lost. These homes would go on pavement we already have.

Now traffic, because I know that's the concern. I'll be honest with you: some mornings around eight, traffic already backs up past my house. I can't stand here and promise you this project changes nothing. The study says it adds about 310 trips a day. Without changes, the intersection at Alder and 4th would drop from a C to a D at rush hour.

But Cascade Housing Partners, the nonprofit builder, has offered to pay for a traffic signal at that intersection. The same study says that with the signal, the intersection stays at a C. That's why I'm asking you to make the signal a condition of approval. That way it's a requirement, not just an offer.

On parking: the plan has 72 spaces for 64 apartments, so every apartment gets a space and there are eight extra. St. Luke's has also put in writing that neighbors can use the church's parking on weekdays when there's room.

So here's the choice. If you say no, the lot stays empty six days a week, and 412 households keep waiting. If you say yes, with the signal as a condition, 64 of those households get a home, and the intersection keeps the same rating it has today.

I'm the neighbor who will live with this, and I'm asking you to approve it. Thank you.
