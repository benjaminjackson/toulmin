Last summer, local farms offered us roughly 9,000 pounds of fresh produce over three months. We had to turn all of it down, because we had no way to pick it up cold or keep it cold.

We are a volunteer-run food bank in a town of about 9,000 people. Each month we hand out about 38,000 pounds of food to 610 households. About 6,000 pounds of that is fresh produce, milk and eggs. Last summer showed us that the limit on fresh food is not always supply. Often the limit is whether we can move the food cold.

The Foundation has supported us before, with $12,000 for shelving in 2023. We owe you a plain account of that grant: our final report was four months late. Our treasurer at the time was in the hospital for most of the reporting period. Our current treasurer, Dave Lin, will be personally responsible for reporting on this grant. Below we set out what we will send and when.

We are asking the Foundation for $50,000 toward a $68,000 refrigerated van. We will cover the remaining $18,000, more than a quarter of the cost, from our own reserves.

The van lets us accept the food we have been turning away. Two of the farms that made offers last summer have written letters saying they will give again if we can pick up cold. Last summer's offers averaged about 3,000 pounds a month during the harvest season. On top of our current 6,000 pounds, that would mean half again as much fresh food for our households in those months. Harvests vary and no farm can promise a fixed amount, so we are not promising one either. Our first-year target is to accept what we had to turn away last summer: about 9,000 pounds over the harvest months.

Four of our 23 regular volunteers have committed to driving the van on a weekly schedule, so pickups do not depend on any one person.

So that the Foundation can see whether the van is doing what we say, we will send you our fresh food pounds every quarter, compared against the 6,000 pounds a month we distribute now. Dave Lin will submit the final report within 30 days of the end of the grant year.

Without the van, next summer will go the way last summer did. Local farms will offer fresh food for the 610 households we serve, and we will have to say no.
