Dear Admissions Committee,

I am the farm manager at Hollis Dairy, a 400-cow operation, and I am writing to recommend Tomás Reyes for your two-year agricultural technology program. Tomás has worked for us for three years, since he was 19. He is one of the most dependable people I have employed.

Five days a week, Tomás runs our 3 a.m. milking shift on his own. There is no supervisor on the farm at that hour. I trust him with the whole herd and the parlor, and he has never given me a reason not to. In three years he has missed two shifts. When he has needed time off, he has arranged cover for his shifts himself before he came to me.

What makes him a strong fit for your program is the work he has done with our herd management software. Tomás learned the system and now uses it to flag sick cows for our veterinarian. Last winter he caught nine cases of mastitis early, and our vet estimates that saved the farm about $8,000. He watches the animals, reads the data, and connects the two. That is the kind of work your program trains people to do, and he is already doing it.

I'll be direct about one area where he is still growing. Tomás's spoken English is good, but his written English is weaker. He knows it, and he is doing something about it: he is taking a night English class on his own time, on top of a shift that starts at 3 a.m. That tells you how seriously he will take his coursework.

To support him while he studies, I will keep his job at Hollis Dairy open part-time on weekends for the length of the program.

I recommend Tomás without reservation. If you have any questions, please call me at 555-0187.

Sincerely,

Mike Hollis
Farm Manager, Hollis Dairy

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
