**An Open Letter to Our Property Management Company**

From the Tenants' Association

Our building has nine stories, 112 apartments and one elevator.

This year that elevator has been out of service on 41 days, roughly one day in every eight. In August it was out for 12 days in a row.

For most of us a breakdown means climbing stairs. For some of our neighbours it means far more. Twenty-three residents are over 75, and six use wheelchairs or walkers. For a resident in a wheelchair on an upper floor, a broken elevator can mean no way to reach a doctor's appointment or bring home groceries, and no easy way out of the building in an emergency.

In June you told us a new controller part was on order and would arrive in six to eight weeks. It did not arrive. Now you are telling us the same thing: six to eight weeks. We don't think it's unreasonable to want more than the same estimate a second time.

We know the city inspected the elevator in September and issued a passing certificate. We don't dispute it. But a certificate tells us the elevator is safe when it runs. It tells us nothing about how often it runs, and that is the problem we live with.

Seventy-four households, two out of every three in this building, signed our petition asking for a second elevator. You have told us it would cost about $400,000 and can't be done in this building. We take the cost seriously. But "can't be done" is a conclusion, and we have never seen what it is based on.

We are asking for three things:

1. **The facts about the repair, in writing.** Who the part was ordered from, when it was ordered, the date it is due, and what you will do if that date passes.

2. **A plan for outages until the repair is done.** How residents who cannot use the stairs will be helped while the elevator is down, and how every household will be told when it breaks down and when it is expected back.

3. **The basis for "can't be done."** If you have an engineering or feasibility assessment for a second elevator, share it with us. If you don't, we ask you to commission an independent one so that residents can see the options and their costs for themselves.

We want this building to work for everyone who lives in it, including the neighbours who depend on the elevator most. We are posting this letter in our lobby and sharing it with local news because this has gone on for most of a year. We look forward to your written reply on each of these points.

The Tenants' Association
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
