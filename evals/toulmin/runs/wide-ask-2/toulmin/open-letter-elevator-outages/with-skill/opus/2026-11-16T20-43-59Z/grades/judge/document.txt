**An Open Letter to Our Property Management Company**
**From the Tenants' Association**

Our building has nine stories, 112 apartments and one elevator. This year that elevator has been out of service on 41 days, about one day in every eight. In August it was out for 12 days in a row.

When the elevator is down, the stairs are the only way in or out. Twenty-three of our neighbors are over 75. Six use wheelchairs or walkers. For them a broken elevator is not an inconvenience. A resident in a wheelchair on an upper floor has no way down until it is fixed.

In June you told us a new controller part was on order and would arrive in six to eight weeks. That would have been August, the month of the 12-day outage. You have now told us the same thing again: six to eight weeks. We would like to believe it, but we heard the same promise five months ago.

We know the city inspected the elevator in September and issued a passing certificate. We do not question that the elevator is safe when it runs. Our concern is how often it doesn't. A certificate does not keep an elevator in service.

Seventy-four households, about two-thirds of the building, signed our petition asking for a second elevator. You have told us it would cost about $400,000 and cannot be done in this building. We are prepared to hear that answer, but we need to see the reasons behind it, not just be told.

We ask for four things:

1. A confirmed delivery date for the controller part, in writing, and an explanation of why the part that was on order in June has not arrived.
2. A written plan for what will happen during the next outage for residents who cannot use the stairs.
3. A copy of the assessment behind the $400,000 estimate and the finding that a second elevator cannot be built.
4. A meeting with the association to go through all three.

If nothing changes, we have no reason to think next year will be different: more weeks without service and the same neighbors unable to leave their floors.

We would rather solve this with you than around you. We are posting this letter in the lobby so every resident knows where things stand, and sharing it with local news because the people in this building deserve a clear answer in public. We look forward to your reply.

Sincerely,

The Tenants' Association
Secretary, on behalf of the Association
