**To:** General Manager
**From:** Assistant Front Office Manager
**Re:** Application for Front Office Manager

I'm applying for the Front Office Manager position. For the past seven weeks I've been doing the job on my own, so you've already seen how I'd run the department. This memo covers what I've done in my six years here, what I still need to learn, and how I plan to learn it.

**I've kept front desk staff from leaving.** In the year before I became assistant manager, 9 of our 14 front desk employees left, which is 64% turnover. Last year 5 of 14 left, or 36%. That means four more experienced people on the desk each year, and four fewer jobs to recruit and train for.

**I've made training faster.** I rewrote the front desk training program. New hires used to need 9 shifts before they could work the desk alone. Now they need 5. Fewer training shifts means less payroll spent before a new hire can handle the desk alone, and less time when experienced staff are pulled away from guests to train.

**Guests have noticed.** Over the last 18 months our check-in score on the main review site went from 7.9 to 8.6. The lobby and rooms were renovated during that time, and that renovation surely helped. But check-in is the part of the stay the desk handles directly. A more experienced team that stays with us and is trained faster helped raise that score, and it will keep the score up after the renovation stops being new.

**I've already done the job.** For seven weeks I've run the front office alone, handling staffing, schedules, guest problems and the daily operation. I've been here six years, two of them as assistant manager, so I know this hotel, this team and our guests.

**What I haven't done yet.** I have never managed a budget, and this role owns a $1.1 million payroll budget. I'm not going to downplay that. I'd like to work through the next payroll budget with you before I take full ownership of it, so you can see how I handle it while there's still time to make corrections. Much of my work so far, keeping staff and training them faster, has been about using payroll better. Managing the budget is the next step in that work.

If you hire from outside, the new manager will start with a team they don't know, a training program they didn't write and a hotel they're learning from scratch. I can keep moving forward without that break. I'd welcome the chance to talk this through before Friday.

**From:** Assistant Front Office Manager
