**To:** General Manager
**From:** Assistant Front Office Manager
**Re:** My application for Front Office Manager

I'm asking to be appointed Front Office Manager. For the past seven weeks I've done the job alone. I'd like to keep doing it with the title and the full responsibility, including the payroll budget.

**What I've changed at the front desk**

**Turnover is down by nearly half.** In the year before I became assistant manager, 9 of our 14 front desk staff left. Last year 5 of 14 left. That's a drop from 64% to 36%, and it meant four fewer people to recruit, train and cover for.

**New hires are ready faster.** I rewrote the front desk training. New hires now work the desk alone after 5 shifts instead of 9. That's nearly half the training time for every person we bring on.

**Check-in scores are up.** Our check-in score on our main review site rose from 7.9 to 8.6 over the last 18 months. The lobby and room renovation happened at the same time and deserves real credit for that. What a renovation can't provide is the person behind the desk. Over those months, the staff at the desk have stayed longer and learned the job faster than before.

**I'm already doing the job.** I've worked here six years, the last two as assistant manager. Since our front office manager left seven weeks ago, I've run the department on my own. You've now seen seven weeks of how I manage it, which tells you more than an interview can.

**What I haven't done yet**

I have never managed a budget, and this role owns a $1.1 million payroll budget. The two biggest things I've changed, turnover and training time, are both payroll costs. So I've been working on what drives that budget even though I haven't owned it.

To close the gap, I propose this: in my first 90 days, I'll go through the payroll budget with you line by line. After that, I'll review actual spending against the budget with you every month until you're confident I have it in hand.

**The alternative**

If the role goes to an outside hire, the department waits through a search. Then the new manager starts by learning a team, a hotel and a training program that I already know, because I built the training.

I'd welcome the chance to talk this through with you before you decide.
