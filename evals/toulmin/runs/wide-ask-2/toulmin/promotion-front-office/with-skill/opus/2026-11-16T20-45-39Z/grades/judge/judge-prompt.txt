**To:** General Manager
**From:** Assistant Front Office Manager
**Re:** My application for Front Office Manager

I'm applying for the front office manager position. I've worked at this hotel for six years, the last two as assistant front office manager. For the past seven weeks I've run the front office on my own, and I'd like to keep running it with the title and accountability that go with the job.

**What I've already changed**

- **Turnover.** In the year before I became assistant manager, 9 of our 14 front desk staff left. Last year, 5 of 14 left. That cut turnover from 64% to 36%. We now have four fewer people to recruit and train each year, and the guest is more likely to find an experienced person at the desk.
- **Training.** I rewrote the front desk training. New hires now work the desk alone after 5 shifts instead of 9. With lower turnover on top of that, we run about 25 training shifts a year instead of 81. That's 56 fewer shifts paid twice for one position.
- **Guest scores.** Over the last 18 months our check-in score on our main review site rose from 7.9 to 8.6. The lobby and room renovation in the same period helped, and I'm not claiming the whole rise. But a check-in score depends on the people at the desk as much as on the lobby, and over that period the desk got a steadier team trained to a written standard. Holding 8.6 once the renovation is no longer new depends on the front office, and that's what I'd be accountable for.

**The last seven weeks**

Since the position opened, I've covered it alone while still doing my own job. You've seen how the front office has run in that time, and I'd ask you to judge me on it.

**The budget**

I have never managed a budget, and this role owns a $1.1 million payroll budget. That's the main gap in my experience, so I'm raising it myself. For two years I've been managing the things that drive that payroll: how many people we hire, how long they train before working alone and how many of them stay. The fall in turnover and training shifts is already showing in those costs. What I haven't done is forecast the budget and answer for it. I'd propose that we review the payroll budget together each month for my first quarter, so you can see how I handle it before it's fully mine.

**What I'm asking**

I'm asking to be promoted to front office manager. If you promote me, the person who built the current training and team stays on to run them. I'd welcome the chance to talk this through with you before you decide.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.
