Dear Admissions Committee,

I am writing to recommend Tomás for the two-year agricultural technology program. I manage a 400-cow dairy farm, and Tomás has worked for me for three years, since he was 19. He is one of the most dependable people on my crew. He is also the one who has done the most to use our technology well.

Five days a week, Tomás runs our 3 a.m. milking shift on his own. Nobody supervises that shift. The herd gets milked on time and done right because he does it. I give that job only to someone I trust completely, and he has held it for a long time.

What sets him apart is what he has done beyond the milking. He learned our herd management software and now uses it to spot cows that are getting sick and flag them for our veterinarian. Last winter he caught nine cases of mastitis early. Early detection is what keeps a mastitis case treatable and keeps it from spreading or costing us milk, so that work matters on a dairy. Nobody required him to take this on. He saw what the data could tell him and learned to read it. That is the kind of student your program is built for.

On attendance: in three years Tomás has missed two shifts. The one exception was last summer, when his father became seriously ill in Mexico. Tomás told me two days before he left and was gone for five weeks. When he came back, he went straight back to the 3 a.m. shift. I see that as a family emergency handled by a man who came back and picked up his responsibilities, not as a pattern.

You should also know that his written English is weaker than his spoken English. He knows this, and he is taking a night class to improve it, on top of a full work schedule that starts at 3 a.m. I have no doubt he will put the same effort into his coursework.

Tomás already does part of the job your graduates are trained for, and he learned it on his own. With formal training I expect him to go much further. I recommend him strongly and without reservation, and I would be glad to answer any questions.

Sincerely,

Farm Manager
