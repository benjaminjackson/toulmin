Every month our food bank hands out about 38,000 pounds of food to 610 households in a town of about 9,000 people. Only about 6,000 pounds of that is fresh produce, milk and eggs. Fresh food is the hardest thing for us to get, because it has to stay cold from the moment we pick it up.

Last summer, local farms offered us roughly 9,000 pounds of produce over three months. We had to turn all of it down. We had no way to collect it cold and no way to store it. The food was offered; what we lacked was a cold way to move it.

The foundation has supported us before. In 2023 you gave us $12,000 for shelving. Our final report on that grant was four months late. That was our failure, and we will file every report for this grant on the schedule the foundation sets.

We are asking for $50,000 toward a $68,000 refrigerated van. We will cover the remaining $18,000, more than a quarter of the cost, from our reserves. That will leave us with $11,000 in reserve. We are prepared to draw our reserves down that far because this van closes the gap that cost us the most food last year.

**What the van will do**

With a refrigerated van, we can say yes when farms offer produce. If this summer's offers match last summer's, the van will bring in about 9,000 more pounds of fresh produce during the harvest months, roughly 3,000 pounds a month. That would raise our fresh food distribution during those months by half, from about 6,000 to about 9,000 pounds a month. Across the 610 households we serve, that is roughly 15 more pounds of fresh produce per household over the summer.

**Who will run it**

We are entirely volunteer-run, with 23 regular volunteers. Four of them have already committed to driving the van on a weekly schedule. Pickups will not depend on any one person, and the van will not sit idle for lack of drivers.

**How we will report**

At the end of the first harvest season, we will report to the foundation how many pounds of fresh food the van collected and from how many farms, measured against the 9,000 pounds we had to turn down last summer.

**If we do not get the van**

Next summer will look like last summer: local farms offering fresh food, and our volunteers telling them no.
