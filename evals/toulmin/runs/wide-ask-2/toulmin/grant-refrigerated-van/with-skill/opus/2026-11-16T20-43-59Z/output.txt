**Project Narrative: Refrigerated Van for Fresh Food**

Our volunteer-run food bank distributes about 38,000 pounds of food every month to 610 households in a town of roughly 9,000 people. Only about 6,000 pounds of that is fresh produce, milk and eggs. The reason is not a lack of fresh food. It is that we have no refrigerated transport. We are requesting $50,000 toward a $68,000 refrigerated van.

**The fresh food is already available**

Last summer, local farms offered us roughly 9,000 pounds of produce over three months. We turned all of it down because we had no way to pick it up cold or store it. Had we been able to accept it, our fresh food distribution in those three months would have risen by half, from about 18,000 pounds to 27,000.

**What the van will do in its first year**

With the van, we can say yes to offers like last summer's. That alone adds about 9,000 pounds of fresh food a year, a 12.5 percent increase in our annual fresh distribution. In the summer months, when farms have the most to give, the increase is 50 percent. We will treat 9,000 pounds as our floor and report our results against it.

**How it will run**

We have 23 regular volunteers. Four of them have committed to driving the van on a weekly schedule, so pickups will not depend on any one person.

**How we are sharing the cost**

We will cover the remaining $18,000 from our own reserves, more than a quarter of the van's price. That leaves us with $11,000 in reserve. We are willing to spend that much of our cushion because no other purchase would change what we can offer our households as much as this one.

**Our record with the Foundation**

The Foundation granted us $12,000 for shelving in 2023. Our final report on that grant was four months late. We know that matters when you consider a request more than four times larger. For this grant, we propose reporting the pounds of fresh food collected by the van every quarter, so the Foundation can see results as they come in rather than only at the end.

**If we cannot buy the van**

Without it, we will be in the same position next summer. Farms will offer us fresh produce and we will have to say no. The 610 households we serve will keep receiving mostly shelf-stable food while produce that was offered to them is turned away.

With the Foundation's support, the next offer of fresh food from a local farm will reach those households.
