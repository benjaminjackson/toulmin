Subject: Quarterly update: flat revenue, a lost anchor customer, and our next move

Hi all,

First, an apology. This is the first update you've had from me in six months. I should have written in each of the last two quarters, and most of all in a quarter like this one. From now on you'll get one every quarter, good news or bad.

**The numbers**
- Monthly recurring revenue: $41,000, the same as three months ago
- Clinics: 9 added, 4 lost
- Cash: $480,000. Net burn is about $44,000 a month, which gives us roughly 11 months of runway at the current rate.
- Team: 6

**What happened**
The flat revenue number hides two things moving in opposite directions. We signed 9 new clinics, more than twice as many as we lost. But one of the 4 we lost was our largest customer, a 12-clinic chain paying $5,500 a month, about 13% of our revenue. They moved to the scheduler built into their electronic records system. New sales made up for that loss and the other three, but no more.

That loss matters for more than its size. Records vendors include a scheduler with their systems, and a large group that already runs everything in one system has a strong reason to consolidate. I expect other multi-location customers to face the same choice. If we compete with records vendors on scheduling alone, we will lose on price.

**What's working**
Our automatic text reminders. Clinics that use them have a no-show rate of 7%, against 13% before they started. That's close to half of missed appointments gone. A missed visit is revenue a clinic can't get back, so reminders are the clearest value we deliver.

**What we're doing next**
Next month we start selling reminders on their own for $150 a month. A clinic that schedules inside its records system can still buy our reminders. That means clinics we can't win as scheduling customers become customers for the reminder product. It also gives clinics a much smaller first purchase than switching their whole scheduler.

For scale: every 20 reminder-only clinics adds $3,000 a month, and about 37 would replace what the chain paid.

Next quarter I'll report reminder-only clinics signed, monthly recurring revenue, and how many multi-location customers we've kept.

**Where you can help**
Introductions to physical therapy clinic owners, especially ones who schedule inside their records system. They are the first buyers for the reminder product.

I'm happy to talk any of this through. Just reply or give me a call.

Thank you for your patience,
Ben
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
