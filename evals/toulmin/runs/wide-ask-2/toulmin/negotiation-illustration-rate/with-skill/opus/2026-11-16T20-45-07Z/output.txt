I've written the email around the facts you gave me, but I've left out the line saying every other publisher pays at least $12,000. Your facts don't support it. You have one publisher at $12,500. Your agent's range of $11,000 to $13,000 includes fees below $12,000, so the agent's own figures contradict that line.

Your editor will probably notice. Editors know what the market pays, and an overstatement gives them an easy way to push back on the whole request. The draft says instead that another publisher paid you $12,500 last year and that your agent puts the normal range at $11,000 to $13,000. That still makes $11,500 look modest, and it's all true. If you have more quotes from other publishers, you could make a stronger claim, but say only what you can show.

Other choices I made:
- **Fair, not a lesson.** It says once that your fee hasn't changed since your first book, then moves on. It doesn't do the maths of how much they've saved.
- **Below your outside rate on purpose.** It points out that $11,500 is less than you were paid elsewhere, and says you're choosing that because you value working with them. This makes it easy for them to say yes.
- **No threat to walk away.** You didn't say you would, so the email doesn't suggest it.
- **To add before sending:** your editor's name in the greeting and your name in the sign-off. The email is about 200 words.
