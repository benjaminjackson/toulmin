The 14 tellers and member service staff at the two branches that are closing. <brief>
Help me write an announcement to our staff. I'm the COO of a credit union with 7 branches. We're closing the Maple Street and Route 9 branches on March 31 and moving the people who work there to a new member phone and chat center.

Facts:
- The two branches have 14 staff between them. Every one of them is offered a job in the phone and chat center at the same pay or higher.
- Visits to those two branches fell 44% over the last three years. Phone and chat contacts across the credit union went up 60%.
- Members calling us now wait an average of 9 minutes on hold.
- The center will be open 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. on weekdays. The branches were open 9 to 5. Shifts will rotate.
- Staff in the center can work from home two days a week after 90 days of training.
- Anyone who doesn't take the new role gets 2 weeks of pay for every year they've worked here.

I want the announcement to say that this change will make everyone's job better. About 400 words, sent by email from me.
</brief>



<document>
**Subject: Maple Street and Route 9 branches closing March 31, and our new Member Phone and Chat Center**

Team,

I want you to hear this from me directly. On March 31, we will close our Maple Street and Route 9 branches. Everyone who works at those two branches will be offered a role in our new Member Phone and Chat Center.

**Why we're doing this**

The way our members reach us has changed. Over the last three years, visits to Maple Street and Route 9 fell 44%. Over the same period, phone and chat contacts across the credit union rose 60%. Today, members who call us wait an average of 9 minutes on hold. That isn't the service we want to give, and it puts a lot of pressure on the people answering those calls. The new center puts our staff where our members are.

**What this means for Maple Street and Route 9 staff**

- All 14 of you will be offered a position in the center at the same pay or higher.
- The center will be open 8 a.m. to 8 p.m., Monday through Friday, and shifts will rotate. That's a longer day than the branches' 9-to-5 hours, so some of your shifts will start earlier or end later than you're used to.
- After 90 days of training, you'll be able to work from home two days a week.
- If you decide the new role isn't right for you, you'll receive severance of two weeks' pay for every year you've worked here.

Your manager and someone from HR will meet with each of you individually by [date] to go over your offer, the shift schedule, and training. You'll have until [date] to decide.

**What this means for everyone else**

With more people answering phones and chat, members should wait less, and fewer frustrated calls should reach our other five branches. Your jobs and hours aren't changing.

**A word from me**

I know this is a big change, and people will react to it differently. Some of you will welcome the chance to work from home. Others will miss your branch and the members you've gotten to know over the years, or will find the new hours hard. Those feelings are fair, and I want to hear them.

If you have questions, talk to your manager, contact [HR name, email/phone], or come to an open Q&A on [date/time/location].

Thank you to the Maple Street and Route 9 teams for the years of care you've given our members. You know our members and our products better than anyone, and that's exactly what the center needs.

[Your name]
Chief Operating Officer
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
