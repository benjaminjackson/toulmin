**Subject: Maple Street and Route 9 branches closing March 31 – new member phone and chat center**

Team,

I'm writing to share a decision that affects all of us, and most directly our 14 colleagues at Maple Street and Route 9. On March 31 we will close those two branches. Every person who works there is being offered a job in our new member phone and chat center, at the same pay or higher.

Here's why. Over the last three years, visits to Maple Street and Route 9 fell 44%. In the same period, phone and chat contacts across the credit union rose 60%. Our members have changed how they reach us, and we haven't kept pace. A member who calls us today waits an average of 9 minutes on hold. That isn't the service we want to give. And anyone who has picked up a call from a member after that wait knows what it costs us.

The new center puts experienced people where our members now are. The staff moving into it already know our members, our products and how we do things. That's why we want them in these roles rather than hiring from outside.

I want to be straight about what changes. The branches were open 9 to 5. The center will be open 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. on weekdays, and shifts will rotate, so some of your hours will be earlier or later than they are now. After 90 days of training, center staff can work from home two days a week.

I believe this is a good move for the people taking these roles and a better place for all of us to work. Shorter waits mean calmer members, on the phone and at the counter. But I know a new schedule won't fit everyone's life. If you decide the new role isn't right for you, you'll receive two weeks of pay for every year you've worked here. That's a choice we'll respect.

To the Maple Street and Route 9 teams: you've looked after our members in those branches, and I'm asking you to keep doing that in a new way. Thank you.

If you have questions or concerns, please reply to me directly. I'll answer every one.

Thank you,

COO
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
