Good evening, commissioners. I've lived on Alder Road for nineteen years, two blocks from the church. I'm also a member of that church, so I'll say that up front. I'm here to ask you to approve the sixty-four apartments on its back parking lot.

I want to start with the people this is for. Right now, 412 households are on the county housing authority's waiting list. The average wait is three years. Every one of these sixty-four apartments would rent to a household earning under sixty percent of the area median income. That's enough homes for about one in six of the families on that list.

The county's own housing report says we're short about 2,100 affordable homes. This project won't close that gap by itself. No single project will. The gap closes sixty-four homes at a time, on sites like this one, or it doesn't close at all.

And this site is about as easy as they come. It's 1.8 acres of pavement that sits empty six days a week. Nobody has to move out for this to be built. No open space is lost. It puts land we've already paved to work.

Now, traffic, because I'm the one who'll be sitting in it. The study estimates 310 more car trips a day on Alder Road, and it says the intersection at Alder and 4th would drop from a C to a D at rush hour. I'm not going to stand here and tell you that's nothing. I drive through that intersection all the time. But a D at rush hour means a longer wait at that corner during the busiest part of the day. It doesn't mean the road stops working. If you believe Alder and 4th needs improvement, I'd ask you to make that a condition of approval, not a reason to say no.

On parking, the plan has seventy-two spaces for sixty-four apartments. That's a space for every home, with eight left over.

So here's the choice as I see it. If you approve this, sixty-four households get a home they can afford, and my street gets busier at rush hour. If you say no, the lot sits empty six days a week, the county is still short 2,100 homes, and those 412 households keep waiting.

I've lived on Alder Road for nineteen years. I'll take a slower rush hour on my street so that sixty-four families can stop waiting. I'm asking you to approve this project. Thank you.
