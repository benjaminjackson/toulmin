You are the reader described between the <reader> tags. Read the document between the <document> tags as that reader would receive it.

The brief between the <brief> tags is what the writer was asked to do and shows the facts the writer had. You, as the reader, have not seen the brief. Use it only to check what the document does with those facts.

One page, from me as farm manager.

Dear Admissions Committee,

I manage a 400-cow dairy farm, and I am writing to recommend Tomás [Last name] for your two-year Agricultural Technology program. Tomás has worked for us for three years, since he was 19. In that time he has gone from learning the job to doing work I rely on him to do without supervision. I believe he is ready for the training your program offers.

What makes me most confident he will do well is how he took to the technical side of our operation. He learned our herd management software and now uses it to flag sick cows for our veterinarian. Last winter he caught nine cases of mastitis early, when they are easiest to treat. He is already doing the kind of work your program prepares people for: reading the herd data, connecting it to what he sees in the barn and acting on it. Formal training will let him do much more of it.

He is also someone you can count on. Tomás runs our 3 a.m. milking shift on his own, five days a week. Nobody is there to check on him at that hour. The cows are milked properly and on time because he does it. He has missed two shifts in three years, apart from five weeks last summer when his father fell ill in Mexico and he went home to be with him.

His spoken English is strong. His written English is still developing, and he is taking a night class to improve it. I mention this because you may notice it in his application. I don't expect it to hold him back. He learned a software system he had never used and now catches problems with it that others miss. He works at what he doesn't yet know until he knows it.

I recommend Tomás strongly. I am glad to answer any questions; you can reach me at the phone number or email above.

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
