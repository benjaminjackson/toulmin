You are the reader described between the <reader> tags. Read the document between the <document> tags as that reader would receive it.

The brief between the <brief> tags is what the writer was asked to do and shows the facts the writer had. You, as the reader, have not seen the brief. Use it only to check what the document does with those facts.

<reader>
A member of the admissions committee for the agricultural technology program at a state technical college, which has 40 places and about 150 applicants. The member reads each letter quickly and has no view of this applicant yet.
</reader>

<brief>
I need to write a reference letter. I manage a 400-cow dairy farm, and one of our workers, Tomás, is applying to the two-year agricultural technology program at the state technical college.

What I know about him:
- He's worked for us for 3 years, since he was 19.
- He runs the 3 a.m. milking shift on his own, 5 days a week.
- He learned our herd management software and now flags sick cows for the vet. Last winter he caught 9 cases of mastitis early.
- He left for 5 weeks in the middle of last summer when his father got sick in Mexico. He told me 2 days before he left.
- His written English is weaker than his spoken English. He's taking a night class for it.
- He has missed 2 shifts in 3 years, not counting those 5 weeks.

One page, from me as farm manager.
</brief>

Dear Admissions Committee,

I manage [Farm Name], a 400-cow dairy farm. Tomás has worked for us for three years, since he was 19, and I have supervised him the whole time. I recommend him for your agricultural technology program. On a working farm, he is already doing the kind of work your program teaches.

Tomás runs our 3 a.m. milking shift on his own, five days a week. Nobody else is in the barn at that hour, so the milking gets done because he shows up. Apart from one family emergency, which I describe below, he has missed two shifts in three years.

What sets him apart is how he has taken to the technology side of the job. He learned our herd management software and now uses it to flag sick cows for our veterinarian. Last winter he caught nine cases of mastitis early. Mastitis caught early is easier to treat and costs less in lost milk, so that work has real value to our farm. I believe your program can build on this. He knows the animals, and he has shown he can use data to decide when a cow needs attention.

There are two things you should know. First, last summer his father became seriously ill in Mexico, and Tomás was away for five weeks to be with him. He came back afterward, and he still runs that 3 a.m. shift today. Second, his written English is not yet as strong as his spoken English. He knows this and is taking a night class to improve it.

For three years Tomás has kept a 3 a.m. shift and learned new tools on the job. I am confident he will do well in a two-year program. I would be glad to answer any questions you have about him.

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
