Dear [Hiring Manager's Name],

I am applying for the Training Coordinator position at [Company]. For nine years I have taught high school chemistry. The part of that work I do best is also the core of your role: teaching people to follow a procedure correctly and safely, then making sure they can do it without me.

In 2019 I wrote my school's lab safety program. In the seven years since, we have not had a single lab injury that needed more than first aid. That covers about 140 students a year working with acids, open flames and glassware, many of them for the first time. Building that program taught me that a safety rule only holds when people understand why it exists and have practiced it before it matters.

I also train adults. Every year I run lab-procedure training for our new science teachers, about 12 hours for each one, and we average two new teachers a year. I expect many of your new hires start in a similar place: capable people who need to learn exactly how the work is done here.

I check whether my teaching is working, and I change it when it isn't. In the four years I have taught AP Chemistry, our pass rate has risen from 41% to 58%.

Learning a new set of rules and a new system, and then teaching them to others, is the work I have done for nine years, and I expect to come up to speed quickly.

What draws me to this role is the chance to help new hires get to the point where they can work safely and correctly on their own, as soon as your procedures allow. I live about 30 minutes from the plant and would welcome the chance to talk about how I could help your training program.

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
