GMP training is tied to written procedures and documented qualification, so part of it can't be shortened. Coming from someone who hasn't worked in a plant, a specific promise like that is likely to look naive, and it could cost you the credibility the rest of the letter builds. The letter says instead that getting new hires working safely and correctly on their own sooner is what you want to work on. That's true, it's what they care about, and nobody can argue with it.

**When you could make the claim.** You'd need a real basis. For example, if an interview tells you their current onboarding time and where it runs long, you could make a specific case then. If you still want it in the letter, the safest wording is something like "I'd like to look for ways to shorten time-to-qualification without cutting corners," not a number.
