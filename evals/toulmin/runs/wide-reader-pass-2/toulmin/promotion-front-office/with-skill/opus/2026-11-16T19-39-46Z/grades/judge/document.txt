**To:** General Manager
**Re:** My application for Front Office Manager

I'm applying for the Front Office Manager position. I've been doing the job alone for the last seven weeks, since it became vacant. I've worked here for six years, and for the last two I've been the assistant front office manager. Below is what I've done in that time and what I would bring to the role.

**The front desk has improved.**

- **Turnover is down.** In the year before I became assistant manager, 9 of our 14 front desk staff left (64%). Last year, 5 of 14 left (36%).
- **New hires are ready sooner.** I rewrote the front desk training. New hires now work the desk alone after 5 shifts instead of 9. With fewer people leaving and each one training faster, we spend about 56 fewer shifts a year on training: roughly 25 now, compared with 81 before.
- **The check-in score is up.** On our main review site, guests' rating for check-in rose from 7.9 to 8.6 over the last 18 months. The lobby and room renovation clearly helped, and I'm not claiming all the credit. But guests also rate check-in on the people at the desk: how quickly they're served, how well the staff know their job, and whether those staff are experienced or new. Those are the things I've been working on. A team that stays longer and is ready sooner gives better check-ins.

**I've been doing the job for seven weeks.** Since the Front Office Manager left, I've run the front office alone. You've seen how it has gone in that time. If you promote me, there's no handover. You get someone who already knows our team, our guests and this building. An outside hire would need time to learn all three.

**What I haven't done yet.** I have never managed a budget, and this role owns a $1.1 million payroll budget. That's a real gap and I won't play it down. On a front desk, though, payroll mostly comes down to how many people leave and how long it takes to train their replacements. Those are the two numbers I've already improved. I understand what drives the budget, and I'm ready to learn how to manage it formally.

**What I'm asking for.** I'd like to be named Front Office Manager. I've shown I can build and keep a strong front desk team, and I've been doing the full job for seven weeks. I'd welcome the chance to talk this through with you.
