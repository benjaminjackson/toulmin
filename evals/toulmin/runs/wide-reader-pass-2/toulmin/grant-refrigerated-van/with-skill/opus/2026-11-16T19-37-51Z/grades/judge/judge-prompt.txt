<document>
**Project Narrative: Refrigerated Van for Fresh Food Recovery**

Every month our food bank hands out about 38,000 pounds of food to 610 households in a town of about 9,000 people. Only about 6,000 pounds of it is fresh: produce, milk and eggs.

The fresh food is out there. The problem is getting it here cold. Last summer, local farms offered us roughly 9,000 pounds of produce over three months. We turned all of it down. We had no way to pick it up cold and no way to keep it cold, so we had to say no.

**What the van changes**

A refrigerated van lets us say yes. Our volunteers can collect from farms on a weekly schedule and keep the food cold until it is handed out. If we had had a van last summer, the produce we turned down would have raised our fresh food by half during those three months, from about 6,000 to about 9,000 pounds a month. For each of the 610 households we serve, that is about five more pounds of fresh food every month.

Those 9,000 pounds are only the produce we know about, because farms offered it to us. Once we can collect it, we can accept every offer instead of turning offers down.

**Who will run it**

We have 23 regular volunteers. Four of them have already said they will drive the van on a weekly schedule, so we have drivers before the van arrives.

**Our history with the Foundation**

In 2023 the Foundation gave us $12,000 for shelving. Our final report on that grant was four months late. We know that, and we owe the Foundation better reporting on this grant.

**The request**

The van costs $68,000. We ask the Foundation for $50,000. We will pay the remaining $18,000 from our own reserves. That is more than a quarter of the cost and nearly two-thirds of our reserves, and it leaves us with $11,000. We are making that commitment because we think fresh food is the best use of that money.

**If the van is not funded**

Nothing about our situation changes. Next summer, when farms offer us produce, we will again have no way to collect it cold or keep it cold, and we will turn it down again. Our households will keep getting about 6,000 pounds of fresh food a month when they could have had about 9,000.

With the van, food that local farms already want to give will reach local families.
</document>
