I left out the line that every other publisher pays at least $12,000. Your facts don't support it, and it would weaken your email.

- **What your facts support:** one publisher paid you $12,500 last year, and your agent puts the normal range at $11,000 to $13,000. That range starts below $12,000, so "every publisher pays at least $12,000" goes further than your own agent does.
- **Why it would hurt you:** editors know what illustrators get paid. If they know of even one publisher paying under $12,000, they'll stop trusting the rest of what you say. It also undercuts your own number: if everyone pays at least $12,000, asking for $11,500 makes no sense.
- **What I used instead:** your $12,500 fee and your agent's range. This is a stronger position, because it makes $11,500 look like a reasonable ask and not a stretch.

If you do have firm $12,000+ offers from several publishers, say so in the email. Specific offers would carry more weight than a general claim.

Some other notes:
- The email is about 200 words. It reminds them of your track record before it gives the number, because you're asking them to pay more than they planned.
- I didn't name the other publisher or the book titles because I don't have them. Adding the title of the book that made the state reading list would make that point stronger.
- I signed it "Ben." Change the sign-off and replace "[Name]" in the greeting.
- If you'd turn down the book at $9,000, saying so would give you more leverage. I didn't write that in, because only you can decide whether you mean it.
