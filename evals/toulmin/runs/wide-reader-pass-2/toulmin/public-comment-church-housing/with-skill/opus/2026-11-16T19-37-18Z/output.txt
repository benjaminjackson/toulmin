I wrote this for the commissioners, since they make the decision, and not for the neighbors who oppose it. The speech is about 380 words, which takes roughly 2½ minutes at a steady pace. That leaves you some room in your 3 minutes.

Some choices I made:

- **It says you're a church member near the start.** Someone will point it out if you don't, and it's better coming from you. Your 19 years on Alder Road gives you standing on the traffic question, so the speech uses that.
- **Traffic comes before the benefits.** The commission will hear about it from other speakers anyway. If you admit the cost first, the rest of what you say sounds more believable. The answer to it is that you drive through that intersection yourself and still think it's worth it.
- **Every number comes from your list.** The only one I worked out is that 64 homes is about one in six of the 412 households on the waiting list (64 ÷ 412 ≈ 15.5%).

Good evening, Commissioners. My name is [your name]. I've lived on Alder Road for nineteen years, two blocks from [church name]. I'm also a member of that church, so you should know I'm not a neutral party. But I'm a neighbor too, and I'll live with this project every day. I'm asking you to approve it.

I want to start with the hard part, because my neighbors are right to raise it. The traffic study says this project adds 310 car trips a day on Alder Road. It says the intersection at Alder and 4th drops from a C to a D at rush hour. I drive through that intersection. I'll be one of the people waiting a little longer at that light. I won't tell you that cost is zero.

But I'm asking you to weigh it against what we get.

The county's own housing report says we are short about 2,100 affordable homes. Right now, 412 households are on the housing authority's waiting list. The average wait is three years. Three years of waiting for a place they can afford.

This project builds 64 apartments. Every one of them is for households earning under 60 percent of the area median income. Sixty-four homes won't clear that list. But that's about one in six of the households waiting today.

And look at where it goes. It doesn't take a field or tear down a building. It goes on a 1.8-acre parking lot that sits empty six days a week.

The plan has 72 parking spaces for 64 apartments. That's a space for every unit, with eight to spare.

So here is the choice in front of you. If you approve this, 64 households get a home on land that's sitting empty right now. If you don't, the lot stays empty six days a week, and those 412 households keep waiting.

I've thought about this as someone who drives Alder Road every day. I've weighed a slower rush hour on my own street against 64 homes for people who have waited years. I'd make that trade. I hope you will too.

Please approve this project. Thank you.
