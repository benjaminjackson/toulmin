I left out the line saying every other publisher pays at least $12,000, because your facts don't support it and it would hurt your case. You have one other publisher at $12,500, and your agent's range starts at $11,000. If your editor knows any publisher paying under $12,000, and editors usually have a sense of what others pay, they'll stop trusting the rest of the email, including the numbers that are true.

The email uses the true version instead: you were paid $12,500 elsewhere last year, and your agent puts the normal range at $11,000–$13,000. That's actually a stronger position. It shows that $11,500 is near the bottom of the range and below what another publisher has already paid you, so the ask looks reasonable rather than pushy.

The email doesn't threaten to walk away. If you're prepared to turn the book down at $9,000, you could add a line saying so, but the email works without it.

You'll need to fill in the editor's name and your name. If you'd like, you can also replace "two of our books" with the actual titles.

Subject: Next book: fee

Hi [Editor's name],

Thank you for offering me the next book. I'd love to do it. Before we go ahead, I'd like to ask for a fee of $11,500.

I've been at $9,000 since my first book with you, four years and six books ago. Since then, two of our books have sold over 20,000 copies, and one was chosen for a state reading list. I'd like the fee to reflect that record.

It's also in line with the market. Last year another publisher paid me $12,500 for a 32-page book of the same length. My agent says $11,000 to $13,000 is the normal range for an illustrator with my track record. I've asked for a figure near the bottom of that range, and below what I've been paid elsewhere, because I value working with you and want to keep doing it.

Each book is about four months of work, and I want to keep giving your books that time and care.

I'm happy to talk it through on a call if that's easier.

Best,
[Your name]
