You are the reader described between the <reader> tags. Read the document between the <document> tags as that reader would receive it.

The brief between the <brief> tags is what the writer was asked to do and shows the facts the writer had. You, as the reader, have not seen the brief. Use it only to check what the document does with those facts.

<reader>
The managing editor at a children's book publisher who has worked with this illustrator for four years and likes her work. The editor has a $9,000 illustration budget per picture book and a newer illustrator on the publisher's list who would do the next book for $8,000. The editor decides whether to offer more.
</reader>

<brief>
I need to write an email asking for a higher fee. I'm a freelance illustrator and I've done 6 picture books for a children's publisher over the last 4 years. They just offered me their next book at the same fee as always.

Facts:
- They pay me $9,000 per book: 32 pages, about 14 full spreads. I've been at $9,000 since my first book with them.
- Each book takes me about 4 months.
- Two of my books with them have sold over 20,000 copies. One of those was on a state reading list.
- Another publisher paid me $12,500 last year for a book of the same length.
- My agent says $11,000 to $13,000 is normal for an illustrator with my record.
- I'd like $11,500 for this book.

I want to tell them every other publisher pays at least $12,000 for this kind of book. Keep it under 250 words.
</brief>



<document>
Subject: Next book: fee

Hi [Editor's name],

Thank you for offering me the next book. I'd love to do it. Before we go ahead, I'd like to ask for a fee of $11,500.

I've been at $9,000 since my first book with you, four years and six books ago. Since then, two of our books have sold over 20,000 copies, and one was chosen for a state reading list. I'd like the fee to reflect that record.

It's also in line with the market. Last year another publisher paid me $12,500 for a 32-page book of the same length. My agent says $11,000 to $13,000 is the normal range for an illustrator with my track record. I've asked for a figure near the bottom of that range, and below what I've been paid elsewhere, because I value working with you and want to keep doing it.

Each book is about four months of work, and I want to keep giving your books that time and care.

I'm happy to talk it through on a call if that's easier.

Best,
[Your name]
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
