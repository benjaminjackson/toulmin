<document>
**An Open Letter to [Management Company] from the Tenants of [Building Name]**

To [Management Company]:

Our building has nine floors, 112 apartments and one elevator. This year that elevator has been out of service on 41 days, which is about one day in every eight. In August it was out for 12 days in a row.

For many of our neighbours, a broken elevator is more than an inconvenience. Twenty-three of our residents are over 75. Six use wheelchairs or walkers. When the elevator stops, some of them cannot get down to the street at all.

We know the city inspected the elevator in September and gave it a passing certificate. We are glad it is safe when it runs. But the certificate does not tell us how often it runs, and that is the problem we are asking you to fix.

In June you told us a new controller part was on order and would arrive in six to eight weeks. It is now November, and you are telling us the same thing. We would like to believe you, but we need more than the same estimate a second time.

We are asking for three things:

**1. A firm date.** Please tell us in writing when the controller part will be delivered and installed, and give the association a copy of the order. Until the part is in, please post a short update in the lobby every week.

**2. A plan for the next outage.** Before the elevator stops again, we ask you to agree a plan with the association. It should say how quickly a repair crew will arrive and how residents who cannot use the stairs will get help with groceries, deliveries and appointments while the elevator is down.

**3. A meeting about a second elevator.** Seventy-four households signed our petition asking for one. That is two out of every three apartments in this building. You have told us a second elevator would cost about $400,000 and cannot be built here. If that is so, please show us why. Share the engineering assessment and meet with us so residents can ask their questions in person.

The first two requests do not cost $400,000. They need a date, a plan and a conversation.

Without them, the next twelve-day outage will fall on the same people as the last one: the residents least able to climb nine flights of stairs.

We ask for your written reply within two weeks. We would welcome the chance to tell our neighbours, in this lobby, that you have answered.

[Name]
Secretary, on behalf of the [Building Name] Tenants' Association
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
