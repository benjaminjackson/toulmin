I'm applying for the front office manager position. For the past seven weeks I have run the front office alone, and I'm asking to keep doing it permanently.

**What I've changed at the front desk**

I've worked here for six years, the last two as assistant manager. In that time, two things about the desk have changed in ways that show up in what guests experience:

- **People stay.** In the year before I became assistant manager, 9 of our 14 front desk staff left. Last year, 5 did. That means fewer new hires to train and more experienced people at the desk.
- **New hires are ready sooner.** I rewrote the front desk training. New hires now work the desk on their own after 5 shifts instead of 9, which is four fewer training shifts for every person we hire.

Our check-in score on the main review site rose from 7.9 to 8.6 over the last 18 months. The renovation of the lobby and rooms deserves a real share of that, and I won't claim all of it. But check-in is the part of the stay the front desk delivers. A steadier, better-trained team is part of why guests rate it higher, and keeping that score up depends on keeping that team.

**I've already been doing the job**

Since the front office manager left seven weeks ago, I have covered her role alone. You've seen how the front office has run during that time. Promoting me keeps that running without a gap. An outside hire would have to learn our team, our systems and our guests from scratch, and the role would stay open while you search.

**The budget**

I have not managed a budget, and this role owns a $1.1 million payroll budget. I want to say that plainly. The two biggest drivers of front desk payroll are how many people we hire and how long it takes to train them, and I've already brought both down. What I haven't done is plan and track the full budget.

I propose that I prepare the next payroll budget with you, and that for my first six months we review actual payroll against budget together each month. If I'm off track, you'll see it within the month, not at year end.

**What I'm asking**

I'd like to be named front office manager. I'm glad to talk through any of this before you decide.
