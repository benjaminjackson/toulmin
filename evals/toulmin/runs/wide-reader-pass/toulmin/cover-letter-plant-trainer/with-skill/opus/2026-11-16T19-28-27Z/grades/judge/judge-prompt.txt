You are the reader described between the <reader> tags. Read the document between the <document> tags as that reader would receive it.

The brief between the <brief> tags is what the writer was asked to do and shows the facts the writer had. You, as the reader, have not seen the brief. Use it only to check what the document does with those facts.

<reader>
The HR manager at a pharmaceutical manufacturing plant who is screening applicants for a training coordinator job. She has seen many teachers apply for corporate training roles and assumes most of them don't know how a regulated plant works. She decides who gets a phone screen.
</reader>

<brief>
Help me write a cover letter. I've taught high school chemistry for 9 years and I'm applying to be a training coordinator at a pharmaceutical manufacturing plant about 30 minutes from me.

About me:
- I teach about 140 students a year across five classes.
- I wrote our school's lab safety program in 2019. Since then we've had no injuries in our labs that needed more than first aid.
- Our AP Chemistry pass rate went from 41% to 58% over the four years I've taught the course.
- Every year I train our new science teachers on lab procedures, about 12 hours each. There have been two a year on average.
- I've never worked in a regulated industry. The job posting asks for experience with GMP training records, which I don't have.
- I use Google Classroom. The plant uses a learning management system I haven't used.

I want the letter to say I can cut their new-hire training time in half. One page.
</brief>

I am applying for the Training Coordinator position at [Company]. For nine years I have taught high school chemistry. The part of that job I would bring to your plant is the one that matters most in any lab: getting people to follow a written procedure correctly, every time, where a mistake can hurt someone.

In 2019 I wrote my school's lab safety program. In the seven years since, with about 140 students a year working in our labs, we have had no injury that needed more than first aid. Every year I also train the new science teachers who join our department on lab procedures, about 12 hours of instruction for each one. They are adults who have to be competent before they are responsible for anyone else's safety. Each of your new hires has to reach the same point.

I am also used to being judged on a number that someone outside my classroom measures. In the four years I have taught AP Chemistry, our pass rate has risen from 41% to 58%.

I should be direct about what I have not done. I have never worked in a regulated industry. I have not kept GMP training records, and I have not used your learning management system. Classroom records are not GMP records, and I won't pretend they are. What I bring is training that people remember and act on. The documentation side I would make my first priority to learn. I run all five of my classes through Google Classroom, so moving to a new system means learning how it works, not learning what it is for.

On new-hire training time, I would want to measure before I promise a number. In my first 90 days, I would find out how long new hires currently take to reach qualification and where that time goes. I would then bring you specific changes, each with an estimate of the time it saves and confirmation that it does not weaken compliance.

I live about 30 minutes from the plant and would welcome the chance to talk about how my experience could serve your team. Thank you for your consideration.

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
