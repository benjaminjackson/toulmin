I've written the narrative, but I've left out the "double our fresh food in the first year" claim. Your figures don't support it.

**Why I left out "double"**
- **What the claim needs:** you hand out about 6,000 lb of fresh food a month, or 72,000 lb a year. Doubling means 72,000 more pounds in year one.
- **What your figures support:** the farm produce you turned down was about 9,000 lb. That's 3,000 lb a month over three months. Even if all of it comes back, fresh food goes up about 12% over the year, or by half during the summer months.
- **What a reviewer would say:** "Where do the other 63,000 pounds come from?" Nothing in your figures answers that. Since your last report was late, an overstated first-year target is the thing most likely to cost you credibility.

**What I used instead:** a goal they can check. You'll accept every pound of produce local farms offer, starting with the roughly 9,000 lb you refused last summer. You'll report fresh pounds against the current 6,000 lb a month, and if you collect less than 9,000 lb in the first summer, you'll explain why. If you have other cold sources lined up that add up to about 63,000 more pounds a year (grocery rescue, a dairy, more farms), "double" becomes defensible. Add those sources with their pounds and the claim can go back in.

**Things to check before you submit:**
1. **Running costs.** Your reserves will be down to $11,000, so a reviewer will ask how you'll pay for fuel, insurance and maintenance. You didn't give me those figures, so the draft doesn't cover them. I'd add one sentence with the annual cost and where the money comes from. It's the most likely gap a reviewer will notice.
2. **Promises I proposed that you need to approve:** quarterly reports instead of one final report, and one board member responsible for filing each report on time. I put these in to deal with the late shelving report. Change them if you can't keep them.
3. **Letters from the farms** saying they'd offer produce again would turn "last summer" into "next summer." Attach them if you can get them.
4. **The shelving grant.** If you know what it achieved (more storage, more pounds handled), a short clause in the accountability section would help.

Last summer, local farms offered us roughly 9,000 pounds of fresh produce over three months. We turned all of it down. We had no way to pick it up cold or store it. We are asking the Foundation for $50,000 toward a $68,000 refrigerated van so that we can say yes next time.

**Who we serve**
Our volunteer-run food bank distributes about 38,000 pounds of food a month to 610 households in a town of about 9,000 people. About 6,000 pounds of that is fresh: produce, milk and eggs. Last summer showed that supply is not what limits our fresh food. Cold transport is.

**What the van will do**
The produce we turned down would have added about 3,000 pounds a month over those three months. That is half again as much fresh food as we usually hand out, or about five more pounds of fresh food a month for each household. The van will let us collect that produce and keep it cold from the farm to our shelves.

Four of our 23 regular volunteers have said they will drive the van on a weekly schedule, so pickups will not depend on any one person.

**Our share**
We will cover the remaining $18,000 from our reserves. That is more than 60 percent of what we hold, and it leaves us $11,000. We are willing to make that commitment because the food is already being offered to us. What we lack is the means to accept it.

**Accountability**
In 2023 the Foundation gave us $12,000 for shelving, and our final report on that grant was four months late. That should not have happened. For this grant, we will report every quarter rather than only at the end, and one board member will be responsible for filing each report on time.

Each report will show:
- pounds of fresh food distributed per month, against our current 6,000;
- pounds collected by the van, and from which sources;
- the number of pickups made by volunteer drivers.

**Our first-year goal**
In the first year, we aim to accept every pound of fresh produce local farms offer us, starting with the roughly 9,000 pounds we had to refuse last summer. We will judge the van by one number: fresh pounds distributed per month, compared with our current 6,000. If we collect less than 9,000 pounds in the first summer, our end-of-summer report will say why and what we are changing.

**If the van is not funded**
We will keep handing out about 6,000 pounds of fresh food a month. When local farms offer us produce next summer, we will have to turn it down again.
