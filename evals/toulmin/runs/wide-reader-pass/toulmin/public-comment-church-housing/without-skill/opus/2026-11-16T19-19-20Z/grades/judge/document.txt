Good evening, Commissioners. My name is [NAME]. I've lived on Alder Road for 19 years, two blocks from [CHURCH NAME], and I'm also a member of that church. I want to say that up front. I'm here to support the proposal for 64 affordable apartments on the church's back parking lot.

I'll start with the hard part, because my neighbors and I are the people who will live with it. The traffic study estimates 310 more car trips a day on Alder Road, and it says the intersection at Alder and 4th would drop from a C to a D at rush hour. I drive through that intersection every day. I won't pretend that change doesn't matter. But I've thought about what we'd be trading it for, and I believe it's worth it.

Here's the trade. Right now that lot is 1.8 acres of asphalt, and it sits empty six days a week. The county's own housing report says we are short about 2,100 affordable homes. The county housing authority has 412 households on its waiting list, and the average wait is three years. Three years is a child's entire time in middle school. It's three years of a family paying more than it can afford, or doubling up with relatives, or leaving the county altogether.

These 64 apartments would all rent to households earning under 60 percent of the area median income. These are people who already work here and live here, or want to: home health aides, cooks, retail workers, retirees on fixed incomes. Sixty-four homes won't close a gap of 2,100. But they're 64 real homes on land that's already paved, with no new land cleared and no one displaced. Good chances like that don't come along often.

The plan also includes 72 parking spaces for the 64 units, so residents' cars will be on the property and not on our street.

When people talk about where affordable housing should go, they often mean somewhere else. I'm asking you to approve it in my own neighborhood, on my own road. A slower light at Alder and 4th is a real cost, and I accept it. A three-year wait for a safe, affordable home is a much bigger cost, and right now we're asking 412 families to carry it.

Please approve this project. Thank you.
