<brief>
I'm going to speak at our county planning commission tonight in favor of a housing project, and I want help writing what I'll say. The church I belong to wants to build 64 apartments on its back parking lot. All of them would rent to households earning under 60% of the area median income.

Facts:
- The church lot is 1.8 acres and sits empty 6 days a week.
- The county's own housing report says we are short about 2,100 affordable homes.
- The traffic study estimates 310 more car trips a day on Alder Road, and says the intersection at Alder and 4th would drop from a C rating to a D at rush hour.
- The plan has 72 parking spaces for the 64 units.
- 412 households are on the county housing authority's waiting list. The average wait is 3 years.
- I've lived on Alder Road for 19 years, two blocks from the church.

Each speaker gets 3 minutes.
</brief>



<document>
Good evening, Chair [Name] and members of the Commission. My name is [Your Name]. I've lived on Alder Road for 19 years, two blocks from [Church Name]. I'm also a member of that church, and I want to say that up front.

I'm here to support the 64 apartments on the church's back parking lot.

I'll start with the hardest part, because I'll live with it too. The traffic study says this project adds about 310 car trips a day on Alder, and that the intersection at Alder and 4th drops from a C to a D at rush hour. I drive through that intersection all the time. A D means I'll wait a little longer some mornings. I'm not going to pretend that isn't real.

But I've thought hard about what's on the other side of that trade.

The county's own housing report says we are short about 2,100 affordable homes. Right now, 412 households are on the housing authority's waiting list, and the average wait is three years.

Three years. That's three years of paying rent you can't afford, or living far from your job, or doubling up with family, while you wait for a phone call.

Every one of these 64 apartments would go to a household earning under 60% of the area median income. These are working families, seniors, and people starting over, and they're the ones on that list.

And look at where this would go. Not a field. Not a stand of trees. A 1.8-acre parking lot that sits empty six days a week. We already paved it. The only question is whether it stays empty or becomes homes.

The plan also includes 72 parking spaces for 64 units. That's more than one space for every apartment.

Let me be honest about the scale. Sixty-four homes won't close a 2,100-home gap. It won't clear a waiting list of 412 households. But it means 64 households stop waiting. A gap like ours gets closed one project at a time, on land we already have, when neighbors are willing to say yes.

I'm one of the neighbors who'll sit at that light a little longer. I think it's worth it. I respectfully ask you to approve this project.

Thank you.
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
