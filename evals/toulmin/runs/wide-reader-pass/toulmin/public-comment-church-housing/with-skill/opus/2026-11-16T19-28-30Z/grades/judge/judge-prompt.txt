I'm going to speak at our county planning commission tonight in favor of a housing project, and I want help writing what I'll say. The church I belong to wants to build 64 apartments on its back parking lot. All of them would rent to households earning under 60% of the area median income.

- The county's own housing report says we are short about 2,100 affordable homes.
- The traffic study estimates 310 more car trips a day on Alder Road, and says the intersection at Alder and 4th would drop from a C rating to a D at rush hour.
- The plan has 72 parking spaces for the 64 units.

Each speaker gets 3 minutes.
</brief>

Good evening, Commissioners. My name is [your name]. I've lived on Alder Road for nineteen years, two blocks from [church name]. I'm also a member of that church, so you should know I'm not a neutral party. I'm here to ask you to approve the sixty-four apartments on its back lot.

I want to start with traffic, because I drive Alder every day and I've read the study.

The study estimates three hundred and ten more car trips a day on Alder. It says the intersection at Alder and Fourth would drop from a C to a D at rush hour. I'm not going to stand here and tell you that doesn't matter. I'm one of the people who will be waiting at that light.

But a D at rush hour means a longer wait. And I'll take a longer wait at five o'clock over what we have now. If the commission wants to make approval conditional on a review of the signal at Alder and Fourth, I would welcome that.

On parking, the plan has seventy-two spaces for sixty-four apartments. That's a space for every home, with eight left over.

Now, here's what we have now.

We have a parking lot of almost two acres that sits empty six days a week.

We have four hundred and twelve households on the county housing authority's waiting list. The average wait is three years.

And the county's own housing report says we are short about twenty-one hundred affordable homes.

Every one of these sixty-four apartments would rent to a household earning under sixty percent of the area median income. Not a handful of affordable units mixed in with market-rate ones. All sixty-four.

Sixty-four homes won't close a gap of twenty-one hundred. No single project in front of you will. That gap closes one project at a time. This one already has the land, and it has an owner that wants to build.

Sixty-four homes is enough for about one in six of the households on that waiting list.

If you vote no tonight, nothing on Alder Road changes. The lot stays empty six days a week. And the four hundred and twelve households on that list keep waiting.

I've lived on Alder Road for nineteen years. I'd like the people who move into those apartments to have the chance to say the same thing someday.

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
