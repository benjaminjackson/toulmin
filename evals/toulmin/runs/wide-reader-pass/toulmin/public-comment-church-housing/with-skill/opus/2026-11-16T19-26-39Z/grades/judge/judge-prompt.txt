I'm going to speak at our county planning commission tonight in favor of a housing project, and I want help writing what I'll say. The church I belong to wants to build 64 apartments on its back parking lot. All of them would rent to households earning under 60% of the area median income.

Facts:
- The church lot is 1.8 acres and sits empty 6 days a week.
- The county's own housing report says we are short about 2,100 affordable homes.
- The traffic study estimates 310 more car trips a day on Alder Road, and says the intersection at Alder and 4th would drop from a C rating to a D at rush hour.
- The plan has 72 parking spaces for the 64 units.
- 412 households are on the county housing authority's waiting list. The average wait is 3 years.
- I've lived on Alder Road for 19 years, two blocks from the church.

Each speaker gets 3 minutes.
</brief>



<document>
Good evening, commissioners. My name is [your name]. I've lived on Alder Road for 19 years, two blocks from the church. I'm also a member of that church. I'm here to ask you to approve the 64 apartments on its back parking lot.

I want to start with traffic, because I live on that road.

The county's traffic study says this project will add 310 car trips a day on Alder. It says the intersection at Alder and 4th will drop from a C to a D at rush hour. I drive through that intersection. I'll be one of the people waiting longer at that light.

I've thought about that. And I'm still standing here asking you to say yes.

Here's why.

The county's own housing report says we are short about 2,100 affordable homes.

Right now, 412 households are on the housing authority's waiting list. The average wait is three years. Three years.

Every one of these 64 apartments would rent to households earning under 60 percent of the area median income. Sixty-four homes is about one for every six households on that waiting list.

And the land is already there. It's a 1.8-acre parking lot. It sits empty six days a week. Nobody has to move out for this to be built. No green space is lost. We'd be putting homes where there's now just empty asphalt.

I know parking worries some of my neighbors. The plan has 72 spaces for 64 apartments. That's one space for every apartment, with 8 to spare.

So here's the choice in front of you.

If you say yes, 64 households get homes they can afford, on land that's sitting empty. And those of us on Alder wait a little longer at one light at rush hour.

If you say no, the lot stays empty six days a week. The waiting list stays at 412. The wait stays at three years. And the county is still about 2,100 homes short.

If you want to attach conditions to improve Alder and 4th, I'd support that. But please don't let one intersection's rush-hour grade stand between this county and 64 affordable homes.

A slower rush hour is a cost I'm willing to share with my neighbors. A three-year wait for housing falls on those families alone.

Please approve this project. Thank you.
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
