# Bookkeeping for hair and nail salons. Nothing else.

You run a salon. You don't want to spend Sunday night sorting tips, booth rent and product sales into categories.

I'm a bookkeeper, and every one of my clients is a hair or nail salon. Right now that's 31 salons. I know how commission and booth rent work, how retail sales differ from service income, and why your payroll looks different from a restaurant's or an office's.

## What you get for $199 a month

- **Monthly books.** Every transaction gets categorized and reconciled. Your books for each month are closed by the 10th of the next month.
- **Payroll for up to 10 staff.** Paychecks, tips and withholding, all handled.
- **Sales tax filing.** Filed for you, on time.

There's one flat price and no add-on fees. Everyone pays the same, including clients who've been with me for years.

## What clean books do at tax time

Last tax season, 9 of my clients told me their accountant charged them less because their books came in clean. One of them said she saved $800. I can't promise you'll see the same savings, but your accountant will get organized books instead of a shoebox.

## Honest answers to fair questions

**Do you hit the 10th every month?** Almost always. I missed it three times last year, and I'd rather you hear that from me than find out on your own.

**Do clients stay?** Two clients left last year, and both left because they sold their salons.

**Who will I be working with?** Me. I'm one person, not a firm, so you won't be passed between assistants. I don't take calls on Sundays or Mondays. Those are my days off, and many salons are closed then too.

## Let's talk first

The first call is free and takes 20 minutes. Tell me how your salon runs and what your books look like now. I'll tell you honestly whether I'm a good fit. If I'm not, you won't get a sales pitch.

**[Book your free 20-minute call]**

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
