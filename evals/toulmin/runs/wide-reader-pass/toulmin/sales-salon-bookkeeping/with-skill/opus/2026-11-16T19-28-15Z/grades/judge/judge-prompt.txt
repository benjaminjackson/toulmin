# Bookkeeping for hair and nail salons. Only salons.

For $199 a month, I do your monthly books, payroll for up to 10 staff, and your sales tax filings. You pay one flat price and work with one bookkeeper who already knows salons.

## I work with 31 salons and nobody else

Salons are the only clients I take on. You won't spend your first few months explaining how your business makes money. I've already learned that from 31 other owners.

Clients tend to stay. Two left last year, and both left because they sold their salons.

## Your books are closed by the 10th

I close each month's books by the 10th of the next month. You get to see how last month went while you can still do something about it.

To be straight with you: last year I missed that date three times. The 10th is still the deadline I work to, and I'm telling you about the misses because you'd rather hear about them from me.

## Clean books can lower your accountant's bill

Last tax season, 9 of my clients told me their accountant charged them less because their books came in clean. One owner said she saved $800.

I can't promise your accountant will do the same. But when the books are a mess, your accountant has to bill for the hours it takes to sort them out. Clean books save them that work.

## You'll work with me directly

I'm one person, so you won't be passed between staff or have to re-explain your salon to someone new. The person who does your books is the person who answers your questions.

I don't take calls on Sundays or Mondays. Every other day, you're talking to me.

## What's included for $199 a month

- Monthly bookkeeping, closed by the 10th
- Payroll for up to 10 staff
- Sales tax filing

## Start with a free 20-minute call

Tell me about your salon: how many people you have on payroll, where your books stand now, and what's been a headache. In 20 minutes we'll both know whether I'm the right fit. The call is free.

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
