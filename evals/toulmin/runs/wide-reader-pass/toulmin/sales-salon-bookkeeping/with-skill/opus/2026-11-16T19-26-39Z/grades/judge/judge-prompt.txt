# Bookkeeping for hair and nail salons. Only salons.

You run the chairs. I'll keep the books.

I'm a bookkeeper, and I work only with hair and nail salons. Every month I do your books, run payroll for up to 10 staff and file your sales tax, all for one flat price.

**$199 a month.**

## Salons stay with me

I have 31 salon clients. Two left last year, and both left because they sold their salons.

Salons are all I work on, so you won't spend your first month teaching me how your business runs.

## Clean books can lower your accountant's bill

Last tax season, 9 of my clients told me their accountant charged them less because their books came in clean. One of them said she saved $800.

I can't promise your accountant will do the same. But messy books take an accountant longer to sort out, and you pay for that time.

## What you can count on

- **Your books are closed by the 10th of the next month.** I missed that date 3 times last year, and I'd rather you hear it from me.
- **You deal with me.** I'm one person, not a firm. The person who does your books is the person you talk to.
- **I don't answer the phone on Sundays or Mondays.** Those are my days off.
- **Everyone pays the same.** My price was $149 until January. When I raised it to $199, I raised it for every client, including the ones who've been with me longest. Nobody gets a special rate, and nobody gets charged extra for being new.

## Is this right for your salon?

You're a good fit if you run a hair or nail salon with up to 10 people on payroll, and you want your books, payroll and sales tax done every month without having to think about them.

The first call is free and takes 20 minutes. We'll look at where your books are now and what you need. Then you can decide whether to hand them to me.

**[ Book your free 20-minute call ]**

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
