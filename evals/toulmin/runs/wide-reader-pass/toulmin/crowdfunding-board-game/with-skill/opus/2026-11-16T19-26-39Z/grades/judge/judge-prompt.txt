This is a cooperative board game for 1 to 4 players. Together, you see a lighthouse keeper's family through one winter. You don't compete. You win or lose as a family.

**Where the $28,000 goes**

I have a factory quote for a print run of 1,500 copies: $17,400, or $11.60 a copy. Shipping each copy to a backer costs about $9.

The goal is 572 pledges at $49. Shipping those 572 copies costs about $5,148. Printing plus shipping comes to $22,548, which leaves about $5,450 for the campaign's fees and costs along the way.

So the goal isn't a guess. It's the factory quote plus shipping plus a margin. And if more of you back it than that, there are already 1,500 copies in the quote to send.

I work full time as a land surveyor. I haven't published a game before, and I'd rather you heard that from me than found it out later.

The usual worry with a first-time creator is that the game never arrives, often because the cost of getting a box to your door was underestimated. That's why I priced the printing and the shipping before asking anyone for a dollar. Those numbers are above, and you can hold me to them.

For $49, one copy of the game, shipped to you anywhere in the US.

This campaign is what pays for the print run. At 572 backers, the game gets made and a copy goes to every one of you. Every pledge before then moves it closer.

If you've ever wanted a co-op game where the thing you're protecting is a family rather than a kingdom, I think this is it. Over 140 sessions, the players have told me the same.

Pull up a chair. The winter's long, and the lamp needs tending.

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
