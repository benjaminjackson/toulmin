Subject: Request for promotion to Senior Paralegal

Dear [Name],

I am writing to ask to be considered for promotion to senior paralegal in the January review cycle. I have been a paralegal in the litigation group for five years, and I believe my current work already matches much of what the role requires. I would like to meet with you in person to discuss it.

**Supervising and training paralegals.** A senior paralegal supervises the group's other paralegals. A senior role would let me do this formally and consistently.

**Workload.** I support four lawyers and worked on 23 cases last year, including two that went to trial. I billed 1,640 hours, against the group's target of 1,550.

**Systems for the group.** In 2023 I built the group's tracking sheet for court deadlines. Two other groups in the firm now use it.

I also want to be open about a mistake. Last March I entered a filing deadline incorrectly in that sheet. Another paralegal caught the error, and we filed two hours before the court's cutoff. I think how I handled the mistake shows how I would handle responsibility for other paralegals' work: find where the process failed and fix it, rather than relying on people never making mistakes.

I don't know whether a senior paralegal position is currently open. If it isn't, I would welcome the chance to discuss whether the group's growth, with three new paralegals in two years, supports creating one.

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
