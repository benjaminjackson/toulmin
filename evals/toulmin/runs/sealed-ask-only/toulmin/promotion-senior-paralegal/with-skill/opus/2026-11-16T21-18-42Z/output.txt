Subject: Request for promotion to Senior Paralegal – January review cycle

Dear David,

I would like to be considered for promotion to Senior Paralegal in the January review cycle. I have been a paralegal in the litigation group for five years, and I believe I am already doing much of the work the role requires.

The main difference between my current role and the senior role is supervising the other paralegals. I trained all three paralegals the group has hired in the last two years. The promotion would make official a responsibility I already carry.

Over the past year:

- I supported four lawyers across 23 cases, including two that went to trial.
- I billed 1,640 hours, 90 above the group's target of 1,550.

In 2023 I built the group's court-deadline tracking sheet. Two other groups in the firm now use it as well.

That sheet was also where I made my most serious mistake. Last March I entered a filing deadline incorrectly. Another paralegal caught it, and we filed two hours before the court's cutoff. That was much too close. It showed me that the sheet depended on one person entering every date correctly. I added a second-person check so that someone other than the person who entered a deadline now verifies it. I mention this because a supervisor will see mistakes happen, and when one happens, the right response is to fix the system that allowed it.

As Senior Paralegal, I would keep handling a full caseload. I would also take formal responsibility for training, workload and quality checks across the paralegal team.

I'm sending this ahead of the December 15 deadline so we have time to talk it over. Could we meet in the next two weeks? I'm glad to provide anything else that would help.

Thank you for considering it.

Best regards,
Jenna Morales

The email is about 310 words. A few notes:

- **The March error:** I included it because it supports your case. It shows you fixed the process rather than just the one mistake, and that's what a supervisor needs to do. If you'd rather leave it out, delete that paragraph and the email still holds together.
- **Pay:** I left the $9,000 out. You're asking for the role, and the pay comes with it. Putting the number in tends to make the request read as being about money.
- **Things that would make it stronger:** Before December 15, try to learn whether there's an open senior position and whether any of your four lawyers will back you. If one will, add a line such as "I've discussed this with [name], who supports it." A partner usually gives that more weight than anything else in the email.
