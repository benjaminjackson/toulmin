I wrote this as an email because it's quicker for him to answer and to forward to whoever decides on promotions. You can print it and hand it over if your firm prefers paper.

<final_document>
Subject: Request for promotion to Senior Paralegal

Dear David,

I'm asking to be considered for promotion to senior paralegal in the January review cycle. I'm sending this ahead of the December 15 deadline, and I'd like to meet with you in person to discuss it.

I've been a paralegal in the litigation group for five years. Much of what the senior role involves is work I already do.

**Training and supervision.** I trained all three paralegals the group has hired in the past two years. That covered our filing procedures, our case-management practices and the deadline system. Supervising the paralegal team is the main part of the senior role, and I've been doing it informally for two years.

**Systems.** In 2023 I built the group's court-deadline tracking sheet. Two other groups in the firm now use it.

**Accountability.** In March I entered a filing deadline wrong in that sheet. A colleague caught it, and we filed two hours before the court's cutoff. That was too close. In April I added a second-person check to every entry. Since then, about 600 deadlines have gone through the sheet with none wrong or missed. In a role that's responsible for other people's work, I think you should know how I handled a mistake like that.

**Workload.** Last year I supported four lawyers on 23 cases, including two that went to trial. I billed 1,640 hours against the group's target of 1,550.

I don't know whether a senior position is currently budgeted. If it isn't, I'd like to talk about whether the group's growth makes the case for one. We've hired three paralegals in two years, and someone already has to coordinate their work.

Could we find 30 minutes in the next few weeks? I'm happy to work around your schedule.

Thank you for considering this.

Best regards,
Jenna Morales
</final_document>

The email is about 330 words.

- **The paralegal who left after 8 months:** I didn't mention them. The email says you trained all three, which is true, but it doesn't claim all three are still doing well. If he asks about it, have a short answer ready, especially if the reason they left had nothing to do with their training.
- **Supporting lawyers:** if any of your four lawyers agrees to back you before you send this, add one line before the meeting request, such as "[Name] has said she supports this request."
- **Pay:** I left out the $9,000 difference. A promotion request usually argues for the role and leaves pay for the meeting.
