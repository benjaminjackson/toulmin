**An Open Letter to the [State Employees' Health Plan]**

[Date]

To [name and title] and the [Plan] board:

We are 38 independent pharmacies in [State], and together we make up the Main Street Pharmacy Alliance. About one in five prescriptions we fill is for a state employee, retiree or family member covered by your plan. We are writing because since January your plan has paid us less than it costs us to fill many of those prescriptions. Some of our pharmacies cannot keep doing that.

**What the rates do**

Your plan sets our pay through its pharmacy benefit manager, [PBM]. For the 20 generic drugs we fill most often, it pays us an average of $1.80 less per prescription than it costs us to buy and dispense the drug. Some of us recover part of that loss on brand-name drugs. But a pharmacy can't stay open if it loses money on the medicines it fills most often.

Two of our 38 pharmacies have told us they will stop filling prescriptions for plan members if the rates don't change. Nine independent pharmacies in [State] have closed in the past two years. We don't know how many of those closed because of rates, and we won't claim to. We do know that rates below cost make more closures likely.

**Who it affects**

In 14 counties, the nearest pharmacy for most residents is an independent one. For most people there, the nearest chain pharmacy is more than 20 miles away. If the local pharmacy in one of those counties stops taking your plan, the state workers and retirees who live there will have to drive that distance or wait for mail order to get their medicine.

**What we ask**

Your contract with [PBM] comes up for renewal on July 1. We ask that the new contract pay pharmacies at least what each drug costs them, plus a dispensing fee for the work of filling it.

We will help you check our numbers:

- We will share our purchase invoices for these 20 drugs with your staff, so you can compare what we pay with what you pay us.
- We will meet with your staff at any time before May 1.

Last year my own pharmacy, Reilly Drug in Carver, filled about 3,100 prescriptions for your members. I want to keep filling them, and so do the other 37 pharmacies in the Alliance. Before you sign the next contract, please let us show you our costs.

Sincerely,

Tom Reilly
Owner, Reilly Drug, Carver
On behalf of the Main Street Pharmacy Alliance
