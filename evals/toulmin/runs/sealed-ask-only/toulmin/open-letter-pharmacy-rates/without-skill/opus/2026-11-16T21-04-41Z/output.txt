[Date]

To the Board and Administrators of the [State] State Employees' Health Plan:

We are 38 independent pharmacies across [State]. Every day we fill prescriptions for teachers, troopers, nurses at state hospitals, retirees and their families. We are writing because, under the rates the plan has paid since January, we lose money every time we fill the generic drugs your members need most.

For the 20 generic drugs we fill most often, the plan now pays us, on average, **$1.80 less per prescription than it costs us to buy and fill it.** This isn't a thin margin. It's a loss, and it adds up with every prescription. Plan members account for about 22 percent of the prescriptions at our pharmacies, so this is a large share of our business. At my own pharmacy, I filled about 3,100 prescriptions for plan members last year.

No business can keep doing this. Nine independent pharmacies in [State] have closed in the past two years. We can't say how many of those closures were caused by low reimbursement rates. We can say that rates below our cost make it harder for every pharmacy still open to stay open.

3. **Meet with independent pharmacists** before the contract is renewed, so the people who fill these prescriptions are heard.

We want to keep serving your members. We know them by name, and many of them have come to us for years. We're asking the plan to make sure we can afford to keep doing so.
