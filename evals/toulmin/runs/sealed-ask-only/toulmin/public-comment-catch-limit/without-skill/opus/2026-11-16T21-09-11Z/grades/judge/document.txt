Good evening, Commissioners. My name is [NAME]. For 22 years I've run a charter boat out of [HARBOR], one of 11 charter boats there. I run about 160 trips a season at $900 a trip.

I've read the staff report. I know the summer flounder stock is at 78% of target, and I want that stock healthy too, because my living depends on it. My concern is who pays for the fix.

This has happened to us before. When the limit was cut in 2016, my bookings dropped from 170 trips to 138 the next season. That's 32 trips and about $29,000 gone in one year. Two years later I was back up to 155, but I never got back to 170. And that was one cut. This proposal would last three years.

Our charter association surveyed 70 past customers. 41 of them, nearly 60%, said they'd book fewer trips if the limit drops to 2. Only 12 said they'd book the same. People book a charter for a reasonable shot at taking fish home. If you take that away, a lot of them stay home.

Charter boats land about 9% of this state's summer flounder. Private boats land about 80%. A cut that falls the same on everyone puts a heavy cost on the small part of the fishery whose income depends on it, and saves comparatively few fish.

I'm asking the commission to look at options that share the burden fairly. That could be a separate limit for the for-hire fleet, a 3-fish limit, or a shorter time frame with a review after one season. Whatever you choose, please weigh what it costs the boats and families in harbors like mine.

Thank you.
