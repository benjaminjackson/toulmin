Good evening, Commissioners. My name is [NAME]. I've run a charter boat out of [HARBOR] for 22 years. I'm one of 11 charter boats there.

I'm not here to argue the stock is fine. Your staff says it's at 78% of target, and I want this fishery healthy as much as anyone. My living depends on it. But I'm asking you to look at who pays for this cut and what it actually buys.

Charter boats land about 9% of the state's summer flounder. Private boats land about 80%. Cutting the limit from four fish to two puts a heavy cost on a small part of the catch.

I know what that cost is because I've lived it. When the limit was cut in 2016, my bookings dropped from 170 trips to 138 the next season. That's almost one trip in five gone. Two years later I was back to 155. I never got all the way back.

This time I expect it to be worse. Our charter association surveyed 70 past customers. Forty-one said they'd book fewer trips at a two-fish limit. Only twelve said they'd book the same.

I run about 160 trips a season at $900 a trip. Losing even what I lost in 2016 means about $27,000 out of my business. Multiply that across 11 boats, plus the tackle shops, fuel docks, and restaurants our customers spend money at, and it's a real hit to this harbor.

So I'm asking you to consider alternatives: a three-fish limit, a separate limit for for-hire boats, or a cut that's reviewed every year instead of locked in for three. Please don't put this much of the burden on the boats that take the least.

Thank you.
