**Tenant Defense Project: Eviction Defense Staff Attorney**

The Tenant Defense Project requests $90,000 over two years ($45,000 per year) to fund an additional staff attorney who will represent tenants facing eviction in [County].

**The need.** Last year, 1,140 eviction cases were filed in [County]. Our two attorneys represented the tenant in 163 of them, about one case in seven. We turned away roughly 300 more tenants who asked for help because no attorney was free to take their case. Each of our attorneys carries about 80 open cases. We cannot take on more tenants without adding staff.

**What representation achieves.** Last year, 58% of the cases where we represented the tenant ended with the tenant either staying in the home or getting at least 60 days to move. That time lets a family find new housing and plan the move.

**The project.** The new attorney will carry a full caseload of about 80 tenants a year, drawn from the tenants we now have to turn away. Over the two-year grant, that means about 160 more households will have a lawyer in eviction court. For every case, we will record whether the tenant stayed in the home, got at least 60 days to move, or had another result. We will report these outcomes to [Foundation] each year.

**Budget and match.** The position costs $78,000 a year with benefits, or $156,000 over two years. The grant would pay $90,000 of that. A family fund has pledged $20,000 a year ($40,000 total), and the Tenant Defense Project will pay the remaining $13,000 a year ($26,000 total) from its operating budget. Together these sources fully fund the position for both years.
