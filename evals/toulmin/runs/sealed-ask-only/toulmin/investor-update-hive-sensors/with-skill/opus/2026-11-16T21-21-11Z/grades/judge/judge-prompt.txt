**Subject: Half-year update and bridge round**

Hi all,

Here's where we stand at the half year, and what I'm asking of you.

**The short version**

We have 31 paying beekeepers, up from 12 a year ago. Revenue for the last six months was $212,000. We have $340,000 in the bank and spend about $95,000 a month. We're raising a $600,000 bridge on a convertible note to carry us through next spring's pollination season. An angel investor has already committed $250,000. I'd like you in this round.

**What went well**

- **Customers:** 12 to 31 in a year.
- **Revenue:** $212,000. 70% of it came in March and April, when beekeepers get colonies ready for pollination. That's when our customers buy, and it shapes our whole year.
- **Margins:** Each sensor costs us $38 to make and sells for $55. Customers also pay $4 per hive per month for the app. We make money on every unit we sell.
- **Results:** Our largest customer runs 4,000 hives. He lost 18% of his colonies this winter, compared with 31% the winter before. He says the sensors were part of that. He also changed his mite treatment, so we can't take credit for the whole improvement. But it's the result we built this product to deliver.

**What's harder**

- **Competition.** In May, a large farm equipment company launched a similar sensor at $45. Theirs measures only temperature. Ours also measures sound and hive weight. Three of our beekeepers have asked to test their sensor. We don't yet know whether we've lost any deals to them. I'll report what we learn from those tests.
- **Cash.** At today's spending, our $340,000 lasts until about March. That's just as our selling season starts. The bridge is meant to solve this.

**Will we be profitable next year?**

I can't promise that, and I won't tell you something the numbers don't support. Over the last six months, revenue averaged about $35,000 a month against $95,000 in spending.

Here is what the numbers do support:

- **Lower spending.** We're bringing spending down to $75,000 a month by February by not filling two open roles.
- **Runway through next season.** With the bridge closed, we have cash into autumn 2027 before counting any new revenue. That covers all of next spring's buying season, with room to spare.
- **A real test.** Next spring will show how many existing customers add hives, how many new ones we win against a cheaper competitor, and how close app revenue gets us to covering our costs. I'll share those numbers with you after the season, and they'll tell us honestly how far we are from profitability.

**The round**

- 20% discount, $6M cap

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
