I left out the line that every other publisher pays at least $12,000. Your facts don't support it: you have one $12,500 fee, and your agent's range starts at $11,000. If the publisher knows the market, they could catch the overstatement, and it would weaken an otherwise strong case. The email uses what you can back up: a real $12,500 fee and your agent's range. Your $11,500 ask then looks reasonable, even modest.

<final_document>
Subject: Fee for [Book Title]

Hi [Name],

Thank you for offering me [Book Title]. I've really enjoyed our four years working together, and I'd be glad to do this one.

Before we finalize, I'd like to talk about the fee. I've been at $9,000 per book since our first project, and a lot has changed since then. Two of our six books have each sold over 20,000 copies, and [Title] was chosen for the [State] reading list. Each book is still about four months of work, with around 14 full spreads.

My rate elsewhere has gone up over that time. Last year another publisher paid me $12,500 for a book of the same length. My agent tells me that $11,000 to $13,000 is standard for an illustrator with my track record.

For this book, I'd like to ask for $11,500. That's still below what I've been paid elsewhere, and I think it fairly reflects what our books have achieved together. I value working with you and hope we can make this work.

I'm happy to talk it through by phone if that's easier.

Best,
[Your name]
</final_document>

The email is about 190 words. If your agent usually handles your contracts with this publisher, check with them before you send it, or have them send it, so you're not both negotiating at once.
