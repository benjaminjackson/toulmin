<document>
**An Open Letter to [Management Company]**

From the [Building Name] Tenants' Association
[Address] · [Date]

To [Management Company]:

We are writing for the residents of [Building Name], a nine-story building with 112 apartments and one elevator. This year, that elevator has been out of service on 41 days. One outage in August lasted 12 days in a row.

For many of our neighbors, a broken elevator is more than an inconvenience. Twenty-three residents are over 75, and six use wheelchairs or walkers. When the elevator stops, they may be unable to get to medical appointments, buy groceries or visit family. Everyone else has to climb as many as nine floors carrying children, laundry and shopping.

We know the city inspected the elevator in September and gave it a passing certificate. We are not saying the elevator is unsafe when it runs. Our concern is that it too often doesn't run.

In June, you told us a new controller part was on order and would arrive in six to eight weeks. Five months later, you are telling us the same thing. We have had no explanation for the delay and no firm delivery date.

Seventy-four of our 112 households, about two-thirds of the building, have signed a petition asking for a second elevator. You have told us it would cost about $400,000 and can't be done in this building. We take that answer seriously. But residents deserve to see the reasoning behind it and to hear what will be done instead.

We ask you to:

1. Give us the current status of the controller part order in writing, including the supplier, the order date and a confirmed delivery date.
2. Explain what has caused the delay since June.
3. Put an interim plan in place for outages. It should include advance notice to residents and help for people with limited mobility.
4. Share the assessment behind your conclusion that a second elevator is not feasible. Tell us what other options you have considered, such as modernizing or replacing the current elevator.
5. Meet with the association's representatives by [date].

We want to work with you to fix this. But our residents can't keep waiting for a part that is always six to eight weeks away.

Respectfully,

[Name]
Secretary, on behalf of the [Building Name] Tenants' Association

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
