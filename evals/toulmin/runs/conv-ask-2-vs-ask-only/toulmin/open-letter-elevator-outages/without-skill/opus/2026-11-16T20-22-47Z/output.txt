December 1, 2026

**An open letter to Keystone Property Group**
Attention: Diane Weller, Regional Manager

From the Harbor View Apartments Tenants' Association, 220 Grant Street

Dear Ms. Weller,

Harbor View has 9 floors, 112 apartments and one elevator. This year that elevator has been out of service on 41 days. The longest outage lasted 12 days in August.

For many of our neighbors, a broken elevator means they can't leave home. Twenty-three residents are over 75, and six use wheelchairs or walkers. During the August outage, a resident on the 8th floor missed two dialysis appointments.

In June, Keystone told us a new controller part was on order and would arrive in 6 to 8 weeks. We are still hearing that same answer. We have received nothing in writing beyond that estimate.

We know the city inspected the elevator in September and gave it a passing certificate, and we are glad it is safe. But that inspection only checks safety. It doesn't look at how often the elevator breaks down. A safe elevator that isn't running does nothing for a neighbor who needs to get to dialysis.

74 households signed our petition asking for a second elevator. You have told us it would cost about $400,000 and can't be done in this building. We have no engineer's opinion that says otherwise. So today we are asking for three things that can be done:

1. **A written date for the repair.** We also ask for confirmation that the part has actually been ordered.
2. **A stair-climbing chair at Harbor View, with trained staff.** Residents who can't use the stairs need a way to get in and out of the building when the elevator is down.
3. **A rent credit for every day the elevator is out of service.**

We will do our part too. The association will keep a list of residents who need help on the stairs. We will share it with Keystone so your staff know who to check on during an outage or an emergency. We can meet with you on any weekday evening that works for you.

Please reply in writing. We will post your reply in the lobby next to this letter so every resident can read it.

Sincerely,

Rosa Delgado
Secretary, Harbor View Apartments Tenants' Association
On behalf of the 74 households who signed our petition

A few things to check before you post it:

- **The dialysis resident:** The letter doesn't name them or their apartment, but a resident on the 8th floor who needs dialysis may be easy for neighbors to identify. Ask them before it goes up, even if they stay unnamed. If they agree to be named, or to talk to Channel 7, that will be the strongest part of the story.
- **The 74 signatures:** Those households signed a petition for a second elevator, not for these three requests. Consider checking quickly that they're comfortable having their names behind these requests. If Keystone or the reporter asks, you'll have an answer.
- **The last line:** I added the promise to post Keystone's reply in the lobby. It shows good faith and gives them a reason to answer properly. Cut it if the association doesn't want to commit to that.
- **What I left out:** The letter doesn't say the controller part caused the outages, and it doesn't set a reply deadline, since you didn't know or didn't want those. If you later learn what caused most of the 41 days, it's worth adding.
