**An Open Letter to Keystone Property Group**

To: Diane Weller, Regional Manager, Keystone Property Group
From: Harbor View Apartments Tenants' Association, 220 Grant Street

Dear Ms. Weller,

Harbor View Apartments has nine floors, 112 apartments and one elevator. This year, that elevator has been out of service on 41 days. In August, it was out for 12 days in a row.

For many of our neighbors, a broken elevator is far more than an inconvenience. Twenty-three of our residents are over 75, and six use wheelchairs or walkers. During those 12 days in August, a resident on the eighth floor missed two dialysis appointments because they had no way to get down to the street. Dialysis can't be put off until the elevator is fixed.

In June, Keystone told us that a new controller part was on order and would arrive in six to eight weeks. Five months later, we are being told the same thing: six to eight weeks. Residents can't plan medical care, work or groceries around an estimate that keeps starting over.

We know the city inspected the elevator in September and gave it a passing certificate. We don't dispute that. But the city's inspection checks whether the elevator is safe to ride. It doesn't check how often it breaks down, and the breakdowns are what keep our neighbors stuck in their apartments.

Earlier this year, 74 households signed a petition asking for a second elevator. You have told us it would cost about $400,000 and can't be done in this building. We aren't in a position to dispute that, and it isn't what we are asking for here. We are asking for three things that are within Keystone's control now:

1. **A written date for the repair.** We want a committed date from Keystone or your elevator contractor, not another estimate.
2. **A stair-climbing chair kept on site, with staff trained to use it.** Residents who can't walk the stairs need a way in and out of the building whenever the elevator is down.
3. **A rent credit for every day the elevator is out of service.** Tenants are paying for a building they can't fully use.

None of these depends on a part arriving. All of them can start now.

Please respond in writing to the Tenants' Association. We will share your reply with every household in the building.

Sincerely,

Rosa Delgado
Secretary, on behalf of the Harbor View Apartments Tenants' Association

A few things to check before you post or send it:

- **The dialysis resident isn't named.** The letter doesn't name them and only gives their floor. Before this goes to TV, ask them whether they're comfortable with even that much. A reporter will probably want to talk to them, so it's worth asking whether they'd be willing.
- **There's no deadline.** I left it out as you asked. But a reporter's first question will likely be "what happens if they don't respond?" Adding something like "by December 15" would give the story somewhere to go next.
- **"Keystone told us."** Since you're not sure the June promise was in writing, the letter says "told us," not "wrote to us." If someone finds an email or notice, you can make that line more specific.
- **The word count is about 400.**
