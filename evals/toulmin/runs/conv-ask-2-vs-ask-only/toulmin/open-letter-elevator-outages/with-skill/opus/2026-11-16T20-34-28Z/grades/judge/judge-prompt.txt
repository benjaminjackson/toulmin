<document>
December 1, 2026

Keystone Property Group
Attention: Diane Weller, Regional Manager

**An Open Letter from the Harbor View Apartments Tenants' Association**

Dear Ms. Weller,

Harbor View Apartments has nine stories, 112 apartments and one elevator. So far this year that elevator has been out of service on 41 days, about one day in every eight. In August it was out for 12 days in a row.

For many of our neighbours, those days are more than an inconvenience. Twenty-three of our residents are over 75, and six use wheelchairs or walkers. When the elevator stops, they cannot leave their apartments, or cannot get back to them. During the August outage, a resident on the eighth floor missed two dialysis appointments.

You have told us that a new controller part is on order and should arrive in six to eight weeks. You told us the same thing in June.

We know the city inspected the elevator in September and gave it a passing certificate, and we don't dispute it. A passing certificate means the elevator is safe when it runs. It says nothing about how often it runs, and that is the problem.

Seventy-four households, two-thirds of the building, have signed our petition asking for a second elevator. You have told us a second elevator would cost about $400,000 and cannot be built in this building. You may be right. But when residents' health depends on the answer, they should not have to take it on trust.

We ask Keystone Property Group for four things:

1. **A written date** by which the elevator will be repaired.
2. **A stair-climbing chair** kept in the building, with trained staff available during outages, so residents who cannot use the stairs can still get to medical appointments and back home.
3. **A rent credit** for every day the elevator is out of service. Residents should not pay full rent for days they cannot get to or from their homes.
4. **A feasibility study** for a second elevator, with the findings shared with tenants.

If nothing changes, the next outage will find the same residents in the same position as in August: stuck upstairs, missing the care they depend on.

We are ready to meet with you to work through any of these requests, and we ask that you reply to the association in writing.

Sincerely,

Harbor View Apartments Tenants' Association
220 Grant Street

Rosa Delgado, Secretary

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
