Subject: Quarterly update: flat quarter, a big customer loss, and a new product launching Dec 1

Hi all,

First, an apology. This is your first update in six months. The numbers were flat and I didn't want to send bad news. That was the wrong call, because you should hear bad news as soon as I have it. From now on you'll get an update in the first week of every quarter, with the next one on January 7. I'll also send a one-page set of numbers every month to anyone who wants it. Just reply and ask.

**The numbers**
- Monthly recurring revenue: $41,000, the same as three months ago
- Clinics: 9 added, 4 lost
- Cash: $480,000 in the bank. We lose about $44,000 a month, which leaves about 11 months at the current rate.
- Team: 6 people

**What happened**
Revenue is flat because the new clinics only made up for the ones we lost. The biggest loss was our largest customer, a 12-clinic chain paying $5,500 a month. They moved to the scheduler built into their electronic records system. That is the main risk to our business. When a clinic's records system has a scheduler that's good enough, we're competing with something they already pay for.

This may not be the last such loss. Two other customers use the same records system, and together they pay $3,800 a month. I can't promise they'll stay.

**What's working**
Clinics that use our automatic text reminders have a no-show rate of 7%, down from 13% before they started. A no-show is an appointment slot the clinic can't bill for. Reminders are where we show the clearest results.

**What's next**
On December 1 we start selling the reminder feature on its own for $150 a month. A clinic can buy it without replacing its scheduler. Six clinics have already said they'll buy it at launch. I don't know yet who the main buyer will be: new clinics, or clinics like the chain we lost. The January update will report what we learn.

**Two asks**
1. **A 20-minute phone call with each of you.** I'd like to walk you through the reminder launch and the risk from records-system schedulers, and hear what you think. Please reply with a few times that work for you.
2. **Introductions to clinic chains.** If you know someone who owns or runs a physical therapy chain, an introduction would help a great deal.

I know this isn't the update any of us wanted. You'll hear from me on January 7, whatever the numbers say.

Thanks,
[Your name]

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
