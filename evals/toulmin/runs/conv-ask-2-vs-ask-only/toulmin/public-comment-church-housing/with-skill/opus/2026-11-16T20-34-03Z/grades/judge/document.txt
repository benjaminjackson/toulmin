Good evening, Commissioners. My name is Margaret Shaw. I've lived on Alder Road for nineteen years, two blocks from St. Luke's Lutheran. I'm also a member of St. Luke's, and I want you to know that up front.

I'm asking you to approve the permit for the 64 apartments on St. Luke's back lot, with a traffic signal at Alder and 4th as a condition of approval.

Your own housing report says this county is about 2,100 affordable homes short. Right now, 412 households are on the housing authority's waiting list, and the average wait is three years. Every one of these 64 apartments would rent to a household earning under 60 percent of the area median. That's a home for about one in six of the families on that list.

And it would go on land that is already paved and sits empty six days a week. You won't find a site that asks less of a neighborhood than a parking lot that's already there.

I know the worry about traffic, because I live with it. Some mornings around eight, cars back up past my house. The traffic study says this project would add about 310 trips a day on Alder, roughly five per household. It says that without changes, Alder and 4th would drop from a C to a D at rush hour. That's real, and I won't stand here and promise you Alder Road will never get busier.

Construction will take about twenty months, and there will be trucks on Alder Road. The church has promised to set up a neighbor group for that period, and I've committed to serving on it.

If you say no, the lot stays empty six days a week and those 412 households keep waiting. If you say yes, with the signal as a condition, 64 of them get a home and Alder and 4th keeps the C rating it has today.
