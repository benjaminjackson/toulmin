Subject: Maple Street and Route 9 branches closing March 31

Team,

I'm writing to share a decision that affects our Maple Street and Route 9 branches and the 14 people who work there.

On March 31, we will close both branches. Every person who works at them will be offered a job in a new member phone and chat center at our main office on Elm Avenue, at the same pay or higher.

**Why we're doing this**

The way our members reach us has changed. Over the last three years, visits to Maple Street and Route 9 fell 44%. Across the credit union, phone and chat contacts rose 60%. We haven't kept up, and members calling us now wait an average of 9 minutes on hold. The center puts experienced people where members are actually trying to reach us.

**What the new role looks like**

- Pay starts $1.50 an hour above current teller pay.
- The center is open 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. on weekdays. Shifts will rotate. People with the most years here choose shifts first, but I can't promise anyone a 9-to-5 schedule.
- After 90 days of training, you can work from home two days a week.

I know the change in hours is a real cost for some of you, especially if your life is built around a 9-to-5 day. I'd rather say that plainly than pretend otherwise.

**What happens next**

- Written offers go out by February 14.
- Decisions are due March 1.
- If you'd like a one-on-one with HR to talk through your situation, you'll get one. Just ask HR.
- If you decide not to take the new role, you'll receive two weeks of pay for every year you've worked here.
- If you take the new role and find within six months that it isn't right for you, you can still leave with that same severance.

We'll share the center's opening date and training schedule as soon as they're set.

To everyone at Maple Street and Route 9: thank you. Many of our members have known you by name for years, and that knowledge is exactly what they'll need on the other end of the line. To everyone else: you'll be working alongside these colleagues in new ways, and I'm asking you to help make this move a good one.

[Your name]
Chief Operating Officer

A few notes:

- **Tell the 14 first if you can.** Ideally their managers or you tell them in person before this email goes out. If that happens, you could add a line near the top like "As many of you heard from your managers today…".
- **The claim that it makes everyone's job better is gone.** In its place, the email names the specific gains: higher pay, working from home, and shorter waits for members. It also admits the cost of the new hours. Staff are more likely to believe the good parts when the email is honest about the hard part.
- **Fill in later:** the center's opening date and training start, and an HR contact name or email if you want something more specific than "ask HR."
