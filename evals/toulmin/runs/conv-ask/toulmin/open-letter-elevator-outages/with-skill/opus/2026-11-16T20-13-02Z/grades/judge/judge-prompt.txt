**Open Letter to Keystone Property Group**

To: Diane Weller, Regional Manager
From: The residents of Harbor View Apartments, 220 Grant Street
December 1, 2026

Dear Ms. Weller,

Harbor View has 112 apartments on nine floors and one elevator. This year that elevator has been out of service on 41 days, about one day in every eight. One outage in August lasted 12 days.

For many of us, a broken elevator is an inconvenience. For our 23 neighbors over the age of 75, and the 6 who use wheelchairs or walkers, it means being cut off from the street. During the August outage, a resident on the eighth floor missed two dialysis appointments. Dialysis is not something anyone can safely skip.

In June, Keystone told us a new controller part was on order and would arrive in six to eight weeks, so it should have been here by mid-August. It wasn't, and the 12-day outage came instead. Now we are being told the same thing again: six to eight weeks.

We know the city inspected the elevator in September and issued a passing certificate. We are not saying the elevator is unsafe. We are saying it does not run reliably, and a certificate does not carry anyone up eight flights of stairs.

We are asking Keystone for four things:

1. A written date by which the new controller part will be installed.
2. A stair-climbing chair kept in the building, and staff trained to use it, for residents who cannot walk the stairs during an outage.
3. A rent credit for every day the elevator is out of service.
4. An independent engineer's study of whether a second elevator can be added to this building.

On that last point: 74 of our 112 households, two-thirds of the building, signed a petition asking for a second elevator. Keystone has told us it would cost about $400,000 and cannot be done here. You may be right. An independent study would settle the question either way, and residents would accept a clear answer.

For our part, the association will keep a list of residents who need help on the stairs and share it with Keystone for use in emergencies. We will meet with you on any weekday evening that suits you.

Without a firm repair date, nothing tells us next year will be any different from this one: another promise of six to eight weeks, and our most vulnerable neighbors waiting in their apartments for the elevator to come back.

Sincerely,

Rosa Delgado
Secretary, on behalf of the tenants' association of Harbor View Apartments

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
