<document>
**An Open Letter to Keystone Property Group**
Attention: Diane Weller, Regional Manager
From the tenants of Harbor View Apartments, 220 Grant Street
December 1, 2026

Harbor View has nine floors, 112 apartments and one elevator. So far this year that elevator has been out of service on 41 days. In August it was out for 12 days in a row.

For most of us, that means a long climb. For some of our neighbors, it means not being able to leave home at all. Twenty-three residents are over 75, and six use wheelchairs or walkers. During the August outage, a resident on the eighth floor missed two dialysis appointments. Dialysis can't wait until the elevator comes back.

In June, Keystone told us a new controller part was on order and would arrive in six to eight weeks. Now we are being told the same thing: six to eight weeks. Five months after that first promise, there is still no repair and no date.

We know the elevator passed its city inspection in September. That inspection checks whether the elevator is safe to ride. It does not look at how often it breaks down. A safe elevator that isn't running does nothing for a resident on the eighth floor.

Seventy-four households signed our petition for a second elevator. Keystone has told us that would cost about $400,000 and can't be done in this building. We are not asking you to settle that question today. We are asking for three things that can be done now:

1. **A written date for the controller repair.** We need a date Keystone will commit to in writing, not another estimate in weeks.
2. **A stair-climbing chair kept in the building, with staff trained to use it**, so residents who can't walk the stairs can still get to medical appointments and get out in an emergency while the elevator is down.
3. **A rent credit for every day the elevator is out of service.** Residents pay rent for a building with an elevator. On those days, they don't have one.

We will do our part. The association will keep a list of residents who need help on the stairs and share it with Keystone for use in emergencies. We can meet with you on any weekday evening.

Each time the elevator stops, our oldest and least mobile neighbors are stuck in their apartments until it runs again. We ask Keystone to reply in writing and to meet with us so we can agree on these steps.

Rosa Delgado
Secretary, on behalf of the tenants' association of Harbor View Apartments
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
