<document>
**An Open Letter to [Property Management Company]**

From the [Building Name] Tenants' Association

Our building has 9 stories, 112 apartments and one elevator. This year that elevator has been out of service on 41 days. One of those outages lasted 12 days in a row, in August.

When the only elevator stops, getting home becomes a nine-story climb. For many of our neighbors, that isn't just inconvenient. Twenty-three of our residents are over 75, and six use wheelchairs or walkers. During an outage, some of them can't leave their apartments at all. They miss medical appointments, can't get groceries, and can't get out in an emergency without help. Other neighbors carry children, strollers and shopping up and down the stairs.

We know the city inspected the elevator in September and issued a passing certificate. We don't dispute that the elevator meets code. Our concern is that it doesn't work reliably, and a certificate doesn't change the fact that it has failed on 41 days this year.

You have told us that a new controller part is on order and should arrive in 6 to 8 weeks. You told us the same thing in June. We need more than a repeated estimate.

We have also asked for a long-term solution. Seventy-four households signed our petition asking for a second elevator. You have told us this would cost about $400,000 and can't be done in this building. If that's the case, residents deserve to see the reasons and to hear what you will do instead.

We are asking [Property Management Company] to:

1. **Give us a firm, written timeline** for the controller repair, including the supplier's confirmed delivery date.
2. **Notify residents promptly** whenever the elevator goes out of service, along with an expected repair date.
3. **Put an outage plan in place** for residents with limited mobility, including help with groceries, medications and getting to appointments while the elevator is down.
4. **Share in writing the assessment** behind your conclusion that a second elevator isn't feasible, and tell us what alternatives you have considered.
5. **Meet with the Tenants' Association** within 30 days to discuss these points.

We want to work with you on this. We pay our rent and we take care of our homes. We're asking for a building where every resident, at every age and ability, can reliably get in and out of their own front door.

Sincerely,

[Name]
Secretary, on behalf of the [Building Name] Tenants' Association
[Contact information]
[Date]

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
