**The last seven weeks**

Since the position opened, every front desk shift has been covered, and overtime has come in under the monthly plan. That wasn't a trial run. It was the job, and you've seen how it has gone.

**What I've built in two years as assistant manager**

- **A team that stays.** In the year before I became assistant manager, 9 of our 14 front desk staff left. Last year, 5 did. Turnover went from 64% to 36%.
- **Faster training.** I rewrote the front desk training. New hires now work the desk alone after 5 shifts instead of 9, which cuts nearly half the time before they can carry a shift.
- **Better check-ins.** Our check-in score on the main review site rose from 7.9 to 8.6 over the last 18 months. The renovation helped, because guests notice a new lobby. But check-in is the part of the stay the front desk controls, and the score had already risen from 7.9 to 8.2 in the five months before the renovation began.

**The budget**

I haven't managed a budget, and this role owns a $1.1 million payroll. Here is how I will close that gap:

- I will complete the company's finance course within my first three months.
- I will meet weekly with Ben Oduya on the payroll budget. He has already offered to work with me.

The payroll budget is mostly scheduling and overtime. Those are the decisions I've been making for the last seven weeks, and overtime has stayed under plan.

**Why me**

I've worked at this hotel for six years. The team I would manage is the team I trained, using training I wrote. Someone from outside would first have to learn the hotel, the staff and the systems already in place, and the desk would be in transition again while they did. I can keep it running the way it has run for the past seven weeks, and keep improving it.

I'd welcome the chance to talk this through with you before you decide.
