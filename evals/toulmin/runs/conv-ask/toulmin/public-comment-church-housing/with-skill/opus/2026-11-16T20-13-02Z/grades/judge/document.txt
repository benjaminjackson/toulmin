But I'm speaking tonight as a neighbor, someone who lives with the traffic on Alder Road and will keep living with it after this is built.

I'm asking you to approve the permit, with the traffic signal at Alder and 4th as a condition of approval.

Start with the need. This county's own housing report says we are short about 2,100 affordable homes. The housing authority has 412 households on its waiting list, and the average wait is three years. Every one of these 64 apartments would rent to a household earning under 60 percent of the area median income. That's homes for about one in six of the households on that list.

But the builder, Cascade Housing Partners, a nonprofit, has offered to pay for a traffic signal at that intersection. The same traffic study says that with the signal, the intersection stays at a C. So the problem we're worried about has a fix, and the builder is paying for it. That's why I'm asking you to make the signal a condition of the permit, so it's a requirement and not only a promise.

On parking, the plan has 72 spaces for 64 apartments, which is more than one per unit.

If this permit is denied, the lot stays empty six days a week, and those 412 households keep waiting. Sixty-four of them could have a home here, two blocks from my house.

I'd be glad to have them as neighbors. Please approve the permit, with the traffic signal as a condition. Thank you.
