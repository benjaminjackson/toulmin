**Subject: Cadence Scheduling: quarterly update, and an apology**

Hi all,

First, an apology. You haven't heard from me in two quarters. I skipped those updates because revenue was flat and I didn't want to send bad news. That was the wrong call, because bad news is what you most need to hear quickly. From now on you'll get an update in the first week of every quarter, starting January 7. If you'd also like a one-page set of numbers every month, reply and I'll send it.

**The numbers**
- Monthly recurring revenue: $41,000, the same as three months ago
- Customers: added 9 clinics, lost 4 customers
- Cash: $480,000. We lose about $44,000 a month, which gives us about 11 months of runway, into October 2027.
- Team: 6

**What happened**
Our largest customer, a 12-clinic chain paying $5,500 a month, left for the scheduler built into their electronic records system. That one loss is why revenue is flat. The rest of the business went from about $35,500 to $41,000 this quarter, growth of roughly 15%.

The risk isn't over. Two other customers use the same records system and pay $3,800 a month between them. I can't promise they won't leave the same way. If they do, we'd lose about $48,000 a month and runway would drop to about 10 months.

**What we're doing about it**
Our scheduler competes with the records systems. Our reminders don't. Clinics that use our automatic text reminders have a no-show rate of 7%, against 13% before they started. A built-in scheduler doesn't give a clinic that drop, and every missed visit is lost revenue.

So on December 1 we start selling reminders on their own for $150 a month, to clinics that keep whatever scheduler they already have. That includes clinics on the records system that cost us the chain. Six clinics have told us they'll buy at launch. That's $900 a month, which is small next to what we lost. But it's the first test of whether we can sell to clinics we couldn't reach before. In the January 7 update I'll report how many of the six actually signed and how many more clinics we're talking to.

I haven't set a target for the reminder product yet. I also don't have a plan for what we do if it doesn't take off. I'd like to work through both with you, which brings me to the ask.

**What I'm asking**
1. **20 minutes on the phone with each of you.** I want your view on the reminder product, on what a sensible target would be, and on what we should do if we miss it. Reply with a time that works and I'll call you.
2. **Intros to clinic chains.** Chains whose records system already has a scheduler are the best fit, because the reminder product is built for them.

Thanks for your patience over the last six months. I'll be on time from here.

Sam Patel
Founder, Cadence Scheduling

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
