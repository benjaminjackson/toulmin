Dear Admissions Committee,

I am writing to recommend Tomás Reyes for your two-year agricultural technology program. I am the farm manager at Hollis Dairy, a 400-cow operation. Tomás has worked for me for three years, since he was 19. He is one of the most dependable people on my crew, and he already uses the kind of technology your program teaches.

Five days a week, Tomás runs our 3 a.m. milking shift by himself. At that hour there is no one else to check the work. The cows, the equipment and the start of our whole day depend on him getting it right, and he does. In three years he has missed two shifts.

He also learned our herd management software, and he now uses it to flag sick cows for our veterinarian. Last winter he caught nine cases of mastitis early. Our vet estimates that catching them early saved the farm about $8,000. That is the work of someone who understands both the animals and the data, and I think it is exactly the kind of student your program is built for.

Last summer his father became ill in Mexico, and Tomás was away for five weeks. He gave me two days' notice and arranged for his cousin, a former employee of ours, to cover his shifts. He came back on the day he said he would.

His written English is weaker than his spoken English. He once failed the written test in a dairy safety course and passed it on his second try. He is now taking a night class in English while still working the early shift. He became the person on our farm who reads the herd data and spots trouble before anyone else does, and I expect him to work at his writing the same way.

While he is in the program, I will keep a part-time weekend job open for him at Hollis Dairy.

I recommend Tomás without reservation. If the committee would like to talk with me, please call me at 555-0187.

Sincerely,

Mike Hollis
Farm Manager
Hollis Dairy
555-0187

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
