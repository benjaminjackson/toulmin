Subject: Cadence Scheduling quarterly update, and why you haven't heard from me

Hi all,

You haven't had an update from me in two quarters. I skipped them because the numbers were flat and I didn't want to send bad news. That was the wrong call. From now on you'll get an update in the first week of every quarter, starting January 7. I'll also send a one-page set of numbers every month to any of you who asks for it.

**The numbers**
- Monthly recurring revenue: $41,000, the same as three months ago
- Clinics: 9 added, 4 lost
- Cash: $480,000. We lose about $44,000 a month, which gives us about 11 months of runway.

**What's under the flat number**
One of the four clinics we lost was our largest customer, a 12-clinic chain paying $5,500 a month. They moved to the scheduler built into their electronic records system. Leaving them out, revenue from the rest of our customers grew from about $35,500 to $41,000 this quarter. That's roughly 15%, after covering the other three losses. I don't know why those three left.

**The risk**
Losing the chain wasn't a one-off. Two other customers use the same records system, and together they pay $3,800 a month. I can't promise they won't leave the same way. If they do, monthly revenue falls to $37,200.

**What we're doing**
Text reminders are our clearest result. Clinics using our automatic reminders have a 7% no-show rate, down from 13% before they started. On December 1 we start selling reminders on their own for $150 a month. Six clinics have said they'll buy when it launches. That's $900 a month, which is small next to the $3,800 at risk. It is also the first thing we sell that doesn't ask a clinic to replace its scheduler. One thing I don't know yet is whether standalone reminders work alongside the schedulers built into records systems. The answer decides whether this product protects us from what happened with the chain or just adds revenue.

**Runway**
I haven't decided whether we raise, cut costs or aim for break-even, and I don't have a timeline for that decision yet. I'd rather tell you that plainly than put a plan in front of you that I haven't made.

**What I'm asking for**
1. Twenty minutes on the phone with each of you. Reply to this email and I'll find a time.
2. Introductions to physical therapy clinic chains. Chains are where we grow fastest, and as this quarter showed, they're also where we can lose the most at once.

Thank you for your patience over the last six months.

Sam Patel
Founder, Cadence Scheduling

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
