<final_document>
Subject: Fee for the next book

Dear Nadia,

Thank you for offering me the next book. I've loved making six picture books with you over the past four years, and I'd very much like to do this one.

Before we sign, I need to raise the fee. I've been paid $9,000 a book since my first one with you, and the books have done well since then. Two have sold over 20,000 copies, and the one on the state reading list went into its fourth printing this spring.

The market has moved too. Last year another publisher paid me $12,500 for a book of the same length. My agent tells me $11,000 to $13,000 is the standard range for an illustrator with my record, so $9,000 is now below the bottom of it.

I'm asking for $11,500, which is still less than that publisher paid. In return, I can commit to:

- sketches within six weeks of signing
- final art by September 1
- two extra spot drawings for your marketing, at no charge

I have to be honest that I can't take this book on at $9,000. Each book is about four months of work, and at that fee it no longer works for me. I'd much rather keep making books with you, and I hope we can agree on $11,500.

Warm regards,
Elena Varga
</final_document>

**The $12,000 point:** I couldn't find a true way to say that every other publisher pays at least $12,000. The strongest true versions are that $9,000 is now below the bottom of your agent's range, and that your $11,500 ask is still less than the $12,500 the other publisher paid. Together they make your current fee look clearly out of date, and Nadia can't dispute either one.

**What I left out on purpose:**
- **Your $11,000 floor.** If Nadia makes a counteroffer, you'll know where you stand, and she won't have that number to start from.
- **The gap in your schedule.** Telling her you have no other offer for that slot would make your "I can't take this on at $9,000" less convincing.

If you find the titles and the name of the state reading list before you send it, add them to the second paragraph. Naming them makes the sales point stronger.
