# Bookkeeping for hair and nail salons, from someone who ran one

I'm Carla Benson. Before I became a bookkeeper, I managed a hair salon for six years. Now I work only with hair and nail salons, and I have 31 of them as clients.

**For $199 a month, you get:**

- Monthly books, closed by the 10th of the following month
- Payroll for up to 10 staff
- Sales tax filing

## Clean books cost less at tax time

I don't do income tax returns. Your accountant or tax preparer keeps doing those. What changes is what they get from me: books that are already right.

Last tax season, 9 of my clients told me their accountant charged them less because their books came in clean. One of them saved $800.

## Salon owners stay

Two clients left last year, and both had sold their salons.

In January I raised my price from $149 to $199 for every client, including the ones who'd been with me for years. I raised it because I added payroll for up to 10 staff, which I didn't offer before. Nobody left over the new price.

## If I'm late, you don't pay

I missed the 10th three times last year. Since January, any month I miss it is free.

## It's just me

When you call, you get me every time. I take calls Tuesday to Saturday, but not on Sundays or Mondays.

Because there's only one of me, I can take on about five new clients this year.

## No contract

If it isn't working for you, give me 30 days' notice and stop.

## Start with a 20-minute call

The first call is free. Tell me about your salon, and we'll see whether I'm the right fit.

**[ Book your free 20-minute call ]**

Carla Benson
Chair and Ledger

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
