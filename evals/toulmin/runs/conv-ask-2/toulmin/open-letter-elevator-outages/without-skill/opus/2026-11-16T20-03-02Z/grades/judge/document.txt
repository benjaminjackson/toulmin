**An Open Letter to [Management Company]**

From the [Name] Tenants' Association, [Building Address]
[Date]

To the management of [Management Company]:

Our building has nine stories, 112 apartments and one elevator. This year, that elevator has been out of service on 41 days, including 12 days in a row in August.

For some residents, a broken elevator is an inconvenience. For others, it means being stuck at home. Twenty-three of our neighbors are over 75, and six use wheelchairs or walkers. When the elevator is down, getting to a doctor's appointment, buying groceries or leaving the building at all can become hard or impossible for them. In an emergency, getting them out safely is a serious concern.

In June, you told us a new controller part was on order and would arrive in six to eight weeks. Now you are telling us the same thing. We would like to know what happened to the first order and why we should expect a different result this time.

We know the city inspected the elevator in September and issued a passing certificate. We're glad it met the standard on the day it was inspected. But a certificate doesn't tell residents whether the elevator will be running tomorrow morning. Our problem is reliability, and 41 days of outages speak for themselves.

Seventy-four households, about two-thirds of the building, signed a petition asking for a second elevator. You have told us this would cost about $400,000 and can't be done in this building. We take that seriously, but we have not seen the reasoning behind it, and residents deserve to.

We are asking for the following:

1. **A written repair timeline** that includes when the controller part was ordered, who is supplying it and when it is expected, with updates every two weeks until the work is finished.
2. **A plan for residents with limited mobility during outages**, including advance notice whenever possible, help with groceries and medical trips, and a contact person who answers.
3. **A copy of any engineering or feasibility assessment** behind the conclusion that a second elevator can't be installed. If no such assessment exists, we ask you to commit to commissioning one.
4. **A meeting with the association** within 30 days.

We don't want a fight. We want what you should want too: a building where every resident can get home.

Please respond in writing to [contact name/email] by [date].

Sincerely,

[Name], Secretary
On behalf of the [Name] Tenants' Association
