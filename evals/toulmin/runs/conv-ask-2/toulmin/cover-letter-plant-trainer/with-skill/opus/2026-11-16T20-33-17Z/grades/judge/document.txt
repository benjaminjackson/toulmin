I am applying for the Training Coordinator position at Calder's Westfield plant. For nine years I have taught high school chemistry. Over that time, much of my work has been the same as the work in this role: designing training that gets people working safely, checking whether it worked, and keeping records that hold up under audit.

In 2019 I wrote my school's lab safety program. Since then, no one has been hurt in our labs badly enough to need more than first aid. Every student passes a lab safety quiz before working at the bench, and I keep a signed record of each one. The state has audited our lab safety records twice and found nothing to correct.

Every year I also train our new science teachers on lab procedures, about two a year. In 2020 I rebuilt that training and cut it from 20 hours to 12 without leaving out any procedures. I keep a signed training record for every teacher who completes it. I would bring the same approach to Westfield. I'd find out where new hires actually struggle, keep everything the regulations require, and shorten what doesn't need to be there.
