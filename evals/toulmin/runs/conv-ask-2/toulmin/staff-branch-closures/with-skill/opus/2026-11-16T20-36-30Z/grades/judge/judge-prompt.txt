Subject: Closing Maple Street and Route 9, and opening our member phone and chat center

To all staff,

On March 31 we will close our Maple Street and Route 9 branches. All 14 people who work at those two branches will be offered a job in our new member phone and chat center at the main office on Elm Avenue. No one loses their job, and no one moving to the center will be paid less than they are today.

**Why we're making this change**

Over the last three years, visits to these two branches fell 44%. Over the same period, phone and chat contacts across the credit union rose 60%. Right now a member who calls us waits an average of 9 minutes on hold. Our members have changed how they reach us, and we need to be there when they do. Keeping both branches open is not an option, and this decision is final.

**What the new role offers**

Pay in the center starts at $1.50 an hour above current teller pay. After 90 days of training, you can work from home two days a week.

**What it asks of you**

The center is open 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. on weekdays, and shifts rotate. The branches kept 9-to-5 hours, and I can't promise anyone those hours in the new role. Shift choice goes by seniority, so those with the most years here pick first. This is a real change for anyone whose life is built around a 5 o'clock finish, and I won't pretend it isn't.

Many of you also know that our current phone team lost about 30% of its people last year. I'm not going to brush that aside. So I've made sure the decision isn't final on day one. If you take the role and decide within six months that it isn't for you, you can still leave with full severance.

**If you choose not to take the role**

Anyone who declines will receive two weeks of pay for every year they've worked here.

**What happens next**

- Written offers go out by February 14.
- Decisions are due by March 1.
- Anyone who wants a one-on-one with HR to talk it through can have one.

To the Maple Street and Route 9 teams: you have served those neighborhoods well, and that work is the reason we want every one of you to stay. I would rather you ask me a hard question now than wonder about it later.

Rachel Greene
COO, Northfield Community Credit Union

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.
