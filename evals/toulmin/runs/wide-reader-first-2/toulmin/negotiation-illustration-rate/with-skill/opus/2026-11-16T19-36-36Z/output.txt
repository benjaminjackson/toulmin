I've left out the line saying every other publisher pays at least $12,000. Your facts don't support it. You have one publisher that paid $12,500, and your agent's range starts at $11,000, which is below $12,000. Publishers know what their competitors pay, so if your editor catches one overstatement, they may doubt everything else in the email. The true version still makes the point: you're asking for less than another publisher paid you last year. If you know of other publishers paying $12,000 or more, you could name them, and that would make the case stronger.

The email covers what you've done for them first and gives the number after. That way your editor has reasons they can pass on if someone above them has to approve the fee.

It's about 190 words. Fill in the bracketed name and title before sending.

Subject: [Book title]: fee for the next book

Hi [Editor's name],

Thank you for offering me [Book title]. I'd love to illustrate it, and after six books together I'm glad to keep working with you.

Before I sign, I'd like to talk about the fee. I've been at $9,000 per book since my first book with you four years ago. Since then, two of our books have sold more than 20,000 copies, and one was on a state reading list.

Last year another publisher paid me $12,500 for a 32-page book of the same length. My agent tells me $11,000 to $13,000 is the usual range for an illustrator with my record. Each book takes me about four months, so $9,000 works out to $2,250 a month.

For [Book title], I'm asking for $11,500. That's less than I was paid elsewhere last year, and I've set it there because I value working with you and your team.

If that works for you, I'm ready to go ahead. If it would help to talk it through, I'm happy to get on a call.

Best,
[Your name]
