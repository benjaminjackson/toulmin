The brief between the <brief> tags is what the writer was asked to do and shows the facts the writer had. You, as the reader, have not seen the brief. Use it only to check what the document does with those facts.

The five members of the county planning commission, who vote tonight on the permit, and the 60 or so neighbours in the room. Most of the neighbours oppose the project, and two commissioners have said at earlier hearings that traffic on Alder Road is their main concern.

<brief>
I'm going to speak at our county planning commission tonight in favor of a housing project, and I want help writing what I'll say. The church I belong to wants to build 64 apartments on its back parking lot. All of them would rent to households earning under 60% of the area median income.

Facts:
- The church lot is 1.8 acres and sits empty 6 days a week.
- The county's own housing report says we are short about 2,100 affordable homes.
- The traffic study estimates 310 more car trips a day on Alder Road, and says the intersection at Alder and 4th would drop from a C rating to a D at rush hour.
- The plan has 72 parking spaces for the 64 units.
- 412 households are on the county housing authority's waiting list. The average wait is 3 years.
- I've lived on Alder Road for 19 years, two blocks from the church.

Each speaker gets 3 minutes.
</brief>



<document>
Good evening, Commissioners. My name is ______.

I've lived on Alder Road for 19 years, two blocks from the church. I'm also a member of that church, so I'll be upfront: I'm not neutral. I'm here to ask you to approve the 64 apartments on its back lot.

I want to start with traffic. That's what my neighbors are worried about, and it's my street too.

The traffic study says this project adds 310 car trips a day on Alder Road. It says the intersection at Alder and 4th drops from a C to a D at rush hour. I'm not going to pretend that's nothing. I drive through that intersection. I'll be sitting in that line too.

So let me tell you what we get in return.

The county's own housing report says we are short about 2,100 affordable homes.

Right now, 412 households are on the housing authority's waiting list.

The average wait is three years.

Three years of waiting for a place they can afford.

Every one of these 64 apartments would rent to a household earning under 60 percent of the area median income. That's enough homes for about one in six of the households on that list.

And it doesn't take a single acre of open land. It goes on a 1.8-acre parking lot. Six days a week, that lot sits empty.

On parking: the plan has 72 spaces for 64 apartments. That's a space for every home, plus eight more.

So here is the trade, as plainly as I can put it. One intersection goes from a C to a D at rush hour. In return, 64 households get a home they can afford, on land that's already paved.

I've been a neighbor on this street for 19 years. I'd be glad to have 64 more.

Please vote to approve this project. Thank you.
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
