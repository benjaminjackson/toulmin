Good evening, Commissioners. My name is [your name]. I've lived on Alder Road for 19 years, two blocks from [church name]. I'm also a member of that church, so you should know up front that I'm not neutral.

I'm here to ask you to vote yes on the 64 apartments on our back parking lot.

I want to start with traffic, because it's my street.

The traffic study says this project adds 310 car trips a day on Alder Road. It says the intersection at Alder and 4th drops from a C to a D at rush hour. That's real. I drive through that intersection. I'll be one of the people waiting a little longer at that light.

And I'm still asking you to approve this.

Here's why.

The county's own housing report says we are short about 2,100 affordable homes. Right now, 412 households are on the housing authority's waiting list. The average wait is three years.

Every one of these 64 apartments would rent to a household earning under 60 percent of the area median income. That's enough homes for nearly one in six of the households on that list.

Look at where they'd go. The lot is 1.8 acres of asphalt. It sits empty six days a week. Nothing gets torn down. No one gets moved out. We'd be putting homes on land that right now does nothing most of the week.

I know some of my neighbors are worried about cars parked up and down our street. The plan has 72 parking spaces for 64 apartments. That's more than one space for every home.

So here's how I weigh it, as someone who lives two blocks away. On one side, I wait a bit longer at a light at rush hour. On the other side, 64 households get a home they can afford, in a county that's short 2,100 of them.

For me, that isn't close.

Please vote yes. Thank you.
