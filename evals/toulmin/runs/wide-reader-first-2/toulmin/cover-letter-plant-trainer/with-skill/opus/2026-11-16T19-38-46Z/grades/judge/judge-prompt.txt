Dear Hiring Manager,

I am applying for the Training Coordinator position. I have taught high school chemistry for nine years. The part of my work that fits your plant most closely is teaching people to follow procedures exactly, every time, where a skipped step can hurt someone.

In 2019 I wrote my school's lab safety program. In the seven years since, our labs have had no injury that needed more than first aid. These are labs where teenagers handle chemicals, many of them for the first time. I teach about 140 students a year across five classes, and the program works because it doesn't depend on anyone remembering the rules. It's written down, taught and enforced the same way every time.

I also train adults. Every year I give each new science teacher in our department about 12 hours of training on lab procedures, usually two new teachers a year. They arrive with degrees in their subject but don't yet know our procedures, and they need to be safe and working on their own in the lab quickly. That is close to what your new hires need on the floor.

I also check whether my teaching is working. In the four years I've taught AP Chemistry, our pass rate has risen from 41% to 58%.

I haven't worked in a regulated industry. I haven't kept GMP training records, and I've used Google Classroom rather than the learning management system you use. Still, the discipline GMP depends on is the same one my safety program runs on: written procedures, followed exactly, backed by training that holds up. I would need to learn the record-keeping rules and the software. The habit I already have.

I live about 30 minutes from the plant. I'd welcome the chance to hear how you train new hires now and to talk about where that training could be faster or stronger.

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
