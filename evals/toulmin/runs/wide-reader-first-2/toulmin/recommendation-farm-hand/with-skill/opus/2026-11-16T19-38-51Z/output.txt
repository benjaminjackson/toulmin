I recommend leaving out the two days' notice before his five-week absence, but not the absence itself. The college has no reason to ask about the short notice, and his father's illness explains it. If you wrote "he has missed only two shifts in three years" without mentioning the five weeks, though, the claim would be misleading. So the letter states the absence once, plainly, with the reason.

The letter also mentions his written English, because the admissions committee will probably see it in his application essay. It sits next to the night class he's taking, so it reads as something he's already working on.

Before you send it:
- **Fill in the brackets.** Add Tomás's last name, the college name, and your own name, farm and contact details.
- **Mastitis outcome:** if you know what catching those nine cases early saved (treatment costs, discarded milk, cows kept in the herd), one sentence with that figure would make the strongest paragraph stronger.
