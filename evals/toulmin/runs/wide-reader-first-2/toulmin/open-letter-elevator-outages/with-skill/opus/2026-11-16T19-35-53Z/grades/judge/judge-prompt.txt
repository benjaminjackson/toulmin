<document>
**An Open Letter to [Management Company]
from the [Building Name] Tenants' Association**

Our building has nine floors, 112 apartments and one elevator. This year that elevator has been out of service on 41 days. That's about one day in every eight. The longest outage lasted 12 days, in August.

We know the city inspected the elevator in September and gave it a passing certificate. We're glad it is safe. But that certificate doesn't tell us whether the elevator will be running tomorrow, and that's the problem we live with.

Twenty-three of our neighbours are over 75. Six use wheelchairs or walkers. For a resident in a wheelchair on an upper floor, a broken elevator means being unable to get downstairs or out of the building. For many older residents it means choosing between nine flights of stairs and staying home.

In June, you told us a new controller part was on order and would arrive in six to eight weeks. Those eight weeks ended in August, the month of our longest outage. Now you've told us the same thing again: six to eight weeks. We'd like to believe it. To do that, we need more than the same promise.

Seventy-four households, about two-thirds of the building, signed our petition asking for a second elevator. You told us it would cost about $400,000 and can't be done in this building. We've heard that answer. We're not asking you to change it today. We're asking you to show residents how you reached it.

**What we are asking for**

Please meet with the association's officers and bring these in writing:

1. **The controller part:** when the order was placed, which supplier it's with, and the delivery date that supplier has confirmed.
2. **The next outage:** what you'll do for residents who can't use the stairs the next time the elevator stops.
3. **A second elevator:** the assessment that found one can't be built here.

We're ready to meet whenever you can. Please contact the association at [contact details].

This letter is posted in our lobby so every resident can read it. We've also sent it to [TV station], because our neighbours aren't the only building in this city that depends on one elevator.

Sincerely,

[Name]
Secretary, on behalf of the [Building Name] Tenants' Association
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
