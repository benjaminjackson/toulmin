Commissioners, I'm Captain Ray Dunn. I've run the Sea Lark out of [HARBOR NAME] for 22 years. I'm asking you to set the summer flounder limit at 3 fish, or shorten the season, instead of cutting it to 2.

I don't dispute your staff's numbers. The stock is at 78 percent of target, and it needs to come back. My point is about who pays for the cut.

Charter boats land about 9 percent of this state's summer flounder. Private boats land about 80 percent. A 2-fish limit asks the boats that land the smallest share of the fish to take one of the biggest hits.

I know what that hit looks like because I've taken it. When the limit was cut in 2016, my bookings fell from 170 trips to 138 the next season. That's 32 trips at $900 each, or $28,800 gone in one year. Two years later I was still 15 trips short. This cut would last three years.

Our charter association surveyed 70 past customers. 41 said they would book fewer trips at 2 fish. Only 12 said they'd book the same.

I can't tell you how many of the 11 boats in our harbor would close. I can tell you that two captains have told me they will sell if bookings drop the way they did in 2016.

I'll be straight with you: about 70 percent of my trips for next summer are already booked, so the damage won't show right away. In 2016 it showed up the season after the cut.

Here's what we'll give in return. I will hand your staff my trip logs from 2016 forward. Our association will test a catch-reporting app with your staff next season.

Three fish, or a shorter season. Thank you.
