**An Open Letter to the [State] State Employees' Health Plan**

[Date]

To the Board of Trustees and Administrator of the [State] State Employees' Health Plan:

We own and run 38 independent pharmacies across [State]. Every day we fill prescriptions for state employees, retirees and their families. Members of your plan account for about 22 percent of all the prescriptions we fill.

Since January, the plan has paid us less than it costs to fill those prescriptions. For the 20 generic drugs we dispense most often, the plan pays an average of $1.80 less per prescription than we pay to buy and dispense the drug. We lose money on each of those prescriptions.

At my own pharmacy, we filled about 3,100 prescriptions for plan members last year. Every pharmacy that signed this letter can tell you something similar. No small business can lose money on a fifth of its work for long.

What's at stake goes beyond our businesses. In 14 counties in [State], the nearest pharmacy for most residents is an independent one. Nine independent pharmacies in our state have closed in the past two years. Each closure means some of your members drive farther to pick up insulin or blood pressure medication, wait longer for help, or go without. They also lose a pharmacist who knows them and their medication history.

The plan has a clear chance to fix this. Its contract with its pharmacy benefit manager comes up for renewal on July 1. We ask the plan to:

1. **Require that payments for generic drugs at least cover what pharmacies pay to buy and dispense them** in any renewed or new contract.
2. **Make the payment terms in that contract open** to plan members and the pharmacies that serve them.
3. **Meet with representatives of the state's independent pharmacies** before the contract terms are final.

We want to keep serving your members. We are asking for payment that lets us keep doing it.

Sincerely,

[Name], [Pharmacy], [Town]
*On behalf of the 38 independent pharmacies listed below*

[List of pharmacies and towns]
