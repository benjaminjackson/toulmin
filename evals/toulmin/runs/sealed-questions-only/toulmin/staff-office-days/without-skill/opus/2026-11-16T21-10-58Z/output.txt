**Subject: Working in the office three days a week from September 1**

Hi everyone,

Before Friday's all-staff meeting, we want to tell you about a change so you have time to think about it before we talk.

**From September 1, 2027, everyone at the firm will work in the office on Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday.** This means we're ending fully remote work.

**Why we're doing this**

Two things convinced us.

Last year we lost two large hospital projects at the final interview. Both clients told us that our team seemed not to know each other. In both cases, how we came across as a team cost us the project.

Our review records also show that junior staff hired since 2022 are taking about 30% longer to reach the point where they lead a project. This is not a judgement on them. We think much of how people learn to run a project here happens by sitting near the people doing it, and we haven't given our newer colleagues enough of that.

We're part of the problem. The partners have been coming in one day a week. That changes too. We'll be in all three days.

**For those of you hired as fully remote**

Fifteen of you joined in 2021 and 2022 as fully remote employees. You took those roles on terms we're now changing. We know that is a real ask, and for some of you it means rearranging a lot. We won't pretend otherwise.

Four of you live more than two hours from the office. You can keep working fully remotely until December 31, 2027. From January 2028, we'll ask you to come in one day a week.

**Friday**

We'll go through this at Friday's meeting and take your questions. If you'd rather raise something privately, please talk to any of us before or after.

[Partner name], [Partner name] and [Partner name]

Some notes:

- **Dates:** Today is November 16, 2026, so I read "September 1" as 2027 and "end of next year" as December 31, 2027. Please check both. If you meant September 2026 or a different end date, the years need to change.
- **Private conversation:** The last line commits the partners to being available to talk one-on-one. Remove it if that's not something you want to promise.
- **Tell the 15 first:** I'd strongly recommend talking to the 15 remote hires, and especially the 4, before this email goes out. Finding out in an all-staff email that the terms of their job are changing will land badly, however carefully it's worded.
- **Questions to settle before Friday.** The email doesn't answer these, but people will ask:
  - Are Mondays and Fridays still remote?
  - Is there a process for exceptions, like caring responsibilities or health?
  - Do the 4 choose their office day?
  - Will you help with travel costs?
