**Subject: Request for promotion to Senior Paralegal**

Dear [Name],

I'm writing to ask to be considered for promotion to Senior Paralegal. I've been a paralegal in the litigation group for five years, and I believe my work already matches what the senior role requires.

**Workload and results.** Last year I supported four lawyers on 23 cases, including two that went to trial. I billed 1,640 hours, 90 more than the group's target of 1,550.

**Building systems others rely on.** In 2023 I built the group's tracking sheet for court deadlines. Two other groups in the firm now use it as well.

**Owning mistakes and fixing the process.** Last March I entered a filing deadline incorrectly in that sheet. Another paralegal caught the error, and we filed two hours before the court's cutoff. That was too close. Afterward I added a second-person check to the sheet so that one person's error can't go unnoticed. I mention this because a senior paralegal has to fix the process that let a problem happen, not just the problem itself.

**Training the team.** I trained all three paralegals the group has hired in the last two years. Supervising paralegals is the main part of the senior role, and I have already been doing much of the groundwork for it.

I understand that the senior role includes supervising the group's paralegals and a matching salary increase. I'm ready to take on that responsibility and would welcome the chance to discuss it. Would you have time to meet in the next two weeks?

Thank you for considering my request.

Best regards,
[Your name]

The letter is about 280 words. Some notes on what I chose:

- **I kept the March deadline error in.** Your managing partner probably already knows about it. Raising it yourself and showing the fix you put in place comes across as ownership. If they hear about it some other way, it could look like you were hiding it. Since you specifically mentioned the fix, it seemed like a strength to include. If you'd rather leave it out, the letter still works without that paragraph.
- **I didn't state the $9,000 figure.** The letter mentions the salary increase but doesn't name the number. Leaving the number for the conversation usually goes over better in a first written request. If you want it in writing, you could change that sentence to "...and the $9,000 salary increase that comes with it."
- **I didn't add anything you didn't give me.** For example, I didn't say you already supervise anyone day to day. If you do informally supervise the newer paralegals, such as answering their questions or reviewing their work, add a specific example. It would make the strongest point in the letter even stronger.
