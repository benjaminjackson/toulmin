<document>
**An open letter to the [State] State Employees' Health Plan**

From 38 independent pharmacies of the Main Street Pharmacy Alliance

[Date]

To the Director and Board of the State Employees' Health Plan:

We are 38 independent pharmacies, and together we fill prescriptions for thousands of state employees, retirees and their families. Plan members make up about 22 percent of all the prescriptions we fill. My own pharmacy, Reilly Drug in Carver, filled about 3,100 prescriptions for plan members last year.

Since January, the plan has paid us less than it costs us to buy and dispense the generic drugs your members need most. Across the 20 generics we fill most often, the plan pays us on average $1.80 less per prescription than our cost. Every time we fill one of those prescriptions, we lose money.

Some of us make up part of that loss on brand-name drugs. That does not fix the problem. A pharmacy shouldn't have to rely on one kind of prescription to cover a loss on another.

Two of our 38 pharmacies have told us they will stop filling prescriptions for plan members if nothing changes. Nine independent pharmacies in this state have closed in the last two years. We can't say how many of them closed because of rates, and we won't claim to. We do know that a pharmacy that loses money on its most common prescriptions cannot stay open for long.

The people who would lose the most are your members. In 14 counties, an independent pharmacy is the nearest one for most people, and for most of them the nearest chain pharmacy is more than 20 miles away. If an independent pharmacy in one of those counties stops serving plan members, a retired teacher or a state trooper's family there has nowhere close to go.

You can fix this. The plan's contract with its pharmacy benefit manager is up for renewal on July 1. We ask that the renewed contract pay pharmacies at least what each drug costs us, plus a fee for filling it.

We are not asking you to take our word for the numbers. We will give the plan our purchase invoices for all 20 drugs, and we will meet with your staff any time before May 1 to go through them.

Your members trust us to have their medicine ready when they need it. We want to keep doing that, and we hope you will help us.

Sincerely,

Tom Reilly
Owner, Reilly Drug, Carver
On behalf of the Main Street Pharmacy Alliance
