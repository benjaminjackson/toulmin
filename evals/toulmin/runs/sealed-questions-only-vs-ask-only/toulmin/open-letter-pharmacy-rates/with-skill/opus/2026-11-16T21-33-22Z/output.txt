**An Open Letter to the [State Employees' Health Plan]**

To [Director's name] and the plan's board:

We are 38 independent pharmacies across [State]. Every week we fill prescriptions for state employees, retirees and their families who are covered by your plan. Your members account for about 22 percent of the prescriptions we fill, more than one in five.

Since January, your plan has paid us an average of $1.80 less per prescription than it costs us to buy and fill the 20 generic drugs we dispense most. We lose money on every one of those prescriptions. Some of us make back part of that loss on brand-name drugs. Not all of us do, and no pharmacy can run for long on the hope that one prescription will cover the loss on another.

This is already changing what we can offer your members. Two of our 38 pharmacies have told us they will stop filling prescriptions for plan members if the rates don't change. Nine independent pharmacies in [State] have closed in the past two years. We don't know how many of them closed because of rates, and we won't claim more than we know. We do know what happens to a town when its pharmacy stops serving the people who live there.

In 14 counties, the nearest pharmacy for most residents is an independent one, and the nearest chain pharmacy is more than 20 miles away. If the pharmacy in one of those towns stops taking your plan, your members there face a round trip of more than 40 miles for their medicine, or a wait for it in the mail.

Your contract with your pharmacy benefit manager comes up for renewal on July 1. That is the time to fix this. We ask that the new contract pay pharmacies at least what each drug costs us to buy, plus a dispensing fee for filling it.

You don't have to take our word for the numbers. We will give your staff our purchase invoices for these 20 drugs so you can check them yourselves. We will also meet with your staff at any time before May 1 to go through them together.

Your members count on us, and we want to keep serving them. Please write the July 1 contract so that we can.
