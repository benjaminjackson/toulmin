**An open letter to the State Employees' Health Plan from 38 independent pharmacies**

To [Name], [Title], and the Board of the State Employees' Health Plan:

Since January, the State Employees' Health Plan has paid our pharmacies less than it costs us to fill the 20 generic drugs your members use most. On average we lose $1.80 on each of those prescriptions. That loss comes from what we pay the wholesaler for the drug and what it costs to fill it. Your plan's contract with its pharmacy benefit manager comes up for renewal on July 1. We are asking that the new contract pay pharmacies at least what each drug costs them, plus a fee for filling it.

We write for 38 independent pharmacies across the state. Plan members account for about 22% of the prescriptions we fill, so more than one in five of our customers is a state employee or a family member of one. My own store, Reilly Drug in Carver, filled about 3,100 prescriptions for plan members last year. For the 20 generics we fill most, each one of those prescriptions now costs us more than the plan pays.

Some of us make back part of that loss on brand-name drugs. None of us can make it all back, and no business can keep filling a fifth of its orders below cost. Two of our 38 pharmacies have already told us they will stop filling prescriptions for plan members if the rates do not change. Nine independent pharmacies in this state have closed in the last two years. We don't know how many of those closed because of reimbursement rates, and we won't claim that we do. We do know which way things are moving.

Who would be hurt depends on where they live. In 14 counties, most people's nearest pharmacy is an independent one, and for most people there the nearest chain pharmacy is more than 20 miles away. When a pharmacy there stops taking your plan, your members don't just walk down the street to another one. They drive 20 miles or more for a blood pressure refill, or they wait for a mail-order delivery, or they skip a dose.

We are not asking you to take our word for the numbers. We will give your staff our purchase invoices for all 20 drugs so you can check what we pay. We will meet with your staff at any time before May 1, so that what you learn can go into the contract before it is signed.

The July 1 renewal is the one point this year when the plan can fix this. We ask that the new contract require pharmacies to be paid at least what each drug costs plus a fill fee. We ask you to meet with us before May 1.

Sincerely,

Tom Reilly
Owner, Reilly Drug, Carver
On behalf of the Main Street Pharmacy Alliance (38 independent pharmacies)
