**An open letter to the [State Employees' Health Plan]**

From the Main Street Pharmacy Alliance, 38 independent pharmacies serving state employees across [State]

Since January, your plan has paid us less to fill your members' most common prescriptions than it costs us to fill them.

We looked at the 20 generic drugs we fill most often. On average, the plan pays us $1.80 less per prescription than we pay to buy the drug and fill it. Members of your plan make up about 22% of the prescriptions at our 38 pharmacies. That means we lose money on one of our biggest groups of customers every day. Some of us make back part of that loss on brand-name drugs. That doesn't change what is happening with the generics your members pick up most often.

This is already affecting where state employees can fill their prescriptions. Two of our 38 pharmacies have told us they will stop filling for plan members if the rates stay the same. Nine independent pharmacies in the state have closed in the past two years. We can't say how many of those closings were caused by payment rates. We can say that every closing leaves fewer places for people to go.

In some parts of the state, there is no other place to go. In 14 counties, the nearest pharmacy for most people is an independent one. The nearest chain pharmacy is more than 20 miles away. If one of those pharmacies stops taking your plan, a teacher, a state trooper or a retired state worker there has to drive that distance to fill a prescription, or go without.

Your contract with your pharmacy benefit manager, the company that sets what pharmacies are paid, comes up for renewal on July 1. That renewal is the chance to fix this. We ask that the new contract pay pharmacies at least what a drug costs us, plus a fee for filling it.

We will help you check our numbers. We will share our purchase invoices for all 20 drugs with your staff. I will meet with them at any time before May 1, so this can be settled well before the contract is signed.

Our pharmacies know your members by name. We want to keep serving them, and we're asking for terms that let us.

Sincerely,

Tom Reilly
Owner, Reilly Drug, Carver
On behalf of the Main Street Pharmacy Alliance
