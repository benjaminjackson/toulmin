Dear Members of the Admissions Committee,

I am writing to recommend Priya Nair for admission to your PhD program in marine ecology. I have taught biology at Westbrook College for 17 years and supervised about 30 senior theses. Priya is one of the three best students I have taught in that time.

Priya took three courses with me. In two of them she earned the top grade in a class of 28. Good grades are common among strong applicants, though. What sets Priya apart is what she did once she left the classroom.

For her senior thesis, Priya compared oyster growth at six sites in our bay over 14 months. She designed the study and did all the fieldwork herself, which meant about 90 trips by kayak in every season. Few undergraduates take on a field project of that length alone, and fewer still see it through. Priya never missed her sampling schedule. Her main finding was that oysters grew faster at the two sites near the salt marsh. The result is preliminary: it comes from a single study, and a storm cost one site three months of data. Still, it is a clear pattern worth testing with more sites and more years, and that is the kind of question a PhD lets a student pursue. Priya presented the work as a poster at a regional conference.

Last summer Priya worked as a paid field assistant at a state shellfish lab. For two weeks she ran the lab's water sampling program on her own. Her supervisor there told me he would hire her again without hesitation. That matches what I saw over 14 months of thesis work. Priya can be trusted with real responsibility in the field, and she follows through.

Priya already knows what sustained fieldwork demands, and she has shown she can carry it out. I recommend her to your program with enthusiasm and without reservation. Please contact me if I can provide any further information.

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
