They also changed their mite treatment, though, so we can't tell you how much of the improvement is ours. We're working to get comparable before-and-after numbers from more customers this winter.

**Competition**

In May a large farm equipment company launched a hive sensor at $45, which is $10 below ours. Theirs measures temperature only. Ours measures temperature, sound and hive weight. Three of our beekeepers have asked to test theirs. I don't know yet whether any of them will switch, and I'll tell you when I do.

**Cutting spending**

We are not filling two open roles. That brings monthly spending down from $95,000 to $75,000 by February.

**Why a bridge, and why now**

Even with that cut, if we count no new revenue, our $340,000 lasts until about March. That's the start of the March–April window that brings in most of our year's sales, and selling more sensors in that window means paying to build them first. Going into our most important season with only weeks of cash is a risk I'm not willing to take.

With the bridge we'd have $940,000. At $75,000 a month, that lasts until about November 2027 before counting any revenue. It carries us through the whole 2027 selling season on cash we already hold.

I'm not going to promise profitability next year. The numbers don't support that yet. The bridge buys us a second full season at lower spending, and in that season you'll see three things: how many beekeepers we add, how many hives stay on the app, and whether we keep customers against a cheaper competitor.
