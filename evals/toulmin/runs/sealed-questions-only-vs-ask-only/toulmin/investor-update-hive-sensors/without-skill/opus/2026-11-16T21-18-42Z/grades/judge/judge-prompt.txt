**Subject: Half-year update, and a $600k bridge we'd like you in**

Hi all,

Here's where we are at the half year, including the hard parts. At the end I'm asking you to join a $600,000 bridge round that closes December 15.

**Customers more than doubled**

We now have 31 paying commercial beekeepers, up from 12 a year ago. Revenue for the half was $212,000. Seventy percent of it came in March and April, when beekeepers get their colonies ready for pollination season. That seasonality drives our cash position, and I come back to it below.

Each sensor costs us $38 to make and sells for $55, so we make $17 a unit. On top of that, every hive pays $4 a month for the app.

**What the sensors did for our largest customer**

Our biggest customer runs 4,000 hives. He lost 18% of his colonies this past winter, down from 31% the winter before. That's roughly 520 more colonies alive going into the season. He says the sensors were part of it. He also changed his mite treatment over the same period, so we can't claim the whole improvement, and we won't.

**A competitor has arrived**

In May a large farm equipment company launched a hive sensor at $45, $10 below ours. Their sensor measures only temperature. Ours also measures sound and hive weight. Three of our beekeepers have asked to test theirs. We don't know of any deals lost to them so far, and we haven't decided whether to change our pricing.

**Cash**

We spend about $95,000 a month and have $340,000 in the bank. Without new money, that lasts until about early March, just as next spring's orders start to arrive. That leaves us no margin.

We're cutting spending to $75,000 a month by February by not filling two open roles.

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
