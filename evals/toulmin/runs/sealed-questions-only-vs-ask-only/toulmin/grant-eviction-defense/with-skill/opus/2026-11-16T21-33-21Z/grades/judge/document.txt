**Tenant Defense Project: Keeping Eviction Defense Available in [County]**

Last year, 1,140 eviction cases were filed in [County]. The Tenant Defense Project, a two-attorney legal aid clinic, represented the tenant in 163 of them. In 58% of those cases, the tenant stayed in the home or got at least 60 days to move. We turned away about 300 more tenants because no attorney was free.

Our capacity is about to fall by half. Each of our attorneys carries about 80 open cases, and one leaves in June. Our last attorney search took five months, so we need to begin recruiting as soon as funding is certain.

We request $90,000 over two years ($45,000 per year) toward a staff attorney position that costs $78,000 a year with benefits. A family fund has pledged $20,000 a year for both years. The clinic will cover the remaining $13,000 a year from its general budget. With this grant, we can hire promptly and keep two full-time eviction defense attorneys rather than one.

We will measure results the same way we do now. Every six months, we will report to the Foundation the number of cases opened and how each one ended. Starting in January, we will request court-wide eviction outcome data from the [County] Clerk. We currently have no figure for how tenants without a lawyer fare. With that data, by the end of the first grant year we can compare outcomes for represented and unrepresented tenants and report the difference to the Foundation.

Even at full strength, two attorneys cannot meet the need: last year we turned away 300 tenants. Without this grant, the number of tenants we can represent will fall by half.
