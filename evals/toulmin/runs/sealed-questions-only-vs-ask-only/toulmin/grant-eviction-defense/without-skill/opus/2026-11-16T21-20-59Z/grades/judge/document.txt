**Project Summary: Tenant Defense Project, Eviction Defense Attorney**

Last year, 1,140 eviction cases were filed in our county. The Tenant Defense Project is a two-attorney legal aid clinic, and we represented the tenant in 163 of those cases, about one in seven. We turned away roughly 300 more tenants who asked for help because no attorney was free. Each of our attorneys carries about 80 open cases.

When we represent a tenant, the results are concrete. In 58% of our cases, the tenant either stayed in the home or got at least 60 days to move. That is time to find new housing and keep children in their schools.

Our capacity is about to shrink. One of our two attorneys leaves in June 2027. We ask for $90,000 over two years ($45,000 a year) toward a new staff attorney. The departing attorney will spend her last month training the new hire, so open cases and clients carry over without a gap. Whatever happens with her position, this grant keeps at least two attorneys working for tenants. If we can also replace her, it lets us grow to three.

**Budget.** The position costs $78,000 a year with benefits. A family fund has pledged $20,000 a year for both years. The clinic's general budget covers the remaining $13,000 a year. The plan does not depend on our county contract, which is up for renewal in October 2027.

**Accountability.** Every six months we will report to the Foundation on the cases we opened and how they ended. Starting in January 2027, we will ask the county clerk for court-wide eviction outcome data. That will let us compare outcomes for tenants with and without a lawyer, and we will share the comparison with the Foundation.
