You are the reader described between the <reader> tags. Read the document between the <document> tags as that reader would receive it.

The brief between the <brief> tags is what the writer was asked to do and shows the facts the writer had. You, as the reader, have not seen the brief. Use it only to check what the document does with those facts.

<reader>
The seven board members of the nonprofit newsroom, who must vote on the proposal. Two of them come from advertising and sales backgrounds and will be the hardest to move. All of them know the part-time ad sales person, who has been with the newsroom for 11 years.
</reader>

<brief>
Help me write a memo to our board. I run a small nonprofit newsroom with 9 staff. I want the board to approve dropping display ads and moving to a reader membership program from January.

What I have:
- Ads brought in $84,000 last year, down from $131,000 two years ago.
- We ran a 6-week membership pilot in the spring. 212 readers signed up at $8 a month. We did no marketing beyond two newsletter mentions.
- Our newsletter has 14,000 subscribers.
- A newsroom about our size in another state told me membership now covers 40% of their budget.
- Two of our seven board members come from advertising and sales backgrounds. Our ad sales person is part-time and has been with us for 11 years.

I want the memo to say that membership will replace our ad revenue within a year. Keep it to one page.
</brief>

<document>
**MEMO**

**To:** Board of Directors
**From:** [Name], Executive Director
**Date:** [Date]
**Re:** Approval to replace display advertising with reader membership from January 2027

**Decision requested**
Approve ending display advertising on December 31, 2026 and launching a reader membership program in January 2027, with a progress review at the end of June.

**Why change**
Ad revenue fell from $131,000 to $84,000 in two years, about 20% a year. If that trend continues, ads will bring in about $67,000 in 2027. [Why we are not running ads and membership side by side: one or two sentences.]

**What the pilot showed**
In this spring's six-week pilot, 212 readers became members at $8 a month. The only promotion was two newsletter mentions. That is 1.5% of our 14,000 newsletter subscribers, worth about $20,000 a year in recurring revenue. [X] of them are still paying today.

**The target**
To match last year's $84,000, we need 875 members, or 6% of our newsletter list. To match where ad revenue is heading, we need about 700. Going from 212 to 875 over 2027 takes about 55 new members a month. The pilot added about 150 a month with almost no promotion. We don't expect that pace to hold, because the first people to join are the most committed readers. But the target is about a third of that pace, and in 2027 we will be promoting membership for the first time. A newsroom of our size in [state] now gets 40% of its budget from membership. Their market differs from ours, but they show the model can support a newsroom like ours.

**The cost in 2027**
Ad revenue stops in January, while membership builds month by month. If we stay on target, we will be earning at the $84,000 yearly rate by December, but we will collect about $55,000 during 2027, before cancellations. That is $12,000–$29,000 less than ads would probably have brought in. Ending ads saves $[X] in sales costs. We propose to cover the remaining gap from [reserves / bridge grant / other].

**Checkpoint**
By June 30 we should have about 540 members. If we have fewer than [450], I will return to the board in July with options, including bringing back sponsorships.

**Our ad sales colleague**
[Name] has sold advertising for us part-time for 11 years. I have spoken with [Name] about this proposal, and [plan agreed with them: new role, transition period, or support].

**Recommendation**
Ad revenue has fallen two years running, and the pilot showed readers will pay with almost no prompting. I recommend the board approve the move to membership from January, with the June 30 review as the point to change course if needed.

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
