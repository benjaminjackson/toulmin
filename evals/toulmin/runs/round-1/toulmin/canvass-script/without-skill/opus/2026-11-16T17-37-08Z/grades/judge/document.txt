**YES ON MEASURE 4: DOOR SCRIPT (Ridgeview)**
*Aim for 60–90 seconds. Listen more than you talk.*

**OPEN**
"Hi, I'm [name], a volunteer and neighbor. I'm talking with folks about Measure 4, the library levy on the March ballot. Have you heard about it?"

**THE CORE (say this part every time)**
"Measure 4 does two things. It keeps the Ridgeview branch open, because without it our branch closes this July. And it brings back Sunday hours at all five city libraries.

For a typical Ridgeview home it's about **$10 a month, roughly $120 a year.** It's 19 cents per $1,000 of assessed value, and it **ends after 6 years.** Then voters decide again."

**ASK**
"Can we count on your yes vote in March?"

**IF THEY SAY…**

*"It's a blank check."*
"I hear that. It's a fixed rate, it's for specific things (Sunday hours and keeping this branch open), and it expires in 6 years."

*"I'm on a fixed income."*
"That's fair, and I won't pretend it's nothing. It's about $10 a month for a typical home here. For many neighbors, that's worth keeping a library they can walk to."

*"Does anyone even use the library?"*
"Library visits are up 22% since 2021, more people than before the pandemic."

*"I rent."*
"Renters vote too, and the library serves everyone. Can we count on your yes?"

**CLOSE**
- **Yes:** "Thank you! Can I put you down as a supporter?" (Mark card.)
- **Undecided:** "Totally fair. Can I leave this with you?" (Leave literature.)
- **No:** "Thanks for your time. Have a good day." (Leave politely. Don't argue.)

**REMEMBER:** Use the real numbers only. Don't round down. Never discuss individuals or the newspaper letter. Election day: March [date].
