**YES ON MEASURE 4 — RIDGEVIEW DOOR SCRIPT** (60–90 seconds)

**OPEN**
"Hi, I'm ___, a volunteer with Yes on Measure 4, the library levy on the March ballot. Do you have a minute?"

"Have you heard much about Measure 4?" *(Listen. Don't argue.)*

**THE CASE**
"Here's the short version. If Measure 4 doesn't pass, the Ridgeview branch closes in July. Measure 4 keeps it open, and it brings back Sunday hours at all five branches.

People are using our libraries more than ever. Visits are up 22% since 2021.

On cost, here's the real number. It's 19 cents per $1,000 of assessed value. On a typical Ridgeview home, about $640,000, that's around $122 a year, or about $10 a month. And it ends after six years."

**THE ASK**
"Can we count on your yes vote in March?"
- **Yes:** "Thank you!" *(Record it. Offer a yard sign.)*
- **Unsure:** "What would help you decide?" *(Leave the flyer.)*
- **No:** "Thanks for your time." *(Move on politely.)*

**IF THEY SAY…**

**"I read it's a blank check."**
"I understand the worry. It's a fixed rate, 19 cents per $1,000, so it raises a set $2.3 million a year. It's for keeping Ridgeview open and restoring Sunday hours, and it ends after six years unless voters renew it."

**"I'm on a fixed income."**
"That's fair, and it's why we give you the exact number. It's about $10 a month on a typical Ridgeview home. You can check your own: take your assessed value, divide by 1,000, and multiply by 19 cents."

**"I rent."**
"The levy is billed to property owners. What it pays for, like Sunday hours and keeping Ridgeview open, is for everyone."

*Always: be friendly, keep it short, never argue, and record every response.*
