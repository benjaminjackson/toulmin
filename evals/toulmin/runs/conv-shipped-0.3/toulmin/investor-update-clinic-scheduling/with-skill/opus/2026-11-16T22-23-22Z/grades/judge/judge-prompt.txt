Subject: Cadence Scheduling: quarterly update, and an apology for the gap

Hi all,

I'll start with an apology. I didn't send updates for the last two quarters. The numbers were flat and I didn't want to send bad news. That was the wrong call, because bad news is exactly what you should hear from me early. From now on you'll get an update in the first week of every quarter. The next one goes out January 7. If you'd like a one-page set of numbers every month as well, reply and I'll send it.

**The quarter in numbers**

- Monthly recurring revenue: $41,000, the same as three months ago.
- Clinics: 9 added, 4 lost.
- Cash: $480,000 in the bank. We lose about $44,000 a month, which gives us roughly 11 months, to around October 2027, if nothing changes.

**What happened**

Our largest customer, a 12-clinic chain paying $5,500 a month, moved to the scheduler built into their electronic records system. Revenue held flat only because the rest of the business grew. Without that chain, monthly revenue went from about $35,500 to $41,000, roughly 15% in one quarter.

There's a risk I want you to see clearly. Two other customers use the same records system as the chain that left, and together they pay $3,800 a month. I can't promise they won't leave the same way. If they do, monthly revenue falls to $37,200.

**What's working**

Clinics that use our automatic text reminders have a no-show rate of 7%, against 13% before they started. It's the clearest result we deliver.

**What's next**

On December 1 we start selling reminders on their own for $150 a month. Six clinics have already said they'll buy at launch. I don't yet know whether the reminders will work alongside the schedulers built into records systems. That's the question I most want answered. If they do, a clinic that moves its scheduling to its records system could still stay with us for reminders. I'm not setting a sales target yet. You'll see the real numbers on January 7.

I don't have a fundraising or cost-cutting plan to share yet. With about 11 months of cash, I need to settle that soon. I'd rather work it out with your input than hand you a finished plan.

**What I'm asking for**

1. **20 minutes on the phone with each of you.** Reply with a few times that work and I'll set it up.
2. **Introductions to physical therapy clinic chains.** A chain is the single biggest customer we lost, and it's where one deal moves our numbers most.

Thank you for your patience over the last six months. I'll keep you better informed from here.

Sam Patel
Founder, Cadence Scheduling

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
