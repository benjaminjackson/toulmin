Subject: Cadence Scheduling quarterly update, and an apology for the last two

Hi all,

First, an explanation. I didn't send updates for the last two quarters. Our numbers were flat and I didn't want to send bad news. That was the wrong call. You should hear about flat numbers as soon as I have them. From now on, you'll get an update in the first week of every quarter. The next one goes out January 7. If you'd also like a one-page set of numbers each month, reply and I'll add you to the list.

**The numbers**
- Monthly recurring revenue: $41,000, the same as three months ago.
- Clinics: 9 added, 4 lost.
- Cash: $480,000 in the bank. We lose about $44,000 a month, so at the current rate we have about 11 months of cash left.

**The loss that matters**
One of the four clinics we lost was our largest customer, a 12-clinic chain that paid $5,500 a month. They moved to the scheduler built into their electronic records system. The new clinics made up for the lost revenue, which is why revenue held steady. But it didn't grow.

The same thing could happen again. Two other customers use the same records system. Together they pay $3,800 a month, and I can't promise they'll stay. A scheduler that comes free with a clinic's records system is a real competitor.

**What we're doing about it**
Text reminders are our strongest result. Clinics that use our automatic text reminders have a no-show rate of 7%, down from 13% before they started. That removes nearly half of their no-shows, which a built-in scheduler doesn't offer them.

On December 1 we start selling reminders as a separate product at $150 a month. Six clinics have said they'll buy it at launch. That's $900 a month, which is a start but doesn't fix the problem. We'd need about 37 subscribers to replace the revenue from the chain we lost.

What I don't know yet is whether clinics using their records system's scheduler, especially chains, will buy reminders as a separate product. Next quarter has to answer that question.

**Cash**
I don't have a plan for our remaining cash to share with you yet, and I won't pretend I do. It's the main thing I want to work through with you.

**What I'm asking for**
1. A 20-minute phone call with each of you, about our cash and the reminders launch.
2. Introductions to physical therapy clinic chains, especially ones whose records system has a built-in scheduler. Those are the customers we're most at risk of losing, so they'll tell us fastest whether the reminders product can keep them.

Reply with a time that suits you for a call, or with the name of someone I should talk to.

Thanks,
Sam Patel
Cadence Scheduling

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
