Good evening, Chair and commissioners. My name is Margaret Shaw. I've lived on Alder Road for 19 years, two blocks from St. Luke's Lutheran. I'm also a member of St. Luke's, and I want you to know that up front.

I'm asking you to approve the permit for these 64 apartments, with a traffic signal at Alder and 4th as a condition of approval.

The county's own housing report says we are short about 2,100 affordable homes. Right now, 412 households are on the housing authority's waiting list, and the average wait is three years. Every one of these 64 apartments would rent to a household earning under 60 percent of area median income. That's enough homes for about one in six of the households on that list. It won't close the whole gap, but it's a real step.

And it goes on land that's already paved. The church's back lot is 1.8 acres that sits empty six days a week.

Now traffic, because I live on it. Some mornings around eight, cars already back up past my house. I won't stand here and promise you it can't get worse. The traffic study estimates 310 more trips a day on Alder, and says that on its own, the project would drop Alder and 4th from a C to a D at rush hour. But Cascade Housing Partners, the nonprofit builder, has offered to pay for a signal there. The same study says that with the signal, the intersection stays at C. That's why I'm asking you to make the signal a condition. Then it's required, not just offered.

On parking: the plan has 72 spaces for 64 units, and St. Luke's has committed in writing that neighbors can use the lot on weekdays when there's space.

Construction will take about 20 months, with trucks on Alder Road. I'll be living with that too. The church has promised to set up a neighbor group during construction, and tonight I'm committing to serve on it, so the people on our street have a neighbor to bring problems to.

If you say no, the lot stays empty six days a week, and 412 households keep waiting. Please approve the permit, with the signal as a condition. Thank you.
