**An Open Letter to Keystone Property Group**

December 1, 2026

Diane Weller
Regional Manager, Keystone Property Group

Dear Ms. Weller,

Harbor View Apartments at 220 Grant Street has nine stories, 112 apartments and one elevator. This year that elevator has been out of service on 41 days, or about one day in every eight. One outage in August lasted 12 days.

For many of our neighbors, an outage means they can't leave home. Twenty-three residents are over 75, and six use a wheelchair or walker. When the elevator stops, residents on upper floors who can't manage the stairs can't get to the doctor, the pharmacy or the grocery store. In an emergency, they have no safe way out.

In June, Keystone told us that a new controller part was on order and would arrive in six to eight weeks. Five months later, we are being told the same thing.

We know the city inspected the elevator in September and gave it a passing certificate. That inspection shows the elevator is safe when it runs. It does not look at how often the elevator breaks down, and that is our problem.

Seventy-four households, two in every three in the building, signed our petition asking for a second elevator. Keystone says a second elevator would cost about $400,000 and can't be built here. We accept that this may be true. But if this building will only ever have one elevator, that elevator has to work, and residents need a plan for the days when it doesn't.

We ask Keystone for three things:

1. **A written date** for installing the controller part and returning the elevator to reliable service.
2. **A stair-climbing chair** kept in the building, and staff trained to use it, so that residents who can't walk the stairs can get in and out during an outage.
3. **A rent credit** for every day the elevator is out of service.

We will do our part. The association will keep a list of residents who need help on the stairs and share it with management for use in emergencies. We are also ready to meet with you any weekday evening that suits you.

We are posting this letter in our building's lobby and sending it to local television news. Our neighbors, and the wider community, should know what is happening here and what we have asked for.

We look forward to your written reply.

Sincerely,

Rosa Delgado
Secretary, on behalf of the Tenants' Association
Harbor View Apartments, 220 Grant Street

**What I left out and why:**
- **The dialysis appointments:** I didn't include them. You don't know yet whether the resident agreed, and on a TV broadcast "8th floor" and "dialysis" together could identify them. If they agree, this is the strongest fact you have. I'd add one sentence after "can't leave home": "During the 12-day outage in August, one of our neighbors missed two dialysis appointments."
- **A reply deadline:** The letter only asks for a written reply. If you want a deadline, add a date to the second-to-last line, for example "by December 15." Pick a date you're prepared to follow up on.

**Check before December 1:**
- **The 41 days:** A reporter will ask where that number comes from, so know whose count it is. If it's the association's own log, you could write "by our records, 41 days."
- **Signatures:** If the board decides to sign, add their names under yours. It will carry more weight.
- **The emergency list:** Ask residents before you put them on the list you share with management.
