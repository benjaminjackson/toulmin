**An Open Letter to Keystone Property Group**

To: Diane Weller, Regional Manager, Keystone Property Group
From: Harbor View Apartments Tenants' Association, 220 Grant Street
December 1, 2026

Dear Ms. Weller,

Harbor View has nine floors, 112 apartments and one elevator. This year that elevator has been out of service on 41 days, about one day in every eight. One of those outages lasted 12 days, in August.

For many of our neighbors, a broken elevator means being stuck at home. Twenty-three residents are over 75, and six use wheelchairs or walkers. During the August outage, a resident on the eighth floor missed two dialysis appointments.

In June, Keystone told us that a new controller part was on order and would arrive in six to eight weeks. That would have been by mid-August. Today we are being told the same thing: six to eight weeks.

We know the elevator passed its city inspection in September. That inspection checks whether the elevator is safe to ride. It does not check how often it breaks down. Safety is not the problem we are raising. Reliability is.

We are asking Keystone for four things:

1. A written date for installing the controller part and finishing the repair.
2. A stair-climbing chair kept in the building, and staff trained to use it, so residents who cannot manage the stairs can still get in and out during an outage.
3. A rent credit for every day the elevator is out of service.
4. An independent study of whether a second elevator could be built here.

Seventy-four households signed our petition for a second elevator. We understand that Keystone puts the cost at about $400,000 and says it cannot be done in this building. We have no engineering opinion that says otherwise, and Keystone may be right. But 112 households depend on this one elevator, and an independent study would let all of us see the answer and the reasons behind it.

The association will do its part. We will keep a list of residents who need help on the stairs and share it with Keystone, so that in an emergency staff know who to reach first. We are ready to meet with you any weekday evening.

If nothing changes, the next outage will strand the same neighbors, and once again they will have no date for the repair.

We ask for a written reply to these four requests, and we hope to meet with you soon.

Sincerely,

Rosa Delgado
Secretary, Harbor View Apartments Tenants' Association
On behalf of the 74 households who signed our petition
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
