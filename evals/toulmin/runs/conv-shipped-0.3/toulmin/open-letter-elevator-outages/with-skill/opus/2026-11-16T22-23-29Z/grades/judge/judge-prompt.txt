<document>
December 1, 2026

**An open letter to Diane Weller, Regional Manager, Keystone Property Group**
From the tenants' association of Harbor View Apartments, 220 Grant Street

Dear Ms. Weller,

Harbor View has 9 floors, 112 apartments and one elevator. This year that elevator has been out of service on 41 days, roughly one day in every eight. In August it was out for 12 days in a row.

For most of us, an outage means climbing the stairs. For some of our neighbours, it means not leaving home at all. 23 of our residents are over 75, and 6 use wheelchairs or walkers. During the 12 days in August, a resident on the 8th floor missed two dialysis appointments.

In June, Keystone told us a new controller part was on order and should arrive in 6 to 8 weeks. Five months later, we are being told the same thing. We know the city inspected the elevator in September and gave it a passing certificate. But that inspection checks only that the elevator is safe. It does not measure how often it breaks down, and it does not bring the part any sooner.

We are asking for three things:

1. **A written date for the repair**, so residents can plan around it.
2. **A stair-climbing chair, with trained staff to operate it**, so residents who cannot manage the stairs can get in and out during outages.
3. **A rent credit for each day the elevator is out of service.**

We do not expect management to do this alone. The association can meet with you any weekday evening. We will also keep a list of residents who need help on the stairs and share it with management for use in emergencies.

74 of our 112 households have also signed a petition asking for a second elevator. You have told us it would cost about $400,000 and cannot be done in this building. We are not engineers, and you may be right. We ask that an independent engineer assess it, so that residents can see the answer for themselves.

Without these steps, the next outage will look like the last ones: our oldest and least mobile neighbours shut in their apartments, waiting for a part that has been "6 to 8 weeks away" since June.

We look forward to your reply, and to meeting with you.

Sincerely,

Rosa Delgado
Secretary, on behalf of the tenants' association of Harbor View Apartments

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
