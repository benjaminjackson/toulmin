# Bookkeeping for hair and nail salons, from someone who used to run one

I'm Carla Benson. I managed a hair salon for six years before I became a bookkeeper. Now I work only with hair and nail salons, and I have 31 salon clients.

## What you get for $199 a month

- Your monthly books, closed by the 10th of the following month
- Payroll for up to 10 staff
- Your sales tax filings

I don't do income tax returns. Your accountant or tax preparer keeps doing those, and I give them clean books to work from.

## Clean books can lower your accountant's bill

Last tax season, 9 of my clients told me their accountant charged them less because their books came in clean. One owner said she saved $800. I can't promise your accountant will charge you less. But tidy books leave them less to sort out.

## The 10th of the month, and what happens if I miss it

I close each month's books by the 10th of the following month. Last year I missed that date three times. Since January, if I miss the 10th, you don't pay for that month.

## The price, plainly

It's $199 a month. Until January it was $149, and I raised it for everyone, including my existing clients. There's no contract. If you want to stop, give me 30 days' notice.

Two clients left last year. Both had sold their salons.

## You're working with one person

Chair and Ledger is just me. The person you talk to is the person doing your books, and she knows what a busy Saturday or a week of no-shows does to a salon's numbers. I'm one person, so I don't answer the phone on Sundays or Mondays.

## Start with a free 20-minute call

The first call is free and takes 20 minutes. We'll talk about your salon and whether I'm the right fit. Calls are Tuesday to Saturday. Pick a time on my calendar.

**[Book your free 20-minute call]**

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
