**To:** Mark Ellison, General Manager
**From:** Daniela Ruiz, Assistant Front Office Manager
**Re:** Application for Front Office Manager

I'm applying for the front office manager position. For the past seven weeks I've been doing the job on my own, and I'm asking to keep doing it.

**The last seven weeks**

Since the position became vacant, I've run the front office alone. In that time no front desk shift has gone uncovered, and overtime has come in under the monthly plan. You don't have to guess how the office will run under me. You've already seen it.

**What I've done at the desk**

I've worked at this hotel for six years, the last two as assistant front office manager. In that time:

- **Training.** I rewrote our front desk training. New hires now work the desk alone after 5 shifts instead of 9, so new agents are ready in roughly half the time.
- **Turnover.** In the year before I became assistant manager, 9 of our 14 front desk staff left. Last year, 5 did. Turnover went from nearly two-thirds of the team to about a third.
- **Check-in scores.** Our check-in score on the main review site rose from 7.9 to 8.6 over the last 18 months. I'm not going to claim all of that, because the lobby and rooms were renovated during the same period. But the renovation began five months in, and by then the score had already risen from 7.9 to 8.2. The rest of the rise came while the renovation was under way, and the renovation deserves its share of that.

**The payroll budget**

I have never managed a budget, and this job owns a $1.1 million payroll budget. Here is how I'll close that gap:

- I will finish the company's finance course within my first three months.
- I will meet weekly with Ben Oduya to work through the payroll budget. He has already offered to do this.
- Keeping overtime under plan for the last seven weeks is the part of that budget I've already been managing.

**The outside candidate**

I understand you're also considering a candidate with experience at a larger hotel. I can't match that. What I can offer is six years at this hotel, a front desk team I trained and kept, and seven weeks in this job with no gaps. Someone from outside would spend their first months learning this hotel and this team, and the training and turnover gains above would be riding on how that goes.

**What I'd work on first**

Friday evening check-in waits are still the most common complaint at the desk. That is the first problem I would take on as front office manager.

I'd welcome the chance to talk through any of this before you decide.

Daniela Ruiz

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
