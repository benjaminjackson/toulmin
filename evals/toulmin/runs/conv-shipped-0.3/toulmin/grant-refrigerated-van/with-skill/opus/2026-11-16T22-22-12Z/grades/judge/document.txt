Every month, our food bank hands out about 38,000 pounds of food to 610 households in a town of about 9,000 people. Only about 6,000 pounds of that, roughly one pound in six, is fresh produce, milk and eggs.

Last summer, local farms offered us about 9,000 pounds of produce over three months. We had to turn all of it down because we had no way to pick it up cold. If we had been able to take it, we would have handed out about 50 percent more fresh food over those three months. That is close to 15 extra pounds of produce for every household we serve.

A refrigerated van would change that. Two of those farms have written to say they will give again if we can collect cold, and their letters are attached. We cannot promise they will offer the same 9,000 pounds next season, because what a farm has to spare changes from year to year. What we can promise is that we will no longer have to say no. Of our 23 regular volunteers, four have committed to driving the van on a weekly schedule.

The Foundation has supported us before. In 2023 you gave us $12,000 for shelving, and our final report on that grant was four months late. Our treasurer at the time was in the hospital for most of that period. For this grant, our current treasurer, Dave Lin, is responsible for the final report, which will reach you within 30 days of the end of the grant year. We will also send you our fresh food totals in pounds every quarter, so you can see what the van is delivering as the year goes on.

We are asking the Foundation for $50,000 toward a refrigerated van that costs $68,000. We will pay the remaining $18,000 from our reserves, which will leave us with $11,000. Insurance and fuel will cost about $4,500 a year. That cost is not yet in our budget, and we would rather tell you now than have you find it later.

Without the van, next summer's farm offers will go the way last summer's did, and fresh food will stay at about one pound in six of what our 610 households receive.
