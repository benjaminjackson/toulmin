I've written the email below. It's about 220 words.

- **The $12,000 line:** the strongest version that stays true is "Every figure I have puts a book like this at $11,000 or more." Both the $12,500 fee and your agent's range bear that out. Going any further would claim things about publishers you haven't dealt with.
- **Left out on purpose:** your $11,000 floor and the gap you'd have if they say no. If Nadia knew either, she'd have less reason to meet $11,500. The email does say you can't take the book at $9,000, because that's true and it's what makes the ask matter. If she offers $11,000, you can still accept it.

Subject: The Night Garden – fee

Dear Nadia,

Thank you for offering me The Night Garden. I'd love to illustrate it, and I can commit to sketches within six weeks of signing and final art by September 1.

Before I accept, I'd like to talk about the fee. I've illustrated six books with you over four years, and my fee has stayed at $9,000 since the first. In that time, two of those books have sold over 20,000 copies each, and one was chosen for a state reading list and went into its fourth printing this spring.

Every figure I have puts a book like this at $11,000 or more. Last year another publisher paid me $12,500 for a book of the same length, and my agent tells me $11,000 to $13,000 is standard for an illustrator with my record. Each book takes me about four months.

For The Night Garden, I'm asking for $11,500, which is still well below what I was paid elsewhere last year. I'm not able to take the book on at $9,000, but I very much want to keep working with you, and I hope we can make this one happen.

I know you need my answer by the end of next week, so I'm happy to talk by phone any time before then.

Best wishes,
Elena Varga
