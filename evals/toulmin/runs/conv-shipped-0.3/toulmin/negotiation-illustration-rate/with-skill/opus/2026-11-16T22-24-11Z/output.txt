I couldn't use "every other publisher" because nothing you've told me shows it's true. Your two strongest true points are the $12,500 fee and the $11,000–$13,000 range, and I've put both in the middle of the email. The email is about 200 words.

<final_document>
Subject: The Night Garden

Dear Nadia,

Thank you for offering me The Night Garden. I'd love to illustrate it, and before I give you my answer I'd like to raise the fee.

I've illustrated six picture books with you over four years, all at $9,000, the fee we agreed for my first book. Two of those books have sold over 20,000 copies, and one was chosen for a state reading list and went into its fourth printing this spring.

My fee hasn't kept pace with the market. Last year another publisher paid me $12,500 for a book of the same length, and the usual range for an illustrator with my record is $11,000 to $13,000. Each book takes me about four months, and I can't take on another at $9,000.

I'm asking $11,500 for The Night Garden. That's at the lower end of that range and below what I was paid elsewhere last year. At that fee, I can commit to:

- sketches within six weeks of signing
- final art by September 1
- two extra spot drawings for your marketing, at no charge

I know you need my answer by the end of next week, and I'm happy to talk it through before then if that would help.

Best wishes,
Elena Varga
</final_document>

- **I didn't name the other publisher or your agent.** "Another publisher" is enough for the $12,500 to count. If the email mentions your agent, Nadia might ask to bring the agent in, and you don't know yet whether you want that. If she asks where the range comes from, you can tell her it's your agent's figure.
- **I left out your $11,000 minimum and the fact that you have nothing else lined up for that slot.** Either one would weaken your position. If she offers less than $11,500, you can accept anything down to $11,000.
- **"I can't take on another at $9,000" is a firm line.** You told me it's true, so I put it in. It shows her what saying no would cost. It also means you'll have to stick to it if she won't move.
