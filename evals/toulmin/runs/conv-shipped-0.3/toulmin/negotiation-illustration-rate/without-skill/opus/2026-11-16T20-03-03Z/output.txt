I left out the line about every other publisher paying at least $12,000. Your facts don't support it: you have one publisher at $12,500, and your agent's range starts at $11,000. Editors know what other houses pay, so a claim they can disprove could make them doubt the rest of your case. The email below uses your real figures, which make a strong case on their own.

<final_document>
Subject: Fee for [Book Title]

Hi [Name],

Thank you for offering me [Book Title]. I'd love to illustrate it, and I'm glad we're still working together after six books.

Before I sign on, I'd like to talk about the fee. My rate has been $9,000 per book since our first project four years ago. Since then, two of our books have sold more than 20,000 copies each, and [Title] was named to the [State] reading list. I'm proud of what we've built together, and I'd like my fee to reflect that record.

For context, last year another publisher paid me $12,500 for a 32-page book of the same scope. My agent tells me $11,000 to $13,000 is the usual range for an illustrator with my experience. Each of these books takes me about four months of full-time work.

For this book, I'm asking for $11,500. That's in the lower half of the market range, and it reflects how much I value working with you and your team.

I'm happy to talk this through by phone if that's easier. I'm looking forward to getting started.

Best,
[Your name]
</final_document>

The email is about 200 words. Fill in the bracketed parts: the book titles, the state and the editor's name.
