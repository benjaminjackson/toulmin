I left out the line that every other publisher pays at least $12,000. You know of one publisher that paid $12,500, and your agent's range starts at $11,000, so the claim isn't accurate. The publisher probably knows market rates, and if they catch it, the rest of your case looks weaker. Your real numbers are strong enough, so the email uses those.

<final_document>
Subject: Fee for [Book Title]

Hi [Name],

Thank you for offering me [Book Title]. I've loved working with you over the last four years, and I'd be glad to illustrate it.

Before we finalize, I'd like to talk about the fee. My fee has been $9,000 per book since our first project together, and my track record has grown since then. Two of our six books have each sold more than 20,000 copies, and [Title] was chosen for the [State] reading list. Each book also takes about four months of full-time work.

My rates elsewhere have risen with that record. Last year another publisher paid me $12,500 for a 32-page picture book of the same scope. My agent says the usual range for an illustrator with my sales history is $11,000 to $13,000.

For [Book Title], I'm asking for $11,500. That's below what I've been paid elsewhere, because I value working with you and your team. I'd love to keep making books together, and this fee would let me keep doing that.

I'm happy to get on a call if it would help to talk it through.

Best,
[Your name]
</final_document>

It's about 200 words. Fill in the bracketed parts, and name the book that made the reading list if you can, because a specific title is more convincing. If your agent usually handles these talks, you may want to copy them in or let them send it.
