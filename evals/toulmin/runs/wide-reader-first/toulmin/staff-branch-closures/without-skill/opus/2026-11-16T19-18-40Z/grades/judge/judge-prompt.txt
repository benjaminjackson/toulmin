The brief between the <brief> tags is what the writer was asked to do and shows the facts the writer had. You, as the reader, have not seen the brief. Use it only to check what the document does with those facts.

<reader>
The 14 tellers and member service staff at the two branches that are closing. They heard rumours of the closures two weeks ago, several have worked at their branch for over ten years, and most of them do not want to work on the phones. They decide whether to take the new roles or start looking elsewhere.

<brief>
Help me write an announcement to our staff. I'm the COO of a credit union with 7 branches. We're closing the Maple Street and Route 9 branches on March 31 and moving the people who work there to a new member phone and chat center.

Facts:
- The two branches have 14 staff between them. Every one of them is offered a job in the phone and chat center at the same pay or higher.
- Visits to those two branches fell 44% over the last three years. Phone and chat contacts across the credit union went up 60%.
- Members calling us now wait an average of 9 minutes on hold.
- The center will be open 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. on weekdays. The branches were open 9 to 5. Shifts will rotate.
- Staff in the center can work from home two days a week after 90 days of training.
- Anyone who doesn't take the new role gets 2 weeks of pay for every year they've worked here.

I want the announcement to say that this change will make everyone's job better. About 400 words, sent by email from me.
</brief>



<document>
**Subject: Changes to our branches and a new member phone and chat center**

Dear team,

I'm writing to tell you about a significant change. On March 31, we will close our Maple Street and Route 9 branches and open a new member phone and chat center at [location].

Our members are changing how they reach us. Over the past three years, visits to these two branches have fallen 44%. Over the same period, phone and chat contacts across the credit union have risen 60%. Members who call us now wait an average of 9 minutes on hold. That isn't the service we want to give, and it puts real pressure on the colleagues answering those calls today.

The 14 people at Maple Street and Route 9 are why this works. Every one of them is being offered a role in the new center **at the same pay or higher**. They know our members and our products, and that's exactly what members need when they call or message us. Nobody at these branches is losing their job unless they choose to.

Here is what the new center will look like:

- **Hours:** 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. on weekdays, which is longer than the branches' 9-to-5 schedule. Shifts will rotate, and we'll share how the rotation works before anyone has to decide.
- **Training:** All center staff will complete 90 days of training.
- **Working from home:** Once training is done, center staff can work from home two days a week.

I know a new schedule is a real change, especially for anyone who has built their life around branch hours. We want to hear about your circumstances and will work with you where we can.

If you are at one of the two branches and decide the new role isn't right for you, you will receive **two weeks of pay for every year you've worked here**. You have until [date] to decide, and your manager and [HR contact] are available to talk it through privately at any point before then.

For those of you at our other five branches, nothing changes in your role. You may see some members from Maple Street and Route 9 visiting your branch, and you'll soon be able to send members to a phone and chat team that can answer them faster.

We will hold an all-staff meeting on [date/time] to go over the details and answer your questions. If you'd rather ask privately, my door is open, and so is [HR contact]'s.

I'm grateful to the Maple Street and Route 9 teams for years of serving our members face to face, and I'm confident they'll do the same in this new role.

Thank you,

[Your name]
Chief Operating Officer
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
