<brief>
I'm going to speak at our county planning commission tonight in favor of a housing project, and I want help writing what I'll say. The church I belong to wants to build 64 apartments on its back parking lot. All of them would rent to households earning under 60% of the area median income.

Facts:
- The church lot is 1.8 acres and sits empty 6 days a week.
- The county's own housing report says we are short about 2,100 affordable homes.
- The traffic study estimates 310 more car trips a day on Alder Road, and says the intersection at Alder and 4th would drop from a C rating to a D at rush hour.
- The plan has 72 parking spaces for the 64 units.
- 412 households are on the county housing authority's waiting list. The average wait is 3 years.
- I've lived on Alder Road for 19 years, two blocks from the church.

Each speaker gets 3 minutes.
</brief>



<document>
Good evening, commissioners. My name is ________.

I've lived on Alder Road for 19 years. My house is two blocks from the church. I'll say up front that I'm a member of that church. But I'm here tonight as a neighbor too, because the traffic from this project will go right past my house.

I'm asking you to approve the 64 apartments on the church's back parking lot.

I'll start with traffic, because I know that's the worry.

The county's study says this adds 310 car trips a day on Alder Road. It says the intersection at Alder and 4th drops from a C to a D at rush hour. That's real. I drive through that intersection. I'll be sitting in that longer wait along with everyone else.

I've weighed that, and I still think this project is worth it. Here's why.

First, parking. The plan has 72 spaces for 64 apartments. That's more than one space for every home. The people who live there will park on that lot, not on our street.

Second, the land. That lot is 1.8 acres of pavement. It sits empty six days a week. We wouldn't be losing a field or a park. We'd be using asphalt that's already there.

Third, the need. The county's own housing report says we're short about 2,100 affordable homes. Right now, 412 households are on the housing authority's waiting list. The average wait is three years.

Every one of these 64 apartments would rent to a household earning under 60 percent of the area median income. That's about one home for every six households on that list.

I won't pretend 64 homes closes a gap of 2,100. It doesn't. But it's 64 families who stop waiting. And it's on land that's already paved and almost never used.

If you say no tonight, that lot stays empty six days a week. And the wait for those families stays where it is.

A D at rush hour is a cost. I'll pay it too, every day, on my own street. Three years on a waiting list is a bigger cost, and other people are paying that one right now.

After 19 years on Alder Road, I'd be glad to have these families as neighbors. Please approve this project.

Thank you.
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
