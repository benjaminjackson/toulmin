I'm going to speak at our county planning commission tonight in favor of a housing project, and I want help writing what I'll say. The church I belong to wants to build 64 apartments on its back parking lot. All of them would rent to households earning under 60% of the area median income.

Facts:
- The church lot is 1.8 acres and sits empty 6 days a week.
- The county's own housing report says we are short about 2,100 affordable homes.
- The traffic study estimates 310 more car trips a day on Alder Road, and says the intersection at Alder and 4th would drop from a C rating to a D at rush hour.
- The plan has 72 parking spaces for the 64 units.
- 412 households are on the county housing authority's waiting list. The average wait is 3 years.
- I've lived on Alder Road for 19 years, two blocks from the church.

Each speaker gets 3 minutes.
</brief>



<document>
Good evening, Commissioners. My name is [Your name].

I've lived on Alder Road for 19 years. I live two blocks from the church that's applying tonight. I'm also a member of that church. So I'm here as a neighbor, and I'm not neutral.

I'm asking you to vote yes on the 64 apartments behind the church.

I'll start with traffic. That's what my neighbors are worried about, and I share that road with them.

The traffic study says this project adds 310 car trips a day on Alder. It says the intersection at Alder and 4th drops from a C to a D at rush hour. I drive through that intersection. A D means I'll wait longer there. I'm not going to stand here and tell you that doesn't matter.

I'm going to tell you what we get in exchange.

Your own housing report says this county is short about 2,100 affordable homes.

Right now, 412 households are on the housing authority's waiting list. The average wait is three years.

Every one of these 64 apartments would rent to a household earning under 60 percent of the area median income. That's homes for about one in six of the families on that list.

And look at where it would go. Not a field. Not anyone's backyard. A parking lot. It's 1.8 acres, and it sits empty six days a week. I walk past it. Monday through Saturday, it's just asphalt.

On parking: the plan has 72 spaces for 64 apartments. That's one for every unit, plus eight more.

So here's the trade. Those of us on Alder will wait longer at 4th Street at rush hour. And 64 households stop waiting for a home.

If the intersection is your concern, I'd ask you to deal with it directly. Attach a condition to the approval, like a review of the signal timing at Alder and 4th. But please don't solve it by turning down the homes.

If you say no, that lot stays empty six days a week. And 412 households keep waiting.

I've been lucky enough to live on this road for 19 years. I'd like some of the families on that list to get the chance to say the same.

Please vote yes. Thank you.
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
