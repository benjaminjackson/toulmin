Good evening, commissioners. My name is ______. I've lived on Alder Road for 19 years, two blocks from the church. I'm also a member of that church, and I want you to know that up front.

I'm here to ask you to approve the 64 apartments on the church's back parking lot.

I'll start with traffic, because I know that's the concern.

The traffic study says this project adds 310 car trips a day on Alder Road. It says the intersection at Alder and 4th drops from a C to a D at rush hour.

I drive through that intersection. That means I'll be one of the people waiting a little longer. I've thought about that, and I still think this is worth it.

If the commission wants to attach a condition on the Alder and 4th intersection, I would support it. Approve the homes, and fix the intersection too.

On parking: the plan has 72 spaces for 64 units. That's one for every apartment, plus eight more.

Now, here's why I think it's worth it.

The county's own housing report says we're short about 2,100 affordable homes.

Right now, 412 households are on the housing authority's waiting list. The average wait is three years.

Every one of these 64 apartments would rent to a household earning under 60 percent of the area median income. That's roughly one home for every six families on that waiting list.

And the land is already there. It's 1.8 acres of pavement that sits empty six days a week. Nobody has to tear anything down. No one has to be moved out. It's a parking lot.

This doesn't solve the shortage. Sixty-four homes is about three percent of what the county says we need. But we won't close a gap of 2,100 homes unless we say yes to projects like this one, one at a time.

If we say no tonight, the lot stays empty six days a week. The waiting list stays at 412. And those families keep waiting.

I've been a neighbor on Alder Road for 19 years. I'd like some of the people on that waiting list to become my neighbors too.

Please approve this project. Thank you.
