Good evening, commissioners. My name is ________.

I've lived on Alder Road for 19 years. My house is two blocks from the church. I'll say up front that I'm a member of that church. But I'm here tonight as a neighbor too, because the traffic from this project will go right past my house.

I'm asking you to approve the 64 apartments on the church's back parking lot.

I'll start with traffic, because I know that's the worry.

The county's study says this adds 310 car trips a day on Alder Road. It says the intersection at Alder and 4th drops from a C to a D at rush hour. That's real. I drive through that intersection. I'll be sitting in that longer wait along with everyone else.

I've weighed that, and I still think this project is worth it. Here's why.

First, parking. The plan has 72 spaces for 64 apartments. That's more than one space for every home. The people who live there will park on that lot, not on our street.

Second, the land. That lot is 1.8 acres of pavement. It sits empty six days a week. We wouldn't be losing a field or a park. We'd be using asphalt that's already there.

Third, the need. The county's own housing report says we're short about 2,100 affordable homes. Right now, 412 households are on the housing authority's waiting list. The average wait is three years.

Every one of these 64 apartments would rent to a household earning under 60 percent of the area median income. That's about one home for every six households on that list.

I won't pretend 64 homes closes a gap of 2,100. It doesn't. But it's 64 families who stop waiting. And it's on land that's already paved and almost never used.

If you say no tonight, that lot stays empty six days a week. And the wait for those families stays where it is.

A D at rush hour is a cost. I'll pay it too, every day, on my own street. Three years on a waiting list is a bigger cost, and other people are paying that one right now.

After 19 years on Alder Road, I'd be glad to have these families as neighbors. Please approve this project.

Thank you.
