**Project Narrative: Refrigerated Van for Fresh Food Recovery**

**The Need**

We are a volunteer-run food bank serving a town of about 9,000 people. Each month we distribute about 38,000 pounds of food to 610 households. Only about 6,000 pounds of that is fresh produce, milk and eggs.

Our fresh food is limited by transportation, not supply. Last summer, local farms offered us roughly 9,000 pounds of produce over three months. We had to turn all of it down because we had no way to pick it up cold or store it safely.

**The Project**

We request $50,000 toward a $68,000 refrigerated van. We will cover the remaining $18,000 from our reserves. For a volunteer organization, that is a serious commitment: it reduces our reserves to $11,000. We are making it because cold transport is what stands between our clients and fresh food that is already available in our community.

The van will let us collect produce directly from farms and keep it cold until distribution. The project is staffed from day one: four of our 23 regular volunteers have committed to driving on a weekly schedule.

**Expected Results**

In the first year, our goal is to accept the farm produce we currently turn away: at least 9,000 additional pounds of fresh food. During the harvest months, that would raise our fresh distribution by about 50 percent, from roughly 18,000 to 27,000 pounds over three months. Across the full year, it is an increase of about 12 percent.

We see that as a floor. Once local farms know we can collect cold, we will ask them for more, and we will approach grocers and other donors for refrigerated items outside the growing season. We have not included those possible gains in the figures above.

**How We Will Measure It**

We will record pounds of fresh food received and distributed each month, by source, and compare them with our current baseline of about 6,000 pounds per month. We will also track the number of farm pickups and any offered food we still have to decline, so we can see whether the van is meeting demand.

**Accountability**

The Foundation supported us with $12,000 for shelving in 2023. Our final report on that grant was four months late. That was our responsibility, and we take it seriously. For this grant, we will put every reporting deadline on our board calendar at the time of award and assign one named volunteer to prepare each report, with a board member responsible for submitting it on time.

**Why It Matters**

Much of the fresh food our neighbors need is grown a few miles away. Right now, we have to say no to it. With the Foundation's help and our own reserves, we can start saying yes next summer.
