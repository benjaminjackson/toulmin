**Subject: [Company] quarterly update: flat revenue, a big loss, and what we're doing about it**

Hi all,

First, an apology: I didn't send updates for the last two quarters. That was a mistake, especially going into a quarter like this one. You'll hear from me every quarter from now on.

**The numbers**
- Monthly recurring revenue: $41,000, flat from three months ago
- Clinics: 9 added, 4 lost
- Cash: $480,000
- Net burn: about $44,000/month, which gives us about 11 months of runway
- Team: 6

**What happened**
Our largest customer left this quarter. They were a 12-clinic chain paying $5,500/month, about 13% of our revenue, and they moved to the scheduler built into their electronic records system. The 9 new clinics we added made up for that loss. That's why revenue is flat and not down.

I don't think this was a one-off. Records vendors are bundling scheduling, and it's hard to compete with "good enough and already included" for chains that already run everything inside one system. Independent and smaller clinics are a different situation, but I'd be fooling myself (and you) if I treated this as bad luck.

**What's working**
Clinics that use our automatic text reminders have a no-show rate of 7%, down from 13% before they started. That's nearly half. For a clinic, a no-show is lost revenue they can measure directly, so this is the clearest return we offer.

**What's next**
Next month we start selling the reminder feature on its own for $150/month. There are two reasons:
1. It's the part of our product with proven, measurable value.
2. It lets us sell to clinics that are happy with their current scheduler, including the ones built into records systems, so we don't have to compete with those schedulers head-on.

Next quarter I'll report on standalone reminder sign-ups, churn and revenue, so you can judge whether this is working.
