You are the reader described between the <reader> tags. Read the document between the <document> tags as that reader would receive it.

The brief between the <brief> tags is what the writer was asked to do and shows the facts the writer had. You, as the reader, have not seen the brief. Use it only to check what the document does with those facts.

<reader>
The lead angel investor, who put in $250,000 and has the right to join the next round. The founder skipped the last two quarterly updates, and the investor said so in a short, cold email last month. The investor decides whether to keep backing the company and taking the founder's calls.
</reader>

<brief>
I need to send a quarterly update to our investors. I'm the founder of a scheduling app for physical therapy clinics. We have 6 people.

Numbers for the quarter:
- Monthly recurring revenue is $41,000, the same as three months ago.
- We added 9 clinics and lost 4. One of the 4 was our largest customer, a 12-clinic chain that paid $5,500 a month. They moved to the scheduler built into their electronic records system.
- We have $480,000 in the bank and lose about $44,000 a month.
- Clinics that use our automatic text reminders have a no-show rate of 7%, against 13% before they started.
- I didn't send updates for the last two quarters.
- Next month we start selling the reminder feature on its own for $150 a month.

Under 500 words. It goes to 11 investors.
</brief>



<document>
Subject: Quarterly update – November 2026

Hi all,

First, an apology: this is the first update you've had from me in six months. I should have written through the last two quarters, especially since this one has bad news in it. From now on you'll hear from me every quarter. The next update will reach you by February 15.

**The short version**
- Monthly recurring revenue: $41,000, flat with three months ago
- Cash: $480,000
- Net burn: about $44,000 a month, so roughly 11 months of runway
- Team: 6

**What happened**
We added 9 clinics and lost 4. One of the 4 was our largest customer, a 12-clinic chain paying $5,500 a month, about 13% of our revenue. They moved to the scheduler built into their electronic records system. New clinics and the rest of our base made up the difference, which is why revenue is flat rather than down. But flat is not good enough, and the reason we lost them is a risk I take seriously: records vendors bundling a scheduler that's "good enough."

**What's working**
Clinics that use our automatic text reminders have a 7% no-show rate, down from 13% before they started. That's almost half of their missed appointments gone. This is the clearest result we have, and it's what we're building on.

**What we're doing about it**
Next month we start selling the reminders on their own for $150 a month. That lets us sell to clinics that keep the scheduler in their records system, which is exactly the situation that cost us our largest customer. For scale: about 37 reminder-only customers would replace that chain's $5,500.

**What I'll report in February**
- How many reminder-only customers we've signed. Our goal is 20 by the end of March.
- Monthly recurring revenue and runway.
- Our plan for the next 12 months. With 11 months of cash, I'll tell you whether we're raising, and when, or how we're cutting burn. You'll have that decision in the February update, not a list of options.

**How you can help**
If you know an owner or operations lead at a physical therapy clinic, especially one whose records system includes its own scheduler, an introduction would help more than anything else right now. Just reply with a name and I'll take it from there.

Thank you for your patience, and for sticking with us.

Ben
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
