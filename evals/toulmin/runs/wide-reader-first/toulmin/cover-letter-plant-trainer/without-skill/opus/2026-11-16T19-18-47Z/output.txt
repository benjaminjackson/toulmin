I left out the line promising to cut new-hire training time in half. I recommend keeping it out, for three reasons:

- **You can't back it up yet.** You haven't seen how their training works, how long it takes, or what the regulations require it to include. A hiring manager at a GMP plant will notice that.
- **It can backfire there.** In regulated manufacturing, training content and sign-offs are often fixed by procedure. Promising to cut training in half can sound like you'd cut corners, which is the opposite of what they want.
- **It clashes with your strongest point.** Your safety record and AP results are real and measured. An unsupported claim next to them makes them look weaker.

The letter instead says you'll learn their process, find where new hires lose time, and measure whether changes help. If you get a figure for their current onboarding time in an interview, you can make a specific claim then.
