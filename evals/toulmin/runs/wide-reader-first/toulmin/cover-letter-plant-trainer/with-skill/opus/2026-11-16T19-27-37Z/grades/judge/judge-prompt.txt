<document>
Dear [Hiring Manager's Name],

I'm applying for the Training Coordinator position at [Company]. For nine years I've taught high school chemistry, which means teaching hazardous procedures to people who have never done them before and checking that they can do them safely. I've never worked in a regulated industry, and I have no experience with GMP training records. I'd like to explain why I think I can close that gap quickly, and what you can hold me to if I do.

Every year I teach about 140 students across five classes. In 2019 I wrote our school's lab safety program. In the seven years since, there hasn't been a lab injury that needed more than first aid. I also train each new science teacher on lab procedures, about two people a year, 12 hours each. They are adults who arrive knowing their subject but not our procedures, and they need to be working safely and on their own quickly. I expect your new hires are in much the same position.

I also measure whether my teaching works. In the four years I've taught AP Chemistry, our pass rate has gone from 41% to 58%. I did it by finding where students were failing and changing how I taught those parts, and I would bring that same approach to your training.

I know I'd be learning two things: GMP documentation requirements and your learning management system. My current platform is Google Classroom. My plan is to be fluent in both within my first 60 days. By day 90, I'll give you a measurement of how long new hires currently take to reach sign-off, along with a specific target for shortening that time without weakening compliance and a date for reaching it. If the target isn't met, I'll report why and what I'm changing.

I live about 30 minutes from the plant and would welcome the chance to talk about how your training program runs today.

Sincerely,
[Your Name]
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
