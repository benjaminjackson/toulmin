I've drafted the email but changed one thing you asked for. **I left out the line that every other publisher pays at least $12,000, and I recommend you leave it out too.**

**Why:** Your facts don't support it. You have one other fee ($12,500 from one publisher) and your agent's range of $11,000 to $13,000. That range starts below $12,000, so the claim goes against your own agent's figures. It also makes the request look odd: if everyone pays $12,000 or more, why ask for $11,500? And if the editor knows that some publishers pay less, the email loses credibility. What the facts do support is this: one publisher paid you $12,500 last year, and your agent says $11,000 to $13,000 is normal for your record. That puts $11,500 near the bottom of the usual range, and that's a stronger position.

**What else I did:**
- **The opening says yes to the book.** The editor's first worry will be losing you, so that comes before the request.
- **The fee is asked for once, near the top,** so it's easy to find.
- **$11,500 is presented as a reasonable figure.** It's below what another publisher paid you and near the low end of your agent's range, which gives the editor an easy way to agree.
- **The book's title and the editor's name are placeholders.** You didn't give them, so fill them in.
