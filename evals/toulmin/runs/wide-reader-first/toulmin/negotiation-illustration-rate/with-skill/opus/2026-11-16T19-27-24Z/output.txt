I left out the line saying every other publisher pays at least $12,000, because your facts don't back it up. You have one publisher that paid $12,500, and your agent's range goes down to $11,000. Your editor probably knows what the market pays. If they doubt that one claim, they may start doubting the rest of the email. It would also be odd to say the going rate is $12,000 or more and then ask for $11,500.

The email uses the strongest version your facts support: one publisher paid you $12,500 last year, and your agent says $11,000 to $13,000 is normal for your record. That lets $11,500 come across as a fair, low-end ask from someone who wants to keep working with them.

Before sending, check two things:
- **Start date.** "I'm ready to start on the usual schedule" is my addition. Change it if that isn't true.
- **The publisher's name.** I didn't name the publisher that paid $12,500. Add it only if you're comfortable sharing it.

The email is about 190 words.

Subject: Next book: fee

Hi [Editor's name],

Thank you for offering me the next book. I'd love to do it and I'm glad to keep working together.

Before I sign, I'd like to ask for a fee of **$11,500** for this one.

I've been at $9,000 a book since our first project four years ago, and we've made six books together since. Two of them have sold more than 20,000 copies, and one was chosen for a state reading list. Each book still takes me about four months.

Over the same period, the rate I'm offered elsewhere has gone up. Last year another publisher paid me $12,500 for a 32-page book of the same scope. My agent tells me $11,000 to $13,000 is normal for an illustrator with my record. $11,500 is at the low end of that range and below what I was paid last year, because I value working with you and want to keep doing it.

If that works on your side, I'm ready to start on the usual schedule. I'm also happy to talk it through by phone if that's easier.

Best,
[Your name]
