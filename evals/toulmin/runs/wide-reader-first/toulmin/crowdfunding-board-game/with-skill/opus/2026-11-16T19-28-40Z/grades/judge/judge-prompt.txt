**Get a lighthouse keeper's family through one long winter, together.**

This is a cooperative board game for 1 to 4 players. You don't play against each other. You win or lose as one household, side by side at the table, until spring comes or it doesn't.

**Back it for $49, shipping in the US included.** That gets you one copy of the finished game.

**Who's making it**

I'll be upfront: this is my first published game, and I work full time as a land surveyor. If you've backed a first-time creator before, you may be wondering whether the game is finished and whether the money will be enough. Here's what I can show you on both.

**Is the game finished?**

I've been testing it since 2023. That's 140 play sessions with about 60 different people, many of whom had never met me. I took it to two conventions so strangers could play it without me hovering. Twenty-three of those players have rated it on the main board game ratings site, and the average is 8.1. That's a small number of ratings, and I won't pretend otherwise. But every one of them comes from someone who sat down and played it.

The rules have been rewritten many times in those 140 sessions. What you'd be funding is printing the game, not designing it.

**Is the money enough?**

I have a factory quote: **$17,400 for 1,500 copies**, or $11.60 a copy. Shipping each copy to a backer costs about $9. Here is where the $28,000 goal goes:

- **$17,400:** the whole print run of 1,500 copies
- **About $5,150:** shipping to the roughly 572 backers it takes to reach the goal
- **About $5,450:** platform fees and a cushion for anything that costs more than quoted

The goal pays for the full print run, not a partial one. If we don't reach $28,000, no one is charged and nothing is printed.

**What I'll commit to**

I'll post an update on the 1st of every month until your copy ships: where production stands, what's late and what I'm doing about it. If something goes wrong, you'll hear it from me first, not discover it in the comments.

**Why I made it**

My job is measuring land: coastlines, boundaries, the places where the ground meets the water. I wanted a game about the people who lived at the edge of that map. They kept a light burning for strangers while getting through a winter of their own. It's a game you play with people, not against them, and the 60 people who've sat at that table taught me what it needed to be.

**1,240 people signed up to hear when this launched.** If that's you, thank you. You're why I'm confident enough to press the button. Back it at $49, and let's get this family through the winter.

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
