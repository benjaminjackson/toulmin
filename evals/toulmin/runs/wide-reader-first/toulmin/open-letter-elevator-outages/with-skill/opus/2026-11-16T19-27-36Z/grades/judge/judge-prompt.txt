<document>
**An Open Letter to [Management Company] from the [Building Name] Tenants' Association**

To [Management Company]:

Our building's only elevator has been out of service on 41 days so far this year. That is more than one day in every eight. In August it was out for 12 days in a row.

We are asking you for three things, in writing, by [date]:

1. **The controller part.** Tell us when it was ordered, who supplies it, and the confirmed delivery date.
2. **A plan for outages.** Tell us what happens the next time the elevator stops. For example: advance notice to residents, a number to call, and help for residents who cannot use the stairs.
3. **The second elevator.** Share the assessment behind your view that a second elevator cannot be built here.

Here is why this matters. Our building has 9 stories and 112 apartments, all served by one elevator. 23 of our residents are over 75. Six use wheelchairs or walkers. For them, a day without the elevator can mean a day without leaving home, or a day without getting back to it.

We know the city inspected the elevator in September and gave it a passing certificate. We are not saying the elevator is unsafe. A certificate tells us the elevator is safe when it runs. It does not keep it running, and that is our concern.

In June, you told us a new controller part was on order and would arrive in 6 to 8 weeks. Five months later, you have told us the same thing. We want to believe the new estimate. A confirmed delivery date from the supplier would let us.

74 of our 112 households, about two-thirds of the building, signed a petition asking for a second elevator. You have told us it would cost about $400,000 and cannot be done in this building. We would like to understand why. If a second elevator really is not possible, then the one we have must be reliable, and a plan for the days it is not becomes even more important.

None of these requests costs you anything to answer. We would welcome a meeting with you before [date]. When we receive your written reply, we will post it in the lobby next to this letter so that every resident can read it.

We look forward to working with you on this.

Sincerely,

[Name]
Secretary, on behalf of the [Building Name] Tenants' Association
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
