Good evening, commissioners. I'm Captain Ray Dunn. I've run the charter boat Sea Lark out of our harbor for 22 years, about 160 trips a season.

I don't dispute your staff's numbers. The stock is at 78 percent of target, and it needs help. I'm here to ask you to give it that help with a 3-fish limit or a shorter season instead of cutting to 2 fish.

Here's what I know from my own books. The last time the limit was cut, in 2016, my bookings fell from 170 trips to 138 the next season. That's 32 trips, close to $29,000 at today's rates. It took two years to climb back to 155, and I never got back to 170.

This time looks worse. Our charter association surveyed 70 past customers. Forty-one said they would book fewer trips at 2 fish. Only 12 said they'd book the same.

There are 11 charter boats in our harbor. I can only speak for my own books. But two other captains have told me that if bookings fall the way they did in 2016, they'll sell. Each boat that goes takes crew jobs and customers that the docks, tackle shops and restaurants count on.

Charter boats land about 9 percent of the state's summer flounder. Private boats land about 80. For a private angler, 2 fish means a shorter day. For us, it decides whether customers book at all.

We want to help you get the data right. I'll give your staff my trip logs from 2016 on, and our association will test a catch-reporting app with your staff next season.

Please choose a 3-fish limit or a shorter season. Either one lets the stock recover and keeps our boats running. Thank you.
