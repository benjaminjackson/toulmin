Good evening. I've run a charter boat out of this harbor for 22 years. I take out about 160 trips a season.

I'm not here to dispute your staff's numbers. The stock is at 78 percent of its target, and nobody needs it rebuilt more than the people who fish it for a living. I'm asking you to do it with a three-fish limit, or a shorter season, instead of two fish.

Here's why. The last time the limit was cut, in 2016, my bookings fell from 170 trips to 138 the next season. A fifth of my business was gone in one year. Two years later I was back to 155. Today I'm at 160. Ten years on, I still haven't gotten all of it back.

A drop like that today would cost me about 30 trips, around 27,000 dollars. Our charter association asked 70 past customers what they'd do at two fish. Forty-one said they'd book fewer trips. Only 12 said they'd book the same. Two other captains in this harbor have told me that if bookings fall like they did in 2016, they'll sell their boats. And this cut would run three years, not one.

Charter boats land about 9 percent of the state's summer flounder. Private boats land about 80. Cutting our customers to two fish saves a small share of the fish and takes a big share of our living.

I want to help you get this right. I'll give your staff my trip logs from 2016 forward. Our association will test a catch-reporting app with your staff next season, so your next decision has better charter data behind it.

Three fish, or a shorter season. Either one still cuts the catch, and it gives this harbor a chance to keep working. Thank you.
