Good evening, Commissioners. I'm Captain Ray Dunn. I've run the charter boat Sea Lark for 22 years, one of eleven charter boats in our harbor.

I'm not here to dispute your staff's stock numbers, and we'll accept a cut. I'm asking you to make it a three-fish limit or a shorter season, not two fish.

I can tell you what the last cut did, because I keep the books. In 2016, the season after the limit was cut, my bookings fell from 170 trips to 138. That's almost one trip in five. Two years later I was back up to 155, still fifteen short. This cut would halve the limit, and it would last three years.

Our charter association asked 70 past customers about a two-fish limit. Forty-one said they'd book fewer trips. Only twelve said they'd book the same.

I run about 160 trips a season at $900 a trip. If this cut hits like the last one, that's about 30 trips and $27,000 a year off my boat, three years running. Two other captains in our harbor have told me that if bookings drop the way they did in 2016, they'll sell.

Charter boats land about 9% of this state's summer flounder. Private boats land about 80%. We're a small share of the catch, but on a charter boat the limit decides whether the trip gets booked at all.

Here's what we'll bring. I'll give your staff my trip logs from 2016 on. Our charter association will test a catch-reporting app with your staff next season. And we ask that staff weigh a three-fish limit or a shorter season before you vote on two.

Thank you.
