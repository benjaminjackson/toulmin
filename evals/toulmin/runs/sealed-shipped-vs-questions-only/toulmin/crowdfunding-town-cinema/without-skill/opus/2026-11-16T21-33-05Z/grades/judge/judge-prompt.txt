**Bring the Majestic Back**

The Majestic on Main Street went dark in 2022. Before it closed, it was selling about 180 tickets a week. Since then the nearest cinema has been 38 miles away, so seeing a film means a 76-mile round trip.

We can change that. The building's owner has agreed to rent the Majestic to us for $1 a year for ten years if we raise $85,000 to replace the projector and sound system and fix the roof.

A local bank has pledged $25,000 if we raise the other $60,000 by June 30. Three local businesses have already pledged $11,000 in writing.

**If you gave last time**

Three years ago we tried to raise $120,000. We reached $47,000 and refunded every dollar. This goal is $35,000 lower, and a bank and three businesses have committed before the campaign even opens. The promise is the same as last time: this is all-or-nothing. If we don't reach $85,000 by June 30, every pledge is returned in full.

**If we make it**

Forty-six volunteers have already signed up to run the box office and the snack bar. We plan to reopen by Thanksgiving. Every three months we'll post a public report at the town library showing how the money was spent.

**Two things you should know up front**

Our roof estimate is from last fall, and the contractor has told us it could rise by as much as 15%. We also can't promise every new release in its opening week, because that depends on the film distributors.

The campaign runs from May 1 to June 30. Please pledge what you can, and tell a neighbor. Let's turn the lights back on at the Majestic.

Ruth Albers
Chair, [Group name]

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
