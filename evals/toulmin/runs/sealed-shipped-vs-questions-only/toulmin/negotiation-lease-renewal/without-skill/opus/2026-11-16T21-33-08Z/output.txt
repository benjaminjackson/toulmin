Dear Mr. Halloran,

Thank you for your renewal offer. Pho Lan has been in your building for ten years, and I would like to stay for five more.

I can't pay $5,650 a month and keep the restaurant open. Here is what I can offer:

- $5,100 a month in the first year, going up 3% each year after that. By year five, rent would be about $5,740 a month.
- A new 5-year lease, signed within two weeks of agreeing on terms.
- Rent paid by automatic bank transfer on the 1st of every month.

I know I paid late twice in early 2024. Our walk-in cooler failed and we had to close for nine days. I'm grateful you waived the late fees, and I have paid on time every month since. With automatic payment, you won't need to think about it again.

In 2019 I put about $70,000 into the kitchen, including a new hood system that stays with the building. A new restaurant tenant would pay around $40,000 to install one like it.

I've also noticed that the unit two doors down has been listed at $28 a square foot for 14 months. Renewing with me means no empty months, no build-out, and no break in rent from a tenant who has been here for ten years.

I'd like to settle this before June 1 so I have time to plan before the lease ends in August. I'm happy to meet or talk by phone whenever suits you.

Thank you for considering this.

Sincerely,

[Your name]
Owner, Pho Lan

A few choices I made that you can change:

- **"Mr. Halloran":** If you're unsure, it's the safer choice. If he writes back using your first name, you can do the same from then on.
- **The coffee chain:** I left it out. If the interest is real, raising it could start an argument you can't win. If it's a bargaining move, pointing out that the nearby unit has been empty for 14 months answers it without challenging him.
- **The 6% sales drop:** I left this out too. It does explain why you can't pay more, but it would also make him worry about whether you'll keep paying. If he asks why $5,100 is your limit, you can bring it up in person.
- **Year-five rent:** The "$5,740" figure tells him that your offer passes his number by the end of the lease. Be aware that over the full five years you'd still pay less in total than his $5,650 flat rate. That's only true if his $5,650 stays flat, which I don't know.
