Last year, 1,140 eviction cases were filed in our county. Our clinic's two attorneys represented the tenant in 163 of them, about one in seven. We turned away roughly 300 more tenants who asked for help because no attorney was free. Each of our attorneys carries about 80 open cases, and we cannot take on more without another lawyer.

We request $90,000 over two years ($45,000 per year) to fund an additional eviction-defense attorney.

In the cases where we represented the tenant last year, 58% ended with the tenant staying in the home or getting at least 60 days to move. We do not yet have a comparable figure for tenants without a lawyer. Starting in January 2027, we will request court-wide outcome data from the county clerk so that our results can be measured against it.

The position costs $78,000 per year with benefits. A family fund has pledged $20,000 per year for both years. Together with this grant, that covers 83% of the cost, and the clinic will pay the remaining $13,000 per year from its general budget. That budget includes a $60,000 county contract that comes up for renewal in October, and we cannot guarantee it will be renewed.

One of our two attorneys leaves in June. Our last attorney search took five months. The departing attorney has agreed to spend her final month training the new hire.

Every six months, we will report to the Foundation on the cases the new attorney has opened and how they ended.

Without this position, we will go on turning away tenants at last year's rate of about 300 a year.
