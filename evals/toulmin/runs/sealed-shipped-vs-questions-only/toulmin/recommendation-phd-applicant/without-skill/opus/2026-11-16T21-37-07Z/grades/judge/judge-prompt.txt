Dear Members of the Admissions Committee,

I am writing in strong support of Priya [Surname]'s application to your PhD program in marine ecology. I have taught biology at Westbrook College for 17 years and supervised about 30 senior theses. Priya is one of the three best students I have taught in that time.

Priya took three courses with me. In two of them she earned the top grade in a class of 28.

Her senior thesis asked whether oyster growth differs across six sites in our bay. Over 14 months she carried out all of the fieldwork herself, which took about 90 trips by kayak in every season. That kind of persistence is what long-term field ecology requires, and few undergraduates have shown it at this scale. She found faster growth at the two sites near the salt marsh. This is a single study, not yet repeated, and a storm cost one site three months of data, so the result needs another season before it can be confirmed. I believe the work could form the basis of a publishable paper with an additional season of data. Priya presented it as a poster at a regional conference.

Last summer Priya worked as a paid field assistant at a state shellfish lab. Her supervisor there told me he would hire her again. For two weeks he left her to run the lab's water sampling on her own. Students do not get that kind of trust from a working lab unless they have earned it.

Priya does not yet have a publication. I would ask the committee to weigh what she does have. She has more than a year of sustained fieldwork on a real ecological question and a clear first result she has presented in public. She also has the confidence of a professional lab that has seen her work. What she needs now is the training and time a doctoral program provides to turn work like this into published research. I am confident she will make good use of both.

I recommend Priya to you without reservation and would be glad to answer any questions.

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
