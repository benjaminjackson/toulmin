Dear Admissions Committee,

I am writing in strong support of Priya Nair's application to your doctoral program in marine ecology. I have taught biology at Westbrook College for seventeen years and supervised about thirty senior theses. Priya is one of the three best students I have taught in that time.

I know her work well. She took three courses with me, and in two of them she earned the top grade in a class of twenty-eight.

Her senior thesis is the best evidence of what she would bring to a PhD program. She compared oyster growth at six sites in our bay over fourteen months, and she did the fieldwork herself. That came to about ninety trips by kayak, roughly six a month, in every season of the year. Few undergraduates can sustain that kind of field schedule for more than a year. Priya did, without missing her sampling. She found faster growth at the two sites near the salt marsh, and she presented the work as a poster at a regional conference.

I want to be precise about where the results stand. This was a single study that has not yet been repeated, and one of the six sites lost three months of data after a storm. The salt-marsh result is promising but not yet established. In my judgment the thesis is a sound basis for a publishable paper, and with a second season of data I expect the work could reach a peer-reviewed journal. Doctoral training is what would allow her to pursue questions like this one rigorously.

Last summer she worked as a paid field assistant at a state shellfish laboratory. For two weeks she ran the lab's water sampling on her own. Her supervisor there told me he would hire her again.

Long-term field ecology requires a researcher who keeps going when the work is repetitive and the conditions are poor, and who can be trusted to do it right without supervision. Priya has already shown both, in her own project and in a professional lab. I recommend her without reservation, and I would be glad to discuss her further.

Sincerely,

Dr. Anne Caldwell
Westbrook College
