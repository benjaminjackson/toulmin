Dear Admissions Committee,

I am writing in strong support of Priya Nair's application to your PhD program in marine ecology. I have taught biology at Westbrook College for 17 years and supervised about 30 senior theses. Priya is one of the three best students I have taught in that time.

I know her work well. She took three courses with me and earned the top grade in a class of 28 in two of them. The clearest measure of her ability, though, is her senior thesis, which I supervised.

Priya compared oyster growth at six sites in our bay over 14 months. She did the fieldwork herself, making about 90 trips by kayak. That is roughly one trip every five days, through every season. Fieldwork on that schedule tests persistence as much as skill, and she kept it up for more than a year. She found that oysters grew faster at the two sites nearest the salt marsh, and she presented the work as a poster at a regional conference.

I want to be precise about where the study stands. It covers a single year and has not yet been repeated. The marsh effect rests on two sites, and a storm cost one site three months of data. These are the natural limits of a one-year undergraduate project carried out by one person. Her results are a solid basis for a publishable paper. With a second season of data at the marsh sites, I would expect the work to go to a peer-reviewed journal. That kind of next step is exactly what doctoral training is built to support.

Her work outside the classroom confirms what I have seen. Last summer she was a paid field assistant at a state shellfish lab, where she ran the lab's water sampling on her own for two weeks. Her supervisor told me he would hire her again.

Priya has already shown that she can sustain demanding fieldwork, produce a clear result and present it to other scientists. She is ready for doctoral research, and I recommend her without reservation.

Sincerely,

Dr. Anne Caldwell
Westbrook College
