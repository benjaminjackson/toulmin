## Dinner shouldn't be a math problem

Living with type 2 diabetes means thinking about carbs at every meal. That means reading labels, guessing portions and wondering whether tonight's dinner will push your numbers up. Steady Plate does that planning for you.

Every meal has **45 grams of carbohydrate or less**. I'm Lena Ortiz, RD, and I plan every menu myself. You heat the meal and eat it. That's all.

## What customers have seen

Fourteen of our customers shared their A1C results with me from before they started and after three months. **10 of those 14 saw their A1C go down, by an average of 0.6 points.** Three stayed about the same, and one went up. Everyone's body and treatment are different, so I can't promise any particular result. But these are real numbers from real customers.

Steady Plate meals don't replace your doctor's advice. They're meant to fit alongside the care plan you already have. Keep working with your doctor on medication and testing.

## How it works

- **A call with me first.** Before your first delivery, we'll have a 15-minute phone call so I can understand your needs.
- **10 meals a week**, lunches and dinners, delivered every Sunday.
- **Swap any meal you don't want** for one of 6 other choices each week.
- **Order by Thursday at noon** for that Sunday's delivery.
- **The menu runs on a 4-week cycle**, so you'll get to know your favorites.

## What it costs

- **Trial week:** 10 meals for $59
- **Full plan:** 10 meals for $129 a week, about $12.90 a meal

There's no contract on the full plan. Skip a week whenever you need to, or cancel any time.

## Where we deliver

We deliver within **25 miles of downtown**. If you're not sure you're in range, check your address when you order.

The draft is about 400 words. A few notes:

- **Trial rollover:** You weren't sure whether the trial rolls into the full plan automatically, so the page doesn't say either way. Check this before the page goes live. If it does roll over, add a line saying so next to the price. People who get charged without expecting it tend to cancel and ask for refunds.
- **1 in 5 trial conversion:** I left this out. It's useful for your own planning, but it wouldn't persuade a buyer.
- **The A1C line** gives the full 10 / 3 / 1 split along with the average. Showing the misses makes the result more believable. If a regulator or a doctor reads the page, they'll see you didn't pick only the good results.
- **Audience:** Since you don't know who reads it, the page speaks to the person with diabetes. That copy also works for a family member buying for them. If you learn that many orders come from caregivers or referring doctors, the headline and opening section are what I'd change first.
