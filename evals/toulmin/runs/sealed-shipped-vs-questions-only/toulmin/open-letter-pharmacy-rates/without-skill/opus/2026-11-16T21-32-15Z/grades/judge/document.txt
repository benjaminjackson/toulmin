**An open letter to the State Employees' Health Plan**
**From 38 independent pharmacies, about the July 1 pharmacy contract**

Since January, the State Employees' Health Plan has paid our pharmacies less than it costs us to fill your members' prescriptions. For the 20 generic drugs we fill most often, the plan pays us on average $1.80 less per prescription than our cost.

That is not a small corner of our business. Plan members account for about 22% of the prescriptions filled at our 38 pharmacies, so more than one prescription in five is a state employee or a family member. Some of us make up part of the loss on brand-name drugs. That covers only part of it, and it doesn't explain why a state plan should pay below cost for the generics we fill most.

This matters most to your members outside the cities. In 14 counties, the nearest pharmacy for most people is an independent one. For most people there, the nearest chain pharmacy is more than 20 miles away. Nine independent pharmacies in this state have closed in the past two years. We don't know how many of them closed because of rates. We do know that two of the 38 pharmacies signing this letter say they'll stop filling prescriptions for plan members if nothing changes. When a pharmacy in one of those counties stops, a state employee who needs a refill has a 40-mile round trip or a mail-order wait.

The plan can fix this, and the date to do it is coming up. Your contract with your pharmacy benefit manager renews on July 1. We ask that the renewed contract require pharmacies to be paid at least what the drug costs them, plus a fee for filling the prescription.

You don't have to take our word for the numbers. We will give your staff our purchase invoices for all 20 drugs so you can check our costs against what the plan pays. I'll meet with your staff any time before May 1, early enough for whatever you learn to go into the contract.

We want to keep filling prescriptions for state employees. We've done it for years, in towns where we are often the only pharmacy. We are asking the plan to make sure that in the July 1 contract, filling a prescription doesn't cost us more than we're paid for it.

Tom Reilly
Owner, Reilly Drug, Carver
On behalf of the Main Street Pharmacy Alliance, 38 independent pharmacies
