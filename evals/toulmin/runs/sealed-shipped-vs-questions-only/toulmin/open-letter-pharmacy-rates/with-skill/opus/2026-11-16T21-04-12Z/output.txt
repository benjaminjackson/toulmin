**An open letter to the State Employees' Health Plan**
*From the Main Street Pharmacy Alliance, on behalf of 38 independent pharmacies*

On July 1, the plan's contract with its pharmacy benefit manager comes up for renewal. We ask the plan to use that renewal to make sure pharmacies are paid at least what a drug costs us, plus a fee for filling it.

Since January, for the 20 generic drugs we fill most, the plan has paid us on average $1.80 less per prescription than it costs us to buy and fill it. These are the prescriptions plan members bring us most often. Plan members account for about 22 percent of the prescriptions at our 38 pharmacies. At my own pharmacy, Reilly Drug in Carver, we filled about 3,100 prescriptions for plan members last year.

Some of us make up part of that loss on brand-name drugs, but only part. A pharmacy cannot keep filling its most common prescriptions below cost and count on other drugs to cover the difference.

If the contract renews on these terms, two pharmacies in our alliance have told me they will stop filling prescriptions for plan members. We expect others to follow. Nine independent pharmacies in the state have closed in the last two years. We can't tell you how many of them closed because of rates, and we won't claim they all did. But in 14 counties, the nearest pharmacy for most people is an independent one, and the nearest chain is more than 20 miles away. When a pharmacy there closes or stops taking the plan, plan members in that county don't save money. They drive a round trip of more than 40 miles for a refill.

The plan may say that its pharmacy benefit manager sets these rates. The manager sets them under a contract the plan signs, and on July 1 the plan decides its terms. We are asking the plan to put a floor in that contract: at least the cost of the drug, plus a fill fee.

We don't ask the plan to take our word for our costs. We will share our purchase invoices for these 20 drugs with the plan. We will meet with the plan's staff at any time before May 1, so the plan can check our figures before it signs.

Plan members in 14 counties depend on pharmacies like ours. We want to keep serving them, and we're asking the plan to make that possible.

Tom Reilly
Owner, Reilly Drug, Carver
For the Main Street Pharmacy Alliance
