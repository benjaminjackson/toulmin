Dear Dr. Pierce,

I am applying for the office manager position at Brookside Family Dental. For eleven years I was the office manager at a veterinary clinic with four veterinarians and 14 staff. That practice was close in size to yours, and the work was much the same: scheduling, billing and keeping a busy front office running.

In 2014 I handled the clinic's move from paper records to AVImark practice software. I also worked on collections. Over my last three years there, unpaid invoices more than 90 days old fell from about $38,000 to $9,000.

Seven years ago my mother was diagnosed with a serious illness. I gave the clinic two weeks' notice and became her full-time caregiver until she died last spring. During those years I managed her insurance claims and coordinated her appointments with six specialists. That taught me what patients need from the person who answers their questions about bills and appointments.

Your posting lists Dentrix. I haven't used it yet, so I have enrolled in the 20-hour Dentrix online training course, and I will finish it before my first day. I learned AVImark from scratch when our clinic moved off paper, and I expect to learn Dentrix the same way.

Dr. Sue Kim, the head veterinarian at my former clinic, has agreed to be a reference.

I am ready to return to full-time work, and I would welcome the chance to talk with you about the position.

Sincerely,

Linda Marsh
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
