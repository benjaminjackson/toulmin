I left out the guarantee because it isn't true, and with this audience a claim that isn't true is the biggest risk you could take.

**The numbers:**
- Placing all 61 children costs **$70,150** (61 × $1,150).
- Last December this group gave **$1,240**.
- Suppose every one of the 340 lapsed donors came back at their $85 average. That's $28,900, plus the full $15,000 match, for $43,900. It still only covers about 38 children.

No realistic response gets you to 61. Your former board member forwards these emails with her comments, and a promise people can check and find false is exactly what she'd pick up on.

**What the email says instead.** The match is the strongest true thing you have, so I built the email around it:
- $85 becomes $170.
- $575 pays for one child's full year.
- If returning donors use the whole match, that's $30,000, which places 26 children in January. I wrote that as an "if," so it stays true even if the response is modest.
- It also spells out what happens if they don't give: most of the children will still be on the waiting list in January.

**Eastside.** The email brings up the closure before it asks for anything. These readers are already holding it against you, so leaving it out would read as avoidance. I didn't invent reasons for the closure or claims about what has changed since. If anything has really changed, one specific sentence about it would be the most persuasive thing you could add. Another strong line: if any of the 61 children live in the old Eastside neighborhoods, say so.
