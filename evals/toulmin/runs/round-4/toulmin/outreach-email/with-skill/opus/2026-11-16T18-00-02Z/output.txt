I recommend a different headline from the one you asked for. "We cut picking errors in half" is true for one customer, but you can't say it about the product as a whole. Of the three customers with numbers to report, one went from 4.1 to 1.9 per 1,000 lines (a 54% drop). One went from 2.8 to 2.2 (21%). One hasn't shared. A VP of operations who asks "across all your customers?" will get the 21% figure, and after that the "half" claim looks inflated. So the draft says your largest customer cut errors by more than half, which is completely true, and then puts both results next to her own cost figure.

Even the weaker result pays for itself, and that's your strongest point:

- **Cost:** 5 sites × $1,250 × 12 months = $75,000 a year.
- **At a 21% drop:** about $125,000 a year saved on her $600K. This assumes her return costs fall in line with her mis-pick rate.
- **At half:** about $300,000 saved.
