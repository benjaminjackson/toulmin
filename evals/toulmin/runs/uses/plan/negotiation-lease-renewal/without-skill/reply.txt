## The angle

**Keeping me is cheaper and safer for you than replacing me, and my offer gets you to your number anyway.**

Don't argue that $5,650 is unfair. Show him that your offer pays well over five years and saves him the cost and risk of an empty unit. Keep the tone warm and businesslike. He's been flexible with you before, so write as a long-term partner, not an opponent.

## Check these numbers before you write

- **The comparable unit hurts you a little.** If $5,650 works out to $29 a square foot, your space is about 2,340 square feet. At the $28 asking price two doors down, that's about **$5,450 a month**, not $5,100. Don't present that unit as a price comparison. Use it as proof that $28 doesn't rent: it has sat empty for 14 months, which is roughly $75,000 in lost rent at its asking price.
- **Your offer grows past his.** With 3% raises, you'd pay $5,100, then $5,253, $5,411, $5,573 and $5,740. By year 5 you're above $5,650. Over five years that's about $325,000, compared with $339,000 if his $5,650 stays flat. The gap is about $14,000, or 4%. That framing is your strongest line.

## Order

1. You want to stay. Mention the 10 years and that you appreciate working with him.
2. You're a reliable tenant who has invested in his building. Mention the $70,000 kitchen and the hood that stays. Present it as value you added, not as something he owes you.
3. Gently point out the market: the nearby unit has been empty a long time.
4. Your offer: $5,100, 5 years, 3% a year. Add the line that it reaches his number by year 5.
5. Ask for a short meeting or call, and name a date to settle it well before August.

## Leave out

- **The late payments.** Don't bring them up. If he does, say once that early 2024 was a hard stretch, you've paid on time every month since, and you appreciated his flexibility.
- **The coffee chain.** Don't respond to it, call it a bluff, or ask who it is. If you mention it at all, you look worried. Coffee chains also usually don't need a restaurant hood, so your kitchen work is worth less to them than to another restaurant.
- **Any hint of what moving would cost you, or that $5,100 is your limit.**

## Find out first

- **Your current lease.** Does it give you a renewal option, a set way to calculate renewal rent, or a deadline for giving notice? August is about 9 months away, and notice periods are often 6 to 9 months.
- **His full terms.** Is $5,650 flat or rising each year? Is it gross rent, or are property taxes, insurance and shared maintenance (CAM) charged on top? Is it for 5 years?
- **Whether $5,100 is your opening offer or your ceiling.** If it's your ceiling, decide now what else you could accept instead of a lower rent. Options include a free month, a 2% raise in year one, a renewal option for another 5 years, or relief on a personal guarantee.
- **Other rents in the area.** A commercial broker can tell you this for free, and it's worth knowing in case he pushes back.
- **The best way to deliver it.** You might do better with a short note followed by a conversation in person than with the letter alone.
