## The angle

**Don't build the case on "I'm the main reason guest scores went up."** Your GM signed off on the renovation and watched the scores rise alongside it. If you claim most of the credit, the GM's first thought will be "the new lobby did that." That doubt would then spread to everything else on the page. You have something stronger: **you've already been doing this job for 7 weeks, and the things you own (training and staff retention) have measurable results.** The one-page case is about what you've done and can be checked, not about who caused the score change.

## The argument

- **Claim:** Promote me to front office manager. I'm already doing the job, and the parts of the department I've run have improved.
- **Grounds:**
  - 7 weeks running the front office alone.
  - Training rewritten so new hires work the desk alone after 5 shifts instead of 9, which is 44% fewer shifts.
  - Front desk turnover fell from 9 of 14 (64%) to 5 of 14 (36%), so about four fewer people a year to hire and train.
  - 6 years at the hotel.
- **Warrant** (the unstated link, which you don't need to write out because the GM already believes it): the best predictor of how someone will do a job is how they've already done it, especially the hardest parts of it.
- **Backing:** The GM has watched the last 7 weeks directly. That's your strongest point because they don't have to take your word for it.
- **Rebuttals the GM will raise:**
  1. "The renovation raised the scores, not you."
  2. "You've never managed a budget, and this job owns $1.1M in payroll."
  3. "An outside candidate could bring budget experience."
- **Qualifier on the guest score:** "contributed to," not "main reason," unless the checks below prove otherwise.

## Order on the page

1. **The ask, plus the 7 weeks.** Open with the fact that you've already been running the department. It's the ground the GM cares about most and can confirm personally.
2. **Staff results.** Turnover and training time, stated as numbers. Then a sentence on what they mean in practice: fewer vacancies, less overtime spent covering, a steadier desk.
3. **Guest score, carefully worded.** Something like: "Check-in scores rose from 7.9 to 8.6 over the same period as the renovation. The faster training and steadier team were part of that." Give the renovation its due once and move on.
4. **The budget, faced head-on.** Say plainly that you haven't owned a payroll budget. Then answer it with what you have done (see below) and a concrete plan for the gap.
5. **What I'd do in the first 6–12 months.** Two or three specific goals. This makes the GM picture you in the role rather than auditioning for it.
6. **Close.** Keep it short, with 6 years at the property as context rather than as the argument.

## Leave out

- "Main reason" and anything like it, unless you can back it up.
- Tenure as a reason in itself. Six years helps as context, but "I've been here a long time" isn't a case.
- Any comparison with the departed manager.

## Find out before Friday

1. **When did the check-in score actually move?** Look at it month by month. If it rose after your training change and before the renovation finished, you have real grounds for a stronger claim. If it jumped when rooms reopened, you know to stay with "contributed."
2. **How did the other scores move?** Compare check-in with room, cleanliness and overall. If check-in rose more than the room scores, that points to the desk and not the renovation. If they all rose together, the renovation is the likelier explanation.
3. **Reviews that name staff.** Count recent reviews that praise named front desk staff or check-in service by name. One or two short quotes are good grounds.
4. **What you've already touched on the payroll side.** During the 7 weeks, did you write schedules, approve hours, control overtime, or hire? Any figure here, such as overtime hours compared with the same weeks last year, turns "never managed a budget" into "managed the biggest cost on that budget without owning the spreadsheet."
5. **What turnover costs here.** Ask HR or the controller roughly what it costs to hire and train one front desk agent. Four fewer departures times that figure gives you a dollar saving, which is the language of a $1.1M budget.
6. **What you can commit to on the budget.** For example, sessions with the controller, or a course with a finish date. It has to be something you'll actually do, so decide this yourself.

## Two other things to know

- **The GM's real alternative is an outside hire with budget experience,** or a longer search while you keep covering. Your page is being weighed against that. The 7 weeks and the budget answer are what tip it your way.
- **The GM may pass this up to an owner, management company or HR.** Those readers haven't seen your 7 weeks firsthand. So wherever you can, put the facts in numbers rather than "as you've seen."
