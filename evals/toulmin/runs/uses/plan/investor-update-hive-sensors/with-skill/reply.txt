## The angle

**The bridge gets you to the 2027 pollination season. Your cash runs out just before it.**

Your own numbers show this:

- 70% of $212k (about $148k) came in March and April. That leaves about $64k over the other four months, roughly $16k a month.
- Off-season you're burning about $95k and bringing in about $16k, so you're losing about $80k a month.
- $340k ÷ $80k ≈ 4 months. If $340k is today's balance, you hit zero around mid-March 2027, just as your best two months start.

So the argument isn't "fund us because things are good." It's: "The customer numbers prove people want this. The calendar means we can't reach the season that pays for it without you." Your current investors already own part of the company, so the reason that matters most to them is protecting what they already have in it. The idea connecting the facts to the ask: **a business whose demand is proven and seasonal shouldn't die in the gap before its season.** They'll accept that without being told, so the piece doesn't need to say it outright.

**"The sensors cut his losses from 31% to 18%."** Don't claim that. He changed his mite treatment the same winter, and any beekeeping-savvy investor will spot it.
