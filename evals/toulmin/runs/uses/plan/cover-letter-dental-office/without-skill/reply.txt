## The angle

**You've already done this job, at a bigger practice.** A 4-vet, 14-person clinic and a 3-dentist, 9-person practice are close to the same business: several doctors, appointment-driven, billing-heavy, and run on practice-management software. You ran the larger one for 11 years. The letter should make the hiring dentist think "she's done this" before they think about the gap.

The seven years at home should come across as relevant experience, not an apology. Most vet billing is paid by the client at the counter, so the clinic never had to deal much with insurance. Dental practices deal with it all the time. Managing your mother's claims and her six specialists taught you insurance and complicated scheduling from the patient's side. That's a real addition to what you learned at the clinic.

## Order (rough word budget)

1. **Opening (~40):** The role you're applying for, and the one-line case: 11 years managing a practice of similar size. Mention any local tie here.
2. **Results (~90):** Unpaid invoices over 90 days cut from $38K to $9K. Say briefly *how* you did it, because that's what makes the number believable. Then the move from paper to software. That move also answers the Dentrix concern before you raise it.
3. **The gap (~60):** One plain sentence on the caregiving, then what it taught you: insurance claims and juggling several providers.
4. **Dentrix (~35):** Say it directly: you know AVImark, Dentrix is new to you, and here's what you're already doing about it.
5. **Close (~30):** Ask for a conversation.

## Leave out

- **Your age.** Also skip anything that hints at it, like "seasoned" or "decades."
- **Details of your mother's illness or death.** Something like "full-time caregiver for a family member" is enough. You can mention that that period has ended so they know you're available, but keep grief out of it.
- **Apologies or hedges** such as "despite my time away" or "I may be out of date."
- **The year 2014.** "Led the move from paper to digital records" makes the point without dating it.
- **A list of duties.** Results and judgment matter more than tasks.

## Find out first

1. **How you cut the unpaid invoices.** New collections process? Payment plans? Billing at time of service? One clause of method makes the number much stronger.
2. **Your exact role in the software move.** Did you choose the system, run the data migration, train the staff? "Led" is better than "handled," but only if it's true.
3. **Who you supervised.** All 14 staff directly, or through leads? That number is your best match to their 9.
4. **Start on Dentrix before you send it.** Henry Schein, the company that makes Dentrix, has tutorials and training. Even a few hours of it lets you write "I've begun Dentrix training" instead of "I haven't used it."
5. **Reread the posting for its priorities.** Check whether it stresses insurance verification, collections, HR and payroll, or OSHA/HIPAA compliance, and tune paragraph 2 to match.
6. **Who to address it to, and any local connection.** Find the hiring dentist's name. In a small town, being a patient there or knowing someone on staff is worth one line.
7. **References.** Check that someone from the clinic, ideally a vet you worked under, can still vouch for you. A strong reference makes a seven-year gap matter much less.

Once you have answers to 1–4, the letter will be easy to write under 300 words.
