## The angle: "You nearly did this once. This time the target is smaller and the deal is better."

Your failed campaign is your best evidence. The town pledged $47,000 toward a goal that was too big. If the bank's $25,000 counts toward the $85,000, you need **$60,000 from the public**, only $13,000 more than people already offered. And you refunded every dollar, so you can show you keep your word with donors' money. Don't apologise for the last campaign. Use it as proof.

## Suggested order

1. **What's at stake, in one or two lines.** Nearest screen is 38 miles away, so a movie means a 76-mile round trip. Say who that hurts most: teenagers, older residents, families.
2. **The deal, and why this time is different.** Smaller goal, $25,000 from the bank, $1 a year for 10 years from the owner. Each of these says someone with money has already looked at this and said yes.
3. **What $85,000 buys.** Projector, sound, roof, with a rough figure for each if you have quotes. Specific numbers build trust.
4. **Why it will last.** 46 volunteers running the box office and snack bar means low staff costs. This section has to answer the obvious question: "It closed once. Why won't it close again?"
5. **The ask and the deadline.** June 30, and what happens if you fall short.

## Find out first

- **Is the public target $60,000 or $85,000?** Confirm that the bank's $25,000 counts toward the total, and what exactly triggers it. If it's a lump sum released at a threshold, don't call it a "match."
- **Why did it close in 2022?** If the answer is "the owner retired" or "the projector died," say so, because it helps you. If it was falling attendance, you need a better answer before you go public.
- **Why was the last goal $120,000 and this one $85,000?** People will notice. The likely answer is the $1 lease, but check.
- **Do you have a basic operating budget?** Film rental, utilities, insurance, against about 180 tickets a week plus snacks. You don't need it in the pitch, but you'll need it when someone asks.
- **Why are donors paying to fix the owner's roof?** Have a one-line answer ready. The $1 lease is probably it.
- **Campaign mechanics:** which platform, all-or-nothing or keep-what-you-raise, any reward tiers, whether donations are tax-deductible. Also, the deadline is over seven months away, which is long for most platforms, so decide when the public campaign actually launches.
- **What happens after the 10 years, and who owns the equipment?**

## Leave out or play down

- **Nostalgia.** One line is enough. Donors want to know it will work, not how much they miss it.
- **The 180 tickets a week, unless you can explain the closure.** On its own it invites "so why did it close?"
- **The full story of the last campaign.** One sentence: "You pledged $47,000; we returned every cent; here's why this time is different."
- **Anything about the decline of cinema in general.** It's off-message.

Once you have the answers on the target, the reason it closed and the platform, the draft should be straightforward.
