## The angle

**This time it's within reach.** Last time you asked the town for $120,000 and got $47,000. This time you're asking for $60,000, because the bank covers $25,000 of the $85,000. That's $13,000 more than the town already pledged once. Last time you were $73,000 short. The pitch shouldn't hide the failed campaign. It should rest on it.

## The argument underneath

- **Claim:** Pledge by June 30 and the cinema reopens.
- **Grounds:** The $1-a-year, 10-year lease. The bank's $25,000. A $60,000 gap against $47,000 raised last time. 180 tickets a week when it closed. 46 volunteers. 38 miles (a 76-mile round trip) to the nearest screen.
- **Warrant:** A town that has already pledged $47,000 can find $60,000 when the rest of the plan is in place.
- **Backing:** You refunded every dollar last time. That shows you handle backers' money honestly.
- **Objections readers will raise:** "Didn't this fail before?" "Why did it close, and why won't it close again?" "Why was it $120,000 then and $85,000 now?"
- **Qualifier:** Probably none. The match and the deadline give the claim real conditions without one.

## Order (under 350 words)

1. **What's lost:** closed since 2022, nearest screen 38 miles away. Two sentences at most.
2. **What's changed:** the owner's lease and the bank's $25,000, so the ask is $60,000.
3. **Last time, said plainly:** $120,000 target, $47,000 raised, everyone refunded. Then: "This gap is $13,000 bigger than what you gave us last time."
4. **Why it will last:** no rent, volunteer staff, and 180 tickets a week when it closed.
5. **What the money buys:** projector, sound system, roof.
6. **What happens if you miss:** the bank's pledge lapses on June 30.
7. **The ask, with the date.**

## What to leave out

- The failed campaign must stay in. Your past backers know about it.
- Leave out volunteer job details beyond the number 46, long nostalgia, and anything that suggests the $47,000 is still "banked."
- A per-resident figure ($60,000 ÷ 6,500 ≈ $9) is optional. It can read as a bill.

## Find out first

1. **Why did it close in 2022?** This matters most. Readers will ask whether it can survive this time. If the projector died, or the operator retired, or rent was the problem, then one fact answers the objection. If it was losing money at 180 tickets a week, you need running numbers instead.
2. **Running costs and break-even:** ticket price, monthly costs, and how many tickets a week you need with no rent and volunteer staff.
3. **Why $85,000 instead of $120,000?** What dropped out, and are the costs firm quotes?
4. **Terms for backers:** all-or-nothing again? Does the bank's $25,000 need the full $60,000, or does it match part of it? Does the lease offer end if you miss June 30? Are donations tax-deductible?
5. **Promises you can keep:** a target opening date, and the names of the bank and the owner, if they agree.
6. **Anything readers will learn anyway:** pledges already in hand, the roof's condition, or ongoing costs the lease leaves to you.

Once you have the answers, this is enough to draft from.
