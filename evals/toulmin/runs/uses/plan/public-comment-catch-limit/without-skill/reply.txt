## The angle

**Don't argue that the cut will put every charter boat out of business.** Your own numbers don't support it, and the commission will notice. After the 2016 cut you lost 32 trips (about 19%) and were back to 155 within two years. That hurt, but you survived it. If you claim total collapse and someone remembers 2016, the commission may stop listening to the rest of your comment.

Your strongest argument is that **charter boats would carry a large share of the economic cost for a small share of the conservation benefit.** Charters land about 9% of the state's summer flounder and private boats land about 80%. Cutting your customers' limit saves few fish, and your business depends on what customers can keep. You have real evidence of the harm:

- **Your 2016 experience:** 170 trips fell to 138, about $28,800 in lost revenue at $900 a trip, and you hadn't fully recovered two years later. A 3-year cut would be longer than the period you've already seen.
- **The survey:** 41 of 70 past customers (59%) said they would book fewer trips.

Then **offer a solution instead of just opposing the cut.** Commissions respond better to someone who accepts that the stock needs protection and proposes a different way to do it. Don't dispute the science. Being at 78% of target is a real problem, and saying so makes you more credible.

## Suggested order (two minutes is about 250–300 words)

1. **Who you are, in one sentence:** 22 years, about 160 trips a season, one of 11 boats in the harbor.
2. **Your request, stated early:** for example, a separate limit or season for for-hire boats, a shorter cut with a review after one year, or a slot limit. Pick one or two, not five.
3. **Why it's fair:** 9% of landings vs. 80%.
4. **What it costs, with proof:** 2016 in one or two sentences, then the survey result.
5. **Who else is affected:** crew, bait and tackle shops, fuel docks, motels, restaurants. Use this only if you can put a number on it.
6. **Close:** repeat your request.

Use no more than 4 or 5 numbers when speaking. The rest can go in writing.

## Leave out

- The "out of business" claim, or anything close to it. If you want to talk about risk, say something you can back up, like "a second cut in ten years will push some boats over the edge," and only if you know of specific boats that are struggling.
- Blaming private anglers. Point out the difference in landings and leave it there. Many people in that room fish from private boats.
- Arguments about whether the stock assessment is right.
- Your total gross revenue ($144,000). It raises questions you don't want to answer at a microphone. The per-trip loss makes the point.

## Find out first

1. **What the staff report says about alternatives.** Did staff look at separate rules for for-hire boats, slot limits or season changes? If they rejected one, find out why so your request answers their objection.
2. **How much of the needed reduction the 2-fish limit gets from charter boats.** If charter landings contribute almost nothing to the target, that's your best single fact.
3. **What 78% means under the state's rules.** Does it trigger a required rebuilding plan, and by what deadline? This tells you how much room the commission has.
4. **Whether economic impact is a required consideration** under the state's rules for this decision. If it is, say that the economic analysis should count.
5. **Details on the survey.** How were the 70 chosen, and what did the other 17 say? The commission will discount a survey run by the charter association, so be upfront about who ran it and how. If the other 17 said they'd stop booking entirely, that helps you.
6. **Whether the other 10 boats saw the same drop in 2016.** One boat is a story. Eleven boats is evidence. Ask the association for numbers.
7. **Whether written comments are accepted.** If so, keep the spoken version short and put the full data in a written comment.
8. **Whether other captains are speaking.** If so, split up the points so you don't all say the same thing.

Once you've checked those, especially items 1, 2 and 6, you'll know which version of the request to make and can start drafting.
