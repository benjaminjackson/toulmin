## The angle

The commission reads that the stock is at 78% of its target and expects a cut. You won't win by arguing against the cut. You can win by arguing about **who bears it and how**. Your case is that charter boats land 9% of the harvest, but this cut puts most of its economic cost on you, and there's a way to get the conservation without that cost.

Put simply: *a rule should put its cost where the harvest is.* The commission will probably accept that idea. Your numbers already back it.

## Drop "every charter boat out of business"

Your own facts don't support it, and they work against it:

- **You've already lived through a cut.** In 2016 you went from 170 trips to 138, and two years later you were back to 155. Someone on the commission may know that, or may ask. If they do, "out of business" falls apart, and everything else you said loses credibility with it.
- **You only have numbers for one of the 11 boats.** "Every boat" is a guess about ten businesses you haven't measured.
- **The survey says "fewer trips," not "no trips."** 41 of 70 is 59%. 12 said they'd book the same. You haven't accounted for the other 17.

**What your facts do support, stated as strongly as they allow:**
- At today's price your season is about $144,000 (160 × $900).
- The 2016 cut cost you 32 trips, about 19%. That would be roughly **$29,000 a season** now.
- Bookings took two years to recover, and even then they were still 15 trips short. This cut lasts **three years**, so there's no recovery window. If 2016 repeats, you'd lose roughly $80,000 or more over the period.
- 59% of surveyed customers say they'd book less, which fits what happened in 2016.

That's a stronger claim because nobody in the room can knock it down, and it's still serious. If you can honestly say what a 20% drop does to your margins (for example, "that puts my boat below break-even"), that's a claim about your own business that you can stand behind. Use it in place of the harbor-wide prediction.

## Order for two minutes (about 250–280 words)

1. **Who you are and what you're asking for**, in the first 15 seconds: 22 years, one of 11 boats in the harbor, and your specific request.
2. **Accept the problem.** The stock is below target, and you fish it for a living, so you want it healthy too. This takes away their main reason to dismiss you.
3. **What it costs, using your own numbers.** 2016: 170 to 138, two years to come back, and never fully. Then the dollar figure.
4. **The survey, in one line**, saying who ran it. Don't lean on it hard (see below).
5. **Proportion.** Charter boats land 9% and private boats 80%. The cut gets almost all its fish from private boats, but almost all its lost income falls on you.
6. **A specific alternative and a way out.** For example: a separate limit for charter and other for-hire boats, a different season or size limit that achieves the same harvest reduction, or a review after one or two years if the stock improves. This only works if the staff report allows it (see below). You need a concrete ask. "Don't do it" gives them nothing to vote on.

Leave out:
- Any attack on the staff's science, unless you have data of your own.
- Harbor-wide dollar totals, unless the other captains give you their real numbers.

## What to find out before writing

1. **Do your customers actually take 4 fish?** Check your logbooks for how often an angler keeps 3 or 4. If most charter anglers keep 2 or fewer anyway, a 2-fish limit barely cuts charter harvest but still scares off bookings. That would be your strongest point. If they regularly take 4, the commission will say charters do contribute, and you need to know that before you speak.
2. **The staff report.** What harvest reduction is it aiming for? Did staff look at separate limits for charter and private boats, season changes, or size limits? Did the 2016 cut achieve its goal? Your alternative has to fit within the reduction they say they need.
3. **The 2016 details.** What exactly changed, did that limit stay in place, and was anything else going on that year (weather, fuel prices, the economy)? Someone may say the drop had other causes.
4. **The other 10 boats.** Ask the association for their 2016 and current trip counts. That's the only way you can speak for the harbor. These captains are also a second audience: you need to persuade them to share their numbers, or even to speak themselves. One captain's two minutes plus three others backing it up carries a lot more weight.
5. **The survey.** How were the 70 customers picked, what was the exact question, and what did the other 17 say? If the commission asks, "the association surveyed its own customers" will be discounted unless you can explain the method.
6. **The commission's mandate.** Many state commissions are required to weigh economic impacts on fishing communities. If yours is, cite that rule, because it's the standard they have to apply. Also check whether you can file a written comment. Put the full data there and keep the spoken version to the essentials.

Once you have the logbook answer and the staff report's target reduction, you'll know whether to lead with "the cut barely touches charter harvest" or with "here's a cheaper way to hit your number." After that, it can be drafted.
