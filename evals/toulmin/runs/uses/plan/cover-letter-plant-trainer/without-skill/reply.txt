## The angle

You've spent nine years teaching people to do hazardous, step-by-step procedures correctly, and you've checked that they can. That's a training coordinator's job in a GMP plant. Chemistry knowledge and the classroom are supporting points, not the main one.

## Order

1. **Opening (2–3 sentences).** Name the role, then state the angle right away. Don't open with "I've been a teacher for 9 years." Open with what you've been doing: designing procedure-based training and making sure people follow it.
2. **The lab safety program.** This is your strongest bridge to GMP. You wrote a procedure-driven program, rolled it out, and it held up for about seven years with no injuries beyond first aid. Phrase that result exactly. Don't round it to "zero injuries."
3. **Training new teachers.** This is your only experience onboarding adults, which is what their new hires are. Twelve hours each, about two a year. Give the cumulative total ("trained roughly X new science staff since 20XX"), because a cumulative number reads stronger than "two a year."
4. **Training that gets results.** AP pass rate went from 41% to 58%. Keep it to one sentence, and say what you changed, not just the number. Without that, a hiring manager could put it down to a different group of students.
5. **The gaps, briefly.** Address GMP head-on in one or two sentences, ideally pointing to something you've already done (see below). Don't apologize. One clause is enough for the LMS: you've run your courses on Google Classroom and pick up platforms quickly.
6. **Close.** Short and specific. Say you'd welcome the chance to talk about how they bring new hires up to qualification.

## Leave out

- **"Cut new-hire training time in half."** I'd drop this. You haven't seen their program, so you can't back it up, and the first interview question will be "how?" In a GMP plant, much of training time is set by required SOP reading, on-the-job sign-offs and qualification steps. Promising to halve it could make you look unaware of compliance, which is the one thing you most need to show you understand. The safer pitch is getting people qualified efficiently and correctly, the first time. If you have a real idea for speeding things up, save it for the interview, once you know their process.
- **140 students and five classes.** Teaching load doesn't carry over well. Leave it out, or use it only to show volume if you need it.
- **Why you're leaving teaching, and the commute.** Have an answer ready for the interview. The letter doesn't need either.

## Find out first

- **What the posting emphasizes.** Is the role mostly delivering training, writing curriculum, keeping records and running audits, or qualifying trainers? Weight paragraphs 2–4 to match. Also check whether the posting names their LMS. Common ones are ComplianceWire, Veeva and SAP SuccessFactors.
- **Your own paper trail.** Did the safety program include sign-off sheets, competency checks, annual refreshers, or records of who was trained on what? If so, that's very close to GMP training records, and it's the best answer to your gap. Find the specifics.
- **The new-teacher training.** How many years have you done it, what's the total number of people, and was there any check at the end (a demonstration, a checklist)?
- **The AP gain.** What did you change to get it?
- **A quick GMP credential.** Consider an introductory cGMP course before you apply. Several low-cost online options take a few hours to a few days. Then the gap sentence becomes "I've completed X" instead of "I'm eager to learn."
- **The hiring manager's name**, if you can get it, for the greeting.

Once you have those details, the letter fits on one page.
