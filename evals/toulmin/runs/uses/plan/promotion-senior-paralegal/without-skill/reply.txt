## The angle

**You're already doing most of the senior paralegal job, and you're asking for the title to match.**

The main difference between your role and a senior paralegal's is supervision. You've already trained all three paralegals the group hired in the last two years, so you aren't asking the partner to bet on you. You're asking them to make official something they've already seen you do. Every other fact should support that point.

## What to say, in order

1. **The request, in the first sentence or two.** Name the role and say you'd like to discuss it. Partners skim, so don't make them look for it.
2. **Supervision.** You trained the three newest paralegals. This is your strongest evidence because it is the senior role.
3. **The systems you've built, and the mistake.** You built the deadline sheet and two other groups adopted it. Then say plainly that last March you entered a deadline wrong, a colleague caught it, and you added a second-person check so one person's error can't get through. Give the colleague credit by role. This shows the judgment a supervisor needs: you build checks that don't depend on anyone being perfect, including you.
4. **Workload, briefly.** Four lawyers, 23 cases, 2 trials, and 1,640 hours against a target of 1,550. This shows you have capacity, but it's supporting evidence, not the main argument. Hours make a case for a raise, not for a supervisory role.
5. **A forward-looking close.** In one or two sentences, say what you'd do as senior (for example, formalize onboarding, or extend the check process to the groups using your sheet). Then ask for a meeting.

## Leave out

- **The $9,000.** The pay comes with the title. Mentioning the number makes the request sound like it's about money. Save it for the negotiation if one happens.
- **Tenure as an argument.** "Five years" can appear once as context. Don't argue that you've earned it by time served.
- **Comparisons to other paralegals**, especially the ones you trained or the one who caught your error.
- **Any spin on the March error.** Don't minimize it or imply you caught it yourself.

## Find out first

- **Does the partner already know about the March error?** In a litigation group, a filing made two hours before the cutoff probably got noticed. I'd include it either way, because leaving it out and having it come up later is the worst outcome. But knowing what they know tells you how much explaining it needs.
- **Is there an opening?** Does the group already have a senior paralegal, and is there budget for another? If the role is filled, you may be asking for a new position, and that's a different request.
- **Timing and process.** It's mid-November, so year-end reviews and budgets are probably close. Find out whether promotions are decided in that cycle and send the request before it, not after.
- **The written criteria for senior paralegal**, if HR has them. Use their wording.
- **Results you can measure.** Have there been any missed or near-missed deadlines since the second-person check went in? Do the other two groups use the check too? Are the three paralegals you trained still there and doing well?
- **What you did on the two trials.** If you ran trial logistics or exhibits, that's worth one specific line.
- **Support from your lawyers.** Ask one or two of them whether they'd back you if the partner asks. Ideally, find out before you send it.

Once you have those answers, 400 words is plenty.
