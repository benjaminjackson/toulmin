## The angle

**Salon owners get books that are right and on time because you only do salons, and your clients stay.** The reader is a salon owner deciding whether to spend 20 minutes on a call. That's a small ask, so the page doesn't have to sell the whole $199 a month. It only has to make the call seem worth the time.

The idea holding it together, which stays off the page: someone who does the same kind of books 31 times a month gets them right, and an accountant can see that at tax time.

## What to say, in order

1. **Headline: who it's for and what they get.** Bookkeeping, payroll and sales tax for hair and nail salons only, with each month closed by the 10th.
2. **What's included and the price.** $199 a month covers monthly books, payroll for up to 10 staff and sales tax filing. Put the price up front. Hiding it makes the call feel like a sales trap.
3. **Proof, strongest first:**
   - You have 31 salon clients.
   - The only two who left last year sold their salons.
   - 9 clients said their accountant charged them less at tax season because the books were clean. One saved $800.
   - Word the $800 as one client's result, not what a typical client saves.
4. **The limits, said plainly with an answer next to each:**
   - You close by the 10th and missed it 3 times last year.
   - You work alone and don't take calls on Sundays or Mondays.
   - Payroll covers up to 10 staff.

   Readers trust a page more when it states its limits, and they'll find these out anyway.
5. **What doing nothing costs.** For example, a bigger bill from the accountant, or the owner doing the books on a day off. Use only figures you can stand behind.
6. **Button:** "Book your free 20-minute call."

## What to leave out

- **The old $149 price.** New clients don't need it. The one exception is in question 2 below.
- **Anything about working alone that sounds like an apology.** "You'll always deal with me" is the strength. The risk, that there's no backup, gets one line and an answer (question 5).

## What to find out first

1. **What do the 3 missed closes mean?** Is that 3 out of about 370 client-months, or 3 months where you were late for everyone? The first is a strong number (99% on time). The second needs a reason and what you changed.
2. **Has anyone left since the January price rise?** If none of the 31 left after a 33% increase, that's your best proof and worth naming the rise for. If some did, leave the rise out.
3. **What does "salon-only" mean in practice?** Tips, commissions, booth renters versus employees, retail product stock, the booking software you know. Without this, "I only do salons" is just a label. With two concrete examples, it's the whole argument.
4. **The Sunday/Monday problem.** Many salons close on Mondays, so that's often when owners do paperwork. How do you handle it? Email with a reply by Tuesday? Another line of contact? The page needs an answer, not just the closed days.
5. **If you're sick or on holiday, what happens?** Is there a backup, or a promise about deadlines? The page should only promise what you can actually do.
6. **The terms a new client would ask about.** Is there a contract or can they cancel any month? Do you charge to catch up books that are behind? Which states do you file sales tax in? Do you have permission to quote the $800 client, by name or by first name and salon?

Answer these and I'll write the 350 words from them.
