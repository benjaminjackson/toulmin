## The angle

**"I only do salons, and salon owners stay."**

You don't need to argue that your bookkeeping is good. The facts already show it:

- None of your 31 clients has left over the work. The only two who left sold their salons.
- You raised your price 34%, for existing clients too, and they stayed.
- Your clients' accountants charged them less because the books came in clean.

Being salon-only is why a reader should trust you. The fact that clients stay is the proof.

## Order (about 350 words)

1. **Headline and subhead.** Bookkeeping, payroll and sales tax for hair and nail salons only, done by one person who knows how a salon runs.
2. **The reader's problem, in salon terms.** Tips, commission, product sales against service sales, cash days, staff turnover. Keep it short and specific. This is where being salon-only pays off.
3. **What they get.** The three services, stated plainly. Monthly books closed by the 10th, payroll for up to 10 staff, sales tax filed.
4. **Proof.** First, the retention: 31 salons, and nobody has left except to sell. Second, the accountant savings: 9 clients paid their accountant less, and one saved $800. Present the $800 as one client's result, not something everyone should expect.
5. **Price.** $199 a month, stated openly. Next to the accountant savings, it doesn't need defending.
6. **What working with you is like.** One person, so they always deal with you. Mention your hours as a plain fact, not an apology. Many salons are busiest late in the week, so being off Sunday and Monday may suit them anyway.
7. **Button.** "Book your free 20-minute call," plus one line on what happens on the call.

## Leave out

- **The old $149 price.** "I raised my price and kept every client" works without the number. Mentioning $149 makes $199 look like a markup.
- **Any promise that books are "always" ready by the 10th.** Say it's your deadline, or give your record honestly once you've checked it (see below).
- **Making "one person" sound like a weakness.** Don't apologize for it, and don't hide it either.
- **General bookkeeping pitches** like "save time" or "peace of mind." Every bookkeeper says that.

## Find out first

1. **The missed deadlines.** Were they 3 clients' books in a given month, or 3 whole months? Three out of about 370 client-months is about 99% on time, which is worth saying. Three out of 12 months is a different story.
2. **The retention claim, checked against dates.** The two clients left last year, but the price rise was this January. Has anyone left since January? Is 31 your current count?
3. **Permission for testimonials.** Can you name the client who saved $800, or quote her? Would any of the other 9 give you a line?
4. **Your limits.** What happens if a salon has more than 10 staff? Do you handle booth renters and 1099s? Which states do you file sales tax in?
5. **What isn't included.** Do you do tax returns, or catch-up work on messy past books? If catch-up costs extra, say so on the page.
6. **Terms.** Is it month to month? How quickly do you reply on the days you're working?
7. **The call.** What happens in the 20 minutes, and what does the booking link go to?

Once you have answers to 1 and 2, the proof section can be written with confidence.
