## The angle

**This is about the work, not about attendance.** You have two real reasons: you're losing competitive interviews, and you're not developing junior people fast enough. Both come from the same problem: people learn and gel by working near each other. Present the change as the partners fixing something the firm got wrong, and say that you're part of it. That makes it land as a decision about the practice, not a trust issue.

The partners' one-day-a-week attendance helps you most. Don't treat it as a footnote. Own it: "We set the pattern, and it was the wrong one." That one line does more than any statistic.

## Order

1. **The decision, plainly, in the first two sentences.** Three days (Tue/Wed/Thu) from September 1. Don't open with context or softening; people will skim to find this anyway.
2. **Why.** The hospital interviews first, because it's concrete and hard to argue with. Then the development point, framed as the firm failing its juniors, not juniors underperforming.
3. **What the partners are changing.** In all three days, starting when everyone else does or earlier.
4. **What it means for people hired as remote.** Say in general terms that you've spoken to each of them individually. Don't spell out personal arrangements in an all-staff email.
5. **What happens next.** The Friday meeting, what's still open, and who to talk to privately.

## What to find out first

- **Which September?** It's November 2026, so I assume September 2027. If so, "remote until the end of next year" gives the four distant staff only about four extra months, which may read as a token gesture. Check that this is what you mean.
- **Employment contracts.** If the 15 were hired with remote work written into their terms, changing that may need notice, consent or a formal process depending on where you are. Get employment advice before Friday, not after.
- **The other 11 remote hires.** They're the group hit hardest: fully remote to three days, with no transition. What are you offering them, if anything? Is there a phase-in? Commuting support?
- **What happens if someone can't or won't.** Someone will ask this on Friday. Is it a condition of employment? Is there an exceptions process for caregiving, health or disability? You need a clear answer even if it's "talk to us individually."
- **Office capacity.** Fixed anchor days mean all 46 people are in at once. Do you have the desks and meeting rooms for that? If not, the first Tuesday will undercut your whole argument.
- **The 30% figure.** Architects will pick at it. What's it measured against? Could project mix or the market explain some of it? If it won't survive questions, soften it to the direction ("noticeably longer") or present it only on Friday.
- **Agreement between the partners.** Make sure all three agree on the wording and on who answers which questions Friday.

## What to leave out

- **Any language about productivity, trust or "seeing people."** It's not your reason, and it will poison the real one.
- **Project or client names for the lost interviews.** The people on those teams will recognize themselves. Keep it to "two large hospital projects."
- **The four individuals' arrangements.** Describe the policy, not the people.
- **"We'll review this" or "for now"** unless you mean it. It invites people to wait it out.
- **Over-apologizing.** Acknowledge that this is a real change for people who planned their lives around remote work, once and sincerely, then move on.

## Sequencing

**Tell the 15 remote hires individually before the email goes out,** especially the four. Learning that your job is changing from an all-staff email is what people will remember, whatever the email says. Then send the email a day or so before Friday, so people arrive having read it rather than reacting to it in the room.
