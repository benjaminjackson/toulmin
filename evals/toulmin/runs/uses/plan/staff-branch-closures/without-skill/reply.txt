## The angle

**Members have moved to phone and chat, so we're moving our people there with them, and everyone keeps a job.**

That message is true and it holds up. "This will make everyone's job better" doesn't, and I'd drop it. Fourteen people are going from 9–5 at a branch to rotating shifts in a 12-hour day, and from face-to-face service to a queue. Some took branch jobs because of that schedule and that kind of work. If you tell them it's better, the ones who don't think so will trust the rest of the email less. A safer claim: it's a real change, here's what's good about it (no job loss, pay held or raised, work from home two days a week), and here's what's hard (hours, shifts, a different kind of work).

It also helps to name what improves for the other branches. Shorter hold times and phones that aren't overflowing benefit them directly.

## Order

1. **The news, in the first two sentences.** Maple Street and Route 9 close March 31. Every person there is offered a role in the new center.
2. **Why.** Branch visits fell 44%, phone and chat rose 60%, and members wait 9 minutes on hold. Present this as members changing how they bank, not as anyone falling short, and that includes the current phone team.
3. **What the new role looks like.** Pay stays the same or goes up. Hours are 8–8 on weekdays with rotating shifts. After 90 days of training, people can work from home two days a week. Be plain about the hours.
4. **The other option.** Two weeks of pay per year of service for anyone who doesn't take the role. Say it with respect, so it reads as a real choice and not a penalty.
5. **What happens next.** One-on-ones, the decision deadline, who to ask, and when members will be told.
6. **A word to the other five branches.** Say clearly whether they're affected. If you don't, they'll assume they're next.

## Find out before writing

- **Have the 14 heard in person first?** They should hear from their manager or from you before this email goes out to everyone. This matters more than anything else on the list.
- **Decision deadline.** When do they have to choose, and what happens if they don't answer?
- **What "same pay or higher" means.** Is it base pay only, or are benefits included? Is there a differential for evening shifts?
- **How the rotation works.** How often does someone work until 8? Can people with childcare or second jobs ask for set shifts?
- **Where the center is.** Is the commute different from the branches? Where does training happen?
- **Branch managers and leads.** Is there a role at their level, or is it a step down?
- **Severance details.** Is there a minimum for short-tenure staff? Do benefits continue? How are partial years counted? If someone takes the new role and leaves during training, can they still get severance?
- **Member notice.** Check with compliance and your state regulator on the required notice period for branch closures. Staff must hear before members do, and soon enough to answer members' questions.
- **Hold-time target.** Does the center reduce the 9 minutes, and to what? A specific goal makes the "why" believable.

## Leave out

- "Better for everyone," and any upbeat spin on the hours.
- Individual severance math. Handle that in one-on-ones.
- Hints about future closures, unless you can say something definite.
- Corporate filler like "exciting journey" or "transformation." Staff in a credit union this size know you, so it should sound like you.

About 400 words is the right length for this.
