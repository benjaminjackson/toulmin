## The angle

**Every job is kept, and the jobs move to where members now are.** That's the strongest honest version of your message, and your facts fully support it.

"This will make everyone's job better" goes further than your facts can. For the 14 people affected, some things clearly get better: same or higher pay, and two days a week at home after training. Other things get harder for at least some of them. The day stretches from 9–5 to 8–8 with rotating shifts, so some people will work evenings. And moving from face-to-face service to phones and chat is a real change in the work. Those 14 will weigh the good against the bad themselves. If the email says "better for everyone" first, the first person with childcare at 6 p.m. stops trusting the rest of it.

So I'd suggest:
- **Claim:** We're closing two branches because members have moved to phone and chat. Everyone at those branches keeps a job at the same pay or more, and the new role offers real things the branch didn't.
- **Why it holds:** We should put our people where members need us. Visits to those branches are down 44%, phone and chat is up 60%, and members wait 9 minutes on hold. Your staff already accept that idea, so the email doesn't need to spell it out. The numbers make the case.
- **What could undercut it:** the evening hours, the rotating shifts, losing in-person work, and "are other branches next?"

You can still use the word "better," but say better *at what*: steadier work, a path to working from home, and members who don't wait 9 minutes. Saying that and being straight about the hours is more convincing than a blanket promise.

## Order

1. **The news and the job guarantee, together, in the first lines.** Every reader's first question is "is anyone losing their job?" Answer it right away.
2. **Why.** The three numbers, briefly. Point them at member behaviour, not at how the branches performed.
3. **What changes for the 14.** Pay, hours, rotating shifts, training, and working from home. Put the hours change in plainly, right next to the remote-work benefit.
4. **The choice.** The new role is offered. If someone doesn't want it, they get 2 weeks' pay per year of service. Say it with respect, not as the fallback option.
5. **What it means for everyone else.** Members of the closed branches, the other five branches, and the existing phone staff.
6. **Next steps.** Dates, meetings, and who to talk to.

## Leave out

- The blanket "everyone's job better" line.
- Cheerful words like "exciting" or "transformation." To the 14, this is a loss of their workplace.
- Anything about the future of the other five branches that you can't commit to. If you can't say "no other closures are planned," say nothing, but be ready to be asked.

## Do before you send

**The 14 should not learn this from an all-staff email.** Tell them in person, ideally the same day, before or just as the email goes out. That changes the email too. It becomes the follow-up for them and the announcement for everyone else.

You may also have two audiences here. The 14 need the terms. The other staff mostly need reassurance and to know how their own work changes. One 400-word email can cover both if it follows the order above. A separate, more detailed note to the 14 would let the all-staff email stay short.

## Find out first

In rough order of importance:

1. **Hours in practice:** How often will each person work until 8 p.m.? Is it everyone, by rotation, or volunteers first? This is the biggest objection, and the answer decides how you word it.
2. **The decision:** When do people have to decide? Can someone who takes the role and finds it doesn't suit them still get the severance within some window?
3. **Timing:** When does training start, and when does working from home begin (90 days after what date)? Where is the center, and is the commute different?
4. **Pay details:** Who gets "higher," and why? Is there a minimum or cap on severance, and does health coverage continue?
5. **Members:** Where do Maple Street and Route 9 members go? Will the other branches see more walk-ins? Front-line staff will get these questions from members first.
6. **The goal:** Is there a hold-time target for the center? A number like "under 2 minutes by summer" turns "better" into something you can be held to.
7. **Other branches:** Can you truthfully say no other closures are planned? Everyone at the other five will wonder.

Once you have answers to the first four, there's enough for a solid draft.
