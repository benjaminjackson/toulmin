## The angle

**Apologize for how you handled the hail, not for the hail itself.** No one blames you for a storm. What likely hurt was six weeks of half boxes at full price with one short email. Say that plainly and early. Then show what's different. An honest apology to people who left will do more than any amount of selling.

## Order

1. **Admit it.** July 9 hail, half boxes for six weeks, no credit, and not enough communication. Two or three sentences, without defending yourself.
2. **What's changed.** Hail nets over the tomatoes and peppers, a second field on higher ground, and pause weeks with credit. Be exact about what each one covers.
3. **What happens next time.** This is the part they'll actually be wondering about. Even one sentence helps: "If we lose a big part of the crop again, you'll hear from me that week, and here's what we'll do."
4. **Any make-good offer**, if you decide to make one (see below).
5. **The details.** $540, the same as last year. Sign-up closes March 15. Link to sign up.
6. **Personal close.** Invite them to reply, even if they're not coming back.

## Leave out

- **The $31,000.** To someone who felt shortchanged, it can read as "we spent a lot, so please pay us." What matters to them is the changes, not what they cost.
- **The 97 and the 260.** Those numbers are for you.
- **Any explanation of the CSA "shared risk" model.** However true it is, here it will sound like an excuse.
- **Heavy scarcity pressure.** Mention that 52 shares are left once at most, or not at all. Pressure doesn't go over well with people you're trying to win back.

## Find out or decide first

- **Why did they actually leave?** Some moved, changed budgets, or started gardening. If you have no exit survey, think about asking a few people, or add a "tell me why" line. Otherwise the whole email rests on a guess that it was the hail.
- **What can the second field really claim?** Higher ground helps with drainage and frost. It doesn't obviously protect against hail, unless the point is that the crops are spread across two sites. Don't let the email suggest protection you can't promise. The same goes for the nets: they only cover two crops.
- **Will you offer returning members something?** Half boxes for six weeks is roughly three weeks of produce, about $70 of a $540 share. A credit or discount for anyone who had a share last year would show you mean it more than any wording can. This is the biggest decision here.
- **Do you have a crop-loss policy now?** If there isn't one, step 3 is a promise you'd be making up as you write. Decide on it first.
- **Who should it come from?** It should come from you, by name, in plain text, not a newsletter template.

With those six parts, the email will be tight at under 200 words. If you need to cut, shorten step 2 before touching step 1 or step 3.
