# Will it work?

Probably, as an opening. It's polite, short and specific, and it makes a clear offer he can respond to. Expect him to counter rather than accept. The draft's main weaknesses are in strategy, not tone.

# Where it's weak

**1. It opens at your ceiling.** You said you *can* pay $5,100, and the letter offers exactly $5,100. Most landlords will counter, often by splitting the difference at around $5,375. If $5,100 really is your limit, you'll have nowhere to go. You have two options:
- open lower, around $4,950–5,000, so there's room to move; or
- say plainly that $5,100 is the most the business supports, so he doesn't treat it as a first bid.

The draft does neither.

**2. The comparable unit argument works against you as written.** "$28 vs. about $29" looks like a $1 gap, which suggests his number is close to market. The real point is that **nobody has leased that unit at $28 in 14 months**, so the market isn't paying that rate. The draft lists the facts but never draws that conclusion. It would also help to state your offer per square foot. $5,100 works out to about **$26**, which makes the gap concrete.

**3. "Reliable tenant for a decade" invites him to remember the late payments.** He waived two late fees in 2024. A line thanking him for his flexibility then, followed by "on time every month since," builds goodwill. Claiming a spotless decade could annoy him.

**4. The kitchen investment is stated but not argued.** The point to make is that the $70,000 kitchen, hood included, adds to *his* building's value and makes the space ready for any restaurant tenant. Also, "new hood system" sounds odd for something installed seven years ago.

**5. The coffee chain paragraph gives up leverage.** "I know you have options" concedes his strongest card. A better response points out what switching tenants would really cost him:
- a coffee chain would need a new build-out;
- they would probably ask for tenant improvement money or free months;
- there would be broker fees and vacancy in between.

The vacancy math in the draft is correct: two months empty costs more than the $6,600 first-year gap. It's actually stronger than that, since even one month empty nearly equals that gap. Put that point here, next to the coffee chain, rather than in the comparable unit bullet.

# What's fine

- The numbers check out: 3% a year from $5,100 reaches about $5,740 in year five.
- The tone is respectful.
- Offering to meet in person and "ready to sign now" are good closers.
- It's under 300 words.

**Biggest change:** fix point 1. Decide whether $5,100 is an opening offer or a firm ceiling, and make the letter say so.
