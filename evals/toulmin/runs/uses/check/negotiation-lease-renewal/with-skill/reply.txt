**Short answer:** It will probably get you a counteroffer rather than a no, which is about what a first letter can hope for. The tone is right and the math is correct. But it gives up some of your strongest points, says nothing about the one thing your landlord already holds against you, and handles the coffee chain weakly.

## Where it's weak

**1. The late payments are left out, and he hasn't forgotten them.** He waived the late fees himself, so he'll remember. Calling yourself "a reliable tenant for a decade" with no mention of 2024 can read like you're hoping he forgot. Raising it yourself in one plain line, and thanking him for waiving the fees, turns it into goodwill. The two-plus years of on-time rent since then is the answer to it, so put the two together.

**2. "More than the restaurant can carry" works against you.** Next to his memory of the late rent, it tells him you might be struggling. A landlord choosing between you and a chain wants to hear that you're solid. Present $5,100 as your offer, not as the most you can manage.

**3. The coffee chain is never really answered.** "I know you have options… I'd only ask that you weigh that" names his leverage and then asks him to look past it. Your facts already answer it. A chain "asking about" a space hasn't signed anything, and a unit two doors down has sat empty for 14 months at a lower price, so new tenants aren't lining up at his number. Saying that calmly is stronger than hoping he'll be kind.

**4. The best number is buried, and a stronger one is missing.** The point that you'd be paying him about $5,740 by year five, which is more than he asked for, should come first. The letter also compares vacancy to only one year of the gap. If his $5,650 is a flat rate for the whole lease, your offer comes to about $14,000 less over five years. That's roughly 2.5 months of an empty unit. "Less than three months of vacancy would cost more than the entire five-year difference" is much stronger. Check first whether his offer has its own yearly increases. If it does, this comparison and the year-five point both get weaker.

**5. The kitchen point can backfire.** "The hood stays with the building" might make him think his space is worth more, and that a restaurant tenant should pay more for it. The better way to use it is that the space is set up for a restaurant. A coffee chain won't need the hood and will probably want its own build-out. Only say that if you believe it about this chain. Don't present it as a fact.

**6. The $28 vs. $29 line is a bit pointed.** It's a good fact, but it puts his own pricing in front of him. State it once and don't work it out for him.

## What I'd change

- Lead with the year-five number and the five-year total.
- Add one honest line about 2024.
- Cut "more than the restaurant can carry."
- Replace the coffee-chain paragraph with the 14-months-empty answer.
- Reframe the kitchen point as the space being built for a restaurant.
- Name a signing date instead of "well before August."

One decision only you can make: is $5,100 your opening offer or your ceiling? He'll likely read it as an opener and come back around $5,350–5,400. If it really is your limit, the letter should say so plainly.
