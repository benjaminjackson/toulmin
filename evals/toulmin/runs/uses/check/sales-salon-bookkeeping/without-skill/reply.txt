**Mostly yes, it will work.** The page is specific, it's honest, and it leans on the strongest thing you have, which is that you only work with salons. A salon owner will find it believable. The problems are in emphasis and a few places where the writer added claims or got the facts slightly wrong.

## Where it's weak

**1. The opening is generic.** "You didn't open a salon to reconcile bank statements at midnight" could be on any bookkeeper's page. The second paragraph (booth rent, commission splits, tips through the card machine) is the real hook because it shows you know their world. Your best result, the $800 saved at tax time, is buried halfway down. Right now the page opens with a cliché and holds back its proof.

**2. The writer added claims you didn't give them.** You should check these:
- "Tips included" on payroll, and the list of booth rent, commission splits and card-machine tips. These are probably true, but they're promises about what you know and handle. Make sure each one is accurate.
- "Including ones who had been with me for years." You didn't say how long your clients have been with you.
- "No introductory deals." This may be true, but you didn't say it.

**3. The pricing section undercuts itself.** "No surprise increases" sits right after telling readers you raised everyone's price by $50 in January. Your existing clients may see this page, and to them that raise may have felt like a surprise. The point worth keeping is that everyone pays the same rate. The line about "no surprise increases" invites people to question it.

**4. "Missed the 10th three times" is ambiguous, and it matters a lot.** Was it 3 months out of 12, or 3 client closes out of roughly 370? The first means you're on time 75% of the time. The second means about 99%. A reader can't tell which. The follow-up line is also weak: "I don't hide it, and I work hard to make sure it doesn't happen" praises your own honesty and says nothing concrete. It would be stronger to say what happened those three times: how late you were and whether clients knew ahead of time.

**5. The "Someone who stays" heading doesn't match what's under it.** The heading suggests that you stay. The facts show that your clients stay. Those are different claims. The client one is the impressive one: nobody left for any reason except selling their salon.

**6. Sunday and Monday is a bigger issue than the page admits.** Many salons close on Sunday and Monday, so those may be the very days owners have time for admin. The page states you're unavailable and moves on. A reader will want to know how to reach you on those days (email? a reply on Tuesday?) and how quickly you usually respond. You'd need to supply those answers. The writer shouldn't make them up.

**7. Too many disclaimers together.** The price raise, the missed deadlines, "I can't promise," one person, and not answering on Sunday or Monday are each fine alone. In about 350 words they start to sound defensive. Keep the honesty, but put every caveat right after its matching promise. Don't let any of them get their own sentence of self-praise.

**8. The call has no stated purpose.** "20 minutes and free" tells readers what it costs but not what they'll get from it. One line on what happens on the call, or what they'll know afterward, would help more clicks happen.

## Smaller points
- It runs a bit over 350 words. Cutting "I don't hide it" and most of the opening paragraph would fix that.
- "10 or fewer staff": do booth renters count as staff? Owners will wonder.
- The page never says what the owner has to do: which software, what they send you, or what happens after they sign up. Add one line if you have the answer.

## What I'd change first
1. Open with the salon-specific details and the $800 result.
2. Clarify what "3 times" means.
3. Remove the claims you didn't give, or confirm they're true.
4. Say how clients can reach you on Sundays and Mondays.
