Mostly yes. The draft is honest and well organized. It leads with the bad news, owns the missed updates without groveling, and makes a clear case for the standalone reminder product. It's about 475 words, so it fits your limit with a little room. Most of my suggestions add a sentence or two, so you'd need to trim elsewhere to pay for them.

The weak spots are the questions an investor will reply with. Here they are in priority order.

**1. The clinic numbers are wrong as written.** "Clinics: 9 added, 4 lost" mixes up customers and locations. You lost 4 *customers*, and one of them was 12 clinics, so you lost about 15 locations. A careful reader will see the 12-clinic chain inside "4 lost" and either get confused or think you're hiding the size of the loss. The line "9 new clinics, enough to cover the chain" has the same problem. Pick one unit, either customers or locations, and use it everywhere. Check whether the 9 new ones are single clinics or customers with several locations.

**2. The runway section is the weakest part, and it's the one investors care about most.** With about 11 months of cash, "we'll decide within the next quarter whether to cut or raise" reads as late. A raise usually takes around 6 months, so deciding in 3 leaves very little margin. Two fixes:
- **Say what result decides it.** For example: "If X clinics buy standalone reminders by [date], we raise. If not, we cut burn to $Y." Without a number, "the reminder numbers will drive that decision" sounds like you're stalling.
- **Say which way you're leaning.** These 11 people are the most likely source of bridge money. If a raise is likely, they should hear it now, not next quarter.

**3. The standalone product leaves a big question open.** If a clinic keeps its EHR's scheduler, how do your reminders get its appointments? Investors will want to know whether you integrate with those EHRs, which ones, and whether that work is built. They'll also ask whether the EHRs already send reminders of their own. The chain's EHR may well do so. The watch list asks whether results "stay as strong" but skips whether the product works at all without your scheduler. One sentence on this would help.

**4. The watch list has no targets.** "How many clinics buy, and how quickly" can't be judged as a success or a failure. Give a number for next quarter, which also sets up issue #2.

**5. The 7% vs. 13% claim needs context.** How many clinics is it based on, and over what period? Clinics that choose to turn on reminders may already be better at reducing no-shows, and a skeptical investor will spot that. Adding "across N clinics over M months" makes the claim stronger.

**6. The two missed quarters still aren't covered.** The apology works, but investors haven't had numbers for 9 months, and the draft only compares to 3 months ago. Add one line on MRR over the full gap, for example "MRR was $X in [month], $41K now." If it was flat the whole time, that matters and they'll find out anyway.

**7. Customer concentration isn't addressed.** You call EHR schedulers "the biggest risk to our business," but you don't say whether other large customers are exposed. A line such as "our next-largest customer is $X/month, and here's their situation" would show you've checked.

**Easy cuts to make room:**
- Runway appears twice, once in the numbers list and again in its own section. Keep the number in the list and use the section for the decision.
- Delete "I'm taking that seriously." It adds nothing.
- "Clearest" is used twice for the reminder results. Use it once.

Fix #1 before sending, because it's an accuracy problem. Issues #2 and #3 are what decide whether investors finish reading confident in the plan or worried about it.
