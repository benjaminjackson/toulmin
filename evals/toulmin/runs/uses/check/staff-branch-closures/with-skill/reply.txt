**Short answer:** Mostly yes. The draft is honest and in the right order. It leads with the news, says early that nobody loses a job, and doesn't hide the hours change. The 14 people at Maple Street and Route 9 will read it most closely, and it gives them most of what they need. It has gaps they'll notice, though, and it quietly dropped the main thing you asked for.

## 1. The draft never says the change makes everyone's job better

You asked for that claim and the writer left it out without telling you. That was probably the right call, but you should make it knowingly.

For the 14 branch staff, the facts cut both ways. They keep the same pay or more and get two days a week at home. But they lose 9-to-5 hours, lose face-to-face work, and will take calls from members who've been waiting 9 minutes. If you tell them it's better for everyone, they'll set that against the 8 p.m. shifts and trust the rest of the email less. For staff at the other five branches, the draft says nothing is changing, so "better" has nothing behind it.

There is one group your facts do support: **whoever answers calls and chats today.** The draft says the hold times put "real strain on everyone answering those calls," and the new center is the relief. But the draft never speaks to those people. It only covers the closing branches and "other branches." If you want a "this makes jobs better" line, aim it there, and for the 14 say something narrower: a protected job with the same pay or more and a work-from-home option.

## 2. Questions the 14 will have that the email doesn't answer

- **Where is the center?** The draft never says. Commute is probably the first thing they'll weigh.
- **If someone takes the role and it doesn't work out, do they lose the severance?** This decides whether people dare to try it. If there's a trial period that keeps severance available, say so.
- **What does "or higher" mean?** Is there extra pay for early or late shifts? Who gets more? If it's undecided, say it'll be covered in the one-on-ones.
- **How do the rotating shifts work?** How often someone works until 8 p.m. and how much notice they get.
- **Severance terms:** when it's paid, whether there's a cap, whether there's anything to sign, and what happens to benefits.

You know these answers and the writer didn't. Each one you add takes away a reason to assume the worst.

## 3. "Everyone else" isn't really unaffected

Members of the two closing branches will go somewhere, most likely the nearest of the other five. Those staff will get busier, so "your location, role and hours aren't changing" is true but incomplete. The writer flagged this with a bracket and didn't resolve it. Say plainly what you expect.

Also, every branch employee will start getting questions from members. The email should say when and how members will be told, and what staff should say before then.

## 4. Smaller points

- **"Get it right the first time"** suggests the people answering phones now don't. Cut it or reword it.
- **Placeholders:** there are six bracketed blanks. The one-on-one date and the decision deadline matter most, because they turn "you have options" into a real process.
- **The closing line**, "I hope many of you will help us build…", can read as quiet pressure to accept, right after a paragraph saying the choice is theirs. A plain thank-you is safer.
- **Subject line:** it's neutral, which is fine. You could consider signalling job security there, since that's the first fear.

## 5. Not about the wording, but it matters most

The 14 people at those two branches shouldn't learn their branch is closing from an all-staff email. Have their managers tell them in person, ideally with you there, shortly before the email goes out. The email then confirms what they've already heard, and it will work much better that way.
