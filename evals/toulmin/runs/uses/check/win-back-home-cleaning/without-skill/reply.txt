# Feedback on the win-back email draft

## The "same cleaner every time" promise

The writer didn't make the promise you asked for, and they were right. You can't keep it. Seven of 18 cleaners left last year, and your own system has a named backup for sick days and vacations, so customers will sometimes get someone else. A customer who left over cleaner changes is the one most likely to notice a broken promise, and they'd probably leave for good. What the draft promises instead is something you can actually do: a regular cleaner, plus a backup you know by name. Keep that.

## Will it work?

It works for customers who left because the cleaner kept changing. It will mostly fail with customers who left over price. They'll read that you're still $25 more per visit, and a one-time $99 cleaning doesn't change that. Some will book the $99 cleaning and then not continue.

## Where it's weak

1. **The subject line claims too much.** "We fixed it" isn't true for price, and price was the most common reason people left. Price-leavers will open the email and feel misled.

2. **It's written to everyone at once.** "Many of you told us" sounds like a mass email, even though it opens with "Hi [First name]." You know from the exit surveys why each person left. Send two versions: one for price, one for cleaner changes. Each can then talk only about that person's reason.

3. **"You were right about the second one"** suggests they were wrong about price. That sounds defensive. It's better to simply say you cost more.

4. **The price explanation is about your cleaners, not the customer.** "$21 an hour" tells customers why you charge more, not what they get for it. Connect it to them, for example: insurance means you pay if something gets damaged. Don't claim that employees stay longer, though. Losing 39% of your staff in a year doesn't support that.

5. **It doesn't say what happens if a cleaner quits.** Turnover caused the problem, and scheduling doesn't stop people leaving. One line would cover it: if your regular cleaner leaves, your backup, someone you already know, takes over.

6. **The offer is missing details:**
   - What the price is after the $99 cleaning. Say it goes back to $165 so nobody is surprised.
   - Whether there's a deadline. Without one, people put it off.
   - Whether it applies to every home size.

7. **It misses a chance to get personal.** If you can assign the cleaner before sending, naming them ("Maria would be your regular cleaner") makes the promise concrete. That's especially strong in the version for cleaner-change customers.

## What I'd keep

The plain tone, the honest price comparison, the regular-plus-backup idea, the single call to action, and the length.
