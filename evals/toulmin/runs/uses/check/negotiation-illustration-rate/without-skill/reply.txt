**Short answer:** Yes, this should work. It's polite and specific, and it gives the editor numbers they can take to whoever approves budgets. That matters most, because the editor probably can't approve a 28% raise alone. The weak spots are small.

## The $12,000 line was left out, and that's the right call

You asked for "every other publisher pays at least $12,000." The draft doesn't include it. Leave it out, because your own facts don't support it:

- You have one other publisher's fee: $12,500.
- Your agent's range starts at $11,000, which is below $12,000.

Editors know roughly what other houses pay. If they know of even one publisher paying under $12,000, the claim falls apart, and they'll start doubting your other numbers too. The draft makes the same point more safely: one real fee of $12,500, plus a range from your agent. The line "That's still below what I've been paid elsewhere" does the job you wanted the $12,000 claim to do, and nobody can dispute it.

## Where it's weak

1. **"A lot has changed since then" is filler.** The next sentence makes the point, so this phrase adds nothing.
2. **"Each book is still about four months of work" works against you.** "Still" says your work hasn't changed, so why should the price? Either cut it or make it useful. For example, $9,000 over four months is about $2,250 a month, which shows how low the fee is.
3. **"My rate elsewhere has gone up" suggests more than one other client.** You have one. It's not exactly false, but if they ask, you'll be glad you didn't overstate it. "Last year another publisher paid me $12,500…" is enough by itself.
4. **The sales could be more specific.** If you know the actual numbers (say, 28,000 rather than "over 20,000") and the books' titles, use them. Concrete figures are easier for the editor to pass upward.
5. **Fill in the placeholders.** "[Title] was chosen for the [State] reading list" is your strongest line, so make sure it names the book.
6. **The ending is a little soft.** "I hope we can make this work" and the offer of a call are friendly, which suits a four-year relationship. But together they hint that you'll negotiate down. If $11,500 is your real floor, consider dropping "hope." Something closer to "I'd love to do this one at $11,500" works better.

## Smaller points

- Mentioning your agent is fine, but the editor may wonder why the agent isn't handling this. If your agent normally negotiates for you, think about whether they should send it instead.
- It's well under 250 words, so you have room for the specific sales figures from point 4.

**What I'd change:** cut the vague phrases in points 1 and 3, reframe or cut the four-months line, add real sales numbers, and firm up the ending. Keep the overall structure and leave out the $12,000 claim.
