**Mostly, yes.** It will work for the roughly 31 people who were never hired as remote. It opens with the decision, gives two concrete reasons, and the partners commit to the same three days. That last part matters most for credibility.

**It's weakest for the 15 people hired as fully remote.** They'll read it most closely, and they're also the people most likely to forward it to a lawyer.

## Where it's weak

**1. The 11 remote hires who live within two hours get sympathy but no information.** Their bullet says "this is a real change… We aren't taking that lightly" and then stops. It never tells them what applies to them. They have to work out from the bullet above that they're in "most of you," and that they too start three days a week on September 1. That's the worst news in the letter, and it's the one place where the draft goes vague. "We aren't taking that lightly" also sounds empty when nothing follows it. Either say plainly that the same three days apply to them, or say what you're offering (a conversation, a transition period, help with travel). Don't let a reassuring sentence stand in for either.

**2. The groups overlap, so the bullets are confusing.** The 4 who live far away are part of the 15, and "most of you" includes 11 of the 15. A reader can't easily tell which bullet is theirs. Each person should be able to find their own line and know exactly what happens to them.

**3. Check the contract question before this goes out.** "A real change to what you signed up for" admits that their terms are changing. If their contracts or offer letters say fully remote, changing that may need consultation or consent, depending on where you are. It's worth a quick check with whoever handles employment matters before you put that sentence in writing.

**4. The reasons are good, but one is partly made up and one could land as blame.**
- The examples in the junior-staff paragraph ("overhearing a client call, getting a drawing marked up at the desk") came from the writer, not from you. They're plausible. Make sure they're true of your office, because staff will know if they aren't.
- The 30% figure invites an obvious challenge: was it the remote juniors who slowed down, or everyone hired since 2022? If you know the answer, use it. If you don't, "we think" carries a lot of weight. Be ready for the question on Friday.
- "Our team seemed not to know each other" could read to remote staff as "this is your fault." The partners section helps here. It would help more if it said directly that the partners were part of the problem, since you've been in one day a week.

**5. Practical questions it doesn't answer, which will come up on Friday:**
- Are Monday and Friday remote, optional, or something else? The draft implies remote but never says so.
- Is there room for all 46 people on the same three days?
- Is there a process for exceptions, such as caring responsibilities, health, or disability?
- For the 4: which day do they come in, and who pays for travel?

You don't have to answer all of these in the email. But either decide them before Friday or say they'll be covered at the meeting. Otherwise the gaps look like you haven't thought it through.

**6. Smaller points**
- "This ends fully remote work at the firm" isn't quite true, since the 4 stay remote through 2027.
- "From then on" should say "from January 2028."
- "Any of the three of us is happy to talk." Swap "happy" for "available" or "glad to." "Happy" sounds off to someone losing their arrangement.
- Check the dates. The draft assumes September 1, 2027, and "end of 2027" for the 4, which is consistent with "next year" being said now. That only gives the 4 four months after everyone else starts. Make sure that's what you meant.

## What I'd change, in priority order
1. Give the 11 nearby remote hires a clear statement of what applies to them. Replace "we aren't taking that lightly" with something concrete.
2. Rework the bullets so each person can find the line that applies to them.
3. Check the contract position before you send it.
4. Say what happens on Monday and Friday, and mention an exceptions route.
5. Confirm the junior-staff examples are true here, and have an answer ready on the 30% figure.

It's about the right length for one page. Fixing these won't push it over.
