**Short answer: mostly yes.** The draft is credible and sounds like you. It fits in two minutes. It also doesn't say the line you asked for, and I think that's the right call. But two parts of the argument don't connect to the ask, and those are the parts the commission will notice.

## Why "every boat will go out of business" was left out

That's the right decision, though the writer should have told you why. Your own numbers argue against it. After the 2016 cut you lost about 19% of your trips, stayed in business, and got back to 155. The commissioners have heard "this will shut us all down" from every fishery that has faced a cut. If you say it, they'll stop listening to the real numbers that come after it. You also only have your own boat's figures, not the other ten.

There is one way to make a stronger claim. If you know that losing about 30 trips a season, three years running, would push you below break-even, say that, with your number. That's a fact about your boat, and it's hard to argue with.

## Where it's weak

**1. The 9% vs. 80% point doesn't lead to the ask.** The draft argues that charters land a small share, so the cut hurts you a lot for a small gain. But a 2-fish limit applies to private anglers too, so it doesn't fall harder on you. And the ask is a 3-fish limit for everyone, which the 9% figure doesn't support. If the 9% point is going to work, it needs to support a separate limit for charter and for-hire boats. Under a for-hire limit, keeping 3 or 4 fish on charters would cost the stock very little. Some states do set limits by sector. Either ask for that, or cut the paragraph down. As written, a commissioner will think, "so what?"

**2. It never engages the 78% figure.** "I'm not here to argue with the science" is fine. But the commission's job is to rebuild the stock, and it has a staff recommendation in front of it. You're arguing against what they're already planning to do, so you need to give them a reason to vote differently. Right now nothing says a 3-fish limit, or a review, still gets the stock rebuilt. They will discount the ask unless you can say something like "ask staff what 3 fish does to the rebuilding timeline." That puts the question to their own experts without claiming an answer you don't have.

**3. "Review after one season" is the most winnable ask, but it's worded loosely.** One season isn't enough time to measure a change in the stock. The commission may reject it on that ground alone. Ask for a review of landings and bookings data after one season, with the option to adjust. That's something staff can actually do.

**4. "This proposal would last three years, not one" is muddled.** The previous paragraph says recovery took two years, and the line implies the 2016 cut lasted one year. Do you know how long the 2016 cut lasted? If not, rephrase this so nobody can catch it out.

**5. The survey is the softest evidence, and the draft leans on it.** It's 70 people, chosen by your own association, saying what they might do. That leaves 17 people who aren't accounted for. Your 2016 bookings are what actually happened, and that's stronger. Put the 2016 numbers first and use the survey as support: "and our customers tell us it would happen again." Be ready if someone asks about the other 17.

**6. The ask comes last.** At a microphone, commissioners decide early what you want. Say it in the first 20 seconds, then give the reasons, then repeat it at the end.

**7. The harbor is mentioned once and then dropped.** "11 boats" only matters if it adds up to something, such as mates' jobs, the fuel dock, or tackle shops. Don't make up figures. But if you know any real ones, a single sentence about the harbor would make a stronger point than "every one is a small business."

## What to keep

- The opening that gives your 22 years on the water.
- The 2016 numbers and the $29,000 figure (32 trips × $900 is correct; it's lost revenue, not profit, if anyone asks).
- "People don't drive to the coast and pay for a charter to take home two fish." It's the best line in the piece.
- The calm tone. It's what will get you a hearing.

**Biggest single fix:** decide whether you're asking for a separate charter limit or a review clause, and make the 9% argument lead to that ask.
