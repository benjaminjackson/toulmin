Mostly, yes. It's clear and warm, it puts the past failure up front instead of hiding it, and it uses nearly every fact you gave. But there are two places where a skeptical neighbor will hesitate, especially someone who gave last time and got a refund. There are also a few smaller fixes.

## The two big weaknesses

**1. "The goal is smaller" doesn't hold up once someone does the math.** The bank covers $25,000, so the town has to raise $60,000. Last time the town raised $47,000. So you're asking for $13,000 more from the public than you've ever raised. Some of your readers will work that out and feel the draft was trying to slip it past them. The fix is to say it yourself and make it the encouraging point: "Last time you gave $47,000. This time, with the bank's pledge, we need $60,000. We're most of the way there in what this town has already shown it will give." It's the same fact, but now it works for you.

Also, "We learned from it" is a claim with nothing behind it. The four bullets only work as "what's different" if each one is actually new since last time. Did you already have the $1 lease or the volunteers three years ago? If so, those bullets overstate the change. Check each one. If they hold, say plainly what you learned (for example, "we asked for too much at once and had no backer").

**2. The draft never answers "why won't it just close again?"** "180 tickets a week" is used to show demand. But a skeptic reads it as "180 tickets a week, and it still closed." Your facts already answer this, but the draft doesn't connect them. A $1 rent and 46 unpaid volunteers mean the running costs are a small fraction of what sank the old cinema. Spell that out in one or two sentences. If you know why it closed in 2022, that sentence gets much stronger. I'd make this the biggest change.

## Smaller fixes

- **Say what happens to donors' money if you fall short.** The draft says you'd lose the bank's money and the lease, but not what happens to the donor's money. Your refund last time is your best proof you can be trusted, so use it: "If we miss, you get every dollar back, just like last time." Only write that if it's true this time (it depends on the platform or how you're holding the money).
- **"That's the price of one ticket"** isn't based on anything you gave the writer. Use your real ticket price or cut the line. The "under $10 per resident" figure is accurate ($9.23), but it's also a bit misleading, because most residents won't give. If you can, suggest a realistic gift amount instead.
- **Fill in the bank's name and add the year to the deadline**: June 30, 2027, if that's right. Since the deadline is seven months away, give people a date to act by. Also add how to give (link, address) and who's signing. Signing as chair adds weight.
- **"One more time"** at the end sounds like a farewell screening. You're asking to reopen it for at least ten years, so the ending should say that.
- **Length:** the draft is already near 350 words, so the additions need room. The "What your gift does" section repeats the bank match that's already in the bullets, and the per-resident paragraph is the weakest. Those are the places to cut.
