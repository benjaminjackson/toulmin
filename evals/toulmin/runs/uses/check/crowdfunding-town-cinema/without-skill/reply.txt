# Review of the cinema crowdfunding pitch

The draft will work for people who already want the cinema back. It's clear, warm, and the numbers are right: a 38-mile trip each way is 76 miles round trip, and $60,000 ÷ 6,500 is about $9.23 per resident. But the people this pitch has to win are the doubters, especially anyone who gave last time and got a refund. For them it leaves the biggest questions unanswered.

## Main weaknesses, most important first

1. **The 180-tickets figure raises an obvious question.** The draft uses it to show demand. But the cinema closed while selling 180 tickets a week, so a reader will ask why it closed and why it won't close again. The draft never says. Your strongest answer is already in the facts, but it's in the wrong place. 46 volunteers running the box office and snack bar means almost no wages, and $1 rent means almost no rent. Together that's a different business model, not just "people are ready." The pitch should say plainly why it closed and why those costs are now gone.

2. **"The goal is smaller" invites doubt.** Readers will ask how a job that needed $120,000 three years ago now needs $85,000. Did the plan get smaller, or is $85,000 too little? One sentence on what changed is needed. Also, the public now has to raise $60,000, which is $13,000 more than the $47,000 they raised last time. A sharp reader will spot that. It's better to say it openly ("we need to beat last time by $13,000") than to hope nobody notices.

3. **It doesn't say what happens to donations if the campaign falls short.** Last time everyone was refunded. Say whether that happens again. For past donors, this is the line that most lowers the risk of giving.

4. **"The building is secured" overstates it.** The lease only happens if you reach the goal, and the next sentence and the "What your gift does" section both say so. The heading contradicts the text. Change it to something conditional.

5. **"That's the price of one ticket" makes up a fact.** The ticket price isn't in your facts. Either use the real price or drop the comparison. The per-resident framing is fine, but it shouldn't rest on a number you haven't confirmed.

6. **"One more time" sends the wrong message.** It sounds like a farewell screening. You're asking for ten years, so the closing line should look forward.

7. **No clear way to give, and no signature.** There's no link, no giving levels, and nothing saying who is asking. A sign-off from you as chair, with the group's name, makes it come from real people. Also add the year to "June 30."

## Smaller points

- **[Bank name]** is still a placeholder. Naming the bank helps credibility, so get its permission and fill it in.
- **Word count:** the draft is close to the 350-word limit. To make room for points 1–3, trim elsewhere. The "first date / kids' matinee" paragraph and the "We want it back" line are the easiest cuts.

## What's working, so keep it

- The opening ("People here didn't stop going to the movies. We lost the place to go.")
- The honest account of the last campaign
- The rent-free point ("not into rent")
- The way the bank match creates urgency
