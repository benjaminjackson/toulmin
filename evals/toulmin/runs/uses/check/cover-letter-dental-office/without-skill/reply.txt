## Where it's weak, in order of importance

**1. It never says you were the office manager or that you managed people.** "I ran the front office" sounds smaller than your actual title, and your title is the same as the job you're applying for. The posting says the manager supervises 9 people. The letter mentions "fourteen staff" only as the size of the clinic, not as people you hired, trained, scheduled or supervised. If you did manage staff, say so with a number. This is the biggest fix.

**2. It doesn't connect veterinary work to dental work.** The reader may wonder whether vet experience counts. The parallels are strong: several doctors sharing one schedule, recall reminders, records, billing and a waiting room. The letter should say that in one sentence instead of leaving the reader to work it out.

**3. The insurance experience is undersold.** Dental offices deal with insurance constantly, and vet clinics mostly don't. That makes the years with your mother your best link to this job, but the letter calls it "a close view... from the patient's side." If you dealt with denials, appeals, explanation-of-benefits forms or coordinating two insurers, name those things. That's real billing work.

**4. Some lines claim things without showing them.** "I know what it takes to change systems without disrupting patients or staff" asserts that the switch went smoothly but gives no evidence. One concrete detail would do it, such as how long the switch took or that you trained the staff. The $38k to $9k figure is strong, and adding a few words on how you did it would make it stronger.

**5. The Dentrix paragraph is honest but passive.** "Ready to start learning right away" is a promise. If you start Dentrix's online tutorials before you send this and say so, it becomes evidence. It also helps with an unspoken worry: your last software change was in 2014.

**6. The second-to-last paragraph is filler.** Any applicant could write "organized and steady, and I care about making an office run well." Use that space for something only you can say. A good option is the local angle: you live in town and you're looking to stay. Turnover is expensive for small practices, so stability is a real selling point. It also quietly counters any worry about your age.
