# Will it work?

Mostly. The apology is strong. It names what happened, what you didn't do, and says "That wasn't good enough" without hedging. Many farms would skip that, and it's what earns a second read. The deadline, the price and the reply option are all clear.

The weak spot is that it answers "what did you build?" The reader is really asking "if this happens again, am I out $270 with no word from you?"

# Where it's weak

**1. It doesn't say what happens the next time crops fail.** The second field and the nets lower the risk, but the nets only cover tomatoes and peppers. The reader's complaint wasn't really the hail. It was getting half-boxes with no credit and one short email. Nothing in the email promises a different response next time. The pause credit doesn't help either, because it's for a member's own absences, not crop loss. This is the biggest gap, and the writer can't fix it because it's your decision. If you can commit to something, it belongs here. That could be credit when boxes fall below a set level, or weekly updates during a bad stretch. Even "I'll tell you right away and explain what's coming" would help.

**2. It admits the past harm but offers nothing for it.** "You paid for full boxes" is basically saying you owe them, and then there's no remedy. Some readers will notice that. If you can offer a returning-member discount or a free extra week, it would turn the apology into something real. If you can't, think about softening that line so it doesn't point at a debt you're leaving unpaid.

**3. The $31,000 line could backfire.** You mean it as proof you're serious. A reader who lost half their boxes may read it as "we spent a lot, so don't expect money back." It's about your costs, not their experience. I'd cut it, which also frees words for #1 or #2.

**4. The opening assumes too much.** "I think I know why" will sound wrong to people who left because they moved, money got tight or they had a baby. Out of 97 households, that's probably a fair number. A small hedge keeps the direct tone without misreading them.

**5. The pause credit is listed as a hail fix.** It's a good perk, but putting it under "what we changed after the hail" makes the list look padded. List it separately as something else that's new.

**6. The sign-off sounds like a goodbye.** "Thank you for the seasons you spent with us" sounds final in an email trying to win people back. "Seasons" is also wrong for anyone who had only one, which some of the 97 probably did.

**7. Minor:** The bold bullets make it look like a newsletter. In a personal apology, plain text fits better.

# What I'd change, in order

1. Decide on a crop-failure commitment and add it. This matters most.
2. Cut the $31,000 sentence.
3. Hedge the opening line.
4. Move the pause credit out of the hail list.
5. Fix the sign-off.
6. Add a gesture for last year only if you can truly afford it.

The draft is already near 190 words, so cutting #2 is what gives you room for #1.
