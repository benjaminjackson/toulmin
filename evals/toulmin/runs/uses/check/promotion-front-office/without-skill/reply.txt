# Will this memo work on your GM?

Mostly, yes. It's honest, it's specific, and it deals with the budget gap directly instead of hiding it. That makes you look more ready, not less. But a few sections are weaker than they need to be, and the strongest argument you have isn't in it at all.

## First: the draft doesn't say what you asked for, and it's right not to

You wanted it to say you're the main reason guest scores went up. The draft doesn't say that. Don't add it back.

Your GM signed off on a full lobby and room renovation during those same 18 months. If you call yourself the main reason, they'll know that's not provable, and it will make them doubt every other number on the page. Claiming too much is the quickest way to lose a reader who already knows the facts.

## Where it's weak

**1. The check-in score section is the weakest part.**
- The heading "Check-in scores rose while I was focused on the desk" is vague. It sounds like you're being careful, and readers notice that.
- The claim that the check-in score measures "the moment guests spend with our front desk staff" is shaky. A renovated lobby is part of check-in too, and your GM will think of that.
- The fix is evidence, not stronger wording. Look for:
  - **Other categories:** did check-in rise as much as, or more than, categories that only the renovation affected (rooms, cleanliness)?
  - **Timing:** did the score move when your new training started, not when the lobby reopened?
  - **Guest comments:** do reviews name desk staff?

  If you find any of that, the section becomes convincing. If you don't, shorten the section to a line or two.

**2. You have a budget argument already, and the memo doesn't use it.**
- **Training savings:** cutting training from 9 shifts to 5 saves 4 shifts per hire, and new hires usually train alongside a paid trainer. That's real payroll money. Work it out: 4 shifts × hours per shift × hourly wage × hires per year, plus the trainer's time.
- **Turnover savings:** keeping 4 more people a year also means less spent on hiring and training replacements.
- **Your 7 weeks in charge:** in a hotel, payroll is mostly controlled by scheduling staff to match occupancy. If you've done the scheduling, approved overtime, or hit labor targets in those 7 weeks, you've already been running most of that $1.1M payroll.

Put this into the budget section. Then it says "I've managed payroll without the title, and here's how I'll learn the rest." That's much stronger than "I haven't done this, but I'll learn."

**3. The "doing the job already" section has no results in it.**
- Right now it's a bracketed list of tasks and the line "without a gap in leadership," which is just a claim.
- Your GM needs to know how those 7 weeks went: scores, overtime, staffing gaps, problems you solved, anything you improved. This is your most current evidence, and it's the most empty section.

**4. "I built a team that stays" takes credit the former manager shared.**
- She was your boss for most of that period, and your GM knows it.
- Name what you personally did (the training rewrite, how you coached people, how you scheduled) and use "we" for the result.
- It's also worth asking whether wages or the local job market changed. If they did, your GM will have that in mind, and naming your specific actions holds up better.

**5. It looks backward, not forward.**
- Your GM already knows your history. What they need is a reason to pick you over an outside candidate who has run a budget, and something to show whoever has to approve the decision.
- One or two lines on your priorities for the next year would help: the next goal for turnover or scores, and who would fill your current job.

**6. One small point on the budget plan.**
- "Give you a monthly labor-cost report" may be something your GM already gets.
- Something you'll be measured against is stronger, such as "hit the labor budget by month 4" or "build next year's front office labor budget with the controller."

## What to keep
- The memo format and short, clear headings.
- Doing the turnover math for them (64% to 36%).
- Naming the budget gap yourself before your GM can raise it.
- The tone. "I'm proud of my team's part in that number" reads as confident, not defensive.

## Order of changes
Fill in the 7-week results, then put a dollar figure on the training savings and tie it to the budget section, then strengthen or shorten the guest score section. It should still fit on one page.
