**Short answer:** It's a solid base and it probably won't hurt you. But your GM will be judging one thing: can you run this department, including a $1.1M payroll? The draft is strongest on what the GM already believes (you're a good, loyal operator) and weakest on that question.

## What it gets right

- **It doesn't say you're the main reason scores went up.** You asked for that, but leaving it out was the right call, and you should know the writer quietly didn't do it. Your GM oversaw the renovation. Check-in happens in the lobby, and the lobby was renovated. If you claim the score as mainly yours, the reader who knows the opposite will discount the rest of the memo too. If you want a stronger claim here, you need data, not stronger wording (see point 4).
- **It names the budget gap and offers a plan.** Raising it yourself is much better than letting the GM raise it.
- **The numbers check out.** 9/14 is 64% and 5/14 is 36%. 9 to 5 shifts is a 44% cut, so "almost half" is a slight stretch but defensible.

## Where it's weak, most important first

**1. The budget answer is only a promise.** "I'll learn it in 90 days" asks the GM to take a $1.1M risk on faith. You probably already have experience that counts: in the 7 weeks you've been covering, did you build schedules, approve overtime, or flex staffing to occupancy? That *is* managing payroll, just without the title. If you did, put it in the budget section as evidence and keep the 90-day plan after it.

**2. Your training change saves money, and the memo never says so.** "4 fewer training shifts per hire" turns into paid hours: shifts saved × hires per year × hours per shift. Add the 4 fewer departures last year, each with its own hiring and training cost. Work those out with your real wage rates (I haven't, because I don't have them). That moves you from "good with people" to "already saves labor dollars," which answers the budget objection better than any course.

**3. "I've already been doing the job" is just a claim right now.** The bracketed list is the only evidence, and "without a gap in leadership" is something the GM watched for 7 weeks and will judge from memory. Give 2–3 checkable results from those weeks: scores held, labor stayed on target, an escalation you resolved, a schedule you rebuilt. Only list duties you actually did. If the GM knows you didn't handle something, it costs you.

**4. The guest-score section is the weakest paragraph.** Its logic is that check-in scores measure the staff. A GM will answer that check-in scores also measure the lobby, and the lobby was renovated. Better facts would separate the two:
- Did the check-in score rise **before** the lobby was finished, or soon after your training rollout?
- Did check-in rise **more than** the room or cleanliness scores, which the renovation would also have lifted?
- Do reviews mention desk staff by name?

If you have any of these, use them. If you don't, cut this section to a sentence or two. You'll need the space for points 1–3.

**5. Turnover and training are listed as results but don't say what you did.** Turnover fell from 9 to 5, but the memo doesn't say why. Was it the new training, scheduling changes, something else? The GM may wonder whether the job market or the old FOM deserves the credit. One sentence on what you changed links the result to you. Also, the comparison skips the year in between. If that year was also good, include it to show a trend. If it wasn't, be ready to explain.

**6. It only looks backward.** The GM is hiring for the next two years. One or two specific goals as FOM would show you're thinking like a department head, not someone holding the fort.

**7. It doesn't answer "who replaces you?"** Promoting you opens the assistant manager role. If you've trained someone who could step up, that removes a real concern.

**8. Fill every bracket with true details, and only promise what you'll do.** Don't name a course or a monthly report unless you'll deliver it. The GM will hold you to anything in this memo.

## On length

To stay on one page, take the space for points 1–3 out of the guest-score paragraph and the general line about why keeping staff saves money. Your GM already knows that, and the dollar figure from point 2 says it better.
