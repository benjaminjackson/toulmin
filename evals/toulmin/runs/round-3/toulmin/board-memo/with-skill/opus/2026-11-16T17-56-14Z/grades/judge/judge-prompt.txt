Help me write a memo to our board. I run a small nonprofit newsroom with 9 staff. I want the board to approve dropping display ads and moving to a reader membership program from January.

What I have:
- Ads brought in $84,000 last year, down from $131,000 two years ago.
- We ran a 6-week membership pilot in the spring. 212 readers signed up at $8 a month. We did no marketing beyond two newsletter mentions.
- Our newsletter has 14,000 subscribers.
- A newsroom about our size in another state told me membership now covers 40% of their budget.
- Two of our seven board members come from advertising and sales backgrounds. Our ad sales person is part-time and has been with us for 11 years.

I want the memo to say that membership will replace our ad revenue within a year. Keep it to one page.

<document>
**MEMO**

**To:** Board of Directors
**From:** [Your name], Executive Director
**Date:** November 2026
**Re:** Replacing display advertising with reader membership from January 2027

**Decision requested:** Approve ending display advertising on 31 December 2026 and launching a reader membership program on 1 January 2027.

**Why now**
Display ads brought in $84,000 last year, down from $131,000 the year before. That's a 36% drop in twelve months, and the $84,000 is before the cost of selling it. If the decline continues at that pace, ads will bring in roughly $34,000 in 2027. Keeping ads isn't a safe choice. It means relying on a revenue line that is shrinking.

**What the pilot showed**
In a six-week pilot this spring, 212 readers joined at $8 a month, which is $20,352 a year. We did nothing to recruit them beyond two newsletter mentions. That's 1.5% of our 14,000 newsletter subscribers.

**What it takes to replace ads**
Matching $84,000 a year takes 875 members, or 6.25% of our current newsletter list. Starting from 212, we need about 55 new members a month through 2027. The pilot signed up about 35 a week, roughly 150 a month. The pace we need is about a third of what we've already reached, and this time we'll have real marketing behind it. We expect membership to match our ad revenue on an annual basis by the end of 2027.

The case doesn't rest on our numbers alone. A newsroom about our size in another state now covers 40% of its budget through membership.

**What could go wrong, and how we'll know**
The first people to join were probably our most committed readers. Later sign-ups will come more slowly, and some members will cancel. So I'm not asking you to take this on faith. I propose three checkpoints:

- **31 March 2027:** 375 members
- **30 June 2027:** 550 members
- **31 December 2027:** 875 members ($84,000 a year)

If we miss the March checkpoint, I'll bring options to the board in April, including resuming some ad sales.

Membership will build over the year, so we'll receive less than the full annual figure in 2027. If growth is steady, we'll receive about $52,000. That is still more than ads would bring in if last year's decline repeats.

**Why not run both?**
We're a nine-person newsroom. Running two revenue models means splitting our attention between them, when one of them is shrinking. We have the best chance of hitting these targets if we put all our effort into membership.

**Our ad sales colleague**
This change ends the ad sales role as it exists today. [Name] has sold advertising for us part-time for 11 years. I'll speak with them before the board meets and will bring a proposal for their role to the board.

I'm happy to go through the figures in detail before the meeting.
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
