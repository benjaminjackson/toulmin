I wrote the script, but I left out the coffee line because the numbers don't support it, especially in Ridgeview.

- **Typical home in the city ($410,000):** $77.90 a year. That's about $6.50 a month, $1.50 a week or 21¢ a day.
- **Typical home in Ridgeview ($640,000):** $121.60 a year. That's about $10 a month, $2.34 a week or 33¢ a day.

At $10 a month, a Ridgeview household pays well over the price of one coffee. Also, 38% of households rent, so "the average household" doesn't really fit a property tax. Ridgeview has a lot of retirees who know their assessed values, and the association president is already calling this a blank check. If one person at the door does the math and catches a volunteer rounding down, that's exactly the story the opposition wants.

So the card uses the real Ridgeview figure ("33 cents a day") and gives volunteers a simple way to work out any home's cost: $19 a year for every $100,000 of assessed value. If you want a coffee comparison, "about a cup of coffee a week" is defensible in Ridgeview ($2.34 a week). Even so, I'd lead with the dollar figure.

**Before printing, check:**

- **Oversight in the ballot language.** If the measure includes an audit, citizen oversight or a restriction on how the money can be used, add it to the "blank check" answer. It would make that answer much stronger.
- **The first line.** Volunteers should say "neighbor" only if they live in Ridgeview.
- **Other precincts.** For later precincts, swap the cost line to $78 a year, about $6.50 a month.
