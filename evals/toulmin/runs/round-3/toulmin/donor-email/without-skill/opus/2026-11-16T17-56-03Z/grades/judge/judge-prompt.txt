You are the reader described between the <reader> tags. Read the document between the <document> tags as that reader would receive it.

The brief between the <brief> tags is what the writer was asked to do and shows the facts the writer had. You, as the reader, have not seen the brief. Use it only to check what the document does with those facts.

<reader>
A former donor to the community music school who gave between 2019 and 2022 and stopped after the school closed its Eastside branch. This reader was unhappy about the closure and has not given since. The reader decides whether to give this month.
</reader>

<brief>
I need a year-end fundraising email for people who used to give to us and stopped. I'm the development director at a community music school, and I'm the whole development office.

What I have:
- 61 kids are on our waiting list for subsidized lessons. A year of weekly lessons costs us $1,150 per child.
- We have 340 lapsed donors: people who gave at least once between 2019 and 2022 and nothing since. Their average last gift was $85.
- Last December's email to the same group brought in 9 gifts totaling $1,240.
- A family foundation has offered to match gifts from returning donors dollar for dollar, up to $15,000, through December 31.
- A lot of these donors stopped giving after we closed the Eastside branch in 2022. A few wrote angry notes back then. One of them, a former board member, still forwards our emails to her friends with comments.

I want the email to say that their gift this month guarantees every child on the waiting list a place in January. Under 250 words, signed by our executive director.
</brief>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
