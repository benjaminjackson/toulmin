**MEMO**

**To:** Board of Directors
**From:** [Name], Executive Director
**Date:** [Date]
**Re:** Replacing display advertising with reader membership from January 2027

**Decision requested**
Approve ending display advertising on December 31, 2026 and launching a reader membership program in January 2027, with a board review at mid-year.

**Why change now**
Ad revenue fell from $131,000 to $84,000 in two years, a 36% drop. If it keeps falling at that rate (about 20% a year), ads will bring in roughly $67,000 in 2027 and less after that. Staying with ads means managing a decline.

**What the pilot showed**
In a six-week pilot this spring, 212 readers joined at $8 a month. The only promotion was two mentions in the newsletter. That is 1.5% of our 14,000 newsletter subscribers, and about $20,000 a year in recurring revenue for almost no effort. A newsroom of our size in [state] says membership now covers 40% of its budget. One example is not a forecast, but it shows the model can support a newsroom like ours.

**The target**
To match last year's $84,000 in ad revenue we need 875 members, or 6.25% of our newsletter list. To match what ads are likely to earn in 2027, we need about 700. Reaching 875 by December 2027 means adding about 13 members a week. The pilot added 35 a week without a campaign.

**Our goal: membership revenue running at $84,000 a year by December 2027.**

**Why not run both**
An ad-free site is the clearest benefit we can offer members, and dropping ads frees the staff time that now goes into selling them. [Confirm or replace with your reason.]

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
