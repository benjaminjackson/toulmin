<brief>
Help me write a memo to our board. I run a small nonprofit newsroom with 9 staff. I want the board to approve dropping display ads and moving to a reader membership program from January.

What I have:
- Ads brought in $84,000 last year, down from $131,000 two years ago.
- We ran a 6-week membership pilot in the spring. 212 readers signed up at $8 a month. We did no marketing beyond two newsletter mentions.
- Our newsletter has 14,000 subscribers.
- A newsroom about our size in another state told me membership now covers 40% of their budget.
- Two of our seven board members come from advertising and sales backgrounds. Our ad sales person is part-time and has been with us for 11 years.

I want the memo to say that membership will replace our ad revenue within a year. Keep it to one page.
</brief>

The person who wrote the brief adds this about its facts: The two ad figures are for two years in a row. Ads brought in $131,000 in the year before last and $84,000 last year, so the fall of $47,000 (36%) happened in one year.

<document>
**MEMO**

**To:** Board of Directors
**From:** [Name], Executive Director
**Date:** [Date]
**Re:** Approval to replace display advertising with reader membership, starting January

**Recommendation**
I ask the board to approve ending display advertising on January 1 and making reader membership our main source of earned revenue, with the goal of fully replacing ad income within 12 months.

**Why now**
Display ad revenue fell from $131,000 to $84,000 over two years, a 36% drop. If that trend continues, ads will bring in about $65,000 next year and less after that. We would be protecting a shrinking source of income while ads slow our site and undercut the reader relationship we depend on.

**What the pilot showed**
This spring's six-week pilot signed up 212 members at $8/month with almost no effort: two newsletter mentions and no campaign. That is 1.5% of our 14,000 newsletter subscribers in six weeks. [Add: X of the 212 are still paying today.] A newsroom of similar size in [state] now covers 40% of its budget through membership. It is one example, but it shows the model works at our scale.

**The math**
- Replacing $84,000 requires about **875 members** at $8/month, or about 6% of our newsletter list.
- The pilot got to a quarter of that number with no marketing. With a real campaign, annual plans and a higher giving tier, 875 is an ambitious target, but we can reach it.
- Membership revenue builds over the year. If growth is steady, year one brings in about **$50,000**, leaving a gap of about **$30,000**. I propose covering it from [reserves / a bridge grant from X].

**Checkpoints the board can hold us to**
- **March 31:** 400 members
- **June 30:** 550 members. If we are below 450, staff will come back with options, including bringing back some advertising.
- **December 31:** 875 members, or $7,000/month in recurring revenue

**Staffing**
Our part-time ad sales lead has 11 years of relationships with local businesses. I propose moving that role to sponsorships and major-donor outreach, which supports membership instead of competing with it. [Confirm with staff member before sending.]

**Risks**
The main risk is slower growth than planned. The checkpoints let us see that by midyear and adjust. The bigger long-term risk is staying tied to an ad market that is shrinking every year.

**Requested action:** Approve ending display ads effective January 1, the membership plan and targets above, and [bridge funding source] to cover the first-year gap.
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
