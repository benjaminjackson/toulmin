What I have:

<document>
**MEMO**

**To:** Board of Directors
**From:** [Name], [Title]
**Date:** November 2026
**Re:** Approval to end display ads and launch reader membership on January 1, 2027

**Decision requested**
I'm asking the board to approve ending display advertising on December 31 and launching a reader membership program on January 1. Our target is that by December 2027, membership will be bringing in $84,000 a year, the same as our ads did last year.

**Ads are declining, so staying put is not a safe option**
Ad revenue fell from $131,000 to $84,000 in one year, a 36% drop. If that rate continues, ads will bring in about $54,000 this year and $35,000 in 2027. Keeping ads means relying on a shrinking source of income.

**What the pilot showed**
In a six-week spring pilot, 212 readers joined at $8 a month. We did no marketing beyond two newsletter mentions. That's about 35 new members a week, and $20,352 a year in revenue, already a quarter of last year's ad income. Those members came from just 1.5% of our 14,000 newsletter subscribers.

**What the target takes**
To bring in $84,000 a year, we need 875 members, about one in every 16 newsletter subscribers. Starting from 212, that means adding about 55 members a month through 2027, or roughly 13 a week. The pilot added 35 a week with almost no effort. A newsroom of our size in [state] now gets 40% of its budget from membership, which shows this model can work for outlets like ours.

**What could go wrong, and how we'll handle it**
- *The pilot drew our most loyal readers first.* Later members will be harder to win. I propose promoting membership in every newsletter and across the site from January, which the pilot never did.
- *Members cancel.* We'll track cancellations every month and report them to the board each quarter.
- *Why not keep ads alongside membership?* An ad-free site is the clearest thing we can offer members for their money, and ad income is falling either way.

**Checkpoint**
By June 30, 2027, we'll aim to have 500 members, worth about $48,000 a year. If we're short, I'll bring the board a revised 2027 budget in July, along with a recommendation on whether to resume ad sales.

**Our ad sales colleague**
[Name] has sold our ads part-time for 11 years. I propose [transition plan: e.g., a role in membership and sponsor outreach / a transition period / severance terms].

**If the board says no**
We keep relying on income that dropped by a third last year, and we lose the momentum of 212 readers who signed up with almost no prompting.
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
