Help me write a memo to our board. I run a small nonprofit newsroom with 9 staff. I want the board to approve dropping display ads and moving to a reader membership program from January.

What I have:
- Ads brought in $84,000 last year, down from $131,000 two years ago.
- We ran a 6-week membership pilot in the spring. 212 readers signed up at $8 a month. We did no marketing beyond two newsletter mentions.
- Our newsletter has 14,000 subscribers.
- A newsroom about our size in another state told me membership now covers 40% of their budget.

I want the memo to say that membership will replace our ad revenue within a year. Keep it to one page.
</brief>

<document>
**MEMO**

**To:** Board of Directors
**From:** [Name], Executive Director
**Date:** [Date]
**Re:** Ending display advertising and launching reader membership in January 2027

**Decision requested**
Approve ending display advertising on December 31, 2026, and launching a reader membership program on January 1, 2027.

**Why change now**
Display ad revenue fell from $131,000 to $84,000 in two years, a drop of 36%. If that trend continues, we should expect about $67,000 in 2027 and less after that. The decline is happening across the industry, and more selling on our side won't reverse it.

**What the pilot showed**
In a six-week spring pilot, 212 readers joined at $8 a month. Our only promotion was two newsletter mentions. That's 1.5% of our 14,000 newsletter subscribers, reached with almost no effort, and it already adds up to about $20,000 a year. A newsroom our size in another state now covers 40% of its budget through membership.

**The target**
By December 2027, membership should bring in $84,000 a year, matching last year's ad revenue. That takes about 875 members, or 6% of our current newsletter list. We'll grow the list and convert readers through a regular membership campaign: a newsletter welcome series, appeals placed in our stories, and year-end and spring drives.

**Year-one gap**
Because members join over the course of the year, 2027 membership revenue will be lower than the year-end target. If we reach 875 members by December, we expect about $50,000 in 2027, compared with about $67,000 from ads. We plan to cover the gap of roughly $17,000 with [reserves / a matching-gift campaign / sponsorships]. We'll show the board actual figures against this plan every quarter.

**Checkpoints**
- **March 31:** 400 members
- **June 30:** 575 members
- **September 30:** 725 members
- **December 31:** 875 members

If we're more than 20% behind at the June checkpoint, I'll bring the board options. These include higher-tier memberships, a sponsorship program, or limited non-display advertising.

**Staffing**
[Name], our part-time ad sales lead for 11 years, knows our audience and our local businesses better than anyone. I propose [offering them a role leading membership growth and sponsorships / describe your plan].

**Why this is the right direction**
Ad revenue goes up and down with a market we don't control. Membership revenue comes from readers who value our work. It recurs every month, it grows as our audience grows, and it strengthens our independence.
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
