I've written the memo, but not with the flat line "membership will replace our ad revenue within a year." Your own figures make that sentence easy to pick apart, and two board members with sales backgrounds will do it.

**Where the flat claim breaks:**
- **The target.** Matching $84,000 takes 875 members at $8 a month. You have 212.
- **The pace is the good news.** You need about 55 new members a month. The pilot brought in about 150 a month with almost no promotion.
- **The cash isn't there in year one.** Even if you reach 875 by December 2027, members join gradually through the year. Steady growth gives about $52,000 in 2027, not $84,000. Saying "replace within a year" invites someone to point that out.
- **The fair comparison is smaller than $84,000.** If ads keep falling at the same rate as the last two years, they'd earn about $67,000 in 2027. Against that, the year-one shortfall is about $15,000, not $32,000.

So the memo keeps your goal but puts it in a form the board can check: 875 members by December 2027, with checkpoints of 380 by March 31 and 545 by June 30, and a promise to come back to them if you fall short. The checkpoints assume steady growth from the 212 pilot members.

**Gaps I couldn't fill (marked in brackets):**
- **How many of the 212 still pay.** This is your strongest missing fact. A good retention number answers the "early adopters will quit" objection. If many have lapsed, lower the starting number and recheck the checkpoints.
- **How you'll cover the roughly $15,000 first-year gap.** Money saved from ending ad sales may cover part of it.
- **Why not keep ads and add membership.** Your ad-background board members will almost certainly ask this, and nothing you gave me answers it. I've left a slot for your reason.
- **Your ad salesperson's future.** Talk to them before the memo goes out, and think about briefing the two ad-background members one-on-one beforehand.
- **The peer newsroom.** Name it if you can. One unnamed example shows this can work, not that it will work for you, which is why the memo uses it lightly.
