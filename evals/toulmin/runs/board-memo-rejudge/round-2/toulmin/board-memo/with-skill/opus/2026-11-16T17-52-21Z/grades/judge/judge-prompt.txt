Help me write a memo to our board. I run a small nonprofit newsroom with 9 staff. I want the board to approve dropping display ads and moving to a reader membership program from January.

What I have:
- Ads brought in $84,000 last year, down from $131,000 two years ago.
- We ran a 6-week membership pilot in the spring. 212 readers signed up at $8 a month. We did no marketing beyond two newsletter mentions.
- Our newsletter has 14,000 subscribers.
- A newsroom about our size in another state told me membership now covers 40% of their budget.
- Two of our seven board members come from advertising and sales backgrounds. Our ad sales person is part-time and has been with us for 11 years.

I want the memo to say that membership will replace our ad revenue within a year. Keep it to one page.
</brief>

**MEMO**

**To:** Board of Directors
**From:** [Name], Executive Director
**Date:** [Date]
**Re:** Replacing display ads with reader membership from January 2027

**Decision requested:** End display advertising on December 31, 2026, and launch reader membership on January 1, 2027, with a progress checkpoint on June 30, 2027.

**Ads are shrinking.** Ad revenue fell from $131,000 to $84,000 in two years, a 36% drop or about 20% a year. If that continues, ads bring in about $67,000 in 2027 and $54,000 in 2028. So the choice isn't between $84,000 of ads and an untested idea. It's between a revenue line that keeps shrinking and one we can grow.

**The pilot worked with almost no effort.** In six weeks this spring, 212 readers joined at $8 a month, or $20,352 a year. Our only promotion was two newsletter mentions. That's 1.5% of our 14,000 newsletter subscribers, about 35 new members a week. A newsroom of our size in [state] now gets 40% of its budget from members. Their experience doesn't predict ours, but it shows the model can work for a newsroom like us.

**What it takes to replace ads.** 875 members at $8 a month bring in $84,000 a year. To get there by the end of 2027, we need about 13 new members a week, less than half the pilot's pace. That's 6% of our newsletter list. The gap leaves room for cancellations and for slower sign-ups once our keenest readers have joined. To match what ads would probably bring in 2027, we need about 700 members.

**Why stop ads rather than run both:** [Your reason, e.g. reader experience, staff time spent on ad sales, or making an ad-free site part of what members get.]

**What the board should weigh:**
- *A smaller 2027.* Ad income stops in January, but membership builds over the year. If it grows steadily, we collect about $52,000 in 2027 against the roughly $67,000 ads would probably have brought in. We will cover the $15,000 difference by [reserves / bridge grant / other].
- *Our colleague.* [Name] has sold our ads for 11 years. I propose [their new role, e.g. leading business memberships and sponsorships, where their local relationships are an asset].

**Checkpoint.** To be on track, we need about 540 members by June 30, 2027 (about $52,000 a year). If we have fewer than 450, I'll bring options to the July board meeting, including bringing back sponsorships.

**Recommendation:** Approve the switch to membership from January 1, 2027, with the June 30 checkpoint above.

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
