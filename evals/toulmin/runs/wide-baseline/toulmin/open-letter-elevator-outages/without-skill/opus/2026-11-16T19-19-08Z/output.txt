From the Tenants' Association of [Building Name], [Address]

To [Name/Title of Property Manager]:

Our building has nine stories, 112 apartments and only one elevator. This year the elevator has been out of service on 41 days, including a 12-day stretch in August. We are writing publicly because our private requests have not solved the problem.

For many of our neighbors, a broken elevator is more than an inconvenience. Twenty-three of our residents are over 75, and six use wheelchairs or walkers. When the elevator stops, some of them can't leave home. They can't get to medical appointments, buy groceries or get out safely in an emergency. During the August outage, some residents were stuck in their apartments for almost two weeks.

We know the city inspected the elevator in September and gave it a passing certificate. We are not saying the elevator is unsafe to ride. Our concern is that it keeps breaking down. An elevator that passes inspection but often doesn't run still leaves our most vulnerable neighbors stranded.

In June you told us a new controller part was on order and would arrive in six to eight weeks. It is now November, and we are being told the same thing. We need a clear and believable plan, not the same promise again.

Seventy-four of our 112 households signed a petition asking for a second elevator. You have told us it would cost about $400,000 and can't be done in this building. We take that seriously, but residents deserve to see the basis for that conclusion.

1. Give residents a written update with the supplier, the order date and a confirmed delivery and installation date for the controller part.
2. Post written notice in the lobby every time the elevator goes out of service, with the expected repair time.
3. Work with us on a plan to help residents with limited mobility during outages, including checking on them and helping with deliveries.
4. Share the engineering assessment or other documents behind your conclusion that a second elevator isn't possible, and tell us what other ways to improve reliability you have considered.
5. Meet with the Tenants' Association within [number] days to discuss these steps.

We want to work with you. Our members pay rent every month and expect to be able to get to and from their homes. We hope you will respond quickly and in good faith.
