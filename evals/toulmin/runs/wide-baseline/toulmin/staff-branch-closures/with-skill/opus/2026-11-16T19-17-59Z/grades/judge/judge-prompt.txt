<brief>
Help me write an announcement to our staff. I'm the COO of a credit union with 7 branches. We're closing the Maple Street and Route 9 branches on March 31 and moving the people who work there to a new member phone and chat center.

Facts:
- The two branches have 14 staff between them. Every one of them is offered a job in the phone and chat center at the same pay or higher.
- Visits to those two branches fell 44% over the last three years. Phone and chat contacts across the credit union went up 60%.
- Members calling us now wait an average of 9 minutes on hold.
- The center will be open 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. on weekdays. The branches were open 9 to 5. Shifts will rotate.
- Staff in the center can work from home two days a week after 90 days of training.
- Anyone who doesn't take the new role gets 2 weeks of pay for every year they've worked here.

I want the announcement to say that this change will make everyone's job better. About 400 words, sent by email from me.
</brief>



<document>
**Subject: Maple Street and Route 9 branches, and our new member phone and chat center**

Team,

On March 31 we will close our Maple Street and Route 9 branches. Each of the 14 people who work there is being offered a job in our new member phone and chat center, at the same pay or higher. No one is losing their job because of this change.

I want to explain why we're doing this, and be straight about what changes.

Our members have changed how they reach us. Over the last three years, visits to these two branches fell 44%. Over the same period, phone and chat contacts across the credit union rose 60%. A member who calls us today waits an average of 9 minutes on hold. That's too long. Calls keep rising, so the wait will get longer unless we put more of our people where members are trying to reach us.

The new center does that. It will be open 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. on weekdays, so members who can't call during their own working day can still reach a person. Our other five branches stay open, and Maple Street and Route 9 members are welcome at any of them.

**For our colleagues at Maple Street and Route 9:** some things get better. Your pay stays the same or goes up. After 90 days of training, you can work from home two days a week. Some things are harder, and I won't pretend otherwise. The branches were open 9 to 5. The center runs until 8 p.m. and shifts will rotate, so some of your shifts will be in the evening. The work is different too: you'll be helping members by phone and chat instead of across a counter. [Add anything you're doing to help with the hours, such as input on shift schedules.]

If the new role isn't right for you, you'll receive two weeks of pay for every year you've worked here. [Say when people need to decide by.] Whatever you choose, thank you for what you've built at those branches. Members trust us because of the work you've done there.

**For everyone else:** a shorter hold time helps every one of us who talks to members. Fewer people will start a conversation with us after 9 minutes of waiting.

Your manager will meet with each of you who is affected [this week]. I'll also hold an open Q&A on [date] for anyone with questions, and my door is open before then.

Thank you,

[Name]
Chief Operating Officer
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
