Last summer, local farms offered us roughly 9,000 pounds of fresh produce over three months. We had to turn all of it down. We had no way to pick it up cold or keep it cold, and we could not accept food we knew would spoil before it reached a family.

We are a volunteer-run food bank serving 610 households in a town of about 9,000 people. Each month we hand out about 38,000 pounds of food. About 6,000 pounds of that, roughly one pound in six, is fresh produce, milk and eggs. That is the food we have the hardest time getting, because it has to stay cold from the moment we collect it.

We are asking the Foundation for $50,000 toward a $68,000 refrigerated van. We will cover the remaining $18,000 from our own reserves. That is most of our savings and will leave us with $11,000. We are willing to make that commitment because the van closes the biggest gap in what we can offer our neighbors.

**What the van will change**

With a refrigerated van, we can say yes when farms call. If we had been able to accept last summer's offers, the fresh food we handed out over those three months would have risen from about 18,000 pounds to about 27,000 pounds. That is half again as much fresh food for the same 610 households.

Our goal for the first year is to collect at least the 9,000 pounds of farm produce we turned away last summer. Outside the growing season, we will use the van to seek other donations of produce, milk and eggs that we could not consider before because we had no way to keep them cold.

**Who will run it**

We have 23 regular volunteers. Four of them have already committed to driving the van on a weekly schedule. Like everything else we do, the van will be run by volunteers.

**Our record with the Foundation**

The Foundation funded $12,000 in shelving for us in 2023. Our final report on that grant was four months late. We take responsibility for that, and we want this grant to be handled differently. For this grant, we will:

- submit a report after our first summer with the van, showing the pounds of fresh food collected, the farms it came from, and how the total compares with our 9,000-pound goal; and
- submit our final report on or before its due date, with the deadline tracked by our board.

If we fall short of 9,000 pounds in the first summer, that report will say so and explain what we will change.

**If the van is not funded**

If farms offer us produce again next summer, we will have to turn it down again. Fresh food will stay at about 6,000 pounds a month for 610 households, and food grown a few miles away will not reach the families who need it.
