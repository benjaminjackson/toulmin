**Project Narrative: Refrigerated Van**

Last summer, local farms offered us roughly 9,000 pounds of fresh produce over three months. We turned all of it down. We had no way to pick it up cold and nowhere to keep it cold once it arrived. We are asking the Foundation for $50,000 toward a $68,000 refrigerated van so that we never have to refuse food like that again.

**Who we serve**
We are a volunteer-run food bank in a town of about 9,000 people. Each month we distribute about 38,000 pounds of food to 610 households. About 6,000 pounds of that is fresh produce, milk and eggs. That is less than one-sixth of what we hand out.

**What the van will do**
The produce we turned down would have raised our fresh food distribution by half during the growing season, from about 6,000 pounds a month to about 9,000. For each of the 610 households we serve, that is roughly five more pounds of fresh food a month. In the van's first year, we expect to distribute at least 9,000 more pounds of fresh food than we do now. That figure counts only the produce farms have already offered us. Once we can collect it cold, we will go back to those farms and ask for more.

**Who will drive it**
We have 23 regular volunteers, and four of them have already committed to driving the van on a weekly schedule. With four drivers sharing the route, one person's absence will not cancel a pickup.

**Our share**
We will pay the remaining $18,000 from our own reserves. That is more than a quarter of the van's cost, and it leaves us with $11,000. We are prepared to commit that much of our savings to this van.

**Our record with the Foundation**
The Foundation granted us $12,000 for shelving in 2023. Our final report on that grant was four months late. That was our failure, and we will not repeat it. If this grant is awarded, we will send the Foundation a short update six months after the van enters service and file our final report on or before its due date.

**How we will measure results**
Every month we will record how many pounds of fresh food we collect with the van and how many pounds of fresh food we distribute. We will compare both against our current baseline of 6,000 pounds a month. Our six-month update will show whether we are on pace for 9,000 additional pounds. If we are behind, the update will say why and what we are changing.

**If we do not get the van**
Next summer will look like last summer. Farms will offer us fresh food, and we will have to tell them no. Last year that was about 9,000 pounds of produce that could have gone to the 610 households we serve.
