I've drafted the letter below. There were three judgement calls, since I couldn't check them with you:

- **I mentioned the five weeks away, briefly.** Saying "two missed shifts in three years" on its own would leave out the five weeks. Then the letter would be misleading, and that could come back on you if the college ever asks. Put next to his father's illness, the absence reads as a family emergency, not a reliability problem. I left out the two days' notice: in an emergency it's understandable, and the committee doesn't need it.
- **I included the written English, with the night class as the answer.** The committee will probably notice it in his application anyway. It's better coming from you along with the fact that he's already working on it. If you'd rather leave it out, delete the fifth paragraph and the letter still holds together.
- **I didn't make up any results.** I left out cost savings, cows saved, or anything the vet said about him, because you didn't give me those. If you have details like that, they'd strengthen the mastitis paragraph. For example, whether all nine cows went back to the milking string, or what your vet thinks of his flags.
