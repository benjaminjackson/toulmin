# Bookkeeping for hair and nail salons. Only salons.

You opened a salon to do hair and nails, not to sort receipts at midnight. I do your monthly books, run payroll for up to 10 staff, and file your sales tax, so you don't have to.

Every client I have runs a salon. I have 31 of them right now. I don't spend your money learning how a salon works, because salons are all I work with.

## Clean books cost you less at tax time

Last tax season, 9 of my clients told me their accountant charged them less because their books came in clean. One owner told me she saved $800 on her accountant's bill. When your accountant doesn't have to fix your books first, you don't pay for those hours.

## Your books, closed by the 10th

I close each month's books by the 10th of the following month, so you always know how last month went while it still matters.

## $199 a month

That covers monthly books, payroll for up to 10 staff, and sales tax filing.

My price was $149 until January. Then I raised it to $199 for everyone, existing clients included. None of them left over it. Two clients left last year, and both left because they sold their salons.

## What you should know before you call

I'm one person, not a firm. You'll work with me every month, not with whoever is free that day.

I don't answer the phone on Sundays or Mondays. And I'll be straight with you: I missed my 10th-of-the-month deadline three times last year. I'd rather tell you that now than have you find out later.

## Start with a 20-minute call

The first call is free and takes 20 minutes. Tell me about your salon, how many people you have on payroll, and how your books look right now. I'll tell you honestly whether I'm a good fit. If I'm not, you'll know in 20 minutes and it won't cost you anything.

**[Book your free 20-minute call]**

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
