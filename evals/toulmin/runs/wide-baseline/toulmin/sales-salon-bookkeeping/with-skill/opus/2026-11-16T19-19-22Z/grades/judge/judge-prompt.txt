# Bookkeeping for hair and nail salons. Only salons.

I work with 31 salons and nobody else, so I already know how your money moves. Booth rent and commission staff, tips, retail sales and service sales, and sales tax on the products you sell. None of that needs explaining to me.

## What you get for $199 a month

- **Monthly books.** I close each month by the 10th of the next month. Last year I missed that date 3 times, and I'd rather you hear it from me than find out later. The other 9 months I hit it.
- **Payroll for up to 10 staff.** Your team gets paid correctly and on time.
- **Sales tax filing.** It gets filed, so you don't have to remember it.

The price was $149 until January. I raised it to $199 for every client, including the ones who'd already been with me. Everyone pays the same rate. No low price to sign you up and then a higher one later.

## Clean books cost you less at tax time

Accountants charge for the hours they spend sorting out a messy year. Last tax season, 9 of my clients told me their accountant charged them less because the books came in clean. One of them saved $800.

If you do your books yourself in the evenings, or hand your accountant a shoebox in March, you're paying for it twice: once in your own time and again on the accountant's bill.

## Clients stay

Last year 2 salons left me. Both left because the owners sold their salons.

## Who you're working with

I'm one person. You won't get passed around a firm or have to re-explain your salon to someone new each quarter. You'll deal with me every time.

I don't answer the phone on Sundays or Mondays. The rest of the week, I'm the one who picks up.

## Start with a 20-minute call

The first call is free and takes 20 minutes. Tell me about your salon, your staff and how you keep your books now. I'll tell you honestly whether I can help. If I'm not the right fit, you've lost 20 minutes.

**[Book your free 20-minute call]**

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
