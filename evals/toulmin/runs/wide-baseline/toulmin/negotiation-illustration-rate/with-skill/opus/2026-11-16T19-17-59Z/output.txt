I left out the line that every other publisher pays at least $12,000, because your facts don't support it. You have one publisher at $12,500, and your agent's range starts at $11,000. If the publisher knows the market, they could easily point to someone paying less, and then the rest of the email loses credibility. The claim also works against you: if everyone pays $12,000 or more, asking for $11,500 sounds like you doubt your own number.

I'd use what you can actually prove instead. The $12,500 fee and your agent's range are both specific and checkable, and $11,500 sits below that fee and in the middle of the range. That makes your ask look reasonable, even generous.

Before sending, fill in the brackets, including the state and which book made the reading list. The draft is about 200 words.

Subject: Fee for [Book Title]

Hi [Name],

Thank you for offering me [Book Title]. I'd love to illustrate it, and I'm glad we're continuing to work together.

Before I sign, I'd like to talk about the fee. I've been paid $9,000 per book since my first book with you four years ago. We've done six books together since then. Two of them have sold more than 20,000 copies, and [Title] was chosen for the [State] reading list.

Last year another publisher paid me $12,500 for a 32-page book of the same scope. My agent tells me that $11,000 to $13,000 is the usual range for an illustrator with my track record. Each book takes me about four months, so at $9,000 I earn around $2,250 a month on your projects.

For this book I'm asking for $11,500. That's less than I was paid elsewhere last year and in the middle of the usual range. I've kept it there because I value working with you and your team, and I want to keep doing it.

I'm happy to talk it through by phone if that's easier. I'm excited about this book and hope we can make it work.

Best,
[Your name]
