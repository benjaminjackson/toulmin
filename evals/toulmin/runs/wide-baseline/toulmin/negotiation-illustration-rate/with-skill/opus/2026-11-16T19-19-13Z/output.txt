I left out the line that "every other publisher pays at least $12,000," and I'd recommend you keep it out.

**What your facts actually show:** one other publisher paid you $12,500, and your agent says $11,000–$13,000 is normal for your record. That doesn't support "every other publisher pays at least $12,000." Your agent's own range starts below $12,000.

**What could go wrong with it:**
- Your editor knows what picture books pay. A claim about every publisher is easy for them to doubt, and if they catch one overstatement they may doubt your other numbers too.
- If everyone pays $12,000 or more, asking for $11,500 looks odd.

**What the draft says instead:** a real offer of $12,500 for a book of the same length, plus your agent's range. Against that, $11,500 looks like a reasonable ask rather than a reach.

I also didn't threaten to take the book elsewhere, since you didn't say you would. The $12,500 makes that point quietly.

The draft is about 200 words.

Subject: Fee for [Book Title]

Dear [Editor's name],

Thank you for offering me [Book Title]. I've loved working with you on six books over the past four years, and I'd be glad to do this one.

Before I sign, I'd like to talk about the fee. I've been paid $9,000 a book since my first one with you, and in that time our books together have done well. [Title] and [Title] have each sold more than 20,000 copies, and [Title] was chosen for the [State] reading list.

The rest of the market has also moved. Last year another publisher paid me $12,500 for a book of the same length. My agent puts the usual rate for an illustrator with my record at $11,000 to $13,000. Each book takes me about four months, so at $9,000 I'm earning well below that rate.

For [Book Title], I'm asking for $11,500. That's still below what I've been paid elsewhere, because I value working with you and want to keep doing so.

I'm happy to talk this through by phone if that's easier. I'm looking forward to getting started.

Best wishes,
[Your name]
