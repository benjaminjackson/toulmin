I recommend leaving the "cut new-hire training time in half" claim out of the letter. The letter below makes a narrower promise instead.

**Why the 50% claim doesn't work:** You don't know how long their training takes now, how it's structured, or how much of it regulators require. Your facts show you can train people well and run safe procedures. They don't show that you can halve someone else's program. In pharma manufacturing, much of new-hire training is documented, required content. A hiring manager there may read "I'll cut it in half" as a sign that you don't yet understand GMP. That would make your biggest gap, no regulated-industry experience, look worse. It's also a number they could hold you to.

**What the letter promises instead:** In your first 90 days you'll review their onboarding and bring back a written proposal. It will show where new hires could become competent faster without cutting required content. That's a promise you can keep, and they can check whether you kept it. If you still want the 50% figure, you could get their current training length from someone at the plant first. Then you'd at least know what you're claiming to halve.

**Other choices I made:**
- **The lab safety program comes first.** It's the closest match to their world: you wrote procedures, trained people on them, and can point to a measurable safety record. "Since 2019" with no injuries beyond first aid is seven years, which sounds stronger than "since 2019."
- **I added up your totals:** about 1,260 students over nine years, and about 18 new teachers trained at 12 hours each. The AP pass rate going from 41% to 58% is a 17-point rise, about 40% better than where it started.
- **The GMP gap is stated openly,** with a concrete plan for learning it. A plan is a better answer than hoping they won't notice. I didn't say you've already started studying GMP, because you didn't tell me that. If you have, add it, since that would help.
- **The LMS gets one sentence.** It's a small gap, and you already run five classes a year through a similar system.
