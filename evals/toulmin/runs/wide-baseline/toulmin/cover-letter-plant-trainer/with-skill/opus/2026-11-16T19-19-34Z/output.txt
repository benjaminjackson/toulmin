**Why I left out "half":**
- **Nothing in your facts supports it.** You don't yet know how long their training takes or what's in it. A hiring manager will see that you're promising to halve something you haven't seen.
- **It can sound like a risk in their industry.** In drug manufacturing, training is part of how the plant meets regulations, and auditors check it. Promising to cut it in half can read as "this person will cut corners." That hurts most when the letter also has to admit you haven't done regulated work.

The letter promises something you can deliver instead. Within 90 days you'll map their current new-hire program and bring them a plan to shorten it, with the compliance requirements unchanged. That gives them a date and a result to hold you to.

If you still want the bold version, put this in place of the fourth paragraph's last sentence: *"I believe I can cut your new-hire training time in half without weakening compliance, and in my first 90 days I would show you how."* Expect to be asked in the interview how you know.

**How the letter handles the rest:**
- **The argument:** your job has been teaching people to follow written procedures exactly around hazardous chemicals, and checking that they can. That's the core of this job too.
- **Lead evidence:** about seven years without a lab injury beyond first aid, under a safety program you wrote. Next comes your training of new teachers, because it's the closest match to training new hires.
- **The gaps:** the letter says plainly that you haven't worked in a regulated industry or kept GMP training records. It's better that they hear it from you. It also says you haven't used their learning system.
