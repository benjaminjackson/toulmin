Dear [Hiring Manager Name],

I'm applying for the Training Coordinator position at [Company Name]. For nine years I've taught high school chemistry, and much of that work has been the job you're hiring for: writing procedures, training people to follow them exactly, and making sure they still follow them when no one is watching.

In 2019 I wrote my school's lab safety program. In the seven years since, our labs haven't had a single injury that needed more than first aid. Each year, about 140 students, many handling chemicals for the first time, work under procedures I wrote and trained them on. That's about 1,260 students so far. I've learned that people follow a procedure when they understand why each step is there. A procedure they've only memorized doesn't hold up as well.

I also train adults. Every year I bring our new science teachers up to speed on lab procedures in about 12 hours of training each. Over my time at the school that's been about 18 teachers. They're experienced professionals who need to learn our way of doing things quickly and correctly, which I expect is true of many of your new hires as well.

I measure whether my teaching works. In the four years I've taught AP Chemistry, our pass rate has risen from 41% to 58%. I got there by finding where students were getting stuck and redesigning those parts of the course. I'd bring the same approach to your training program.

I'd want to work alongside your Quality team in my first weeks so I understand your documentation requirements before I change anything. The same goes for your learning management system. Within my first 90 days, I'd review your new-hire onboarding from start to finish. I'd then give you a written proposal showing where new employees could become competent on the floor sooner, without cutting anything your regulations require.

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
