I'm going to speak at our county planning commission tonight in favor of a housing project, and I want help writing what I'll say. The church I belong to wants to build 64 apartments on its back parking lot. All of them would rent to households earning under 60% of the area median income.

Facts:

- The county's own housing report says we are short about 2,100 affordable homes.
- The traffic study estimates 310 more car trips a day on Alder Road, and says the intersection at Alder and 4th would drop from a C rating to a D at rush hour.
- The plan has 72 parking spaces for the 64 units.
- 412 households are on the county housing authority's waiting list. The average wait is 3 years.
- I've lived on Alder Road for 19 years, two blocks from the church.

Each speaker gets 3 minutes.
</brief>



<document>
Good evening, Commissioners. My name is [NAME]. I've lived on Alder Road for 19 years, two blocks from the church. I'm also a member of that church, so you know where I stand. But I'm speaking tonight mostly as a neighbor, because I'm one of the people who will live with this project every day.

Your own housing report says this county is short about 2,100 affordable homes. That shortage has names. Right now 412 households are on the housing authority's waiting list, and the average wait is three years. This project would give 64 of those households a home. That's roughly one in every six families on the list, and every unit would go to households earning under 60 percent of the area median income.

The land is already there. The back lot is 1.8 acres of asphalt, and it sits empty six days a week. Nobody has to be displaced and no open space is lost. A parking lot becomes homes.

I want to talk about traffic, because I know you'll hear about it tonight and because I drive Alder Road every day. The traffic study is honest about it. It estimates 310 more trips a day on Alder, and at rush hour the intersection at Alder and 4th drops from a C to a D. I'll be sitting at that light too, so I'm not going to pretend that's nothing. A D rating means some extra waiting at rush hour. It doesn't mean the intersection fails.

Parking is covered. The plan has 72 spaces for 64 units, so every household gets a space and there are eight spaces left over. These cars won't be pushed onto our street.

So here's the trade as I see it. If you approve this, my neighbors and I wait a little longer at one intersection during rush hour. If you deny it, the lot stays empty six days a week and 412 households keep waiting, many of them for three years. Sixty-four homes won't close a 2,100-home gap. No single project will. The gap gets closed one site at a time, and this site is ready now, with a willing owner and a plan that already accounts for parking.

I've been on Alder Road for 19 years. I'd like the next people who move onto it to be families who finally got off that waiting list.

Please approve this project. Thank you.

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
