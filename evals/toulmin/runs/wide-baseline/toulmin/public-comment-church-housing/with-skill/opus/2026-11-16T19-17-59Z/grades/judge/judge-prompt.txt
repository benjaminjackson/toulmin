<brief>
I'm going to speak at our county planning commission tonight in favor of a housing project, and I want help writing what I'll say. The church I belong to wants to build 64 apartments on its back parking lot. All of them would rent to households earning under 60% of the area median income.

Facts:
- The church lot is 1.8 acres and sits empty 6 days a week.
- The county's own housing report says we are short about 2,100 affordable homes.
- The traffic study estimates 310 more car trips a day on Alder Road, and says the intersection at Alder and 4th would drop from a C rating to a D at rush hour.
- The plan has 72 parking spaces for the 64 units.
- 412 households are on the county housing authority's waiting list. The average wait is 3 years.
- I've lived on Alder Road for 19 years, two blocks from the church.

Each speaker gets 3 minutes.
</brief>



<document>
Good evening, commissioners. My name is [Your Name]. I've lived on Alder Road for nineteen years, two blocks from [Church Name]. I'm here to ask you to approve the 64 apartments planned for the church's back lot.

I want to start with what some of my neighbors are worried about, because it's my street too. The traffic study says this project adds about 310 car trips a day on Alder, and that the intersection at Alder and 4th drops from a C to a D at rush hour. I drive through that intersection. I'm not going to pretend that number is zero. A D means that some evenings I'll wait a little longer at that light. That's a real cost. It's also a modest one. It has been measured, and it's in the study in front of you.

Here's what's on the other side of the scale.

The county's own housing report says we are short about 2,100 affordable homes. Right now, 412 households are on the housing authority's waiting list, and the average wait is three years. Three years. A child who starts kindergarten the year her family signs up is in third grade before they get the call.

Every one of these 64 apartments would rent to a household earning under 60 percent of the area median income. That's enough homes for about one in six of the households on that waiting list.

And they'd be built on land that's doing almost nothing. That lot is 1.8 acres of pavement that sits empty six days a week. I walk past it. On a Tuesday afternoon, there's nobody there.

On parking: the plan provides 72 spaces for 64 units. Every apartment gets a space, with eight left over.

So the question in front of you isn't whether this project has any impact. Every project does. The question is whether a longer wait at one light at rush hour is worth homes for 64 families who are waiting right now. I'm one of the people who'll be sitting at that light, and I think it plainly is.

If you say no, that lot stays empty six days a week, and the 412 households on that list keep waiting.

Please approve this project. Thank you.
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
