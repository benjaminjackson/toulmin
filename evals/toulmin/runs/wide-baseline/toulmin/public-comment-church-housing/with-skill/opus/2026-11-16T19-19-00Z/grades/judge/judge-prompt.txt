I'm going to speak at our county planning commission tonight in favor of a housing project, and I want help writing what I'll say. The church I belong to wants to build 64 apartments on its back parking lot. All of them would rent to households earning under 60% of the area median income.

Facts:
- The church lot is 1.8 acres and sits empty 6 days a week.
- The county's own housing report says we are short about 2,100 affordable homes.
- The traffic study estimates 310 more car trips a day on Alder Road, and says the intersection at Alder and 4th would drop from a C rating to a D at rush hour.
- The plan has 72 parking spaces for the 64 units.
- 412 households are on the county housing authority's waiting list. The average wait is 3 years.
- I've lived on Alder Road for 19 years, two blocks from the church.

Each speaker gets 3 minutes.
</brief>



<document>
Good evening, Commissioners. My name is [NAME]. I've lived on Alder Road for 19 years, two blocks from [CHURCH NAME], and I'm a member of that church. I'm here to ask you to approve the 64 affordable apartments planned for its back parking lot.

I want to start with the part that affects me most directly: traffic. The study says this project adds about 310 car trips a day on Alder, and that the intersection at Alder and 4th drops from a C to a D at rush hour. I'm not going to pretend that's nothing. I drive through that intersection. I'll be one of the people waiting a little longer at that light.

And I'm still here asking you to say yes. Here's why.

The county's own housing report says we are short about 2,100 affordable homes. Right now, 412 households are on the housing authority's waiting list, and the average wait is three years. Three years is a long time to wait for a place you can afford.

This project would house 64 of those households. That's roughly one in every six families on that list. Every one of these apartments rents to households earning under 60 percent of the area median income.

And it does this on land that's already paved. The lot is 1.8 acres, and it sits empty six days a week. Nobody loses a home or a field for this. It's a parking lot.

On parking: the plan provides 72 spaces for 64 apartments. Every apartment has a space, with eight to spare.

So here is the trade as I see it. If you approve this, my rush-hour drive gets somewhat slower at one intersection. If you don't, that lot keeps sitting empty six days a week, and 412 households keep waiting, with 64 fewer homes coming to shorten the line.

If your concern is Alder and 4th, I'd ask you to deal with it directly. You could attach a condition to the approval that requires a review of signal timing or other improvements at that intersection. Please don't deal with it by turning away 64 homes.

I've been a neighbor on Alder Road for 19 years. I would like to welcome 64 more. Please approve this project. Thank you.
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
