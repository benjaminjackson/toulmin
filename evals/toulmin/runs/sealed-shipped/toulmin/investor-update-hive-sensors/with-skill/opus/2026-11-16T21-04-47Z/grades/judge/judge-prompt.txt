**Subject: HiveSense half-year update and bridge round**

Hi everyone,

Here's where HiveSense stands, and what we need from you before December 15.

**Growth**

We now have 31 paying beekeepers, up from 12 a year ago. Revenue over the last six months was $212,000. It arrives with the seasons: 70% came in March and April, as beekeepers prepared colonies for pollination.

Every sale earns a margin. A sensor costs us $38 to make and sells for $55. Each hive then pays $4 a month for the app. That means a hive brings in $103 in its first year and $48 a year after that.

**Results in the field**

Our largest customer runs 4,000 hives. He lost 18% of his colonies last winter, compared with 31% the winter before. Across 4,000 hives, that's about 520 more colonies alive. He says the sensors were part of it. He also changed his mite treatment that year, so we don't claim the whole improvement.

**Competition**

In May a large farm equipment company launched a similar sensor at $45. Theirs measures temperature only. Ours also measures sound and hive weight. Three of our beekeepers have asked to test theirs. We haven't yet decided whether to keep our $55 price. At $45, our margin on each sensor would fall from $17 to $7. App revenue would stay the same.

**Cash**

We spend about $95,000 a month and have $340,000 in the bank. By February we'll bring spending down to $75,000 a month by leaving two open roles unfilled. Even with that cut, and before counting revenue, we would start March with under $30,000. That's less than half a month of spending, just as the season that brings in most of our revenue begins.

**The bridge**

We're raising $600,000 on a convertible note with a 20% discount and a $6 million cap, closing December 15. One angel has committed $250,000, which leaves $350,000 to raise. Before counting any revenue, the full round covers spending at $75,000 a month until about October 2027. That takes us through the spring season with room to spare.

**Profitability**

I won't promise profit next year. Here's what it would take. After the cut, we spend $450,000 every six months. We brought in $212,000 in the last six. To break even, revenue has to more than double. Our number of paying beekeepers grew 2.6 times last year. If revenue grows at that pace this spring, profitability in 2027 is within reach. If it doesn't, it isn't. March and April will tell us which, and I'll tell you.

**The ask**

I'd like every current investor in this round. You backed us from 12 beekeepers to 31. This note carries us into the season that decides what comes next. If you're in, please let me know before the December 15 close.

Thank you,
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
