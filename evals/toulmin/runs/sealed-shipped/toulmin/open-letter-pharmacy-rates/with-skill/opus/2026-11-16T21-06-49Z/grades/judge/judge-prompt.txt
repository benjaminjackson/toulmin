<document>
**An open letter to the state employees' health plan**
*From the Main Street Pharmacy Alliance*

On July 1, the plan renews its contract with its pharmacy benefit manager. We are 38 independent pharmacies across the state. We ask that the new contract pay pharmacies at least what each drug costs us, plus a fee for filling the prescription.

Since January, the plan has paid us an average of $1.80 less per prescription than it costs us to buy and fill the 20 generic drugs we dispense most. These aren't rare drugs. They are the everyday medicines your members pick up month after month. Plan members account for about 22 percent of the prescriptions at our pharmacies. No pharmacy can fill more than a fifth of its prescriptions below cost on its most common drugs for long.

Some of us already can't. Two of our 38 pharmacies have told us they will stop filling prescriptions for plan members if nothing changes. Nine independent pharmacies in our state have closed in the past two years. We can't say how many of them closed because of rates. We can say that paying below cost makes the next closure more likely.

Your members would feel those closures most where they have no other choice. In 14 counties, the nearest pharmacy for most people is an independent one, and for most people there the nearest chain pharmacy is more than 20 miles away. For a state employee in one of those counties, losing the local pharmacy means a round trip of more than 40 miles for every refill.

Some of us make back part of the loss on brand-name drugs, but we don't know how much. We don't expect the plan to take our word for any of this. We will share our purchase invoices for all 20 drugs with the plan, so your staff can compare what these medicines cost us with what the plan pays.

At Reilly Drug in Carver, we filled about 3,100 prescriptions for plan members last year. We want to keep filling them. So do the other 37 pharmacies in our alliance.

We ask the plan to do three things before July 1. Write into the new contract that pharmacies will be paid at least the cost of the drug plus a fill fee. Review our invoices. And meet with us. I will meet with your staff any time before May 1.

The renewal is the plan's best chance to fix this before more of us have to choose between serving your members and keeping our doors open.

Tom Reilly
Owner, Reilly Drug, Carver
For the Main Street Pharmacy Alliance
</document>
