**An Open Letter to the [State Employees' Health Plan]**

To the [Board of Trustees / Director] of the [State Employees' Health Plan], and to the people of [State]:

We are the owners of 38 independent pharmacies across [State]. Every day we fill prescriptions for teachers, state troopers, road crews, office staff and retirees, the members of your plan. We are writing to tell you, and the public, that since January we have been losing money on many of those prescriptions.

For the 20 generic drugs we fill most often, the plan now pays us an average of $1.80 less per prescription than it costs us to buy and dispense the medicine. These are not rare or specialty drugs. They are everyday medicines that your members take month after month.

Plan members make up about 22 percent of the prescriptions at our pharmacies, more than one in every five. Last year my own pharmacy filled about 3,100 prescriptions for plan members. No small business can keep serving that share of its customers below cost.

This affects far more than our balance sheets. In 14 counties in [State], the nearest pharmacy for most residents is an independent one. When one of those pharmacies closes, people there can't just go to the store down the street, because there isn't one. They face longer drives, mail-order delays, or going without. Nine independent pharmacies in our state have closed in the past two years. We don't want your members in those counties to be the next to lose theirs.

There is a clear chance to fix this. The plan's contract with its pharmacy benefit manager comes up for renewal on July 1. Before that contract is signed, we ask the plan to:

1. **Require that pharmacies be paid at least what it costs to buy and dispense each drug.**
2. **Require the pharmacy benefit manager to show the plan what it charges for each prescription and what it pays pharmacies**, so the plan and the taxpayers who fund it can see where the money goes.
3. **Meet with independent pharmacists** before the new contract is finalized.

We are not asking for a windfall. We are asking to be paid what it costs to care for your members, so that we can keep caring for them.

Sincerely,
