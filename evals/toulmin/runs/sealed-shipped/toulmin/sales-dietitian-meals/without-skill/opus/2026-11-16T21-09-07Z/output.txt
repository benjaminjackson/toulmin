I've written the page, but I didn't use the line "our meals will lower your A1C." Your own numbers don't back it up yet:

- **Small, self-selected group.** You have results for 14 of 88 customers, and those 14 chose to share. People whose numbers improved are more likely to share them.
- **Not everyone improved.** 3 stayed about the same and 1 went up. "Will lower" promises a result to every reader, and that didn't happen for 4 of the 14.
- **Medication wasn't tracked.** If some customers started or changed a diabetes drug during those three months, you can't say how much of the drop came from the meals.
- **Regulatory risk.** The FTC expects health claims in ads to be backed by solid evidence. As a registered dietitian, your licensing board and the Academy's ethics code also hold you to that standard. A guaranteed health outcome is the kind of claim that draws complaints.

The real numbers still sell well when you state them plainly. That's what the page below does. It's about 400 words, and the brackets are placeholders for you to fill in.
