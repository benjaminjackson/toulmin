Commissioners, I've run a charter boat out of this harbor for 22 years. I'm asking you not to cut the summer flounder limit to two fish. We can live with three fish, or with a shorter season instead.

I know the stock is at 78 percent of its target, and I want it back as much as anyone. But this cut puts the pain on the boats that catch the least. Charter boats land about 9 percent of the state's summer flounder. Private boats land about 80. Whatever fish you save from charter customers is a small share of the total. The cost falls on a handful of small businesses.

I know that cost because I've paid it. When the limit was cut in 2016, my bookings went from 170 trips to 138 the next season. That's 32 trips. At today's price, that's $28,800 from one boat in one year. It took me two years to get back to 155, and that was still short of where I started.

This cut would run for three years. That's longer than it took me to recover last time.

Our charter association asked 70 past customers what they'd do at two fish. Forty-one said they'd book fewer trips. Only twelve said they'd book the same.

There are eleven charter boats in our harbor. Two of those captains have told me that if bookings drop like they did in 2016, they'll sell. That isn't my guess. It's what they've told me they'll do.

So I'm asking for a three-fish limit or a shorter season: something that helps the stock without putting charter boats in this harbor up for sale.
