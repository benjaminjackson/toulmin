Good evening. My name is [NAME], and I've run a charter boat out of [HARBOR] for 22 years. I run about 160 trips a season at $900 a trip, and I'm one of 11 charter boats in our harbor.

I'm not here to argue with your staff's science. If the stock is at 78% of target, it needs to be rebuilt, and nobody wants that more than the people whose living depends on it. My concern is who pays for the rebuilding.

Charter boats land about 9% of this state's summer flounder. Private boats land about 80%. A cut from four fish to two applies to both the same way, but it doesn't hit us the same way. A private angler still goes fishing. A charter customer decides whether a $900 trip is still worth it.

I know how that decision goes because I've been through it. When the limit was cut in 2016, my bookings fell from 170 trips to 138 the next season. That's a 19% drop and about $29,000 out of one small business in one year. Two years later I was back to 155, which means I never fully recovered.

This year our charter association surveyed 70 past customers. 41 said they would book fewer trips at a two-fish limit. Only 12 said they'd book the same.

So I'm asking the commission to do three things:

First, look at a separate limit for the for-hire fleet, or a three-fish compromise, so the sector that takes 9% of the harvest doesn't carry the heaviest economic load.

Second, review the limit after one year instead of locking it in for three.

Third, count what this costs the charter fleet as part of your decision.

We want this fishery to recover. We just want to still be here when it does. Thank you.
