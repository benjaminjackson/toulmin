I'd advise against saying the limit will put every charter boat in the harbor out of business. Your own numbers argue against that claim. After the 2016 cut you went from 170 trips to 138, and two years later you were back to 155. That was a real loss, but you stayed in business. The commission staff may have that history too, and if someone calls the claim an exaggeration, your other numbers lose credibility with it.

Your actual numbers are strong enough. The draft below uses them and makes a specific request, which commissions can act on more easily than a general objection. It runs about 270 words, which fits in two minutes at a steady pace.

<final_document>
Good evening. My name is [NAME], and I've run a charter boat out of [HARBOR] for 22 years. There are 11 charter boats in our harbor, and every one of them is a small business.

I take out about 160 trips a season at $900 a trip, for up to six people. That's how I pay my mortgage, my mate, my fuel, and my insurance.

We've been through a cut like this before. When the limit dropped in 2016, my bookings went from 170 trips to 138 the next season. That's 32 trips, about $29,000, gone in one year. It took two years to climb back to 155, and I never got all the way back. This proposal would last three years, not one.

Our charter association surveyed 70 past customers. Forty-one said they would book fewer trips if the limit drops to two fish. Only twelve said they'd book the same number. People don't drive to the coast and pay for a charter to take home two fish.

I'm not here to argue with the science. I want this stock healthy more than anyone, because my living depends on it. But charter boats land about 9 percent of the state's summer flounder. Private boats land about 80 percent. A cut that falls just as hard on the smallest share of the harvest puts a heavy cost on our harbor for a small gain to the stock.

I'm asking the commission to consider a 3-fish limit, or to review the cut after one season instead of locking it in for three years.

Thank you.
</final_document>

A few things to fill in or check:
- **Names:** Fill in your name and the harbor's name.
- **"I never got all the way back":** I added this line because 155 is still below 170. Cut it if bookings later went above 170.
- **The request at the end:** Change it if your association is backing a different alternative, such as a different limit for charter boats or a change to the season. Asking for something specific usually works better than just opposing the cut.
- **Timing:** Read it aloud with a timer once. If it runs long, cut the line about the mortgage first.
