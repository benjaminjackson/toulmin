**Let's Turn the Lights Back On at the [Cinema Name]**

When the [Cinema Name] closed in 2022, it was still selling about 180 tickets a week. People hadn't stopped coming. Since then, seeing a film on the big screen has meant driving to the nearest cinema, 38 miles away.

We want to bring it back, and this time the numbers add up.

**What we need**
We need $85,000 to replace the projector and sound system and repair the roof. If we raise it, the building's owner will rent the cinema to us for $1 a year for 10 years.

**Why it's within reach**
A local bank has pledged $25,000 if we raise the rest by June 30. That means we need $60,000 from our community, which is half of what we asked for last time.

**About last time**
Three years ago we tried to raise $120,000. We reached $47,000, fell short, and refunded every dollar. We learned from that. We've cut the goal by $35,000, secured a 10-year lease for almost nothing, and found a partner ready to put in $25,000 once we've done our part. Last time, this town gave $47,000. This time, we need $13,000 more than that.

**Who will run it**
46 volunteers have already signed up to run the box office and the snack bar. We have the people and the lease. What we still need is a working projector, a sound system and a solid roof.

**How you can help**
Give what you can before June 30. Share this page. Tell everyone who has made that 38-mile drive just to see a movie.

Each dollar you give gets us closer to $60,000, and reaching $60,000 unlocks the bank's $25,000.

Let's fill those seats again.
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
