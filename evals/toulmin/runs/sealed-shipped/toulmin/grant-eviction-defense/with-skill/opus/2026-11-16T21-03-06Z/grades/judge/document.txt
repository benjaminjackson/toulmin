**Project Summary: Tenant Defense Project**

The Tenant Defense Project requests $90,000 over two years to fund one of our two staff attorney positions. Without it, our capacity to defend tenants facing eviction will be cut in half this June.

Last year 1,140 eviction cases were filed in our county. Our two attorneys, each carrying about 80 open cases, represented the tenant in 163 of them. We turned away about 300 more because no attorney was free. Only about one in three tenants who asked us for help got a lawyer.

In June one of our two attorneys is leaving, and we have no other funding to refill her position. With one attorney, we could represent about 80 tenants a year, and the number we turn away would rise to about 380.

In the cases we handled last year, 58% ended with the tenant staying in the home or getting at least 60 days to move. We do not yet have a comparable figure for tenants without a lawyer. Starting in January we will request court-wide outcome data from the county clerk so our results can be measured against it.

The position costs $78,000 a year with benefits. This grant would provide $45,000 a year. A family fund has pledged $20,000 a year for both years, and we will cover the remaining $13,000 from our general budget. Our $60,000 county contract comes up for renewal in October, and renewal is not guaranteed. Together, this grant and the family pledge would fund 83% of the position whatever the county decides.

The departing attorney has agreed to spend her final month training her successor. We will report to the Foundation every six months on cases opened and how they ended.

Maria Chen, Managing Attorney
