**Project Summary**

[Clinic Name] requests $90,000 over two years ($45,000 per year) to hire a third staff attorney to represent low-income tenants facing eviction in [County].

**The need.** Last year, 1,140 eviction cases were filed in [County]. Our two attorneys represented the tenant in 163 of them, about 14 percent. We turned away roughly 300 more tenants who asked for help because no attorney was free. Each of our attorneys carries about 80 open cases, which is as many as we can handle responsibly.

**Our results.** In the cases where we represented the tenant last year, 58 percent ended with the tenant staying in the home or getting at least 60 days to move. That is enough time to find new housing.

**The project.** At our current pace of about 80 cases per attorney per year, a third attorney would let us represent about 80 more tenants each year, or about 160 over the grant period. That would cut the number of tenants we turn away by roughly a quarter. [One of our current attorneys leaves in June; that position will be filled by/with ________, so the grant-funded attorney is a net addition to our staff.]

**Budget.** The position costs $78,000 a year including benefits. The Foundation's grant would cover $45,000 a year, and the remaining $33,000 a year will come from [source]. [Clinic Name] also receives $60,000 a year from [County] under a contract that comes up for renewal in October [status/expectation].

**Measuring success.** We will track cases opened, tenants turned away, and case outcomes (tenant stays in the home or gets at least 60 days to move), and report these to the Foundation [every six months].
