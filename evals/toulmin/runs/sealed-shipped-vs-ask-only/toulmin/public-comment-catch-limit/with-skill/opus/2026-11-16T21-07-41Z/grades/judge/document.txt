I've run a charter boat out of our harbor for 22 years. There are 11 charter boats in that harbor. I don't dispute your staff's numbers on the flounder stock. I'm asking you to meet that goal with a 3-fish limit or a shorter season instead of cutting to 2 fish.

Here's what the last cut did to my boat. In 2016 I ran 170 trips. The season after the limit dropped, I ran 138. I lost almost one trip in five. Two years later I was back to 155. Today I run about 160 trips a season. Ten years later, I still haven't gotten back to where I was.

At $900 a trip, a drop like 2016's would cost me about $27,000 a season. Your staff recommend this cut for three years. That's about $80,000 for one boat.

And it isn't only my boat. Two other captains in our harbor have told me they'll sell if bookings drop the way they did in 2016. Our charter association asked 70 past customers what they'd do at a 2-fish limit. 41 said they'd book fewer trips. Only 12 said they'd book the same.

Charter boats land about 9 percent of this state's summer flounder. Private boats land about 80 percent. For a private angler, a 2-fish limit means a shorter day on the water. For us, it means customers who don't book at all.

So here's what I'm asking. If a 3-fish limit or a shorter season can get the stock where it needs to be, choose that. Charter boats land the least flounder in this state. Please don't choose the option that costs us the most.

Thank you.
