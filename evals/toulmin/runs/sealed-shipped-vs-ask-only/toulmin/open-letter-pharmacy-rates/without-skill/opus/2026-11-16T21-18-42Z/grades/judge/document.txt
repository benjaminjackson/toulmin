**An Open Letter to the State Employees' Health Plan**

From the Main Street Pharmacy Alliance

We are 38 independent pharmacies across this state. Members of your plan make up about 22 percent of the prescriptions we fill. That's more than one in five people at our counters. Some are teachers and state troopers, others are office staff and retirees. We want to keep serving them, but at your current rates we're losing money every time we do.

Since January, on the 20 generic drugs we fill most, your plan has paid us an average of $1.80 less per prescription than it costs us to buy the drug and fill it. That's the gap between what we pay our wholesalers and what the plan's pharmacy benefit manager pays us. A pharmacy can't absorb that loss on one prescription in five for long.

Some of us are already at the limit. Two of our 38 pharmacies have told us they will stop filling prescriptions for plan members if nothing changes. Nine independent pharmacies in this state have closed in the past two years. We can't say how many of those closings were caused by reimbursement rates, but we know what rates like these do to a small pharmacy's margins.

This matters most outside the cities. In 14 counties, an independent pharmacy is the nearest one for most residents, and for most people there the nearest chain pharmacy is more than 20 miles away. If the independent stops taking your plan, members in those counties face a long drive or a mail-order wait for medicine they need today.

You have a clear chance to fix this. The plan's contract with its pharmacy benefit manager comes up for renewal on July 1. We ask that the new contract pay pharmacies at least what each drug costs us, plus a fee for filling it. That's the basic cost of putting a prescription in a member's hand.

We will help you see these numbers for yourselves. We will share our purchase invoices for those 20 drugs with the plan, so your staff can check our costs against what the plan pays. We will meet with your staff at any time before May 1, so there's time to act before the contract is signed.

Your members count on us, and we're asking for a contract that lets us keep serving them.

Sincerely,

**Tom Reilly**
Owner, Reilly Drug, Carver
On behalf of the Main Street Pharmacy Alliance, 38 independent pharmacies
