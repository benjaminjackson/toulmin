**An Open Letter to the State Employees' Health Plan**

To the Board of Trustees and Executive Director:

We are the Main Street Pharmacy Alliance, 38 independent pharmacies across the state. We are writing because the plan now pays us less than it costs us to fill its members' prescriptions, and that can't continue.

Since January, the plan has paid us an average of $1.80 less than it costs us to buy and fill each prescription for the 20 generic drugs we fill most often. Each of those prescriptions loses money. Plan members make up about 22 percent of the prescriptions at our pharmacies, more than one in five. No small business can keep doing a fifth of its work at a loss.

Some of us have reached the limit. Two of our 38 pharmacies have told us they will stop filling prescriptions for plan members if nothing changes.

Here is what that means for your members. In 14 counties in this state, the nearest pharmacy for most people is an independent one. For most people in those counties, the nearest chain pharmacy is more than 20 miles away. If an independent pharmacy there stops taking the plan, your members lose the pharmacy in their own town. They will have to drive 20 miles or more for a refill.

Nine independent pharmacies in this state have closed in the past two years. We can't say how many of them closed because of rates, and we won't pretend to know. But we do know that every pharmacy still open is now losing money on its most common plan prescriptions.

The plan has a clear chance to fix this. Its contract with its pharmacy benefit manager comes up for renewal on July 1. **We ask that the renewed contract pay pharmacies at least what each drug costs us, plus a fee for filling the prescription.**

We don't expect you to take our word for the numbers. We will:

- **share our purchase invoices** for these 20 drugs with the plan, so your staff can check our costs for themselves; and
- **meet with your staff at any time before May 1**, wherever and whenever suits you.

Last year my pharmacy, Reilly Drug in Carver, filled about 3,100 prescriptions for plan members. They are teachers, road crews, office staff and retirees. We want to keep serving them. We are asking for a contract that lets us.

Sincerely,

Tom Reilly
Owner, Reilly Drug, Carver
On behalf of the Main Street Pharmacy Alliance, 38 independent pharmacies
