**An open letter to the State Employees' Health Plan**
**From the Main Street Pharmacy Alliance, 38 independent pharmacies**

Since January, the State Employees' Health Plan has paid us less than it costs to fill the generic drugs your members need most. For the 20 generics we fill most often, the plan pays us on average $1.80 less per prescription than we pay to buy and fill it. We ask you to fix this when your contract with your pharmacy benefit manager comes up for renewal on July 1.

Your members are a large part of our work. They account for about 22 percent of the prescriptions filled at our 38 pharmacies. My own pharmacy, Reilly Drug in Carver, filled about 3,100 prescriptions for plan members last year. Every one of those that is one of the 20 generics now costs us money to fill.

Some of us make back part of that loss on brand-name drugs, but not all of it, and not all of us can. No pharmacy can fill a fifth of its prescriptions on these terms for long. Two of our 38 pharmacies have already told us they will stop filling prescriptions for plan members if nothing changes.

The people who would lose the most live outside the cities. In 14 counties, the nearest pharmacy for most people is an independent one, and for most people there the nearest chain pharmacy is more than 20 miles away. When an independent pharmacy stops filling for plan members, or closes, state employees and their families in those counties have to drive that far for a refill.

Nine independent pharmacies in our state have closed in the last two years. We don't know how many closed because of what they were paid, and we won't claim to. We do know that a pharmacy losing money on the drugs it fills most cannot stay open for long.

We understand that your pharmacy benefit manager sets the rates we are paid. But the plan sets the terms of that contract, and those terms are open on July 1. We ask one thing: in the renewed contract, require that pharmacies be paid at least what the drug costs plus a fee for filling it.

You don't have to take our word for what these drugs cost. I will share our purchase invoices for all 20 drugs with your staff, and I will meet with them at any time before May 1.

We have filled prescriptions for your members for years, and we want to keep doing it. For that, the plan has to pay at least what the medicine costs us.

Tom Reilly
Owner, Reilly Drug, Carver
On behalf of the Main Street Pharmacy Alliance
