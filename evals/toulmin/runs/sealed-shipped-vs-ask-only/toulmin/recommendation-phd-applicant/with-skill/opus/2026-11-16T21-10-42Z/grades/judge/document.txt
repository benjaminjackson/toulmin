Dear Admissions Committee,

I am writing in strong support of Priya Nair's application to your PhD program in marine ecology. I have taught biology at Westbrook College for seventeen years, and Priya is one of the three best students I have taught in that time.

I know her work well. She took three courses with me and earned the top grade in a class of 28 in two of them. I also supervised her senior thesis. Of the roughly thirty theses I have supervised, hers asked the most of its author in the field.

Priya compared oyster growth at six sites in our bay over fourteen months. She did all of the fieldwork herself, about ninety trips by kayak. That works out to one trip every four to five days for more than a year, through every season. Field ecology depends on this kind of steady, unglamorous persistence, and Priya showed it long before anyone required it of her.

Her data show faster growth at the two sites near the salt marsh, a pattern worth pursuing. This is a single season of work, and after a storm one site lost three months of data, so the result needs a second season to confirm it. With that second season, I expect the study to become a publishable paper. She has no publications yet. This thesis is the most likely first one. She has already presented it as a poster at a regional conference.

Last summer Priya worked as a paid field assistant at a state shellfish lab. For two weeks she ran the lab's water sampling on her own. Her supervisor there told me he would hire her again. A professional lab trusted her with its routine monitoring, unsupervised, before she had finished her degree.

A doctoral program needs students who can keep a long field study going on their own, and whom a lab can rely on when no one is checking. Priya has done both. I recommend her without reservation, and I would be glad to answer any questions about her.

Sincerely,

Dr. Anne Caldwell
Westbrook College
