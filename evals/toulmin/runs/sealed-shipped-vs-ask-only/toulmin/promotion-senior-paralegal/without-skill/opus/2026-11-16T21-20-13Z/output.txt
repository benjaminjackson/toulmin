Subject: Request for promotion to Senior Paralegal

Dear [Name],

I am writing to ask to be considered for promotion to senior paralegal in the January review cycle. I have been a paralegal in the litigation group for five years, and I believe my current work already matches much of what the role requires. I would like to meet with you in person to discuss it.

**Supervising and training paralegals.** A senior paralegal supervises the group's other paralegals. I trained all three paralegals the group has hired in the last two years, so I already do much of the work of bringing new staff up to speed. A senior role would let me do this formally and consistently.

**Workload.** I support four lawyers and worked on 23 cases last year, including two that went to trial. I billed 1,640 hours, against the group's target of 1,550.

**Systems for the group.** In 2023 I built the group's tracking sheet for court deadlines. Two other groups in the firm now use it.

I also want to be open about a mistake. Last March I entered a filing deadline incorrectly in that sheet. Another paralegal caught the error, and we filed two hours before the court's cutoff. The filing was on time, but it was too close. Afterward I added a second-person check to the sheet, so no deadline is relied on until a second person has verified it. That check now protects every group that uses the sheet. I think how I handled the mistake shows how I would handle responsibility for other paralegals' work: find where the process failed and fix it, rather than relying on people never making mistakes.

I don't know whether a senior paralegal position is currently open. If it isn't, I would welcome the chance to discuss whether the group's growth, with three new paralegals in two years, supports creating one.

Would you have 30 minutes to meet before December 15? I'm happy to work around your schedule.

Thank you for considering my request.

Sincerely,
[Your name]

A few notes:
- The letter doesn't mention the $9,000. The partner knows the pay difference, and putting the number in the opening request makes the cost the first thing they think about. If the meeting turns to pay, you can talk about it then.
- The letter is about 340 words. If you find out what you did on the two trials, adding one specific detail to the Workload paragraph would make it stronger.
- If any of the four lawyers you support would back you, ask them before you send this. Then add a line like "I've discussed this with [names], who support the request."
