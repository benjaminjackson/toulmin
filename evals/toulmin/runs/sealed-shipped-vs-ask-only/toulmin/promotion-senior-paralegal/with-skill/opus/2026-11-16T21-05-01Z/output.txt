Subject: Request for promotion to senior paralegal

Dear David,

I'm asking to be considered for promotion to senior paralegal in the January review cycle. I've spent five years in the litigation group. Over the last two, I've done much of the training and process work that the senior role involves, and I'd like to take it on formally, along with supervising the group's paralegals.

Last year I supported four lawyers on 23 cases, two of which went to trial. I billed 1,640 hours against the group's target of 1,550.

In 2023 I built the group's tracking sheet for court deadlines. Two other groups in the firm have since adopted it.

I made one serious mistake in that sheet. Last March I entered a filing deadline incorrectly. Another paralegal caught it, and we filed two hours before the court's cutoff. That was too close. In April I added a second-person check to every entry. Since then the sheet has logged about 600 deadlines, and none has been wrong or missed. I think that response is part of the case for this promotion. A senior paralegal is responsible for the systems the rest of the team relies on. When something goes wrong, the job is to fix the system as well as the single error.

I also trained the three paralegals the group hired over the past two years. Two are still with us. One left after eight months.

As senior paralegal, I would supervise the group's paralegals and keep building on the training and deadline-control work I already do. I understand the role carries a $9,000 increase, and I believe the work described above has earned it.

If the promotion isn't possible this cycle, I'd appreciate knowing what you would want to see from me before the next one. I'm glad to talk about any of this before the December 15 deadline.

Thank you for considering it.

Best regards,
Jenna Morales
</final_document>

The letter is about 320 words.

- **How to address him:** I used "Dear David." Most partners at a 60-lawyer firm go by first names with staff. If people in your group say "Mr. Kessler," use that instead.
- **The March error is in the letter:** You don't know whether David knows about it. I included it and put it next to the 600 clean entries since. That way, if he hears about it later, he hears it from you, along with how you fixed it. If you take it out, I'd take out the 600-entry figure too, because that number only means something next to the mistake.
- **The paralegal who left:** I said one left after eight months, with no explanation, because you didn't give one. If you know they left for reasons that had nothing to do with their training, like a move or another job, one short clause saying so would help.
- **Worth doing before you send it:** Ask one of your four lawyers whether they'd support the request. If they agree, add a sentence naming them. Right now the letter has no outside voice backing it, and a lawyer's support would be the strongest thing you could add.
