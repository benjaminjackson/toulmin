**Subject:** Request for promotion to Senior Paralegal

Dear David,

I'd like to be considered for promotion to senior paralegal in the January review cycle. I'd also like to meet with you in person before then to talk about it.

I've been a paralegal in the litigation group for five years. Last year I supported four lawyers on 23 cases, two of which went to trial. I billed 1,640 hours against the group's target of 1,550.

I already do much of what the senior role involves. I trained all three paralegals the group has hired in the last two years. Two are still with us, and one left after eight months. If I'm promoted, I'll hold a short weekly check-in with the paralegals I supervise, so that their questions and problems come up early and not when a deadline is close.

In 2023 I built the group's tracking sheet for court deadlines. Two other groups in the firm now use it.

That sheet is also where I made my most serious mistake. Last March I entered a filing deadline wrong. Another paralegal noticed, and we filed two hours before the court's cutoff. I can't promise I'll never make another error. What I could do was make sure that one person's error doesn't reach a filing. In April I added a second-person check to the sheet. Since then there have been no wrong or missed deadlines in about 600 entries. I'd bring the same approach to supervising: build checks that catch mistakes instead of counting on nobody making one.

I will also complete the state paralegal certification by June 2027.

Could we meet at a time that suits you before the January review? I'd value the chance to hear what you'd need to see from me in the role.

Thank you for considering this.

Best regards,
Jenna Morales
</final_document>

Some decisions you may want to change:

- **Pay:** I left the $9,000 out. David knows what the role pays. Naming the figure would make the conversation about cost when it should be about whether you're ready for the job. If he raises pay in the meeting, you'll be ready for it.
- **The trainee who left:** I included it, stated once and followed by your weekly check-in commitment. David almost certainly knows about it, and mentioning it yourself makes your other numbers more believable. I didn't give a reason for the departure because you didn't give me one. If it had nothing to do with the training (for example, the person moved away), adding half a sentence would help.
- **Greeting:** I used "Dear David," which is normal for an internal email at most firms. If your group is more formal, change it to "Dear Mr. Kessler."
- **Worth finding out before the meeting:** whether there's an open senior role, and whether one of your four lawyers would speak up for you. Either would strengthen your case, and neither needs to be in the email.
