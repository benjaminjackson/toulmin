**Subject: [Company] half-year update, and a bridge round we'd like you in**

Hi all,

In short: we're growing, we have new competition, and we're raising a $600,000 bridge to get us through the 2027 pollination season. I'd like each of you to take part. Details are at the end.

**Customers and revenue**
We have 31 paying beekeepers, up from 12 a year ago. Revenue for the half-year was $212,000. Our sales are very seasonal: 70% came in March and April, when beekeepers get their hives ready for pollination. Each sensor costs us $38 to make and sells for $55. On top of that, the app brings in $4 per hive per month.

**What we're seeing in the field**
Our largest customer runs 4,000 hives. Over the winter he lost 18% of his colonies, compared with 31% the winter before. He says the sensors were part of that, but he also changed his mite treatment, so we can't say how much of the improvement came from us. We find it encouraging, but it isn't proof.

**Competition**
In May a large farm equipment company launched a similar sensor at $45, which is $10 below ours. Three of our beekeepers have asked to test it. Their sensor measures only temperature. Ours also measures sound and hive weight, and that's how we pick up a weak colony. We don't yet know whether that difference outweighs the price for our customers. This spring's orders will tell us, and I'll report back on what we learn.

**Cash and costs**
We spend about $95,000 a month and have $340,000 in the bank. By February we'll bring spending down to $75,000 a month by not filling two open roles.

We're not profitable, and I won't give you a date for it until we have a model I'd stand behind. Here's the arithmetic: at $75,000 a month, we break even at about $900,000 a year in revenue. That means a half-year like this one would need to bring in about $450,000, a little more than double what we did. Whether we get there depends on how many customers come back and grow this spring, and on how we hold up against the $45 sensor.

**Why the bridge, and what it buys**
Without new money, and counting spending alone, we'd run out of cash around the end of March. That's in the middle of the season that brings in most of our revenue, and we'd be depending on those sales to stay open.

With the $600,000 and the cost cut, we have cash into late 2027 before counting any revenue. That takes us through the 2027 pollination season, which is the test that matters. It will show whether customers renew and grow, and whether the cheaper competitor costs us business. After the season we'll have real numbers to build a profitability plan on and to decide on a next raise.

**Terms**
- Convertible note, 20% discount, $6M cap
- $250,000 already committed by an angel investor; $350,000 still open
- Closes December 15

If you'd like to take part, please reply to me by **December 1** with the amount. I'm happy to talk it through before then.

Thanks for backing us,

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
