I didn't include the line saying their gift guarantees every child on the waiting list a place in January, because the numbers don't support it:

- **Cost of the whole list:** 61 children × $1,150 = **$70,150**.
- **What this group might give:** Last December they gave $1,240. If they gave ten times that, the match would bring it to about $25,000.
- **Even in the best case:** If the full $15,000 match were used, the total would be $30,000. That's still under half the list.

If the email makes that promise and most kids are still waiting in January, it could come back on you. Your former board member forwards these emails with her comments, and an unkept promise would give her an easy target. The match is a strong enough reason to give, so the draft uses it instead. Each matched $575 pays for one child's full year, and that's a promise you can keep.

**Please check before sending:**
1. **January start:** Can children funded in December really start lessons in January? The draft says so.
2. **Match rules:** Does the foundation count everyone who hasn't given since 2022 as a "returning donor"?
3. **Eastside line:** The second paragraph mentions Eastside without explaining the closure or apologizing for it. People who left over Eastside often respond better when it's named than when it's skipped. Your executive director should approve it, and you can cut it if they'd rather not raise the subject.
4. **Lesson math:** "About two months" assumes $1,150 pays for roughly 52 weekly lessons, so $170 covers about 7–8 weeks. Adjust it if your term is shorter.

The draft is about 200 words.
