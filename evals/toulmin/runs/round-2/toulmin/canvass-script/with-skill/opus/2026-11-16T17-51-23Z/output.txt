I didn't use the coffee line, and I recommend you don't either. **"Less than a cup of coffee a month" isn't true.** The levy is 19 cents per $1,000 of assessed value:

| | Per year | Per month | Per week |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical city home ($410K) | $77.90 | $6.49 | $1.50 |
| Typical Ridgeview home ($640K) | $121.60 | $10.13 | $2.34 |

$6.49 a month is more than one coffee, and Ridgeview homeowners pay more than that. The neighborhood association president will do this math. If a volunteer says "a coffee a month" at the door, it backs up the "blank check" letter that's already in the paper.

**What I did instead:** The script gives Ridgeview's actual figure, "about $10 a month." For retirees on fixed incomes, a real number sounds more honest than a coffee comparison, which can come across as brushing off their concern. It also says "a typical home" rather than "the average household," because 38% of households rent and "median" doesn't mean "average." If you still want a coffee comparison, "**less than a cup of coffee a week**" is true in both the city and Ridgeview.

**Check before printing:**
- **"Blank check" answer:** This depends on the ballot language actually committing the money to Sunday hours and keeping the Ridgeview branch open. If the measure doesn't say that, change the answer to "the levy is fixed and ends after six years."
- **Fixed-income answer:** If your state or county has a property-tax exemption or deferral for seniors, add one line about it here. It would be your strongest answer in Ridgeview. I didn't include one because I don't know whether you have it.
- **The 48–52 loss:** I left this off the card on purpose. It's useful when you brief volunteers, but at the door it doesn't give anyone a reason to vote yes.

**Timing:** Read at a normal pace, the intro, main pitch and close take about 60–75 seconds. The answers to common objections are only for when someone raises them.
