**Our students are exhausted in first period. Let them start at 8:30.**

I'm the nurse at [School Name], and I can tell you which class period keeps me busiest. It's first.

Last fall I logged 412 visits to my office during first period: headaches, stomachaches, students who had fallen asleep at their desks. That's more than second and third periods combined (198 and 171). By midmorning, the line outside my door thins out. Nothing about the coursework changes between 7:25 and 9:30. What changes is that students have been awake a little longer.

They are not getting enough sleep. When our health class surveyed students, 640 of the 890 who responded, more than seven in ten, said they sleep less than seven hours on a school night. Teenagers need eight to ten. This isn't just a matter of willpower or phones. During adolescence the body's internal clock shifts later, so teens have a hard time falling asleep early no matter how responsible they are. A 7:25 start asks many of them to learn while short on sleep, every single day.

That's why the American Academy of Pediatrics recommends that high schools start no earlier than 8:30. On April 14, our school board will vote on doing exactly that. I urge them to vote yes.

We don't have to guess how this might go. A district two counties over moved its high school start to 8:30 three years ago. Its first-period tardies dropped 31 percent, and its superintendent told our board that attendance rose by about a point.

That matters for more than the morning bell. Our four-year graduation rate is 84 percent, which means about one in six of our students doesn't finish on time. Students rarely fall off track all at once. It happens one missed first period, one failed morning quiz, one absence at a time. Attendance is one of the clearest early warning signs a school has. A change that gets more students into their seats, awake, gives more of them a real chance to finish. No honest person can promise that a later start will raise our graduation rate by a set number. But I know which direction it pushes.

The concerns about this change are real, and I don't want to wave them away.

Our athletic director is right that from November on, practices would end after dark, and some away games would pull athletes out of last period. Parents who drop their kids off on the way to 8 a.m. shifts would have to rework their mornings. And the district estimates the bus contract would cost an extra $180,000 a year.

These are problems to solve, not reasons to stay put. Practice schedules and lighting can be adjusted. Game times can be negotiated with conference schools. The building can open early with a supervised space for students whose parents start work early. As for the money, $180,000 is a real cost, and the board should weigh it carefully. It should weigh it against what the current schedule is already costing us in lost learning, missed school and students who never make it to graduation.

Every school morning I see that cost firsthand. It looks like a sixteen-year-old lying on the cot in my office at 8 a.m., not sick exactly, just worn out. I would like to see fewer of those students. Moving the start to 8:30 is the most direct way I know to make that happen.

Please contact the school board before April 14 and ask them to vote yes.

*[Your Name] is the school nurse at [School Name].*
