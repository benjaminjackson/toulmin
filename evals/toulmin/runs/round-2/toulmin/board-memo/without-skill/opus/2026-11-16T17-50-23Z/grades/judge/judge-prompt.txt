<brief>
Help me write a memo to our board. I run a small nonprofit newsroom with 9 staff. I want the board to approve dropping display ads and moving to a reader membership program from January.

What I have:
- Ads brought in $84,000 last year, down from $131,000 two years ago.
- We ran a 6-week membership pilot in the spring. 212 readers signed up at $8 a month. We did no marketing beyond two newsletter mentions.
- Our newsletter has 14,000 subscribers.
- A newsroom about our size in another state told me membership now covers 40% of their budget.
- Two of our seven board members come from advertising and sales backgrounds. Our ad sales person is part-time and has been with us for 11 years.

I want the memo to say that membership will replace our ad revenue within a year. Keep it to one page.
</brief>

<document>
**MEMO**

**To:** Board of Directors
**From:** [Name], [Title]
**Date:** [Date]
**Re:** Replacing display advertising with reader membership, starting January

**Decision requested**
Approve ending display advertising and launching a reader membership program in January, with a target of fully replacing ad revenue within 12 months and a formal board review at six months.

**Why change now**
Ad revenue fell from $131,000 to $84,000 over two years, a 36% drop. If that trend continues, we should expect about $65,000–70,000 next year and less after that. We aren't giving up $84,000 a year; we're giving up a declining source of revenue.

**What the pilot showed**
In a six-week pilot this spring, 212 readers signed up at $8 a month. Our only promotion was two mentions in the newsletter. That is about 1.5% of our 14,000 newsletter subscribers, reached with almost no effort, and it already brings in about $20,000 a year. [Add: X of the 212 are still paying today.]

A newsroom of similar size in [state] now gets 40% of its budget from membership.

**The math**
To match last year's ad revenue we need about 875 members at $8 a month. Allowing for payment fees and normal cancellations, our target is **1,000 members by the end of 2027**. That is about 7% of our current newsletter list. We need roughly 800 more members, or about 15 a week. That is less than half the pilot's pace, and this time we will be marketing actively.

**The plan**
- January launch with a dedicated campaign across the newsletter, the site and social media
- An ongoing membership ask in every newsletter and on article pages once ads are removed
- Member benefits [e.g., member events, early access, a members' newsletter]
- [Ad salesperson's name], who has been with us for 11 years, will [proposed role, e.g., move into membership and donor relations]. Their experience building relationships with our community is directly relevant to this work.

**Risks and safeguards**
The pilot's early sign-ups were likely our most loyal readers, so growth may slow. To manage this, we will report membership numbers to the board every month. At six months, we will present a full review against a midpoint target of 500 members. If we are well short of that, we will bring the board options, including adjusting pricing, increasing marketing, or bringing back limited sponsorships.

**Recommendation**
Approve the move to membership beginning January, with monthly reporting and a six-month review.
</document>

Score the document on the seven items below, each from 0 to 10, and give each score one sentence of reason.

1. agree: would this reader, including the one who resists, come to agree or act as the writer wants?
2. honest: no claim is stated more strongly than the facts in the brief carry, and no fact or number is invented.
3. works_the_facts: figures are derived from what the brief gave (rates, totals, the pace a target needs against the pace already reached, what happens if nothing changes), and each is put in its most favourable true form.
4. right_comparison: the alternative to what is proposed is described as the facts show it, including any trend.
5. qualification: any condition or hedge names something that could turn out either way and that the reader can check, with a date, a number and a consequence if it is missed. Give a score of null when the document needs no qualification.
6. objections: the resisting reader's objection is met, and no objection is raised that the facts in the brief already answer.
7. fit: the document has the form and length the brief asked for.

Use the whole scale from 0 to 10. A 5 is an ordinary competent draft.
